PM brief · drawn 2026-10-16

The tournament phone bar follows the event’s phase

What changes

On a phone, an organizer gets around their tournament with the bar along the bottom of the screen. Today that bar has only two states. Before the event is launched it shows the setup tools (Setup · Teams · Schedule · Site). From the moment it is launched it shows the game-day tools: Results · Check-in · Schedule · Chat.

A launched event can sit months before its first game. The owner’s U13 Battle of the Bats 2027 launched 259 days out, so for the rest of the year its organizer opens the app to a bar that leads with an empty Results screen and a Check-in screen that has nobody to check in. Meanwhile the work of those months (teams registering, coaches signing up, messaging the teams) sits behind More.

After this change the bar reads the same phase the event’s header already shows:

The header saysThe bar shows
DraftDashboard · Teams · Schedule · Site (ruled 2026-10-16; Setup in More)
Open (launched, before game day)Dashboard · Teams · Schedule · Chat
Game dayDashboard · Results · Schedule · Chat (ruled 2026-10-16; Check-in in More)
Completed / ArchivedDashboard · Results · Schedule · Chat (ask A3)

The switch to the game-day tabs happens at the moment the header changes to “Game day”: the event’s first day, or earlier if a game has already started.

Why it matters

  • The bar stops pointing at empty screens. For most of a tournament’s life, two of its four tabs lead nowhere useful.
  • The home screen is one tap away. The dashboard tells the organizer what needs doing months out, and today it is buried in More.
  • The header, the dashboard and the bar agree. The September redesign gave the header and dashboard one shared game-day rule. The bar was built before that rule and never learned it.

Who sees what

Everyone who runs a tournament on a phone: owners, admins, staff, treasurers and officials. Dashboard and Teams are open to every role, so each role gets the same four tabs. An invite-only event (the free plan) has no Check-in or Chat, so its Open bar is Dashboard · Teams · Schedule · Messages. Desktop is unchanged, because its sidebar lists everything.

Priority and size

Small. The rule already exists and only needs reading in one more place. No data or permissions change. Help copy that names the bar’s tabs gets one line.

Success looks like

  • An organizer months out opens their event and the bar reads Dashboard · Teams · Schedule · Chat, with Dashboard lit.
  • On the first day of the event (or when the first game starts) the bar reads Dashboard · Results · Schedule · Chat, the same moment the header reads “Game day”.
  • Nothing becomes unreachable: whatever leaves the bar is in More.

Plan · written 2026-10-16

The tournament phone bar follows the event’s phase

Owner ask, 2026-10-16 (on his phone, U13 Battle of the Bats 2027, header “OPEN”, 259 days out): “this tournament is well before its dates, why is check in and results in the bottom nav? I thought that was only for during the event.” Then: “mockup the bottom bar.”

1 · What the code does today

  • The admin’s phone bar (the tournament bar inside a tournament) picks its four tabs by the tournament’s status: draft gives Setup · Teams · Schedule · Site; active, completed and archived give Results · Check-in · Schedule · Chat. An invite-only event (no Check-in, no Chat) shows Dashboard · Results · Schedule · Messages once launched.
  • The event header, the dashboard (Draft / Weeks-before / Game-day / Finished boards) and the context strip above the bar all read the phase: Draft · Open · Game day · Completed · Archived. “Game day” means the event’s dates have arrived or its first game has started, and it lasts until the event is marked complete (redesign G1, 2026-09-29). The context strip, which the bar component itself renders, already computes that phase.
  • So a launched event reads “OPEN” in its header and shows the weeks-before dashboard, while the bar under it shows the game-day tabs. The free-tier review recorded the gap as a fact on 2026-10-12 (“it never reads game day”). Nobody had ruled on it.
  • The draft bar’s ruling (2026-06-23) set the model: a bar rewards the screens revisited in that phase, with Schedule fixed in the third slot so the bar never feels like a different app when it switches. When that ruling was made, “live” meant launched, because the Open phase did not exist yet.

  • The switch moment is the header’s game-day rule, read the same way (A2).
  • Nothing becomes unreachable. More already lists every row the bar doesn’t show, so Results and Check-in appear there during Open, and Dashboard leaves More when it is on the bar.
  • Chat’s unread count already moves to More whenever Chat isn’t on the bar. Chat is on the Open bar, so this changes nothing.
  • Teams carries its waiting count (teams to review) on its tab, as it does on the draft bar.
  • Roles: Dashboard and Teams carry no role gate, so every role gets four tabs.
