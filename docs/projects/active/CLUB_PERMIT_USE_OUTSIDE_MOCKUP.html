Your three permits would show nothing in “Outside it”. So the list isn't “everything at that diamond”. Your conclusion still holds, for three reasons:

  • aIt's a question about the park, asked on a permit. “Which of our bookings have no permit behind them?” has nothing to do with this permit's hours. A booking lands here only because this permit's dates happen to include it, so a season-long permit becomes the catch-all for every unpermitted booking at that park.
  • bIt's longest when it's least true. A club that has entered one permit but not the rest sees every other team's booking at that park. Coaches can't enter their own team's town permit yet. The list then reads “nobody typed it in”, not “we're on a field we don't hold”.
  • cThe same booking shows up on more than one permit. It's listed on every permit at that park whose dates include its day.

4The ask

Take “Outside it” off the permit. Add nothing in its place for now.
What changes
  • The permit window's Use band goes to three tiles: Held · Booked · Unused.
  • The four (up to eight) “Outside it” rows go. One row per field stays.
  • The Use export drops its “Outside the permit” sheet and summary line.
  • The Venue library help article drops the “Outside it” term and the FAQ sentence that cites it.
  • The §296 walk step that checks the figure is rewritten.
What doesn't
The booking forms' quiet line, the calendar read window's line, the house league generator's count, and the Permits list's Used column (which never counted it).
What you give up
The facilities person loses one list of the club's bookings with no permit behind them. Each booking still says so on its own form and in the calendar. If the club later wants that list for renewal (“we played Lions Park on Wednesdays twelve times without a permit”), it belongs on the venue as one quiet line, and gets its own drawing first.
Not recommended
Keep it, renamed “Booked outside the permit”, with each row named by team. That fixes the words but keeps the catch-all and the double-listing.
