What Stage 4 makes true

A parent who hears about the club opens one link on their phone. The first screen shows them the club in its own colours, whatever is open right now (a tryout, a house-league sign-up), and the club's teams. Two taps get them to a team, its schedule and its tryout. Every page has the club's name in the title and a proper card when the link is pasted into a group chat. A club with its site turned off, or a cancelled club, says so in one sentence on every page, instead of showing some pages and a system error on others.

Behind it: the club admin's Public Site page decides which sections show and in what order, and refuses a bad link with a reason instead of dropping it quietly. The head coach finds their team page's address, with a copy button, beside the schedule switch they already own.

Round 2 (ten real club sites, Milton Girls Softball in depth): the club can post a one-line notice when fields close tonight; every team's tryouts sit on one club Tryouts page that says open, not open yet or finished by itself; teams carry the birth years they are for, with a picker that answers "which one is my daughter?"; sponsors get a managed strip; and an About page holds role contacts, documents and policies.

Which ask decides the rest: Ask 3, the home page's shape

What the home leads with (what is open now, then the programs, then tournaments) sets the list of sections the editor controls (Ask 6), makes "Teams" a tab (Ask 4), and defines what "absent" means for a program the club does not run. Ask 1 (type and ground) decides how every frame looks, but not its structure. Asks 2, 5, 7 and 8 stand on their own.

The three questions that decide the shape

The prompt asked for these before anything was drawn. Each is argued from what the code does today, not from the plan's description of it.

Question 1

Whose page is the team page?

Today it is already split, and the split is clean. The club admin's team record edits everything the public reads about a team: its name, division, colour, the "about" text, its address, and opening or closing tryout sign-ups. The record already shows the public address with an "Open the public page" link, and its "What the club sees" panel calls these "Yours to run". The head coach controls exactly one thing: whether the team page shows the schedule (Staff only / Families / Public link). The coach portal never shows the page's address.

Recommendation: keep the split. The editor half of Stage 4 stays in the admin. The coach gets a door: the address, Copy and Open, beside the Public link choice they already own. The October ruling that took the "Public site" pill out of the coach's header does not stand in the way. Its reason was that the pill opened the club's home, which a coach cannot change. The team page's schedule is the coach's own setting.

Question 2

A club with house league as well as rep teams

The Club plan carries house league, so the code shows the League section to every Club org that has published a season. A rep-only Club org gets no League tab (correct), but its /league address still says "No seasons have been published yet. Check back soon."

Drawn both ways: a rep-only club (Lakeshore Lynx) and a rep + house league club (Harbourview FC). The rule: a section shows when the club actually runs it (a team, a published season, a tournament), and is absent otherwise. It is never empty and never "check back soon". A section the plan does not carry is absent from the editor too.

What families need from a club's site

The research came from three places: ten club-site platforms (RAMP, TeamLinkt, Spordle, SportsEngine, TeamSnap, LeagueApps, PlayHQ, Pitchero, Spond, and the platform behind Ontario's minor-hockey association sites), several real Canadian club sites read on a 390 px phone, and the privacy, safe-sport, link-preview and accessibility sources that apply in Canada. This tab gives the findings that shape the drawings, then the ones deliberately left out.

The seven promises the drawings keep

  1. Screen one answers "is there something for my kid, and is it open?" Every good site puts registration or tryouts in the first screen (NYBA's hero, Oakville's header button). The sites that bury it in a menu (most of the Ontario hockey network) are the ones parents need nine-step guides for.
  2. Find my team, then its schedule, in two taps. "Team Finder" is the most-used feature on every Ontario association site. Barrie Minor Hockey published a nine-step guide to reach a team's calendar.
  3. A tryout page that never dead-ends. When, where, how to sign up, and who to ask, before any prose. When tryouts are closed, it says when to expect them and who to ask; it is never "no longer available" or "coming soon".
  4. Nothing is typed twice and nothing goes stale. PlayHQ's strength: the site reads the live system. Schedules, teams, tryout days and the house-league season come from what the club already runs. Nothing on the site depends on someone remembering to update it.
  5. No player on a public page, by default and by guard. Hockey Canada's code of conduct (Sept 2026) requires consent before posting a minor's images or personal information. Canada Soccer warns that a name plus a club makes a child identifiable. Coaches appear only when they opt in, and never with personal contact details.
  6. The club's colour, always readable. White text on gold, orange, green or sky blue fails contrast, and so does a dark navy used as text on a dark page. The site maps any colour the club picks onto safe roles (fill, text on the fill, ink for links, border, focus), so no choice can produce an unreadable page.
  7. A link pasted in a group chat looks like the club. Messenger and WhatsApp show a title, a line of text and a 1200 × 630 image. Today every club page shares one title and shows an image only if the club uploaded a logo.

The rules that come from outside

Privacy of minors

Hockey Canada's Code of Conduct (effective 2026-09-01) requires appropriate consent before posting images or personal information of minors. Canada Soccer's Guide to Safety (s.13.16) names the risk: a full name with a club makes a child identifiable, and children in care or in custody disputes are the high-risk cases. Clubs that publish anything use first name and last initial, with consent at registration. The privacy commissioner found that a volunteer's name and contact details on a club page need consent and must come down when the role ends.

For us: no player data on any public page, ever (a build guard). A coach's name appears only by their own opt-in; contact goes through the club's address. The listing ends with the role, because it reads live staff records.

Safe sport and policies

Community clubs inherit their codes of conduct and reporting routes from their provincial or national body. Rowan's Law is a registration duty in Ontario, not a website duty. The norm on good sites is a visible "Safe sport and policies" door.

For us: give clubs a place to link their own policies (a short "Club links" list in the footer), and never claim compliance on their behalf. → Ask 10

Accessibility

Ontario's AODA binds organizations with 50+ employees and the public sector, so most volunteer clubs are outside it. Municipal recreation departments, a likely future customer, are inside it.

For us: build the public pages to WCAG 2.2 AA anyway: 4.5:1 text, 3:1 for buttons and focus rings, 44 px targets.

Quebec

The Charter of the French language reaches organizations' websites, and not-for-profits must serve in French. Whether a volunteer club's page counts is unconfirmed and needs counsel.

For us: out of Stage 4. Logged for /strategy before any Quebec club is sold to.

Link previews

Facebook and Messenger: og:title, og:description, og:image at 1200 × 630. WhatsApp: the head must sit in the first 300 KB, image under ~300 KB, title two lines, about 80 characters of description. iMessage reliably uses the title and image; it shows previews only between iPhones. A preview already sent is a snapshot.

For us: one generated card per club, team and tryout, from club and team data only. The changeable facts (dates) live on the page, not only in the image.

Search

Google treats a subpath on a shared domain much like any other; no evidence that a custom domain ranks better. Events need their own URL, date and address to be eligible for event results; invitation-only events are not. The sitemap helps new pages get found.

For us: teams and open tryouts in the sitemap; titles that name the team, the club and the sport; a team marked up as a sports team (never its athletes). Custom domains stay a later question for /strategy.

What the ten say, in one paragraph

Clubs put registration, rep and select tryouts and sponsors first, then the things that pay the bills beyond dues (tournaments, apparel, clinics), then volunteers and umpires, then safety and conduct. Families need four answers: how do I sign up and what does it cost, which division is my child, is tonight on, and when are tryouts. Every one of the ten fails the same way: seasonal words nobody took down, such as a tryout "register now" from September, a "2024" label, or a house-league page for last year. None of them shows whether fields are open tonight. Those two gaps are the biggest openings for a site that is built from the club's live data.

Milton Girls Softball (miltongirlssoftball.ca)

How it is built

TeamSnap's website builder (a managed WordPress site) with Google Forms for tryouts, TeamSnap forms for house league, and Google Drive for every document. Schedules, rosters and rain cancellations live in the TeamSnap app, not on the site. The only tournament content is a 2023 page that no menu reaches.

What is important to them, by prominence
  1. Select and Rep tryouts, and coach recruiting. The two biggest things on the home page are tryout flyers, and 8 of the 10 newest posts are tryouts or "coaches wanted".
  2. House league registration. Second in the menu, a full page with fees by division and birth years.
  3. Pride and brand. "HUSTLE. HIT. NEVER QUIT.", "every girl can play", growth counters (300 house league, 120 Select, 120 Rep).
  4. Sponsors. A home section, a page, a menu item and a sponsorship form.
  5. Inclusion. A grant for families facing barriers, a referral program, a beginner FAQ and an equipment page.
  6. Volunteers and umpires. A full umpire page with pay and training; "does not run without volunteers like you".
Where families are let down
  1. Tryout dates, times and venues are only in flyer images, with no text a phone can read aloud or search. The post text gives no fee or birth years.
  2. Stale where a parent looks first. The home page still says register now for tryouts that ended on Sep 13; the registration page still quotes a May 2026 deadline; a "2025 season" notice is live.
  3. No team pages, schedules or field status on the site. Rainouts: "Before 3:00 PM the Town of Milton may close diamonds… after 3:00 PM MGSI and/or the coaches will make the decision", announced in TeamSnap.
  4. Which division is my daughter? Answered only for house league, on the registration page. The Select page gives no ages; Rep has no page.
  5. Dead ends. Menu labels that are not links, a blank Resources page, "coming soon" on About Us, five policies listed as text with no link, a password page in the public menu.
  6. On a phone the hero fills the first screen, the menu is three taps deep, and the fee and board tables run off the screen sideways.
  7. Privacy. Older recruiting pages (2023–24), still public, show minors' photos, schools, grades and parents' phone numbers, and ten of fifteen board contacts are personal email addresses.
What a generated site must not lose

The proud voice and the purple; a one-page registration with honest caveats; fees with early-bird dates and divisions by birth year; the 15-question FAQ; the transparent Select page (timeline, uniforms, cost range); the umpire page; the inclusion pages; a contact form routed by topic; sponsor recognition.

Where the rules meet a public site and need a ruling (Ask 13)

The home's team grid. The list standard does not cover card grids ("its own treatment"), so no exception is needed, but the treatment must be written: no tile chevrons, a one-column fallback when a name is too long for half the width (drawn at 360).
No mono on the club's pages. The warm rule allows mono on eyebrows and chips; on today's public pages that is where the terminal feel lives. Worded for the club's pages only, because tournament pages keep game codes in the data face.
The chip's face. The standard still says a chip is the data face, uppercase, 10 px; the product builds Inter pills. The drawings use the product's pill.
Public type sizes. The drawings use only the product's steps (12, 14, 16, 20, 24, 32). A public ladder of its own (a 15 px body, say) belongs to The first look and Public Face Warm, not Stage 4.
Band and heading face. The standard says the display face for bands and column headings. A club that picked DM Serif would then show three families in one table. The drawings use the club's text face in capitals.
Past seasons. Today's public team page lists past seasons that lead nowhere. A door to one would be a new look-back surface; the drawings drop the list.
