Q1

Two forms, one screen, two different depths

“Why are payee, notes, tags optional in a hidden dropdown for payables but in the main modal for expenses?”

No principle behind it — half a pass

The payable form had eleven fields, so an earlier pass folded it into “what it’s for / how much” up top with the rest tucked into groups. The expense form never got the same treatment. The same four fields now sit at two different depths on two buttons that live side by side.

Your own screenshots make the case: the expense modal is the one that scrolls — its Description field is cut off above the fold — while the payable modal fits.

The one field that does not hide

Paid by stays at top level. It doesn’t describe the expense, it changes what the record means: choosing a family turns the entry into money the team now owes them, saved as a credit against their dues. A field with a consequence that size can’t be discovered behind a disclosure.

Q2 · Q3

Three bands of chrome before the first row of money

“The buttons sit under the expenses/payables tabs… there is no space between ‘Add tags’ and the description.”

Both confirmed — and Q3 now resolves by deletion

Both questions are about the same screen, so they share a frame. You cross three stacked bands before any data: hub tabs, sub-tabs, then a full-width strip holding Export and the two Add buttons — a strip carrying nothing on its left when the team has no tags.

The tag link was a second, smaller bug: it’s meant to sit on its own line, and does once a row has tags, because the chips push it down. With none, nothing pushes it. Since you’ve chosen to remove it (tags move into the edit form), that bug now disappears rather than gets fixed — and the chips stay, read-only, where they always were.

Why no Tags column

Tags are sparse by design — most rows will never have one, so a column would sit blank four rows in five, on a table that already carries five columns and folds into cards on a phone.

More importantly, Category already owns that slot. It’s the structured one, tied to your budget plan, and it’s what drives Budget vs. Actual. A Tags column immediately beside it would invite “which of these two do I fill in?” on every entry — two fields competing visually while meaning different things. Chips under the description stay out of that fight, and cost nothing on rows without them.

Two real gaps do get closed: payable rows show their chips too (today you must expand Payment details to see them), and tags join the export — today you can filter the list by tag, export it, and the tag is missing from the spreadsheet.

Q4

A typo in an expense is permanent

“Why can’t I edit the expense, I can only add tags?”

Never built — and there is no delete either

Expenses are the only money record in the coaches portal without a delete. Dues payments, dues credits, payouts, payment requests and budget lines all have one. A coach who types $1,300 instead of $130 has that number on the books permanently.

It stalled on a real judgement call: marking an expense paid writes an entry to the team’s ledger. Editing the amount afterwards, or deleting the record, would leave the ledger disagreeing with the list.

Unchanged “Payment details” keeps its chevron — expanding in place is a different intent from editing, and it stops the click so opening the pair never also opens the form. Mark Paid keeps its own labelled button on unpaid rows.

Why no “⋯” menu — the correction that produced a rule

The first draft of this put a kebab on every row. Surveying the Money hub showed that would have been the sixth idiom for the same job, not the third: pencil + row click (Budget Plan), a bare chevron with the row clickable (Dues roster), chevron + label (Payables, Payment requests), a plain text button (Mark Paid, Recategorize), and an icon-only pencil beside an unstyled trash (Dues drawer). No coach surface has ever used an overflow menu.

Sorted by what the action does, three rules fall out and nothing new is needed: open this record → pencil + row; show more of this row here → chevron; do one specific thing → a labelled button.

The kebab only existed to hold two items. Once Delete moved into the edit form’s footer — which Budget Plan had already established — one action was left, and one action doesn’t need a menu. Now recorded as a binding design decision, so a seventh idiom can’t appear.

Descriptive edits stay open on a paid record — rename, recategorise, re-note, re-tag. Amount and Paid by lock. Delete always works and reverses the ledger entry, stating the dollar consequence first.

Type is set at creation. A saved record never switches between expense and payable — see Q8.

What this uncovered

Marking something paid doesn’t record which ledger entry it created. The link is discarded at the moment it’s made, so “delete reverses the entry” currently has nothing to reverse against. Recording it is a small database change, and therefore a deploy step.

Records paid before that change are still safe: the entry can be identified by matching it, which is reliable precisely because no edit feature existed to change the description out from under it. Where two identical paid expenses make a match ambiguous, the delete refuses and says so rather than voiding the wrong one.

One trade this creates

Tagging gets slower. Today it’s two clicks in place; afterwards it’s open the row, edit, save. For a coach tagging a whole season of past expenses in one sitting, that’s a real cost. I think it’s the right trade — tagging mostly happens at entry time, in the Add form — but it is a trade, not a free win.

Q5

“E-transfer”, “etransfer”, “e transfer”

“Should payment method be treated like tags — free text but selectable from past ones — so you don’t have three spellings of the same method, in case we want downstream reporting later?”

Yes — and it’s already the odd one out

Payment method is raw free text everywhere it appears: coach expenses, payables, payment requests, and the club-level ledger. Nothing dedupes it, nothing suggests a past value. Your three spellings are exactly what happens.

It’s also the only free-text field left in its own form. Sitting beside it: Category is a structured picker, Payee is a search-past-or-add combobox, Tags is a search-past-or-create combobox. Payment method is the one that never learned.

And the reporting concern is live today, not hypothetical — payment method already flows into the payment-requests export and the club ledger, so downstream reporting is already reading a dirty field.

The part that decides whether this actually works

A learn-only list canonicalises on whatever the first person typed. If the first coach types “etransfer”, that becomes the entry every other coach then picks from — and you’ve cleanly standardised on the wrong spelling.

So the list ships seeded, not empty: E-transfer, Cash, Cheque, Credit card, Debit, Bank transfer, Other. Free text still works and still adds to the list — but the common cases are already correct on day one, and the club’s own additions accumulate on top.

I’d scope the learned list to the club, not the team, for the same reason: reporting that spans teams needs one vocabulary, and a coach joining a second team shouldn’t start from an empty list.

Worth knowing before you commit

This tidies methods entered from now on. Existing entries keep whatever was typed — so if you want clean historical reporting, someone eventually needs to merge the old spellings. The tag manager already does exactly that job (rename, merge, delete) and the same pattern would apply here. I’d leave that out of the first cut and add it only if the mess proves big enough to be worth a screen.

Q6

Recurring payables don’t exist

“How do we set up recurring payables?”

You don’t — there is no such feature today

Right now the answer is: add twelve payables one at a time, or paste a list into the importer. The portal does have recurrence — the schedule has a proper “repeat weekly” with a preview — but nothing in Money uses it.

The good news is the shape is already settled by that schedule feature: a recurring series is treated as a set of proposed rows you review before anything is created, which is precisely how the payables importer already behaves. So this reuses two proven pieces rather than inventing a third pattern.

What I’d deliberately leave out of v1

No linked series. Each occurrence is created as an ordinary, independent payable. That means no “edit this one / this and future / all occurrences” question, no series delete, no rule stored anywhere — and every one of them can be edited or deleted with the plain Q4 controls the moment they exist.

Skipping the series link is what makes this small. A true linked series is the expensive half of the schedule’s recurrence feature, and payables don’t obviously need it: a dome block that changes price mid-season is edited once, on the month it changes, not across a series.

Monthly is the cadence that matters — dome and field blocks, insurance, equipment financing. The schedule’s existing engine is weekly-only, so the date maths here is new even though the review-before-commit pattern isn’t.

Scope flag

This one is a new feature, not a fix — the other six items are corrections to things already built. It deserves its own plan and its own sign-off, and it shouldn’t hold up anything else on this sheet.

Q7

Nobody tells you which button to press

“How do we differentiate and explain to the user the difference between a payable and an expense?”

The explanation exists — just never where the decision happens

The help guide covers it well (“A payable is any commitment, not just a tournament”), and both empty states say it in plain words. But the empty state only shows when the list is empty — the moment you have four expenses, the distinction vanishes from the screen. And the help is behind the “?” in the corner.

So the explanation is present exactly when you don’t need it, and one click away when you do. Standing in front of two lime buttons, there’s nothing.

Your call on round 2 was to leave the list alone and let the empty states carry it — which is right, and consistent with Q2: a permanent caption line under the sub-tabs would have quietly given back part of the band we just saved. So this lands in two places instead of three, and the list stays clean.

What this covers, and the one moment it doesn’t

Never used it before → the empty state teaches the distinction, with examples. About to create one → the modal subtitle names it, on every entry forever. Confused later → the help guide, which already covers it well.

The gap is a coach browsing a list that already has rows, who won’t see a definition anywhere on screen. I don’t think that moment needs one — nobody reads a definition while scanning a list they’ve already built — and the modal catches them the instant it actually matters.

The honest complication — worth knowing, not worth acting on yet

The boundary is genuinely blurrier than the copy admits. An expense you haven’t marked paid is, functionally, a payable without a due date. That overlap is structural, and no amount of wording makes it disappear.

Which is why the copy above leans on the one distinction that is always true and always visible: a payable has a due date and appears on the Payment schedule. That’s testable by the coach, and it stays true regardless of how the two records are stored.

Merging the two concepts would be a much larger conversation — one that touches the Payment schedule, Budget vs. Actual and the importer. I’m not proposing it, only flagging that if coaches keep picking wrong after this copy lands, the concepts are the problem, not the words.

Q8

One Add button, and the choice moves inside

“Can we also just have 1 add button where in the modal we select between expense or payable?”

Yes — and it supersedes the subtitle just shipped

With one refinement: the type is pre-selected from the sub-tab you’re on. On Expenses, Add opens as an Expense; on Payables or Payment schedule, as a Payable. The common case stays one click and a form — the switch only matters when you’re on the “wrong” tab.
