Signed-in family accounts · Stage 4b · drawings

Families answer In, Out or Maybe in the app, and the coach sees who is coming

The parent taps In, Out or Maybe on each upcoming game or practice. The coach sees a headcount on the schedule, copies the answers into the attendance book in one tap, reminds the families who haven’t answered, and sees when a family changes their mind after the lineup is made. The parent’s sheet was drawn in Stage 4; the coach’s side is new. The four asks that change the shape of the stage come first in tab 10. The guardian tier stays off.

What the code says today, where the plan had it wrong (5)
  • The event window has no tabs any more. Attendance is a row under “On this event” that opens a full-screen attendance room: five count chips that filter, All in and Reset, one row per player, and a tap raises the four-choice sheet. The old inline RSVP editor was removed in September. The family’s answers go on the room’s rows.
  • The coach’s own words are already In, Late, Out and No reply. The family’s In / Out / Maybe would put the same words on a second record. So every family answer on a coach screen reads “Family said …”, and ask 9 renames the coach’s “No reply”.
  • There is no “Not playing” pool. The lineup builder’s pool is “Not in the lineup”, everyone not placed, and it shows no attendance. Its warnings read the coach’s book only.
  • The house-league coach’s page is a different page: read-only, outside the coaches portal, with no attendance book, no lineup and no settings screen. Its games and practices are kept in different records from a rep team’s.
  • On day one, nobody is connected. A rep parent connects only when the coach presses Send invitation on that player’s Guardians card, one player at a time, and nothing shows the coach a team-wide count. The live demo teams have none. A headcount that read “0 in” would say “nobody is coming”.

Each upcoming row says who is coming, on its second line

The Schedule’s list view on a phone at 390 px. The count joins the row’s second line, after the date, where the row already puts extra words. The top line stays as it is, so the record chip, Scrimmage and Game day keep their room. It counts each player once: your own mark wins, and a family’s answer counts only where you haven’t marked the player. Showing it writes nothing to your book.

Why one switch

  • It removes a whole state, “not asked yet”. With a per-event ask, a parent sees buttons on some events and not others, and has to wonder why.
  • The event form, which has no family toggle today, stays as it is.
  • The coach sends one kind of message, a reminder, with its 24-hour limit (tab 4). The first reminder does what “ask” would have done.

What it costs

  • Families can answer events the coach never meant to ask about. Those answers show only to the coach.
  • No email tells families a new event is open. They find it on their page, or when the coach reminds.

If you choose the plan’s shape (ask 1 B)

  • An “Ask families” button on the event window’s Attendance row, and an “Ask families if they’re coming” tick under “This is a scrimmage” on the event form.
  • Every row then has a fourth state, “not asked”, and the parent’s page shows buttons only on asked events.

The reminder is an email about a child

Which side of the line

  • It says only that an event is coming up and the coach asked whether the player is coming. No offer, price or sign-up. /legal reads it as not a commercial message on its face.
  • It goes only to parents who joined the app (ask 2), through the family email door, which honours the team’s unsubscribe list and fails closed. The build rule: no reminder path that ignores a stop or an unsubscribe, tested.
  • The join tick-box (“email me about {child}’s team”) is a privacy consent, not CASL consent, so the reminder doesn’t lean on it.

Open CASL flags you hold (second-parent memo)

  • (a) The reply path needs a real address. Closed for teams with the tier on (O1, O4).
  • (b) An unsubscribe matches the exact address; a “+alias” of it isn’t caught.
  • (d) A mixed first- and second-parent address gets the wrong Stop wording.
  • (e) A second parent’s copy carries two footnotes. The reminder would be one more email with both, unless ask 14 drops the Stop line for parents who joined.
  • (c), the sign-up and stop race, was closed by the Stage 4a database rule.

The answer is a record about a child

  • /legal wrote in Stage 4 that the parent tick-box is enough while the page shows team, times and places, and stops being enough when it shows attendance or notes. 4b crosses that on a literal reading. The read side is mild; the write side (a stranger answering Out) is the risk. Ask 5.
  • The bell carries no note (tab 6). The sheet asks parents to keep health details out.
  • The consent words need three lines added (/legal draft 0.2): a parent can answer once the coach turns it on; the coaches see answers and notes and when; the other parent sees your name and answer, not your note. New joins only; the sheet’s hint is the notice for parents who joined earlier.
  • How long notes and answers are kept is not chosen (ask 15). No retention promise is published until a purge exists.
