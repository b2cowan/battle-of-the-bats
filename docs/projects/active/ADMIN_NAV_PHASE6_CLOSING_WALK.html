One person, three portals, finding the way every time

Every navigation change has been walked on its own. This is the one walk that follows a single person: someone who runs a club, runs a tournament and coaches a team, moving between the three on a computer and on a phone. You sign in once and never type an address to get from one portal to another. After each move you should know where you are and how to get back, without thinking. Seven short walks. About 35 minutes.

Before you start

  • Nothing here changes any data. Every step opens a page, a menu or a sheet. There is nothing to read back afterwards.
  • Never type an address to move between portals. The only address you type is the first one in each walk. Moving by the sidebar’s doors, the phone’s More sheet and the Workspaces menu is the thing being tested.
  • Walk 1 to 7 in order. Each opens with a stop check.
  • The standard these steps land on is the navigation standard, rules N2 to N13: the club lists places only; the cell is one button; the foot’s doors are named by where they go; the More sheet keeps one order; pages are tabs; the “?” is in one place; one level down the arrow is the only way back.
  • The test person is one account that holds all three roles. If a step says you land somewhere unexpected, write down where, and which door you used.
