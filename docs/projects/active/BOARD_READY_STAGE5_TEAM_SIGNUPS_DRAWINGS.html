Three kinds of team sign-up, and only one of them needs anything new on the public side

A club’s own team already has a money line to its club: the club bills it, the coach presses “We’ve sent it”, and the club confirms. A team order for the banquet uses that line, so it lands in both books. Another club’s team has no such line, so it signs up the way a family does, through its own link, with a names-only list. What the list does, and doesn’t do

  • Only the name comes from the roster. No birthday, medical note or contact goes onto an event list (/legal).
  • A tick isn’t a parent’s consent, and the words never say it is. The team asks its families; the list says who told the team yes.
  • Meal is optional and never a health question. A blank meal reaches the club as “Not chosen”.
  • “We’re not coming as a team” answers the club, so the team doesn’t sit in “Not answered”. Families can still sign up themselves if the club lets them.
  • Changing it after sending: adding people raises the bill; taking people off asks the club, which decides under its refund policy .

The bill lands on the team’s Club tab beside the club’s other bills, shows the tax it includes, and pays the shipped way

Sending the list makes a Bill for event tickets from the event’s prices, due on the club’s “Teams pay by” day . Today’s team bills carry no tax and no event, so this is a new kind of bill, not an Allocation with a note on it . In the club’s books it splits into Event tickets and GST/HST collected, exactly as a family’s ticket does; in the team’s book it is one line to the club, with no tax split .

Teams coming to play are a tournament, with one new choice: who can enter

A Saturday jamboree with three neighbouring teams is a tournament: outside teams register without an account, pay as one team, pay by card through their own link, and the club can split one transfer across teams. What’s missing is the private version. Today a tournament is public or a draft, and “invite only” (the club adds every team) comes from the plan, not from the tournament . Why this isn’t built into events

  • Tournaments already do the hard parts for a team that comes to play: divisions, spots, a schedule, scores, entry fees, a pay link per team and shared payments.
  • An event with teams that play would need all of that again. A second team registration is the drift this repo keeps paying for.
  • The club’s own teams enter free on every plan (“Our team”), so a jamboree with Riverdale’s own teams in it works today.
  • Handed to the tournament side as one item, with its own drawing, if you rule 48A.
