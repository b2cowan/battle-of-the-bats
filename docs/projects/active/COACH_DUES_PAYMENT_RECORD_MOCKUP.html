The Receipt Book

Today an installment is simultaneously the bill and the receipt: "paid" is a stamp, with no amount, no date the money arrived, and no way to record part of it. This draws the separation — an installment stays a plan; a payment becomes a fact (what arrived, when, how much) — and shows what every screen it touches looks like afterwards.

Call 1

A payment record, or a part-paid amount on the installment?

Everything below is drawn as a payment record. The smaller alternative breaks the moment two part-payments land on one installment — argued under §1.

Call 2

What happens to installments already marked paid?

Proposed: each becomes one payment for the full amount, keeping its ledger link. The alternative is legacy rows the new code reads forever. Under §2.

Call 3

Does the ledger take the date the money arrived?

Drawn in §1: the coach types it, it defaults to today. This is what makes the month-by-month cash view honest — and it changes history's shape.

Call 4

Do credits stay credits?

Proposed: yes, untouched. A fundraiser rebate lowers what a player owes and is not income — the standing ruling. Payments end the need to fake them.

Call 5

Overpayment: automatic credit, or the coach decides?

Drawn in §3 as a one-question prompt with a recommended default, rather than a silent conversion.

Call 6

What do reminders chase once a balance can be partly paid?

Drawn in §5: the unpaid remainder of the overdue installment, and a thank-you for what's arrived. Today they chase the full amount.

The problem, measured on one family

Dues are set at $300 per quarter. The family decides to send $100 a month instead — same money, different rhythm, and by October two transfers have arrived. Here is what the product knows:

The family has sent

$200.00

$100 in September, $100 in October

The product records

$0.00

No installment fully paid — nowhere to put a partial amount

So the reminder says

$300.00

"No dues payments yet" — to a family that is paying

Every surface inherits this: the unpaid badge counts them, the "Haven't paid anything yet" panel names them, the digest and Ask the Front Office repeat it, and the team's income shows $0 from them.

The workaround makes it worse. The only place a coach can type a partial dollar figure today is a credit — and credits post nothing to the team's books. Log the $100s as credits and the export row reads "Paid $0.00 · Status Fully paid", the ledger is short $300 with no trace, and the Season Refund Calculator schedules that family to be refunded $300 it thinks they never owed. A faithful payer becomes a double-payout risk.

Recording a payment

One new door, opened from a player's drawer (and from the roster row's actions): Record payment. Three facts — how much, when it arrived, how — and the sheet shows where the money lands before anything is saved. Amounts spread across installments oldest-first; the coach never allocates by hand.

Record a Payment — Ledger, Omar New ✕
Amount received $100.00 Date received Call 3 2026-10-12 ▤ Method E-transfer ▾ Note (optional) e.g. from Dana's account
Where it lands
Installment #1 — due Sep 30 +$100.00 $100.00 already received · after this payment: $200.00 of $300.00 — $100.00 to go
↳ Posts $100.00 income to the team ledger, dated Oct 12 — the day it arrived, not the day you typed it.

New: the entire sheet. Date received defaults to today, so the quick case is two taps; a coach catching up on a month of e-transfers backdates each one and the books stay honest. Removed (by consequence): nothing — "Mark paid" survives as a shortcut that records a payment for the installment's full amount in one tap.

Call 1, argued. The smaller build — a part-paid amount stored on the installment itself — handles this frame, and fails the next real Tuesday: two $100 transfers land on the same installment and the product can only show their sum, one date, one method. Reversing the wrong one becomes guesswork, and the ledger can't tie an income line to an actual transfer. A payment record costs one more table and buys receipts that behave like receipts.

3

When a payment overshoots

Within the schedule, overshoot isn't a question — money rolls forward oldest-first, and the sheet shows it doing so. The question only arises when a payment clears everything left on the schedule and keeps going. That gets one visible choice, not a silent conversion.

✓ $50.00 more than Quinn's schedule — saved as an overpayment credit. Quinn shows "In credit" · counted in the season-end refund Call 5 — ruled: automatic

New: everything in this frame. Ruled (owner, 2026-08-13): the excess becomes an overpayment credit automatically — the product already has the "overpayment" credit type and the "In credit" status waiting for exactly this. The strip states what happened; a mistyped amount is corrected by removing the payment, which also removes the credit it created. Call 4 sits here too: the credit created is an ordinary credit, same as a fundraiser rebate — no new money concept, and the standing rebate ruling is untouched.

Changing dues after money has moved

The owner's question that started this: dues rise from $1,200 to $1,400 mid-season, and Omar has paid $200. Today the bulk flow skips him entirely to protect his receipts. With payments as their own records, his money no longer lives on the schedule — so the schedule can change and the receipts simply stay.

What chases a family now

The sharpest customer-facing edge of the old model was the email. Reminders are the only dues figure a family ever sees — there is deliberately no family-facing money screen — so when the email is wrong, the product's entire voice to that family is wrong.

New: the remainder figure and the received-so-far line, in all three reminder paths (send-now, 30-day, 7-day). Call 6: the wave still fires on the installment's due date — what changes is the number it quotes. Removed: a family with payments on record can no longer receive the "no dues payments yet" nudge; the unpaid badge and "Haven't paid anything yet" panel stop counting anyone with a recorded payment, which is what makes the Overview's count true again.

What this deliberately leaves alone

Credits stay credits

Fundraiser rebates, contributions and overpayments still reduce what a player owes and still post nothing as income — the standing double-count ruling holds.

Posted ledger entries are never rewritten

Payments add entries; corrections void and re-post. The audit trail only ever grows.

Two balances, two names

Outstanding (credits excluded) and rolling Balance keep their separate jobs and labels — this project makes their inputs true, not their definitions different.

Live seasons only

Dues move money, so nothing here opens in an archived season. The archive allow-lists are not touched.

Coach rep dues only

Org-level allocations, payment requests and the free-coach fee path have the same shape and are deliberately not in scope — the payment record is designed to be liftable later.

The table recipe

Every number lands in the shared money cell and column-heading recipe from the table-consistency pass. No new treatments.
