Ledger Toolbar Rows

The goal you named: everything above the table on one row, or failing that, organized on purpose. The reason it wraps today isn't the number of controls — it's that three pills spend ~330px announcing their resting state ("Every budget item", "Every tag", "All"). A filter at rest is saying nothing; the row can stop paying for it. Three options below, whole-toolbar at 1440, worst-case widths (longest labels) drawn deliberately.

Standing decisions honoured in every option: five filters of one shape (the 08-25 pill ruling); Export lives on the tab and exports what's shown; "Add a bill" is the tab's one create; the toolbar carries nothing about configuring vocabulary (your tagging ruling). "Cash on hand next to Add" was ruled 08-19 with a written flag to re-look once real — Option C takes that second look.

Option A · recommended

Quiet defaults — a pill earns its words only when it's narrowing

At rest a filter shows just its name: Item ▾, not Item Every budget item ▾. The moment it narrows, it gains its value and the olive tint — so an active filter is twice as visible as today, while resting ones shrink to a word. The row fits at 1440 with ~180px to spare even with Status narrowed, and it degrades honestly: with three or four filters active it may wrap again — which is then a row full of information, not defaults.

Option B

Two decks, on purpose — operate above, narrow below

If two rows are inevitable on smaller desktops anyway, make them designed rows: the top deck is what you do (view, the figure, Export, Add), the lower deck is a quieter narrowing strip — smaller pills, hairline above, clearly subordinate. Nothing ever wraps unpredictably because the split is fixed.

Option C

One Filters door — five dropdowns behind one button

The maximal squeeze: a single Filters button carrying a count of active narrows, opening a small panel with the five dropdowns stacked. Guaranteed one row at any width — but it puts a click between the coach and every filter, and it walks back some of the spirit of your five-pills decision (which existed to make narrowing one visible grammar). Drawn so you can reject it with confidence rather than wonder.
