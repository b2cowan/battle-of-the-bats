Sponsorships: a club sells a package, the business fills in one link, the logo shows where the deal says

Drawn on Riverdale Minor Ball in March 2027, its first season selling sponsorships, with a house league, a spring tournament and an HST registration. Built on Stage 4’s form (the sponsor link’s questions), the shipped sponsor list (Club Tier 4d), the team pay link, the coach’s sponsor invoice, the General ledger and online payments (Stage 12). Tabs follow the club’s path: get ready, make a package, offer it, the business’s page, follow the deal, the invoice, a cheque, where the logo shows, and next year. Card payment is drawn as it will be once online payments are on, and also as it must be until then (no card choice at all). Click any number, tag or red flag to read what it refers to. The last tab holds your asks, ranked by how much each changes; the first answer is my recommendation and is already selected.

The sponsor list moves here from the site editor, because a sponsor can now show on a division, a team or a tournament, not only the club site. Station I: a club with no packages yet lands on Deals, which says what to do first. Riverdale already typed six sponsors into its website last year; they arrive on the Sponsors tab untouched.

Why a place, and why these tabs

  • Not inside Money: a sponsorship lead works packages, offers and banners, mostly not money. Money already has nine tabs. The deal still lands in the General ledger (tab 7).
  • Who opens it: the owner, admins and the treasurer. A sponsorship volunteer gets in through one of those seats until Stage 3’s people groups exist (ask 12).
  • Setup holds the sponsorship form (a page one level down, Stage 4’s editor) and the invoice details (tab 6). Done once, so not on the working tabs.
  • The checklist is three rows of the shipped row list, not a wizard. Each row opens its window or page. It goes away for good once a package exists.
  • Plans: League Plus and both Club bands (ruled 2026-11-10). A Tournament or Tournament Plus organizer never sees this place; its sponsors are the tournament drawing’s (tab 10).

What this tab settles

  • “For” is a kind of place (the club, a house-league division, a house-league team, a tournament). “A club event” joins this list when Stage 5 exists. Nothing else about a package or a deal changes when it does.
  • The logo switch is the only item the product acts on. When it is on, a paid deal puts the logo at the deal’s place. Every other item is a line the club ticks by hand (tab 5). Switch it off for a package that is only a banner.
  • Full is the amber chip in the row’s one chip place. Open offers count (ask 7), so a business never opens a link for a place that has already gone. In the Offer window a full package is listed but can’t be picked, with its reason.
  • No delete: a package with deals is archived (the body’s last line). Deals keep the package as it was when they were offered.
  • Price is before tax. Whether a deal adds GST/HST is the deal’s switch (tab 6).

What goes out, and what doesn’t

  • One link per deal, like the team pay link: signed, no account, and replaceable with “Send the link again” from the deal page. That is also where “Copy the link” lives, for a club that hands it over in person.
  • The PDF isn’t attached. Attachments get mail filtered. The link page opens it.
  • CASL (from /legal): an offer to sell advertising is a commercial message, even to a business. There is no business-to-business exception. The club is the sender. The window requires one of four reasons (paid you in 2 years, asked in 6 months, gave you the address, published it for business), keeps it with its date, and the foot says it back. Referral isn’t offered in the first version. F9
  • The unsubscribe stops that club’s sponsorship offers to that address only. Invoices and receipts for a live deal still go, and so does family email to a sponsor who is also a parent. An address on the club’s family opt-out isn’t offered either. Nobody at the club can clear it. A business that says stop by reply or phone gets the deal page’s “Do not offer again”.
  • Send waits for the mailing address. Until Invoice details has one, the window’s foot says “Add your mailing address in Setup first” and the button is absent.
  • The club’s note is optional. The rest is written for the club and can’t be edited, so the package, the price and the tax are said once, the same way on the email, the link and the invoice.
  • The mailing address comes from the invoice details (tab 6). The product has no club address today F2.

The rules the page keeps

  • No processing fee line, by default. Ruled Nov 22 (ask 3): sponsorships have their own choice on the Payouts card, and its default is “The club pays it”, so the business pays exactly the invoice, as drawn here. A club that picks “The sponsor pays it” shows the fee as its own line before the business pays, with the club’s GST/HST on that line. That case isn’t drawn yet (round 2). Paying by cheque or e-transfer never carries a fee. F10
  • The logo line (from /legal) says the business may use the logo and lets the club show it as the package says. The card statement must show the club’s name before the page says anything about it.
  • “Send the link again” replaces the link. The old one, like a withdrawn offer’s, shows no business details.
  • The global error box (Stage 4) counts what is missing and names the first. Not drawn again.
  • Name and logo can change until the deal is paid, from this same link. After that the club changes them on the Sponsors tab. A business that comes back sees its answers and Change, as a family does on Your sign-up (Stage 4 ruling 20).
  • A full package can’t reach this page: open offers hold their place (ask 7). A withdrawn offer’s link says “This offer was withdrawn. Contact Riverdale Minor Ball.”, with the contact.
  • Tax shown is the club’s tax on the deal. A deal with the switch off shows one line, the amount, with no word about tax.
  • The club’s privacy link shows when the club has one (Stage 4.P). It collects a named contact and a phone, so /legal reads this page.

Where I disagree with the plan’s list F1

  • Payment and delivery don’t happen in a line. A banner can go up before the cheque comes in, and a club can be paid in February for a July banner. A single Offered → Delivered line would force one to wait for the other. So the state follows the money (Offered · Form received · Invoiced · Paid), the checklist counts delivery beside it, and Delivered is reached only when both are done.
  • “Form received” is usually skipped. When the business chooses a cheque or e-transfer, the invoice goes out at once, because their accounts payable need one to pay. The deal goes straight to Invoiced, and History still says when the form came in. A deal only waits in Form received when a card payment was started and not finished (ask 4).
  • Withdrawn isn’t one of the five. It is how an offer ends without a sale. It leaves the list for a “Withdrawn” fold under Delivered and frees its place.

The tax shape, as accepted (§9), and the two things it leaves open

  • The club setting is “Registered for GST/HST” with the number, off by default. It lives in Invoice details here, and Stage 7 camps read the same setting.
  • The deal’s switch shows only for a registered club. It is off by default on every deal, even for a registered club, because recognition alone usually isn’t a supply. The club turns it on for a deal that is real advertising.
  • The rate is never hard-coded. It comes from the province of the business’s invoice address, since for a service the place of supply generally follows the buyer’s address, and is named as the rate is (“HST 13%”, “GST 5%”). F3 A professional confirms this rule and the PST provinces. The product isn’t sold in Québec yet.
  • The deliverables are listed on the invoice, under the line, so the invoice says what was supplied.
  • Tax isn’t income. In the books the HST goes to its own line (tab 7).
  • One document: the paid invoice is the receipt, as the coach invoice is. A separate “Receipt” PDF would put the word that sits next to “tax receipt” in a club treasurer’s mind on a sponsorship (ask 10). Stripe’s own card receipt email isn’t turned on as a second document.
  • Our name stays off it. FieldLogicHQ’s GST/HST number and name never appear on a sponsor’s invoice, receipt or page. We aren’t the supplier (/accounting).
  • Drawn differently from the coach PDF, on purpose, for you to rule: a Subtotal, HST and Total block under the line, where the coach PDF has no totals row. A taxed invoice needs the tax shown against its base.

  • A place from a deal is changed on the deal, because the invoice names it. A typed place is changed here.

The rules of a renewal

  • This year’s price, with last year’s amount as a hint when it was different. A club that wants to hold a price changes it on the deal after it is offered. Before it is opened, that sends a corrected offer.
  • The place: the club place and a tournament carry over (a tournament by its series, if 2028’s exists, or the row asks for one). A division carries over by name if the new season has one. A team never carries over.
  • From one old deal too: a Delivered deal’s page ends with “Offer again”, which opens the Offer window filled in.
  • Nothing is sent until the press, and the foot says how many emails go.
  • The reason to email carries over only while it holds (from /legal). A business that paid within the last two years can be offered again, and the row says so. An offer that was never paid isn’t a purchase, so it isn’t ticked. An unsubscribed address can’t be ticked and can’t be cleared.

Ruled Nov 22 (C, with the club paying by default): sponsorships get their own choice on the Payouts card, separate from the registration choice, which stays as it is and never decides for sponsorships. Our fee, its rate and its tax are the same either way. Only who pays it moves.

Why the club pays by default: an invoice that says $1,130.00 should be paid with $1,130.00. Why the club may still pass it on: under the 2026-11-17 ruling, what a club passes on to its payers is the club’s own charge, and so is the card networks’ 2.4% cap (this fee is 3.63% of the total) F10. Passed on, the fee is part of the club’s sale, so the club’s HST applies to the line (no tax line when the deal’s HST switch is off). Owed to the build: whether that line is grossed up for the few cents Stripe takes on the larger charge. /accounting reviewed both cases: nothing changes in how we charge or record our fee F11.
