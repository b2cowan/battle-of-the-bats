Try it
  • Click the month name to jump by month and year. This matters for a player's birthdate, which today means paging back a hundred months.
  • Arrow keys move a day or a week. Page Up / Page Down change the month. Enter picks the day and Esc closes.
  • Typing still works. In the real field you can type "2026-10-31" or "Oct 31 2026", and the app reads it when you leave the field.
  • Today jumps to and picks today, and fades when today is outside the allowed dates. Clear empties the field. It only appears on optional dates, so a required date has no Clear at all.

4 · Phones and tablets UNCHANGED

Any touch screen, and any window 768px wide or narrower, keeps the device's own picker: the wheel on an iPhone, the calendar sheet on Android, the same on a tablet. People know these, they are built for a thumb, and they already follow the device's light or dark mode. That also means no 36px days on a tablet, below the 44px tap size the app keeps for touch.

5 · What it takes

Counted in the code today. The calendar gets built once and every field switches to it. The switch is routine work, but each screen still needs a look in the browser afterwards.

Order of work

  • Build the one calendar and its field, and a check that stops anyone adding a browser date field again.
  • Switch fields over portal by portal: coaches, then the club admin, then tournaments, then platform admin. Each step is small enough for you to walk.
  • Update the help guides where a screenshot or wording mentions picking a date.

Where the risk is

  • A date landing on the wrong day. This is the classic bug with a hand-built calendar. The calendar only ever handles plain dates like the ones shown in section 2, and this is what the review checks first.
  • Keyboard and typing. These have to be at least as good as the browser's, or regular users will notice it got worse.
  • Required fields and earliest/latest dates. The browser checks these for us today, so the new field has to keep doing it.

6 · Your calls

1

Is it worth it?

That means 116 calendar fields across the product (138 counting times), plus a guard so the browser calendar doesn't creep back.

My view: yes, but only as one piece of work and not screen by screen. The house date style alone ("Oct 31, 2026" rather than "yyyy-mm-dd") fixes the one place a form still speaks the browser's language.

2

How does the picked day look?

Solid olive, the field menus' chosen mark, or the lime of the primary button.

Recommended: solid olive. Our own rules keep lime for the one main button on a screen. An olive ring is ruled out, because that is already how today looks on every calendar.

3

Should a paired end date shade its range?

It's a small extra, and it shows the length of the season at a glance.

Recommended: yes, and grey out days before the start.

4

What about times?

The 11 date-and-time fields sit in the same windows as plain dates. Left alone, one window would show our calendar next to the browser's clock. Themed times for only those 11 would also leave the other 22 time fields in the browser's format, so the app would write a time two ways.

Recommended: all or nothing. Either every time field gets the time box (33 fields, all drawn as above), or times stay native everywhere and only the calendar changes. I lean towards all 33, because a time is the other half of most of these windows.
