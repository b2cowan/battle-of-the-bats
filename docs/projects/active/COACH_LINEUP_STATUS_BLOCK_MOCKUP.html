01 · The finding

The card is desktop furniture, worn on a phone

This block was drawn as one horizontal strip: mark, state, sentence, door, then the button on the right. Stage 3 re-cut everything around it for a phone — Setup became one row, the tools became four squares, the grid became one inning at a time — but the status strip was never given a phone form. At 390 it simply wraps, and a wrapping strip leaves its leftovers hanging on the left.

  • Line 2“Review” is orphaned. It is the last item on a wrapping row, so it drops to its own line and sits hard left with ~270 px of nothing beside it. On a row list that word belongs at the right edge — everywhere else in the portal it is.
  • Line 3The button is auto-width. “Mark ready · 6 innings open” takes ~248 px of a 345 px card and leaves the rest bare. Every other primary button on a phone screen in this portal spans its container.
  • CopyThe count is printed twice, 40 px apart. The sentence says “Open roles in 6 innings”; the button says “6 innings open”. On a desktop row those sit far apart and read as one fact restated at the point of action. Stacked vertically they read as a stutter.
  • ScreenThe batting order starts below the fold. Page header, meta line, view switch, status card, Setup row, tool row, stepper and dots come to ~456 px of the 734 px a 390 × 844 phone gives the body. Four rows survive. This card is the biggest block of the eight, and the only one still at its desktop size.

06 · The row below it

The green Setup row — you are right, and it is worse than it looks

You asked why that dropdown is highlighted green. Two answers: what it was for, and what it is actually doing.

  • IntentIt marks where a lineup begins. On a game with no lineup, this row is the only way to reach Auto-fill, so it was given the lime “do this next” tone. Once a lineup exists it is meant to go quiet.
  • DefectIt never goes quiet. The tone is decided once, when the screen opens, and never re-checked — deliberately, so the controls cannot shift under a coach’s thumb mid-tap. So: open an empty game, tap Generate, and the row stays lime for the rest of the session even though the job is done. That is exactly your screenshot — a full seven-inning lineup with the row still shouting.
  • ConsistencyNothing else in the portal is a lime row. Lime is a button or a chip here — every primary action, the game-day “Live” chip, an “on” toggle, a selected catalog chip. This is the only surface filled lime edge to edge with a title, a caption and a chevron in it. It does not read as “important”; it reads as a very large button, which is why it fights the Mark ready pill eight pixels above it.
  • LabelNothing on the closed row says it generates a lineup. Behind it the panel is explicitly split into Setup (format, innings) and Auto-fill (mode, generate, reshuffle) — and on a desktop those are two separate controls, one of them a button that literally says Auto-fill. On the phone they were merged into one unlabelled row, and “Auto-fill · Balanced” reads as a setting being reported, not an action you can take.

Why S1 over S2

S2 answers the question you asked. S1 answers it and three more: it removes the frozen-tone defect outright (there is no tone left to freeze), it names the action in the place this portal always names actions, and it makes the two rows at the top of the screen read by one rule instead of two.

The tradeoff, named: S1 makes Auto-fill a one-tap action, where today it is tap-row → panel → Generate. That is better on a phone, but it is a behaviour change, and the pill disappears after you press it. I think that is fine — it vanishes because you pressed it, and the filled lineup underneath is the confirmation — but it is the part worth your eye, because the original rule here exists specifically to stop this row changing under a coach’s thumb.

07 · Calls this needs

Two of these are yours, not mine

Call 1 · the duplicated count

Does the button keep saying “6 innings open”?

The ruling that put the count on the button had a reason worth keeping: a coach must not be able to press Mark ready without having read what is still open. On a desktop row that reason is load-bearing — the button sits an inch away from the sentence.

In option B the sentence and the pill are touching. The reason is served by adjacency, and repeating the number makes the row read twice. So the drawing says “Mark ready” and lets the caption carry the count once. If you would rather the count stay on the button, the pill becomes “Mark ready · 6” — it still fits at 390 and wraps at 361.

Call 2 · the Ready sentence

Does “an edit returns this to Draft” move into the check?

It is a true and useful promise. It is also a sentence about something the coach has not done yet, printed permanently under something they have. On a phone it doubles the block.

Proposal: the row says what happened — marked ready, at this time, with these innings still open — and the promise lives one tap behind it in the Lineup check, where the rest of the explanation already is. Desktop keeps it inline; there it costs nothing.

Not a call — flagging it

Option C is the one that answers the bigger problem

B saves 64 px, which is real. But the screenshot’s actual complaint, the one underneath the one you made, is that the batting order — the thing this screen exists for — begins in the last quarter of the phone. B gets it to six rows. Only C, which moves the commitment to the end of the job where it belongs, changes the shape of the screen.

I am not recommending C today because it relocates a control coaches have already learned, and that is worth deciding deliberately rather than as a side effect of tidying a card. If you want the screen re-cut rather than the card fixed, say so and I will draw C properly — including where the button sits when the order runs past one screen.

What I would ship

Option B for the status block and S1 for the Setup row, phone only, desktop untouched — plus the copy trims above. Together they give the top of the screen one rule: a quiet row reports, and a lime pill is the single thing you can do about it.

  1. The status block becomes one row below 640 px — mark, state, sentence, the Mark ready pill, chevron. Above that the strip is exactly as it is today.
  2. The Mark ready pill drops the count; the caption keeps it.
  3. The Ready state’s “returns to Draft” promise moves behind the row on a phone.
  4. The Setup row loses the lime wash and becomes the portal’s ordinary quiet row, in every state — which also retires the rule that froze its tone at open.
  5. An “Auto-fill” pill rides that row while the game has no lineup — it is the label, it is the green, and it makes generating one tap.
  6. Nothing else on the screen moves — view switch, tools, stepper, dots and the inning list stay exactly as stage 3 walked them.
