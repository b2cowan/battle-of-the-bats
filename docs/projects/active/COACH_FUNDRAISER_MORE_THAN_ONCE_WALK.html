Twenty-six checks and three calls, in eight parts. Part A

The board you already know, untouched

The load-bearing promise of this change is that it costs the ordinary case nothing. Before anything is recorded, the board must be exactly what it was — and it is worth looking hard now, because after Part C you will not be able to tell from memory.

A1

⚠ No chevrons, no indent, nothing new

Money → Fundraising → tap Chocolate sale to open its room. Look at the entries table.

Expect the board that shipped before this change. Every name flush against the left edge of its cell, its date inline under Received, Edit and Remove on every row. No chevron anywhere and no blank space where one would go. If any name looks nudged to the right, that is a defect and this is the step that catches it.
A2

Write down the fraction before you change anything

Read the quiet facts line above the table — "N of M players logged", and after it "plus N team entries" if the drive has any. Write both down here.

Expect them to be what they were. You will check both again in C6 and D3 — the players count must not move when somebody hands in a second time, and the team count must.
A3

Note a player who already has an amount, and one who has none

From the board, pick a player who is on it (they have handed something in) — note their name and the amount beside it. That is your repeat player for the rest of the walk.

Expect their row to have one date and one amount, with Edit and Remove on it. Nothing to open.

Part B

Every player is in the list

This is the half you actually felt on the §157 walk: the Record door hid every player who had already handed something in, which is exactly the three most likely to hand in again.

B1

⚠ Your repeat player is in "Who raised it"

Press Record in the drive's room. The drive is stated in the band at the top. Open the Who raised it dropdown.

Expect the whole roster. The whole team first, then a Players group holding every active player — including the repeat player you noted in A3, and everyone else who is already on the board. Count them against the roster if you like; nobody is missing.
B2

The sentence under it is gone

Look immediately under the dropdown, with a player chosen.

Expect nothing there. The line that said "N players already have an amount logged — change those with Edit on their row" is deleted. Choosing The whole team still shows its own sentence about no family share — that one stays.
The one note that survives is for a team with an empty roster ("Nobody is on this team's roster yet — log it for the whole team"). This fixture has a roster, so you should not see it.
B3

⚠⚠ The sentence names the resulting total

Choose your repeat player. Type 90 into Amount raised and pick today for the date. Read the green consequence line above the buttons.

Expect three clauses, the third of them new: the drive's total rises by $90.00 · a credit to that family (this drive pays 15%, so $13.50) · and "[player]'s total for this drive becomes $X" — where X is the amount you wrote in A3 plus $90.00. Check that addition yourself; it is the whole defence against recording the same money twice by accident.
This is the step I most want your eye on. The old behaviour refused a second entry outright. We took that away and replaced it with this sentence. If it does not stop you in your tracks when the number is wrong, the trade was a bad one.
B4

…and only for somebody who already has one

Without saving, switch Who raised it to a player who is not on the board. Leave the amount at 90.

Expect the third clause to disappear. Two clauses only — the drive's total, and the family credit. Nothing about a total, because for them $90.00 is the total and saying so twice would be noise. Switch back to your repeat player and it returns.
B5

Save it

Put Who raised it back to your repeat player, confirm the amount is 90 and the date is today, and press Save.

Expect it to save. No refusal, no 409, no "an entry already exists". The form closes and the room re-reads.
If this refuses, migration 287 has not reached the database you are pointed at. Stop here and say so — every step after this one depends on it.

Part C

One row, two hand-ins

The board's answer to "who raised what" has to survive at a glance, so a player stays one row. What is behind that row is now reachable.

C1

⚠ One row, carrying the sum

Find your repeat player on the board.

Expect ONE row, not two. Its Amount is what they had in A3 plus $90.00 — the same figure the form promised in B3. There is a chevron in front of their name now, and no Edit or Remove on this row at all: a total is not a thing you can correct.
C2

Received is the most recent, and the row climbed

Read the Received date on that row, and notice where the row now sits in the table.

Expect today's date — the later of their two hand-ins, because the column answers "when did money last come in from them". And the board is still ordered largest total first, so the row may have moved up past somebody it used to sit below. That is correct: they have raised more than they had.
C3

It opens onto the two hand-ins, oldest first

Tap the row (or its chevron).

Expect two rows to appear underneath it, stepped in, on a quieter ground: the older one first, then today's. Each shows its own date in the leading column (the Received column beside it is empty — the date is not repeated), its own amount and its own credit, and each carries its own Edit and Remove.
The amounts under the fold are quiet ink, not the money green. The green figure is the participant's total above them; its parts should not compete with it.
C4

⚠ The two credits add up to the row's credit

Add the Credit figures on the two hand-ins and compare with the Credit on the row above them.

Expect them to match exactly. The row sums what each hand-in was stamped with; it never re-multiplies the total by the drive's current rate. That distinction has teeth — the whole-team entry is stamped 0% precisely so a correction cannot mint a credit for a family the row does not name.
C5

Everybody else kept their row

Look at the other players on the board — the ones who have handed in once.

Expect their rows unchanged in every way but one: date inline, Edit and Remove still on the row, no chevron. The one change is that their names now line up with the folding row's name — an invisible step where its chevron sits — so the column reads straight instead of ragged.
C6

⚠⚠ The players fraction did NOT move

Read "N of M players logged" again and compare with what you wrote in A2.

Expect the identical fraction. Your repeat player was already counted; handing in a second time does not make them two people. This is the single most likely thing to be wrong in this build — four different places used to count players by counting rows, and they were the same number right up until today.
C7

The tiles rose by exactly what you typed

Read the three tiles at the top of the room — Raised, Team keeps, Credited to families.

Expect Raised up by $90.00, Credited to families up by $13.50, and Team keeps up by $76.50 — the two halves the $90.00 divides into. And Raised should equal the sum of the Amount column, counting each participant row once.

Part D

The whole team folds the same way

One grammar for the board. This was ruled knowingly against §157 step F3b, which liked seeing two team entries as two separate rows — so it is worth deciding again on the built screen.

D1

Record a second whole-team entry

Record → Who raised it → The whole team → 60 → today → Save. (If the drive has no team entry yet, do this twice so there are two.)

Expect one "The whole team" row carrying the sum of its entries, with a chevron, opening onto them the same way a player's does.
D2

Still no family share, on the row and under it

Read the Credit column on the team row, and on each hand-in under it.

Expect an em dash in all of them, and Credited to families unchanged by the $60.00. Nobody raised it individually, so nothing comes off anyone's dues — summing two zeroes must not have invented one.
D3

⚠ "plus N team entries" counts ENTRIES, and went up

Read the facts line again and compare the team count with A2.

Expect it to have gone UP by one — the opposite of the players fraction in C6, and deliberately so. Two hoodie tables on two weekends are two events; that count says entries and must keep meaning entries even though they now share a row.

Part E

Edit and Remove act on one hand-in

They moved off the summary row and onto the thing they change.

E1

Editing one hand-in moves only that one

Open your repeat player's fold. Press Edit on today's $90.00, change it to 100, save.

Expect the older hand-in untouched, today's now $100.00, the row's total up by $10.00, and the row's credit up by $1.50. The other hand-in's credit must not have been recomputed.
E2

⚠ The remove confirm names the date

Press Remove on that same $100.00 hand-in and read the dialog — but cancel it.

Expect the amount, the player and the DATE — "Takes the $100.00 logged for [player] on [date] off the team's books", plus what it takes back off their family's credit. The date is new: without it, two same-sized hand-ins by one player would raise two identical dialogs and you could not tell which one you were about to undo.
E3

Removing one leaves a plain row again

Now actually Remove the $100.00 hand-in.

Expect the player back to exactly the row they had in A3 — original amount, original date, no chevron, Edit and Remove back on the row itself. The fold disappears because there is nothing left to open, and the players fraction still has not moved.
E4

The team's entries come off the same way

Open The whole team's fold and Remove the $60.00 you added in D1.

Expect the team back to however many entries it had before D1 — one row with no fold if that leaves one, off the board entirely if that leaves none — and "plus N team entries" back to the number you wrote in A2.

Part F

On a phone

The board is a card list below 640px, and a hand-in has to read as belonging to the card above it without an indent to do it with.

F1

Record one more, then narrow the window

Record 50 for your repeat player again so there is a fold to look at, then narrow the browser to about 390px (or open dev-tools device mode).

Expect the participant card to carry the name as its title with the chevron beside it on the same line — not stacked above it — plus Received, Amount and Credit as labelled lines.
F2

A hand-in card reads as nested

Open the fold at phone width.

Expect each hand-in as its own card, inset from the left with a heavier edge down that side — so it plainly belongs to the card above rather than looking like another participant. Its date leads the card, its Edit and Remove sit at the foot, and the empty Received line is not drawn at all.
F3

Put the fixture back

Back at full width, Remove the $50.00 you added in F1.

Expect the drive back where the §157 walk left it — your repeat player on one plain row, Raised back to its earlier figure, and both counts in the facts line matching what you wrote in A2.

Part G

What a row is worth everywhere else

Four places counted "how many players" by counting rows. Three of them are behind screens; this is the one a customer can hold in their hand.

G1

⚠⚠ The export's Players column counts people

Record 40 for your repeat player one more time so they have two hand-ins. Go back to the Fundraising list and export it. Open the file and find the Chocolate sale row.

Expect the Players column NOT to have gone up. It counts distinct people who raised something, not rows in the table — a player who hands in three times is one player under a heading that says "Players". Check Raised did go up by $40.00 in the same row, so you know the export re-read.
G2

The ledger has two dated lines, not one moved one

Money → Ledger → Timeline. Find the fundraiser income from this drive.

Expect two separate lines for your repeat player — one on the original date, one today — each for its own amount. This is the thing the old model could not do: overwriting the amount also overwrote the date, so most of the money ended up filed in the wrong month.
G3

The family's dues show both credits

Money → Player dues → open your repeat player's record and look at their credits.

Expect two fundraiser credits from this drive, each dated with its own hand-in, and their family's position lower by both. Neither should have been recomputed when the other was written.
G4

Put it back one last time

Remove the $40.00 from G1.

Expect the drive, the ledger and the family's dues all back to where Part F left them. This walk should leave the fixture exactly as it found it.

The coach sandbox shows a fold

Nothing left saying players who have logged something are hidden.

Part I

Three calls

Tick the option you want. The tick is the answer — Copy findings prints which one you chose, so it travels without a note.

I1

Should the whole team really fold?

Ruled yes on the mockup, knowingly against §157 step F3b — which celebrated seeing two team entries as two rows. Now you have seen it built.

I2

Are the hand-in amounts quiet enough — or too quiet?

Under the fold the amounts are plain ink; the money green is reserved for the participant's total. The mockup drew it that way so a total does not compete with its parts.

I3

Is the consequence sentence enough on its own?

Recording $60 twice by mistake used to be refused. It now saves, and the form's third clause is the only thing standing between you and a quiet double. The rejected alternative was a soft confirm on a same-day, same-amount repeat.

⚠⚠ READ THIS BEFORE YOU TICK — the review found two cases where the sentence is SILENT, and they are the cases it exists for. Both are new, and both used to be caught by the database rule this change removes.

1 · Two tabs, or you and an assistant on two devices. Both open Record for the same drive before either saves. Both read "nothing logged yet" for Avery, both type $60, both save. Both now succeed — and neither form shows "Avery's total becomes…", because each one's snapshot was taken before the other's write. The safeguard is quiet in exactly the double it was built to catch.

2 · A dropped connection on a phone. The server finishes writing, the reply is lost, you press Save again. That used to hit a clear "an entry already exists for this player". It now writes a second income row and a second family credit, silently.

What is NOT affected: double-clicking Save in one window is still safe — that has its own guard, and the review confirmed it fires before anything is sent.
