Decisions · for the owner to rule on

Eight decisions, one paste-back

Each carries my recommendation. Pick a ruling, add a note where you disagree, and build the block at the foot to paste into the chat. Nothing leaves this page until you paste it.

A · The card skin

Does every kit card take one ground, the neutral hairline and radius 8?

Today three card skins sit on consecutive screens: the dashboard card (white · olive 15% border · 8px · flat), the Overview tile family (--white-8 · olive 30% · 12px · shadow — the One Thing card and the six tiles), and the table frame (white · neutral hairline · 8px). In warm the grounds coincide; in dark the tile family is a translucent 8% white over near-black while the other two are the opaque card — three greys for "a card". The portal has been converging on card + neutral hairline since the 2026-07-30 ink ruling (the Staff screen, the nudge panels, the table standard's F-10 on 09-06); the dashboard's olive tint predates that.

Recommendation: yes. One ground token (the table frame's), the neutral hairline, radius 8 — a card and a table on one page share an edge. Radius 12 stays the button/input radius the token table names it as.
B · Two card roles

Report card and door tile — two roles on one skin, rather than one card or three?

The dashboard card reports (no hover, the doors are in its foot band — the 2026-08-11 "reports, doesn't shout" ruling). The Overview tile is a door: the whole card is a link, it lifts on hover, an arrow sits in its eyebrow. That difference is real and a coach uses it. Everything else that differs between them — ground, border, radius, eyebrow face, figure face, bar height — is accident.

Recommendation: yes. CoachCard with a door role: the elevation, hover lift and arrow belong to the role, not to the screen.
C · The team Overview adopts the kit

Do "Your team at a glance" and the One Thing card move onto the kit — and does the One Thing card come with them?

This is the one visible change on the coach's front page: the six tiles take the kit's card (door role), its mono eyebrow and its Inter figure; the 5px lime bar becomes the kit's 12px bar. The One Thing card keeps its kicker, headline, chips, lime action and 3px accent edge, and takes only the skin underneath (radius 12 → 8, tint → hairline). The Overview page's own comment said it kept its tile language "so re-skinning it would not be a second card system" — then Money became one anyway. Screen 2 draws it whole.

Recommendation: yes, both. If the front page is to move at all, the One Thing card and the tiles should move together — a hairline board under a tinted, rounder card is the two-skins problem on one screen. The alternative is tiles only (rule "Change" and say so).
D · The Insights band

Does the Insights scoreboard band take the kit's eyebrow, figure and caption — keeping its borderless shape?

The band is deliberately not cards and stays that way. Its label is 10px (the badge step) at 0.12em, its figure the display face, its caption 11px. On the kit: the mono 11px eyebrow, the Inter figure, a 12px caption. Screen 3.

Recommendation: yes, type only.
E · The list toolbar

Does the row above a list become one component, with one lede role?

Five toolbar recipes across 15 files do one job with four gaps, two spacings below and the 44px tap floor hand-copied three times. CoachListToolbar takes a lede (one role: 12px, tertiary, 64ch — the Skills & Goals count line; the Roster's 14px fact joins it), a lens slot, an actions slot, and the sticky behaviour the Ledger already needs. Coverage's bold "4 of 12 players have a result" line is the report's finding, not a lede, and is left alone. The frame under the toolbar is already one class and the empty state already one component — a fourth wrapper around all three would add a layer without removing a copy, so the kit stops at the toolbar. Screen 4.

Recommendation: yes. Money's geometry is the kit's; the visible change elsewhere is 1.6px of spacing and one count line dropping a size.
F · The phone line

Do the three phone-line pairs fold into the shared one, in the secondary ink?

The practices build promoted a shared pair on 09-16 and named this project as where the other two fold in. The one open question is the line's ink: the shared pair inherits tertiary (a caption's ink); the two copies use secondary with the figure in primary. The line replaces hidden columns — data a coach reads — so it is not a caption. Screen 5.

Recommendation: yes, secondary. Results and Coverage are pixel-identical after; the Drills line goes one shade darker.
G · The rail

Does the rail of doors become one component, on Money's values?

Two rails; Money's folds to two columns above 940px and Skills & Goals' cannot, because the container rule was not copied; the chevrons are 12px and 16px. Screen 6. The Money Ledger rows and the Skills & Goals attention list sit in the same card slot but hold different things (records; findings) — they stay per-screen and are named to the F-17 row-list session for binning.

Recommendation: yes.
H · What stays different, on purpose

Confirm the list of deliberate differences the kit does not touch.

Money's plan-vs-actual bars and cash mini-bars (approved on their own mockup); the Ledger and attention rows (G); the Insights band's shape (D); the One Thing card's kicker, headline, chips, action and accent-by-kind; the Coverage report's own selector toolbar and bold finding line; the Roster's own phone reflow (owner-ruled phone chrome, 2026-08); the closed-season page (2026-08-18); and everything under F-17 (the div-row lists). Each is written into the kit's docblock as an exemption so the next reader knows it was seen.

Recommendation: confirm.

Product-manager brief

Coach shared style kit

What changes for the coach

Almost nothing they would name, and that is the point. Money's Overview and Skills & Goals' Overview already look like one product; after this they are one — built from one set of parts, so the next dashboard cannot come out a shade different. The screens that had quietly gone their own way come back into line: the team Overview's six tiles and its front card take the same card, the same small label and the same numeral as the money cards two clicks away; the Insights scoreboard's labels stop being the smallest text in the portal; the row of controls above every list sits at the same height with the same breathing room; and the one-line summary a card shows on a phone reads in one ink on the drills list, the results list and the coverage report alike. In the dark skin one thing a coach may actually notice: the Overview's cards stop being a different grey from every other card.

Why it matters

Each of these shapes was built well, once, and then copied by hand into the next screen — the Skills & Goals dashboard was copied from Money's on the owner's instruction, and had already drifted in three places by the third day. Copies drift silently; a reader sees "slightly off" without being able to say why, and the product reads as several products. A shared kit turns "keep these the same" from a discipline into a fact.

Customer impact

Every head coach and assistant, on the four screens they open most (Overview, Money, Skills & Goals, Insights) and on every list in the portal. If we do nothing, the next dashboard-shaped screen is a fourth copy, and the F-17 row-list work landing beside it will be matching a moving target.

Priority

Now, before anything else styles a dashboard — the owner's own close-out condition on the Development project. It sits beside F-17 (the row lists), which owns the row; this owns the card, the toolbar and the phone fold. The two are planned separately and built in the order the plan sets.

Success criteria

  • Both Overviews render from one kit; the second stylesheet is gone. Money's and Skills & Goals' pages look as they do today.
  • The team Overview and the Insights band pass the same eyebrow/figure guard the money cards pass.
  • One toolbar component behind every list; the five recipes deleted; the tap floor asserted once.
  • One phone-line family; the two private pairs deleted.
  • A build guard fails when a coach stylesheet declares a card, eyebrow, figure, toolbar or rail row outside the kit — the same "no thirtieth branch" pattern the season-close rule uses.
  • The layout gate's screen baseline re-measured on the Overview (the only screen whose pixels move) and green across the rest; both skins.
Full assessment · source-based, 2026-09-16

Coach shared style kit — plan

The file of record is docs/projects/active/COACH_SHARED_STYLE_KIT_PLAN.md; this tab mirrors it.

Origin and scope

Owner, 2026-09-14, on hearing that Skills & Goals had been given Money's dashboard grammar by copying the stylesheet: "let's make sure this is discussed when this project is over. Shared stylings are important for consistency across the app." The Development lifecycle re-evaluation closed 2026-09-16; this is that discussion. Ruled the same day: coach-portal first, door open (the kit on tokens so it is portable; tournament-admin and public-site candidates listed below, not drawn), and two projects with a wall — F-17 (the row-list recipe) owns the row; this project owns the card, the chrome around a list and the table-row-on-a-phone fold.

Foundation, and its state. The table standard's F-24/F-25 pass (ledger §194) is built on dev and uncommitted at the time of this assessment; its hunks sit in the coach stylesheet, the Development pages and both design docs, with five peer sessions live. This assessment reads the working tree as truth (it is what renders). The build lands as a private-index commit.

Method

Source-based, as the Development re-evaluation's §2–§3 were: every *.module.css under the coach area with a dashboard, overview, table, list or card in it or its selectors was read and the look-alikes diffed. Each finding is tagged Code finding (follows from what the code does), UX hypothesis (a coach would plausibly notice; not yet observed) or Proposal.

Built 2026-09-16 (plan §7.1). Every step landed in the order above. Three things the build corrected in the plan: the toolbar fold was three live recipes, not five (two had no callers; the practice document's action row is not a list toolbar); the kit's tap floor is ≤ 768, the standard's number, which retired ten rows of tablet debt; and every screen-set inner rhythm (padding, gap, accent) reaches the kit through custom properties, because the two stylesheets' order is not guaranteed and the One Thing card lost its padding to the kit's on the first build — found by measurement, fixed, and guarded (a caller reaching into a kit class now fails the build).

Door left open — not drawn

Tournament-admin dashboard (admin/tournaments/dashboard: drag cards, checklist, its own owner-ruled density system), the club-admin accounting pages (5 of ~180 selectors shared with the coach money pages — not twins), the public-site state cards. The kit is written on tokens so any of these could adopt it; adopting them is its own session with its own mockups.

Sibling wall — F-17

F-17 owns what a record row looks like (schedule chips, the closed-season shelves, tags, notifications, scouting) and whether a row list sits on the card. This project owns the card, the toolbar above any list, the table frame's phone fold and the rail of doors (F-17's bin C). Build order: this kit's toolbar before F-17's build, so F-17's lists sit under the same toolbar; F-17's "does a row list sit on the card" ruling before the kit's card is declared final, so one ground token serves both.
