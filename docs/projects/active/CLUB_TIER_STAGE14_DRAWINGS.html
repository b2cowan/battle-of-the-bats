Club Tier · Stage 14 · drawings · 2026-11-04 · round 2

One child, every season

Today a child is typed in again every season, and on every team. Their papers are collected again on a new record, and a parent’s connection points at one season’s roster row. This page draws the season window asking what the team is doing (14a), bringing a past player back, the “same child?” question on every add door, a child’s page across three seasons, joining duplicate records, and the parent link that Signed-in family accounts Stage 4 stands on. Only the window’s two choices are ruled. Everything else waits on the asks in the last tab.

What the code does today (read 2026-11-04)

Five readers went through the code. I checked each claim below at the cited lines before writing it here. The plan’s census from 2026-10-22 was mostly right. Where it was wrong is in the third column.

Already true, and worth keeping

  • The Families book already reads a rep child as one child across a team’s seasons, by following the confirmed history links. Stage 14 makes that a stored fact instead of something worked out on every read.
  • The season roll copies the child’s medical note, emergency contact, positions, bats and throws, jersey size and the coach’s pinned note into the new season. The plan said nothing carries. Documents don’t carry. These do, as copies that drift apart once anyone edits one.
  • The returning-player check and the Families book’s duplicate-parent queue both propose, then a person decides, and both remember a “no”. That is the model for joining children.

Broken now, and Stage 14’s to fix

  • The roll does both of the ruled answers at once. It always carries the active kids (“same group moving up”), and it always moves the birth years one year later (“new group, same age”). A team that keeps its kids gets its birth years wrong. A team that takes a new cohort gets last year’s kids.
  • A parent’s connection stays on last season’s row. It isn’t moved, and it isn’t closed. That parent still gets the team’s schedule, so access doesn’t end with the season. A new invitation to this season’s row is refused: “That address already has a connection to this player.” The guardian tier is off, so no family meets this today.
  • Accepting a tryout for a child who is already on the roster makes a second entry, with no warning.
  • On the tryout list, the returning-player chip shows a strong match and a weak one with the same words. The strong tier lets twins through: same birth date, same family email, different first name.

Where the records were wrong

  • The data dictionary says a parent’s link “is re-established at continuity-confirm”. No code does that. Corrected today.
  • The coach’s Start next season window promises “each returning player’s profile will offer to bring their open goals forward”. That offer was removed on 2026-09-16. Its first line also reads “This closes the 2026 Season season.” 14a fixes both.
  • The plan says a “released” player isn’t carried. There is no released status. What isn’t carried is inactive players and call-ups.
  • Standalone coaches have no family library. The Families book is a League and Club feature, and nothing on the coach side reads it.

Also found (not Stage 14’s; recorded in the plan as findings)

  • When a roster entry is deleted, the check for what hangs on it skips dated notes, observations and goal reviews. An entry holding only notes can be deleted, and the notes go with it. Added to the task list.
  • The test that keeps coaches’ private notes out of the family file checks old table names. It would not catch a new one being added.
  • The club admin’s roster read shows medical notes for any season, without the coach-side privacy filter. That is by design today. Ask 9 decides whether that holds once a child spans teams.

What the coach gains

  • A returning child is picked and not retyped. Their birth date, family, positions and medical note come back as they were last recorded.
  • Every row names the season it reads from (“Last played the 2025 Season”), so a stale phone number is visibly old.
  • Unpaid dues from a past season are shown and left on that season’s book. Nothing moves money. The birth-year flag warns and never blocks (Ask 12).

The look-back rule: the three questions

  • Record or instrument? A record read that feeds one write to the working season, the add. Nothing in the picker changes a past season.
  • Does the whole subtree carry the year? The picker has no subtree. Picking adds to 2027, and no row opens the old season.
  • Could the coach tell which season they are reading? Yes, each row names its season. The build registers the route on purpose as a cross-season reader; it does not take a year.

Held, on purpose

  • Children from other teams or the house league aren’t in this list until Ask 9 (privacy) is ruled. That needs the club-level record from Ask 5, which this is built on.
  • Call-ups appear only if they once played for this team.
  • An assistant without the roster-details grant sees names and seasons only.

“Same child?” on every add door

When what is typed matches a child this team has had, the door asks before it adds anyone. A match is only ever a question. Nothing joins without a yes (Ask 4). The question uses the same rule everywhere: the same first and last name, checked against this team’s seasons. A birth date or the family’s email makes the question more certain. It never answers it.

What the record is

  • One child, with each season’s entry pointing at them. An entry keeps what belongs to that season: number, positions, status, dues, attendance, stats and the coach’s notes.
  • What belongs to the child is kept once: name, birth date, medical note, emergency contact, documents with their history, and the parents.

Documents, honestly

A waiver is usually good for one season, so the win isn’t skipping it. The win is one place with its history and a plain “Needs signing for 2027” state. The club decides, per document, whether it is signed each season or good until replaced (Ask 10). Photo consent doesn’t exist as a document today. It would be added as a document like any other.

What the coach does not see

The coach’s page lists seasons with their own team only. Maya’s house-league season shows on the club’s page and not the coach’s, until Ask 9 is ruled.

Joining records that are one child (14d)

Every club already has years of the same child typed again. The Families book’s duplicate-parent queue is the model, extended: a Parents · Children switch on the same Duplicates tab, with the same card. Records the roll already linked, or a coach already confirmed, are joined when the build first runs, because those links were made from a known fact or by a person. Everything else is proposed here.
