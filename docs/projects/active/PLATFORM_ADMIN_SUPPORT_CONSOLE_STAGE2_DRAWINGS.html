Stage 2 changes the front half of every support job: find the customer, then read what has happened to them. Every drawing is at true size (1440 pixels wide, a desk screen). On a narrower window it scrolls sideways inside its own box. Before is a capture from today's dev server, in the Stage 1 look you walked on Oct 25. Anything marked example is invented for the drawing. Anything marked Stage 3, 4 or 5 is not part of this stage and is not drawn as if it were built.

WHAT STAFF WILL SEE

Five changes, in plain words

  • Find, on every page. One box in the top strip, opened with /. Type an email, part of a name, a club's name or web name, an owner's email, or a Stripe customer or subscription number. Clubs, people and Stripe matches come back in one grouped list. A person shows their clubs underneath. It searches every account, not the first 1,000.
  • The menu by job. Customers, Health, Growth, Product and Records, then your private Bookkeeping where it is today. Each role sees the same menu with the parts it cannot open taken out, and the eye marks what it can only read.
  • Today replaces Overview. The front door is the list of what is waiting on us, grouped by job, with a one-line health check at the top. The business numbers stay, quiet and below, one door away.
  • The customer record reads first. It opens on the club's Story, one dated list of what happened, who did it and why. Edit forms move behind a pencil, every live change confirms in one sentence and asks why, and a plan, limit or section change can be put back from the Story. A person gets a record too, which is new.
  • One rulebook. The menu, the pages and the server agree on who may do what. Today the menu thinks nobody edits a club, so the read-only marker never shows there, even for a read-only account.

Deliberately not in Stage 2: Stripe's charges and refunds on the Money tab and the reason column in the audit log (Stage 3). Free time as one idea, with Founding Season folded in (Stage 4). The Status page, the health line's build and database checks, and the sent-email log (Stage 5).

BEFORE THE DRAWINGS

Seven things the code showed that the drawings prompt did not say

I checked every lead in the prompt against the code. Four of them change what can honestly be drawn.

  • The sign-in service cannot search. Its only way to list accounts is one page of up to 1,000 at a time, with no email or name filter. That is why Customer users stops at 1,000. A real server-side Find needs a small, read-only lookup over the sign-in records inside the database. That is a database change, reviewed as a security change below. It needs no change to sign-in and none to the gate every request passes through. Name search is possible through the same lookup.
  • Charge numbers for our own subscriptions are kept nowhere. We store a club's Stripe customer and subscription numbers, and the payment numbers of families paying a club online. A charge on a club's own subscription exists only in Stripe. So Stage 2's Find can match customer and subscription numbers and a family's payment, but not a subscription charge. That arrives when Stage 3 reads Stripe. Question 10.
  • A customer's own purchase is not recorded. We record a downgrade, a cancellation, a missed payment and a recovery. A first purchase or an upgrade the customer makes leaves no record on our side. A Story built today would show "downgraded" but never "upgraded". Question 4 says how the drawing handles that.
  • Account emails are not logged (password resets, invitations, receipts), so "I never got the email" cannot be answered from the Story until Stage 5. Campaign emails are logged, and they appear.
  • The two rulebooks disagree in more places than Organizations. Organizations is the visible one: the menu's table lists no editors, while support, billing and product edit a club through the older table. Two more: the staff list can be read through the server by any console account, though the menu shows it only to a super admin, and two orphaned plan settings routes are readable by everyone. The full table is under One rulebook.
  • The 1,000 cap is in four places, not one. Customer users, the Organizations list (its owners' last sign-in), Overview's "owner inactive" count, and the Founding Season desk all read the same first page of 1,000 accounts. Stage 2's lookup ends all four.
  • Today's queues are nearly all real already. Overview's Action queue computes eleven counts today. Two from the end state are new but come from data we keep (new errors, new feedback). One is invented before Stage 4: "Free time ending". Until then the honest queue is Overrides ending, which already exists. Question 5.

Also checked: a person has no record page today. Their actions live in a menu on each row of Customer users. A tournament has no record page either; it is a row in the club's People & tournaments tab. Read only and Growth already see the club record with every edit control hidden, never shown and then refused. The one exception is the section switches, which show disabled.

Question 2 · Find's reach: does everyone search everything, or only what their role may read?

Today every console role can open every club and every person, read only included, so "only what your role reads" and "everything" give the same clubs and people. They differ on Stripe numbers, which point at money.

Question 10 · A charge number in Find: wait for Stage 3, or ask Stripe live now?

The prompt promised charge numbers. We keep none for subscriptions. Recommended: (a), wait for Stage 3. A double billing (your task 1) ends on the Money tab anyway, and Stage 3 builds the Stripe read once, with its own review. Before customers pay at volume, a pasted charge number is rare, and the customer number on the same receipt finds the club today.

FIND SECURITY

Find is a new way to read customer data

So it is drawn and reviewed as a security change before any code. This is the design's answer to six questions, then a threat list from an adversarial pass over the design itself.

Who may call itA signed-in console account, checked on the server as the call's first step against the one rulebook, before any search runs. A customer account, a signed-out visitor and a disabled staff account get a refusal and no rows. Your role is read fresh on every call, so a role taken away takes effect on the next search.
What each role seesWhat the rulebook lets it open (question 2). The server builds the answer per role. A group the role may not read is never in the answer at all, not filtered out by the screen.
What is loggedQuestion 3. Today nothing records a staff member reading a customer: the console's visit trail keeps the page address only, never what was typed.
Rate limitingA limit per staff member, counted by account and not by network address (about one search a second, with a short burst). Honestly, it is a speed bump: our hosting runs many copies of the server and each counts on its own. The real bounds are the two-character minimum, the 8-row cap and the trail of records opened.
Server-sideYes. The browser sends the words in the body of the call, never in the address, and gets back at most 8 rows a group, never cached. It never downloads a list to filter, which is what Customer users does today with 1,000 accounts.
Sign-in and the request gateNo change to either. One database change: a narrow lookup over the sign-in records. By default a new database function can be called by anyone holding the public key, so the change must take that right away from signed-out and signed-in customers alike and give it to the server only, and a test proves it with the public key. It returns the account number, email, name, last sign-in and created date, and nothing else.
Found on the wayThe console's staff check never asks whether an email was confirmed. It trusts the email on the session. If our sign-in service lets someone sign in before confirming, an account created with a staff email that has never signed up would pass as staff. That setting lives in the hosted sign-in service, not the code, so I could not confirm the risk is real. It predates Find, but Find makes it worth more. The build adds "a staff account must have a confirmed email" to the one rulebook, after checking every current staff account is confirmed. Also: the owner's own built-in super admin emails cannot be switched off from Staff, only from the hosting settings.

Question 3 · Does Find leave a trace?

A staff search reads personal data: names and emails appear in the panel. Today no read by staff leaves a trace anywhere, in Find's absence or otherwise.

Recommended: record every record opened, not the words typed. One quiet line per opened club or person: who, when, which record, and whether Find opened it. The trail is kept 90 days, read by Super admin in the Audit log under a "Records opened" filter, and never shown on the customer's Story. The security review moved me off keeping the words: they are usually the same email or name again, so storing them for 90 days makes the trail a second store of personal data for little gain. Recording every search as you type would be mostly noise. Opening a record is the read that matters, and it is what you would want to know after a complaint.

The design pass

The design pass read every frame in Warm and Dark against the navigation and table standards and the design log. It found eleven fixes and six places where rules meet. Everything below is applied to the drawings above, except the one ask at the end.

  • Question 6 changed. I first recommended a pencil per tab. Your Oct 1 standard is one pencil for the whole record, and a pencil per section was tried and rejected that day. Redrawn on the standard, with the console's confirm-and-reason as its one named exception.
  • Cancel the subscription is red, by the console's confirm rule (2026-06-26): red for what cannot be undone from the screen. Lime stays for Save changes and Put it back.
  • The phone's Today is one framed list per group, not separate cards (the phone list rule, F-41).
  • Today's icon is a tray, not the house, which the icon dictionary keeps for Home. The tray joins the dictionary when built.
  • The health line has a grey dot and a plain count. A green dot and "no unusual errors" would vouch for builds and databases nothing checks yet.
  • No stage numbers in screen words. Staff do not know stages. Missing reasons say "Not recorded"; missing kinds of entry are named without a stage.
  • Story entries are plain bold, not links, because they open nothing. Emails no longer break in the middle. "Delivered" became "Sent", since delivery is the sent-email log's job (Stage 5).
  • Every change made from Stage 2 on keeps its reason, the way plan changes already do, so the edit window's required Why is never thrown away. Stage 3 still gives the reason its own column.
  • The read-only record keeps its toolbar, search and filter (a reader needs search most) and loses Email the owner, which the rulebook now gives to Super admin, Support and Billing.
  • The person record gained an Account tab, so its rare doors do not vanish when you look at Clubs, matching the club's Access tab.
  • Small ones: the menu's eye and count sit in fixed places; a group with nothing waiting is one line; Find's "too many" state drops its extra note and keeps the door; the box says "Find a club or person" to a role that does not search Stripe numbers.

Checked and fine: a total in a group band's words ("Customers · 17 waiting") is allowed by the table standard. Question 11 · A count on a folded menu group

When a group is folded, its amber counts fold away with it: Today says 17 wait in Customers, but a folded Customers heading shows nothing. No written rule decides this.

Recommended: the folded heading carries one amber pill, the sum of its rows, and loses it when opened. The eye stays on its row beside any count, because they answer different questions: can I change this, and is anything waiting.
