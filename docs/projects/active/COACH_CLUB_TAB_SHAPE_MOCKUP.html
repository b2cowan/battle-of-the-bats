Your rule, stated — and the one place it can't hold

"A whole section should never be invisible below another whole section." I think that's right, and it's a better rule than the one I was working to ("make the page shorter"), because it doesn't quietly expire as a list grows. Two columns satisfies it on a laptop completely.

⚠ It cannot hold on a phone, and no layout makes it hold. There is one column; two sections mean one is under the other. So the rule needs an answer at narrow widths, and there are only three honest ones:

  • Accept it on phones. Columns on desktop, stacked below — with both sections collapsed to a headline row so the second is one screen away rather than three. This is what I'd do.
  • A segmented switch (Billed us · We've asked) at narrow widths only. Both names permanently visible, one list at a time.
  • One open at a time — opening a section closes the other, so both headings always sit on screen. Guarantees the rule; costs you ever seeing both lists together.

⚠⚠ I owe you a correction here. I ruled the switch out earlier on the grounds that it "lets you see only half the relationship at once". That objection is weaker than I made it: the standing band at the top already reports both halves in figures and never moves, so a switch would only change which detail list is open — not which half of the relationship you can see. If you want your rule to hold at every width, the switch is the cheapest thing that does it, and my earlier reason for dismissing it doesn't survive contact with your rule.

What actually moved

Two fields changed places. What is it for? comes up from fifth to third; Amount and Payment method drop from third to fifth. Nothing is added, removed or renamed — the form is the same length.

And one behaviour arrives

With the word chosen before the description, the description can pre-fill from it, exactly as the Record form's does. That is what the 2026-08-15 ruling was protecting, and what the current order makes impossible.

The one judgement in it

The two branch questions stay at the top together. They read as one decision ("money from the club, and it's a grant"), and the second one decides which list the third field offers — so it has to be answered before it, not after.

The ruling it breaks

2026-08-15: the budget word is asked BEFORE the description, and the order is the feature — choosing it names the record, so the description arrives pre-filled with that word, ready to type over. Asked the other way round, "the pre-fill would land on a field they had just finished filling in." The club form asks it last.

So it is missing the behaviour too

Because the club form asks in the wrong order, it also never pre-fills the description from the chosen word. The inconsistency you saw is the visible half; the lost convenience is the other half.

Proposed order

Which way → New money or money back → What is it for? → Description → Amount + Payment method → Notes. Same grammar as Record money: branch, what it is, the words, the figures, the extras — and the pre-fill becomes possible.

Why I wanted this to work

The band already names three states in the right words, already computes them, and never moves. Tapping a figure to see what's behind it is the most natural gesture on the page, and it would have made the dashboard earn its space instead of just reporting.

It counts the wrong things

The band counts installments; the table lists bills. "Still to pay — 6 installments" filters to 3 rows, because those six pieces belong to three bills. A control whose label says 6 and whose result says 3 teaches a coach to distrust the figure — on the screen where the figures are the point.

So: the band reports, the chips filter

⚠ The one thing I would not do: hide settled records by default once the list is long. It is the obvious next step and it is the same mistake as a sticky filter — the screen would open lying about how much has happened this season.

Before the options — the precedent you named, checked

You said a multi-instalment club bill is like a multi-instalment player dues plan, and from the outside that is exactly right: both are one obligation shown in pieces. Inside, they are opposites.

A dues instalment carries Change, Remove and Record, and Change asks a real question — this payment, this and the later ones, or all unpaid? Several questions per instalment across a plan is why that record earned a drawer of its own.

A club instalment is fieldless by design — your own ruling R-D. Its one control is Record as paid, a single tap; the server derives the amount, the date and the description, deliberately, so a form would add a step and ask nothing. The club sets the pieces; the coach cannot change one, delete one, or split one.

So there is no editing surface for a drawer to hold. A club bill has exactly two editable things in the entire product — the category and the item it files under — and they are two fields.

And a window for those two fields is the shape you deleted, twice

2026-08-26 — a modal is for a QUESTION, not for a field; and a record has ONE editor. "A screen that displays a value and then opens a window to change it is telling on itself." The filing dialog on this tab is a window around two fields, and the cell button is a second editor for a record the row already opens.

2026-08-31, yesterday — on the fundraiser drive, a modal was asked for and refused, on the ruling above, and you approved the refusal. A drive's list row now expands in place. Moving club bills the other way, one tab over, would split the two money tabs on the same question seven days apart.

I'm not raising this to refuse the request — your complaint about the cell buttons is right, and the three-door mess above proves it. I'm raising it because the rulings point at a different fix that gets you more of what you actually asked for.
