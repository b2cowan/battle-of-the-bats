The rule, in one line

  • A deal holds its place in the year it was offered. After that year, it holds only while its last day hasn’t passed. The last day is inclusive, in the club’s time zone (the shipped rule for taking a logo down).
  • A deal with no last day frees its place when the year turns, because nothing else says it runs on.
  • Why not only “paid”, as the build prompt suggested: clubs sell spring sponsorships in December. An offer sent on Dec 10, 2027 for a place that runs to May 2028 must still hold it on Jan 15, or a second business gets offered the same place.
  • The Packages tab reads the current year (it has no Year pill). The Deals tab’s figures follow its pill, as today.

What each row says, and why

  • One row per 2027 deal that wasn’t withdrawn . A business with two deals has two rows. A row is left off when the business already holds that package this year (ask 1’s rule), so a December renewal isn’t offered twice.
  • “Paid us on May 2, 2027” is the reason every ready row’s email rests on: a payment in the last two years, the date read from the business’s latest payment that still stands. The club doesn’t type it. Each email keeps its own offer record.
  • The price is this year’s; “last year $900.00” shows only when it differs. The Charge switch copies last year’s deal while the club is still registered. No note, no amount field and no “Make the link only” in the batch. Those stay in the single Offer window.
  • Northgate Auto is new to the drawing: it paid in 2025, was offered in 2027 and didn’t pay, so its last payment is past the two years. Brennan’s Bakery is the archived-package row .
  • Sending waits for the club’s mailing address and contact, as the single window does, with the same words on the disabled button (“Add your mailing address in Setup first”).
  • The count on the callout is businesses that paid in 2027 (13). The window has 14 rows: Lakeview and Northgate were offered but didn’t pay, and Bright Smiles isn’t listed because its two-year club partnership still holds its place (tab 1).

The rules for a place

  • The club place always carries over.
  • A division carries over by name when exactly one live division other than last year’s has it. With none, the row waits. With two or more, it asks which, each named with its season.
  • A tournament asks for this year’s event, pre-picked on a single name match. No tournament series exists in the product, and this doesn’t build one.
  • A team is always picked again. It waits until the new season has teams, then becomes a picker.
  • Last year’s own division, team or tournament is never offered, even when its season was never closed, so a renewal can’t land on last year’s place by accident.

After the press each row stands alone; and one old deal offers again through the ordinary Offer window

The press sends each ticked row on its own: it makes the deal, emails it and keeps the offer record, the same steps the single Offer window takes today, through one shared step that does every check. If one email fails, only that row’s deal is undone. Nothing new is drawn for the single deal: its page gets one more action, and the Offer window opens filled in.

What the code does today, and what each fact changes

Read from the code on Nov 24 and 25, 2026. These are why tabs 1 and 3 differ from round 1.

F1 “Full” counts every year, on production now

A package’s Taken counts every deal that wasn’t withdrawn, from any year. The same count feeds the Packages tab, the Offer window and its refusal, and the “Taken: …” warnings. On the first day of a club’s next fiscal year, every sold-out package refuses every offer. Ask 1 fixes it, in its own commit.

F2 A deal’s place must be live

The product refuses a deal on a division or team whose season is completed, or on a completed tournament. Last year’s are usually finished, so copying a deal’s place forward would be refused. Tab 3’s rows find this year’s match instead.

F3 No tournament series

A tournament is its own record each year, with only a year number. Copying a tournament keeps no link back to the one it came from. Round 1’s “a tournament by its series” can’t be built as drawn, so ask 3 matches by name.

F4 Division names aren’t unique

Nothing stops two divisions in the same season sharing a name. Ask 6 asks which when that happens.

F5 A payment’s date only stands while the deal is still paid

It is cleared when a payment is voided, when the bank takes it back in a lost dispute, or when a refund ends the deal. Ask 8 reads the business’s latest payment that still stands, so none of those count as “paid us”.

F6 The checks before an offer email live in three places

The Offer window, Email the offer and Send the link again each check the stop lists, the reason, the address and the contact on their own, and not the same way: Send the link again replaces the link before it checks anything. The build puts every check and the make, email and keep-the-record steps in one shared step. Every route uses it, and a guard test keeps it that way. Nothing visible changes.

F7 No limit on the Offer press today

The build prompt said to throttle the batch “as the Offer press is”, but the Offer press has no limit. The batch is one press for at most 100 rows (a club holds at most 100 businesses), and one batch at a time per club. Adding a limit to the single Offer press isn’t part of this.

F8 “Send the link again” already exists

It is on an unpaid offer’s page, and it emails the same deal’s link again. “Offer again” appears only on a paid deal from an earlier year, so the two never sit on one page. They are kept apart in the code too.

F9 A renewal sent in December is filed under the old year

A deal’s year is the day it was offered. An offer sent on Dec 10, 2027 for Spring 2028 shows on 2027’s Deals. Asks 1 and 2 keep it from being counted wrong or offered twice. Whether Deals should file it under 2028 isn’t asked here; it is a later call if clubs trip on it.
