Checking the books against the bank

Both portals: the club's Accounting and a coach's team Ledger. The plan, what it corrects, and twelve asks for you to rule. Nothing here is drawn yet. The drawing session comes after the asks.

What a treasurer and a coach do after this ships

Once a month they open the bank statement beside FieldLogicHQ. They type the statement's end date and closing balance, tick each line the bank shows, and watch the difference reach $0.00. Then they mark the statement checked. The club and the team each carry a quiet "Bank checked through March 31", and the club sees that line for every one of its teams.

Club

A Bank tab in Accounting

  • First visit: name the account, its starting balance, and which books belong to it (all of them, by default).
  • Each statement is one check.
  • A Stripe deposit arrives as one line that ticks every family payment inside it, instead of thirty lines to hunt for.
  • Each team's "checked through" date shows on the Teams tab.
Coach

A Bank view of the Ledger

  • A fourth view beside Timeline, Bills and Payment schedule. Same book, arranged against the statement.
  • A cash line that never went to the bank is marked "Didn't go through the bank", so a team that pays refs in cash can still balance.
  • A cheque written before the rollover can still be ticked when it cashes after it.

Why it matters

  • It's the one money job the product sends somewhere else. Today a treasurer keeps the books here and checks them against the bank in a spreadsheet, or doesn't check.
  • Associations require it. Canadian minor-hockey associations commonly want each team's bank reconciliation monthly and at season end. Cochrane: "must be performed on a monthly basis". North Durham: emailed to the Director of Finance by the 10th.
  • It puts back a claim we took off the Club page. "Payment reconciliation" came off every Club page on 2026-10-19 because nothing did it. It goes back after this walk passes, and not before.
  • The team apps don't do it. TeamSnap and Jersey Watch track payments but don't check a statement. QuickBooks and Wave do, but a volunteer doesn't run a team in QuickBooks.

The project index defines this in one sentence: "a treasurer checks each club book against the bank statement and marks the period done; a coach does the same with the team's Ledger." Three parts of that sentence don't hold up against the product, and a fourth is unproven.

P1

A club doesn't check a book. It checks a bank account.

A club keeps a General book, one per tournament, and one per house-league season, and they almost always share one chequing account. A single Stripe deposit can carry tournament and house-league money at once, so a book-by-book check can never reach $0.00. A transfer between two books in the same account never reaches the bank at all.

So: the check is per bank account, and each club book belongs to one account. Ask B1.

P2

A coach's Cash on hand isn't the bank balance.

Cash on hand counts every dollar the team holds: the envelope, an undeposited cheque, the bank. Measured straight against a statement, it is off by exactly the cash that never went in. A bill a family paid straight to the vendor never touched the team's account either.

So: a line can be marked "Didn't go through the bank". Ask B6.

P3

The coach half has a reader the sentence doesn't name: the club.

Associations ask every team for its bank reconciliation. A coach's "checked through" date that the club can read is the half's real payoff, and it stays inside D1: the club reads the coach's fact, and no second team number is created.

So: each team's date shows on the club's Teams tab. A full "send to the club for review" flow is recommended for later. Asks B9 and B12.

P4

Unproven: does a Stripe deposit add up to what the books say?

The books record each family payment at its full amount; the bank receives one deposit, net of Stripe's fee. Whether the book side of a payout sums exactly to the deposit, both when the family pays the fee and when the club does, has not been checked. The first build stage proves it before the grouped line is built. If it doesn't sum, the gap shows as a named line, never a silent difference.

Where the sentence is right

Both portals is right, and one engine should serve both. The arithmetic is identical; only where the lines come from differs.

Club Accounting today
  • No bank account, statement, or "cleared" mark exists anywhere in the product.
  • The only lock is the fiscal-year close: a closed year's lines can't be added, changed or voided. It warns about open money and never blocks; only the latest year reopens, with a reason.
  • Who moves money: the owner, plus admins and treasurers by default. Registrars and coaches can't.
  • The Payouts tab already lists what is inside each automatic Stripe deposit, read live from Stripe, with nothing stored. A manual payout shows only its total.
  • Cash, cheques, e-transfers and desk payments are all typed in by hand. Nothing matches them to the bank.
  • There's no record of who edited a money line or what changed. Only voids, undos and year closes leave a trail.
Coach Ledger today
  • The Ledger is a view over the team's dues, bills, payments, income and fundraising. Nothing is stored in the Ledger itself.
  • Everything belongs to one season. The opening balance is carried once at Start next season.
  • Who writes: the head coach, plus a manager or treasurer by default. Assistants and helpers are off unless the head coach turns them on.
  • Online family payments don't reach a coach's Ledger. Rep-team dues online were left out of Stage 12.
  • The Ledger is Premium only. The free portal has a paid/unpaid fee list.
  • The money income records have no "how it was paid" field, so the product can't guess which lines were cash.

The word "reconcile" already on screen

  • True and kept: the help article on matching a Stripe payout to the bank statement.
  • A different meaning, on the coach's Money hub: the Budget vs. Actual export's header "Reconciliation — spending to cash", and the Budget's "reconcile the estimate". Once a Bank view exists, one hub uses the word two ways. Sent to /marketing.
  • Found, not this project's: three places that still promise invoicing or reconciliation workflows that don't exist: club onboarding, a marketing email default, and an admin catalog line. Each breaks the "claims only what ships" rule today. Sent to /marketing.

How others do it (research, sourced unless marked)

  • QuickBooks Online: type the statement date and ending balance, tick, the difference must reach $0.00; undoing is discouraged.
  • Wave and Xero: the same tick-off, plus statement upload or bank feeds. Even they tell users to prefer bank files over CSV, because layouts vary bank by bank.
  • A live bank connection in Canada: the open-banking law got Royal Assent on 2026-03-26; the regulations are still proposed; accreditation consultation opened 2026-11-02. No start date. Plaid and Flinks cover most banks, but pricing isn't published.
  • Good practice: the person who prepares a reconciliation and the person who reviews it should differ (SectorSource).
  • Inference: a sports platform with a real tick-off would fill a genuine gap.

What it never touches

  • No existing money figure changes. A tick is a mark beside the money, never a change to it.
  • The fiscal-year lock is untouched: ticking a line in a closed year is allowed, because a tick isn't an edit.
  • No family, roster, children, season-window or rollover code (Stage 14's ground).
  • No bank account numbers are ever stored. An account is a name.
  • Nothing FieldLogicHQ charges changes.

Rule first

B3

What is "the period"?

How it meets the year: a statement may straddle a fiscal year end or a rollover. Nothing about a check is tied to a year.

B4

What does "checked" do?

Why A: the club already has one lock, the fiscal year. A second lock with different edges is a second set of rules for a volunteer, and the coach side is bound by "warns, never blocks". If C: it collides with the fiscal-year lock.

B5

Must the difference be $0.00 to mark it checked?

Why A: an unbalanced "checked" is the one thing an association would reject, and the club reads the team's mark. If B: the mark grows a second state everywhere it shows.
