Rulings, as they happen

Product-manager brief

Coach row lists — one recipe

Companion to docs/projects/active/COACH_ROW_LIST_RECIPE_PM_BRIEF.md. Planning and mockups only; the build is a later session, gated on the Decisions tab.

What changes for the coach

Every list of records in the portal that is not a table — the practice and lineup hubs, the schedule's list view, the scouting book, tournaments, announcements, the small lists on a player's page, the staff list, tags, notifications, the closed-season shelves — sits on the same white card the tables sit on, with the same thin line between rows, the same row heights for the same kind of row, and the same words in the same sizes. A coach moving from Results to Scouting Book, or from the Roster to "Off the roster", stops seeing two different products.

What a coach will notice: the practice hub and the lineups hub lose their floating white cards and their big display-face date tiles and become a calm framed list with a date column; the schedule's list view becomes a framed list with month bands and rows the same height as everywhere else (a little taller than today's 31px chips — and, at tablet width, finally tall enough to tap); the scouting book matches the Results tab beside it; "Off the roster" finally has a visible line under its rows. On a phone every list's cards look like every other list's cards.

What a coach will not notice, on purpose: the Week and Month calendar views (their chips stay), the next-practice card, every worded action the practices re-evaluation ruled ("Open the plan", "Plan this practice", "Open"), the notifications feed's day headers and unread tint, the closed-season page's shape and its month folds. The recipe governs rows, not the rooms around them.

Why it matters

The app-wide table standard (2026-09-06) treated a table and a row list as one thing but only ever wrote the sentences for tables. Ten days later the owner found the list tables on the paper (F-24) and, the same afternoon, the Awards leaderboard floating beside a carded table. The row lists are what is left: measured today, seven of them are the phone's card shape drawn on a desktop, on the blueprint grid, one tab from a carded table. A product that looks like two products on adjacent tabs reads as unfinished to a coach — and to a club deciding whether to pay for it.

Customer impact

Every coach, every day: the schedule list and the two hubs are the portal's most-opened screens after the Overview. Nothing changes in what a coach can do; a few things get quieter and one thing (the schedule row at tablet width) becomes tappable. The closed-season page and the notifications feed change by pixels a reader will not see.

Priority

Next in the table-standard sequence (§8 item 7) — the last records surface not on the standard. Sequenced by reader impact: the season's daily screens first (schedule, the two hubs), then the tab-neighbours of carded tables (scouting book, tournaments, announcements), then the small lists, then the closed season and the feed.

Tradeoffs made

  • The schedule's rows grow from 31 to ~37px (and to ~64px cards on a phone, from 52). One recipe with a floor costs a long month a little height; the alternative — a private density for one screen — is the thing the standard exists to refuse.
  • The date tile goes. The hubs' 20px display-face day was a small calendar flourish; it was also the one row in the portal larger than its own page title. A body-size date column reads just as fast and lets the title lead.
  • Two lists stay lists by judgement, not by the letter of the test. The closed-season results and a player's last sessions have three same-kind columns — the table test's wording — but the question a column would answer is already answered above them (the season's record; the attendance boxes). The standard now says so.

Success criteria

  • The rendered gate finds zero lists of records on the paper across the coach sweep, and would fail the day one is added — the ground is read from the ancestor, not the row.
  • Every bin-B list renders through one component; the old row classes are gone (the dead-selector gate proves it).
  • The build's owner walk passes with no list looking different from its neighbour on any axis it did not claim in the register.

Coach row lists — one recipe (F-17) · implementation plan

Companion to docs/projects/active/COACH_ROW_LIST_RECIPE_PLAN.md, which carries the technical detail; this tab is the readable form. Standard §3.10 is the recipe; register F-26–F-40 and K-21–K-24 are its rows.

Problem / context

F-17 recorded "six row treatments for one job" from the 2026-09-06 inventory and was deferred with the rest of the standard's build ("own session, own mockups"). Re-measured on 2026-09-16 at 1440 · 768 · 390 in both skins, with the ground read from the ancestor: the enumeration was stale (one class had left the page; four were never lists) and the real finding is two species — seven gapped card stacks on the paper and nine hairlined lists, eight already on a card. The standard's ground sentence named a table; nothing said who paints a list's ground.

Scope

The seventeen bin-B lists (F-26–F-39), one bin-A conversion (F-40), four KEEPs (K-21–K-24), the component, the three gate extensions and the §-walk. Nothing else.

Out of scope

Every <table>; the money grids; the closed-season page's shape; the practice document and library rooms; the notifications redraw's content and its open item R8; the calendar's Week and Month cells; anything platform- or club-admin.

Approach

The recipe (standard §3.10)

  1. Exactly one painter of the ground — the table's frame when the list stands on the page; the section card or shelf when it sits inside one; never zero, never two; stands down at ≤ 640.
  2. A row is not a card — no per-row border, radius, shadow, fill or gap on a desktop.
  3. Density by content from .rowCaption; type from the ladder on the line; a leading date is a date column; the display face nowhere below a heading.
  4. The label row is a band inside the frame (the feed's day header) or the section title above it.
  5. Hover = tint + pointer on a row that opens; a worded door where a ruling worded it; .rowTapLink for in-row controls.
  6. One lead mark; the colour rail stays in calendar cells.
  7. One component (CoachRowList / CoachRow), never a second row class.

The component and the gates (Q5)

components/coaches/CoachRowList.tsx renders the frame (composing .tableWrap), optional band rows and rows with four slots — lead · main (title + caption composing .rowCaption) · trail · door — as a link, a button or a static row. It stamps data-row-list on the list and data-row-list-row on each row so a rendered gate can find it whatever the hashed class is. CoachEventListRow becomes a thin caller (the date lead + the ruled chip and door). Three gate extensions: (a) the stylesheet guard scans the rowList* family; (b) check:layout's type-ladder reads the rows' text; (c) a new list-ground rule reads the first painted ancestor behind every row at ≥ 641 and asserts the card token — and at ≤ 640 asserts no painted slab between the row-card and main.

Sequence (Q6) — by reader impact

  1. The component and the gates, proven on one list (Scouting book — the Q1 specimen) — the guard must find it before anything else adopts it.
  2. The season's daily screens: Schedule list (F-26), the two hubs and Lineups templates (F-27, F-28).
  3. The carded tables' neighbours: Tournaments (F-30), Announcements (F-31).
  4. The small lists: player sessions/awards, notes, guardians (F-32–F-34), Staff (F-35), Off the roster (F-38), Tags (F-39).
  5. The quiet ones: closed-season rows (F-37), the feed's ladder tokens (F-36), then the opponent table (F-40) when that page is next open.

Open questions

All six are on the Decisions tab with a recommendation each. The two the drawings could not settle alone: whether the schedule's list rows drop the colour rail (Q4), and whether "one frame with bands" is preferred over "two labelled frames" on the hubs (Q2c).

Rollout

No migration, no route, no data change — stylesheet, one component and its callers, three gate extensions. The demo sandboxes follow (the schedule list and the practice hub are tour destinations; the build fails if an anchor stops existing). The help articles name no list furniture; /docs checks the schedule and practice-plans articles after the build.
