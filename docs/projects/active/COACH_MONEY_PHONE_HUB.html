Two departures from the drawing

1 · The create keeps its words — found on the built screen

The drawing had Add Line collapsing to a bare lime + at ≤640, the shape the Schedule already uses. It was built that way, and the screenshot showed why it cannot stay: the Money hub's page header already collapses Record money to a bare lime + on a phone. Budget Plan drew two identical lime squares, 90px apart, meaning “record a payment” and “add a budget line” — two flows nothing like each other, and no way to tell them apart by looking.

Reverted. The toolbar is therefore two rows (96px) at 390 rather than the drawn one — lenses above, the doing below — still down from 148, and the first budget line still clears the bar by 80px. The one-row version is available the day the header's create is re-weighted, which is a Money-hub header decision and not this toolbar's to take.

2 · Fundraising and Club captions left alone

The drawing sketched shortened captions and shortened labels for these two (“Credited”, “Still to pay”, “Waiting”). Only the caption work was done, and only on the tabs that were failing. A label is a name, and renaming one is a vocabulary decision with its own rule behind it — not something to slip into a layout pass. Both tabs sit at 193px instead of 176 because one label wraps; neither was ever failing, and both are now ~300px clear of the bar.

The rule this is measured againstS.4, from the phone walk

“One figure, one qualifier. A phone tile carries a label, a figure and one line. This is the direct answer to ‘are we displaying too much on mobile’ — the tiles are; the cards mostly are not.”

And the precedents that already applied it, all shipped:

  • The team Overview board — six tiles became six rows in one framed list, the icons dropped, the caption kept only when it carries a fact rather than a hint, and the figure stepped down a size. Band 342 → 287; the last row went from 5px clear of the bar to 40.
  • The player profile’s glance tiles — four across became two-up, because four tiles on a phone gives each about 80px, “narrower than None set.”
  • The Insights scoreboard — its stat blocks wrap two-up rather than running off the side.
  • The practice plan’s toolbar — 148px of buttons became a create and one 44px “⋯” holding the rest as a drawer. Exactly the same 148px this walk found on Budget Plan.

The obvious first move, and it does not survive 361px: Headroom +$8,090.02, Season end $11,600.32 and Dues $11,491.30 all overflow their tile at 24px. Recorded because “just make it two columns” is the idea everyone has first, and this is the measurement that answers it.

C · B, with the caption dropped entirely against S.4

Buys another 42px and breaks the rule this walk is applying: S.4 is label + figure + one line, not label + figure. The captions carry things no figure can say — “nobody budgeted this”, “1 overdue”, “lowers their dues”. Shown as the ceiling, not as a candidate.

Two sub-questions inside M1

M1a · the odd tile

With three tiles, which one gets the full-width seat?

Two-up leaves one tile alone on the second row. It can take the whole width rather than sit beside a gap — the question is which tile that should be.

The last one recommended

Positional, no new declaration, and on Budget Plan it lands on Closing balance — which the code already calls “the season’s verdict”. Where a tab disagrees, the tab reorders its own tiles.

A tile declares itself wide

Semantic and always right, but it is a new per-tile flag on a shared component, and a new flag with six callers is a thing that drifts. On Club it would move the wide seat from Settled this season to Still to pay the club, which is arguably the better read.

M1b · the captions

Who rewrites the eleven sentence-captions?

Option D needs Budget Plan (3), Player Dues (3) and one Club caption cut to six words. These are customer-visible money words on the screen a treasurer reads most carefully.

Drafted here, ruled by you, then /marketing checks the words recommended

Each caption is drafted against its tile and shown to you beside the figure it qualifies, so you rule on the reading and not on a word list.

Mark which captions are hints and drop those on a phone

The mechanism the Overview board uses — a tile declares whether its second line is a fact or a hint, and hints do not render on a phone. No rewriting. But most of these captions are facts, so it would leave the long ones long.

What is deliberately not in scope

  • The tables themselves. Money’s tables are the other half of “Money is 3,019px”, and the rule they answer to is different — a table whose columns fit stays a table. That is its own decision with its own drawings.
  • Anything above 640px. The desk arrangement is untouched; every change lands in a phone media query or behind the phone check the toolbar component already makes.
  • The warning notes. They stay where they are and keep their words, except where a caption rewrite removes a figure the note was repeating.
  • The tab strip across the top of Money. Six tabs in a scroller is its own question and the walk did not measure it as a failure.

Risks named up front

The one that has bitten this repo before

A screen built through a shared recipe inherits the recipe’s layout decisions, not just its materials. The Overview board was drawn white, built through the row recipe, and came out olive — nobody caught it because the recipe was the right component. Every one of the six tabs gets looked at after the band changes, not just the two this walk measured as failing.

What makes this cheap

The band is one component and the toolbar is one component. Both already have a phone branch; neither needs a new abstraction. The drawer and the bare-+ create both exist and are in production. The measured work is CSS in two files plus eleven strings.

Product brief

What changes for a coach

The problem, in one sentence

A coach who opens the team budget on their phone sees a summary, a warning and three rows of buttons — and has to scroll past all of it before the first budget line, which today is rendered below the bottom of the screen.

What they will see instead

The same three figures, arranged two across instead of stacked, each with a short qualifier rather than a sentence. The control row becomes one line: the two view controls, a “⋯” holding export and the category tools, and the add button as a lime +. Three or four budget lines are then on screen when the page opens.

Nothing is removed. Every control still exists and every figure still shows; the desk version is untouched.

Who it affects

Every coach and treasurer on a phone, across all six Money tabs — Budget Plan, Budget vs. Actual, Player Dues, Fundraising, Club and the Ledger. Read-only coaches see the same improvement; their toolbar simply has fewer things in the drawer.

Why it matters commercially

Money is the Premium tier’s headline capability and the one a treasurer evaluates hardest. It is currently the only part of the portal that has never had a phone pass, and it is the part most likely to be opened in a parking lot rather than at a desk.

Success criteria

  • Every Money tab’s summary is the same height on a phone — it is 291–405px today.
  • Budget Plan and Budget vs. Actual show real content above the bottom bar at 361 and 390.
  • No control is lost, and no figure stops being readable.
  • Nothing above 640px changes — proven by re-running the layout sweep at 768 and 1440.

Tradeoffs taken

Export and the category tools move one tap further away on a phone. Accepted: neither is a thing done standing at a field, and both stay one tap from where they were on a desk.

Eleven captions get shorter. Some detail moves out of the summary and into the table beneath it. Accepted with the owner ruling on each rewrite, because the summary’s own contract already asked for six words and three tabs had grown past it.
