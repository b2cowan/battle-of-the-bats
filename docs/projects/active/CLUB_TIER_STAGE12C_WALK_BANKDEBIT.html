Pay by bank debit

Written 2026-10-26. One purpose: a family or a team pays by bank debit, the debit is seen as “clearing” everywhere until it clears, and the desk can end a registration while one clears.

What this walk is for

A bank debit takes days, so unlike a card it is neither paid nor owing while it travels. You check what each person sees in that time: the family (a “Bank debit clearing” block and no way to pay twice), the registrar (a “clearing” chip on the Fee cell, a Clearing figure on the Money page, no Mark received, and a question when ending a registration whose debit is still on its way), the owner (both processing-fee rates on the Payouts card, and the Disconnect window counting debits that are still clearing) and a tournament team’s record. Then Stripe’s sandbox settles the debits, and you check that the receipt, the Fee cell, the Money page and the send-back that was asked for all follow by themselves.

You use a family in a private window (no login), the registrar and the owner on a computer, and a team’s contact in a private window.

It passes when

  • While a debit clears the family sees “Bank debit clearing” and cannot pay again; the desk sees “clearing” (never paid, never owing, never past due) and is not offered Mark received or the pay link.
  • On the Money page Owed equals Received plus Clearing plus Still owing, at every moment.
  • Ending a registration while its debit clears asks “When it clears:” first, and what you chose happens by itself when it clears.
  • When a debit clears, the receipt names the bank account and both dates, the Fee cell reads received, and the Clearing figure moves to Received.
  • Both processing-fee rates print from the screen’s own figures; nothing is typed in this page.

Not in this walk

  • A debit that comes back and a debit the bank reverses after it cleared. Stripe’s test-bank sign-in for Canadian debits does not let you choose those outcomes, so they are covered by the database checks (scripts/online-payments-12c-check.sql T12–T16 and T22–T27) and the unit tests, and get walked in the Amplify end-to-end pass.
  • The club’s invoice line for a returned debit (same reason).
  • Refunds to a card, refunds made in Stripe and a tournament part refund: that is walk §389.
  • Emails, beyond the held boxes. A step that sends an email has two boxes, “Did it” and “Held”. Tick “Held” and move on: localhost cannot send, so the emails get one end-to-end pass on Amplify dev.
