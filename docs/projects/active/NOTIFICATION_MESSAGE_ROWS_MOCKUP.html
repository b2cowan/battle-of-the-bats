One opened notice, in rows

The block every frame wears stays the same block: the eyebrow (kind and time), the title, then the message, then the buttons (Open · Done · Close · Delete, in that order, unchanged). Only the message changes. Where it used to be one paragraph, it becomes labelled rows, with an optional list under a row and an optional next-step line. When a Needs-attention notice is handled, the settled line takes the next step’s place.

Drawn on the reference case built on Nov 14 (“The club gave house league time”), because it already has every part. The pins number the parts. The Before is that reference case as built. The After is the pattern, and it changes the reference case in two small places (pins 3 and 4).

A bank debit was returned

Who receives it
whoever may refund: the house-league desk for a family’s debit, the tournament’s refunders for a team’s. Drawn: the house-league desk, Admin portal.
The question
the money rows; where Needs attention and the settled line sit relative to the rows

Answer

Six facts in three sentences become six rows, led by the amount. Needs attention is not inside the opened notice: it is the list’s section heading and the amber edge on the row. The rows sit above the settled line and the buttons. When it settles, the next-step line goes and the settled line takes its place (third specimen).

A card payment was disputed

Who receives it
two copies: the person who set up payouts (they answer it, so theirs carries the deadline), and, more quietly, the desk (theirs says who answers). Drawn: the person who set up payouts, Admin portal.
The question
a deadline as a row versus a highlighted line; the amount held

Answer

The deadline is a row, Answer by, because it is looked up like any other fact. The instruction is one plain line under the rows. No highlight: the amber edge on the row already says it is urgent. Held gets its own row, because how much the club is out of pocket while it waits is the second thing a treasurer asks. The other two treatments are drawn in Q2.

The club changed house league’s practices

Who receives it
house league’s runners: owners, admins and league admins, never the person who sent it. Admin portal.
The question
a list of up to 6 moved games, and what happens past 6

Answer

The counts become rows, and the game lines become a list under Games. Past 6, the notice offers “Show 3 more games”, which opens the rest in place, instead of “and 3 more”, which names games you can’t see. Try it in the After. The “wait in Changes to send” sentence becomes the next-step line. This is the only kind that already writes line breaks, so its Before is the least bad. On a phone it is taller than the sheet, before and after (switch Frame to Phone); Q9 asks what the buttons do then.

Family answers

Who receives it
the team’s coaches who can see attendance. Coaches Portal, warm skin.
The question
a folded list of children and answers plus the headcount, in the coach’s portal

Answer

One child per line: the name, the answer and what it was before. The headcount gets its own row, Now. The facts are already stored, so nothing new is kept. The list row does not change while three or fewer children answered, because today’s line already is a one-line summary. A note’s words are never shown, only that one was left (as today).

A game changed

Who receives it
the free coaches of the team. Bell only: no email, no push to the coach.
The question
the smallest few-facts case. Does a three-row notice read better than its sentence?

Answer: no. Keep the sentence.

“Now Sat, Jul 18, 3:15 p.m. at Diamond 4 · Riverdale Classic” is already one line and already the summary. It was written for a coach in a parking lot (its code says so). The rows triple its height to say the same three facts, and they split “when” from “where”, which a coach reads together. My recommendation is that this kind keeps its sentence and leaves the 28. That is your call, Q8.

Q1Labels: what they look like, how wide, and where they sit on a phone

Style is not really open: sentence case, 14px semibold, quiet ink, as built on Nov 14. The small-capitals eyebrow face is ruled out by the admin type rule. Two things are open: the label column’s width on a computer, and whether a phone puts the label beside its answer or above it.

Width A · 5.5rem, fixed RECOMMENDED

As built. Every label in the five kinds fits (“Tournament” is the longest, about 80px). A fixed edge lets the eye learn where answers start. Rule: a label is one or two short words, 11 characters at most, checked in the words file.

Width B · 7rem, fixed

More air on a computer, but it takes 24px from the answer column on a 335px phone sheet, where moved-game lines already wrap.

Width C · as wide as each notice’s longest label

Tightest, but the answers start at a different edge on every notice, and the bell is read one notice after another.

Phone A · label beside, lists full width RECOMMENDED

Measured on tab 1’s notice: with labels above (as built) its buttons end 75px below the sheet’s first view; beside, the whole notice fits. A list under a row runs the sheet’s full width. The product’s other read-rows already keep the label beside on a phone (the club calendar’s, a family’s record). Changes the Nov 14 reference case, which stacks.

Phone B · label above its answer

As built on Nov 14. Every row becomes two lines, and the scan down the left edge is lost.

Q2The next step or a deadline: a row, a line under the rows, or emphasis

A · The date is a row, the instruction a line RECOMMENDED

A date the reader must meet is a fact (“Answer by · Nov 20 at 11:59 p.m.”), looked up like the others. What to do is one plain line under the rows, starting with a verb. When the notice is settled, the line is replaced by the settled line, so an instruction never outlives its task.

B · Both in one line under the rows

“Answer in Stripe by Nov 20 at 11:59 p.m.” The sentence reads well, but the date is buried in a sentence again.

C · The line, emphasised

Bold, in the warning ink. It says “urgent” twice, since the amber edge on the list row already says it. The house states a status once.

Q3Long lists: how many lines before they fold, and what the fold says

A · 6 lines, then “and 3 more”

As today. Simple, but it names games you can’t see, and the onward button opens the whole schedule, not those three.

B · 6 lines, then “Show 3 more games” RECOMMENDED

The rest open in place, and the fold names its noun like the product’s other folds (“Show 2 more hand-back asks”). The notice keeps up to 30 lines; past 30 the last line reads “and 12 more on the schedule”. Six lines fit the drawer and the dialog comfortably. On a phone this kind outgrows the sheet with or without the fold (measured: the buttons end 131px below the sheet’s first view; today’s paragraph is already 33px below). That is Q9.

C · Never fold

Fine at 9, a wall at 25. A phone sheet scrolls past its buttons.

Q5“Score submitted”: what it should carry

Today it says only who entered it: “Submitted by scorekeeper kim@example.com”. On a tournament day these fold into one bundle (“12 scores submitted”), and a bundle opens as its members’ one-line bodies, never rows. So today you would read the same sentence twelve times.

A · The one line carries the game and score RECOMMENDED

“Ridge 12U 5, Hawks 3 · U13 Boys · by kim@example.com”. It reads right in a bundle, a list row and a push. Cost: one extra read when it is sent (the two team names and the division; today only their ids are in hand).

B · Rows (Game · Score · Division · Entered by)

Only seen when a single score is opened; a bundle never shows rows. Same extra read.

C · Leave it

The link opens the game. The bundle stays unreadable.

Q9On a phone, when an opened notice is taller than its sheet, where do the buttons go?

Found by measuring every phone specimen during the design review. Five of the kinds fit the sheet (at most three quarters of the screen above the bottom bar). Moved games does not: its buttons end 131px below the sheet’s first view after the change, and 33px below today. The 28 include a few more lists (insights, a hand-back answer with several payments), so this will come up again.

A · Pin the button row to the sheet’s foot RECOMMENDED

Only when the message scrolls. The message scrolls above a hairline, and the way on stays under the thumb. This follows the Oct 13 rule that a phone sheet’s foot keeps the door, and the Oct 9 rule that a window’s foot is pinned. It changes the reader’s frame for every kind, which is the Sheet Frame project’s.

B · Fold harder on a phone

3 lines instead of 6. It still doesn’t fit (about 65px short), and it hides more of what you opened the notice to see.

C · Leave it scrolling

As today. The buttons are a scroll away on the longest notices only.
