⚠ Which figures are promises and which are not. The season totals below are pinned to the cent and must match: $13,936.00 planned costs, $3,623.00 planned funding, $10,313.00 costs less funding, $11,308.30 of player installments, and a $995.30 buffer. Part A

The plan finishes its sentence

The grid used to stop at Costs less funding, three rungs short of the List. Four checks that it now closes the same way — and that the two views agree.

A1

Three closing rows, in this order

Money → Budget. Set View to By period and Columns to Quarters. Scroll to the foot of the table.

Expect the table to end on three rows, in this order: Costs less funding → Player installments (with a Scheduled tag) → Shortfall (Buffer). The installments row is green, like every money-in row above it. No new section heading — these are ladder rows, not a fourth band.
A2

The installments row agrees with the tile above it

Read the Total column of Player installments, then look up at the Player installments tile at the top of the screen.

Expect the same money. The tile reads $11,308.30 Scheduled; the grid's Total reads 11,308 — the grid rounds to whole dollars in every cell, which is what it has always done. The pass condition is that they are the same figure, not that they are that figure: if a later change moves the dues, both must move together.
A3

⚠ The close is the same number the List calls a buffer

Read Shortfall (Buffer) in the Total column. Now switch View to List and read the very last row of that table.

Expect one number said two ways. The List's last row reads Planned buffer $995.30. The grid's Total reads (995) — in brackets, because across time the row's sign flips column to column and a row label cannot rename itself per column the way the List's can. Same magnitude, opposite presentation, and that is the whole design.
This is the step I most want your eye on. If the bracket here reads as bad news rather than as a buffer, the paired header has failed and I would rather know now than after it ships.
A4

⚠ Any column you like still adds up

Back to By period. Pick any one column — including No date yet — and do two subtractions with your eye: Planned costs minus Planned funding should be Costs less funding; that minus Player installments should be Shortfall (Buffer). Then do the same down the Total column.

Expect both to hold in every column. On today's fixture the Total column reads 13,936 − 3,623 = 10,313, then 10,313 − 11,308 = (995) — illustration, not the pass condition. What must be true is the relationship, in whichever column you chose.
A dash is not a zero. A column with nothing in a row shows an em dash; treat it as nothing when you subtract. A row that shows 0 would be a defect worth noting.

Part B

Brackets, on both grids, meaning opposite things

Every other money figure in the portal has always shown a negative in brackets. These two grids were the exception. They are not any more — but what a bracket means differs by grid, and that is deliberate.

B1

A bracket on the plan is green

On By period, find any bracketed figure on Costs less funding or Shortfall (Buffer). (Quarters usually shows at least one; if not, switch Columns to Months and page through.)

Expect brackets, not a minus sign — no -63 and no −63 anywhere on the table — and the bracketed figure drawn in the same green the funding rows use, because it is money landing ahead of the bills.
B2

The legend appears only when there is something to explain

Read the notes under the table.

Expect a line saying a figure in brackets on a closing row is a period where the money lands ahead of the bills. It should sit beside the No date yet note, which now also mentions dues not yet on a schedule. The old apology is gone — nothing should say "your season estimate has no dates, so this grid spreads the lines you have entered".
B3

⚠ The twin grid — same brackets, red

Go to Budget vs. Actual → View: Months. Look at the Net for the month and Closing balance rows and find a negative one (try Showing: Cash).

Expect brackets there too — this is the other half of the change — but drawn in red, not green. On a balance a bracket means the account is below zero, which is the one real warning on that screen. Same notation, opposite meaning.
If you find a bare minus sign anywhere on either grid, that is a defect: the whole point was that the product stops showing three notations for one fact.

Part C

The season estimate — a figure the two views used to disagree on

This is the live defect the change fixes, and the fixture has no estimate set, so you have to make one. Step C5 puts it back — please run the whole part in one sitting.

C1

Set an estimate above the lines

On List, under the Planned costs tile, click set an estimated total. Enter 15000 and save.

Expect the tile to read $15,000.00 with a caption saying $13,936.00 is itemized, and the List's cost band to grow Lines so far and Still to itemize rows. That is the List behaving as it always has.
C2

⚠ The grid now shows the money it used to hide

Switch to By period and read the Costs band.

Expect two new rows under the categories — Lines so far (13,936) and Still to itemize (1,064, sitting in the No date yet column, because an estimate has no dates) — and the subtotal beneath them reading Planned costs 15,000. Before this change that subtotal was called "Lines so far" and read 13,936, because the grid could not show the missing $1,064 and so could not honestly claim the name.
C3

⚠⚠ The two views now agree — this is the defect

Read Costs less funding in the grid's Total column. Switch to List and read the row of the same name.

Expect the same number on both — $11,377.00 (15,000 − 3,623). Before this change the List said $11,377.00 and the grid said 10,313 under the identical name, on the same plan, on the same day.
Worth a moment: $1,064 apart is not a rounding difference. This is the kind of thing a coach reads off one screen and quotes from another.
C4

The other branch — an estimate BELOW the lines

Edit the estimate to 12000 and look at the By period Costs band again.

Expect the second row to rename itself to Over your estimate and show (1,936) in brackets, with Planned costs reading 12,000. The List should say the same thing in its own band. This branch is easy to forget when building — it is the reason it is here.
C5

Put the fixture back

Edit the estimate once more and clear it (empty the field and save).

Expect the tile to go back to Planned costs $13,936.00 with the set an estimated total link, and both extra rows to disappear from both views. Leaving an estimate set here would quietly change what every later budget walk sees.

Part D

What this fixture cannot show you

Two states ship in this change and cannot be walked here. Saying so is the point of this part — a walk that quietly skipped them would report coverage it does not have.

D1

Before dues are set, and an over-funded season

Nothing to click. Read this and judge whether you are content to accept it.

Neither state exists in the fixture, and manufacturing them would damage it.

1 · No dues set. The two closing rows should stay away and a note should offer Set dues for all players instead. UAT Test Team's dues are set, and its only other team has no live season at all — so the only way to see this live is to delete the team's dues, which would wreck every dues walk in the ledger.

2 · Funding above the whole plan. The List's Costs less funding row should show a bracketed surplus instead of the $0.00 it used to floor to. Reaching it means adding a money-in line of more than $10,313 and deleting it again.
Both are pinned by unit tests that run on every build — the rows staying away, the bracketed surplus, and the arithmetic behind them. That is the coverage; your eye is not the coverage here, and this step exists so nobody records otherwise.
If you would rather see either one for yourself, say so in the notes and I will build a throwaway team for it rather than touching this one.

Part E

The file says what the screen says

An export's shape is its screen's shape. A file that stopped where the grid used to stop would leave a treasurer doing the subtraction by hand.

E1

Export the By-period view

On By period, use Export → Excel (or CSV). Open the file.

Expect the same three closing rows — Costs less funding, Player installments, Shortfall (Buffer) — with the same figures as the screen you exported from, and the same columns. Installments read positive in the file, as every money-in row does.
The PDF is a different document and is not part of this check: the period grid ships in Excel and CSV only, and the PDF is the whole-season statement.
E2

Read one row out loud

Pick the Shortfall (Buffer) row on screen and say, in your own words, what it is telling you about the season. Write what you said in the notes.

This step is not a check, it is the check. A previous report went 42 for 42 and still shipped three defects, all of them inside a row nobody had read as a sentence. If what you say does not match what the row means, the row is wrong — however well it adds up.

Part F

Three calls for you

Not checks. Tick the option you want — the tick is the answer, and the notes box is for the why.

F1

The swing this design accepts

Switch Columns to Months and look at a month where dues land and few bills fall due. Shortfall (Buffer) will show a large bracketed figure there.

It is arithmetically right and it reads like a swing rather than a state, because this grid has no running balance to say "that money carries into next month". You accepted this over sending coaches to another tab, and deferred balances. This is your chance to look at it in the product rather than in a mockup.
F2

Is the bracket note clutter?

Look at the stack of notes under the table on a phone, or at a narrow window.

There are now up to three: No date yet, the brackets legend, and — before dues are set — the Set dues offer. The brackets legend only renders when a bracket is actually on screen.
F3

“Lines so far” — settle it or leave it

You said you did not like the word lines. It was kept in Part C's two rows because the List already prints those exact words today — matching it is what stops the two views disagreeing again.

If you want it retired, it changes on both views in its own unit of work. Doing it on one view only would re-open the defect this whole change closed.
