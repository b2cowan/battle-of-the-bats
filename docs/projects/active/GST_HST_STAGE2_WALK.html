Owner QA walk · GST/HST · Stage 2 · §471

A buyer is quoted, and charged, the right tax for where they are, on every paid step

Written 2026-11-06 for the Stage 2 closeout (Parts 0–6b and Part 7). It runs on the dev site against real Stripe test-mode checkouts, with real webhooks. It is the only end-to-end look at the tax on the dev site; the automated tests and the scripted Stripe matrix proved the figures, and your ticks prove what a buyer and staff actually see.

What this walk is for

From Stage 2 on, every price we quote says plus applicable taxes, and every paid step adds GST or HST for the buyer’s billing address: HST 13% in Ontario, 14% in Nova Scotia, GST 5% in Alberta. The figure a window quotes is the figure Stripe charges. A GST/HST number typed on Stripe’s page prints on the invoice and leaves the tax in place. A refund from platform admin goes through a credit note that shows the tax given back. Part 7 also changed three things you will see: a part refund lands on the nearest figure Stripe can make, staff without refund rights see no credit-note PDF, and a coach whose billing pays for two portals can’t turn one into a club.

It passes when

  • Every price before Stripe’s page says “plus applicable taxes”, and every quote that charges today shows the tax by name with Due today as the total.
  • Stripe’s page and the invoice charge exactly what FieldLogicHQ quoted, at the rate for the buyer’s province.
  • Changing the billing address changes the tax on the next charge, and a postal code from another province is refused.
  • A GST/HST number shows on the invoice, and the tax stays.
  • Premium → Club quotes and charges at the club’s address, and a coach with two paid portals is told to contact us instead.
  • A refund makes a credit note with the tax reversed; the part refund lands on the nearest figure; the club’s Story says so.
  • The help guide explains the tax, and the live Stripe account shows our real business details.

Not in this walk

  • The Founding next-season choice. It doesn’t open until 2027-06-01. Its checkout uses the same tax setting as the others, and the every-path test pins it.
  • A US buyer. US sales are closed (“We’re not selling in the United States yet.”). Stripe test mode charged a US address no Canadian tax (Part 6).
  • A charge that paid no invoice. Our checkouts always make one. The refund matrix proved a bare refund makes no credit note.
  • A club’s name on its first invoice after a free trial. It needs a 90-day clock. Part 6b proved it on Stripe test clocks with real Checkout pages; D3 checks the name on Stripe’s customer instead.
  • Fee invoices for online payments. Walk §463 covered the address step; the scripted matrix proved the fee tax by province.
