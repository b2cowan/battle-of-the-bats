Part A

The Statement, at a glance

A1

One surface, not a stack of cards

Verdict
A2

The band headings read as headings

fixed a 3-week-old bug

Revenue and Expenses should be small olive capitals on a tinted row, with a little air above each — clearly a heading, not another category.

Worth knowing why this is a check. On the Months view these two headings have been rendering as ordinary dark sentence-case text since mid-August — their styling was being silently overruled. It is visible in the screenshot you sent me. That is fixed, and Months is included below in A6 so you can confirm it there too.
Verdict
A3

Nothing budgeted shows an amber dash, and no amber row

Four rows on this fixture were never budgeted for. In Revenue they are Tournaments and No category; in Expenses they are Events and No category. Each should show an amber dash in the Plan column — and sit on the same white ground as every other row.

This is one of the two things I want you to rule on. The dash ships amber. A plain grey dash is also defensible — the Off-plan tile at the top already totals what nobody budgeted, so the colour may be saying it twice. Tell me which you want; either is a one-line change. (Answer in Part F.)
Verdict
A4

Player dues is an ordinary row now

First row under Revenue. It should look like every other category row — not a card of its own, and carrying no message of its own.

Changed mid-walk, on your ruling. This row used to caption itself “12 families · set on Player Dues”. It was the only category on the report carrying a message, it told a coach something they had just done, and it cost real height — the row ran 52px against every other category’s 36px, making the quietest fact on the report the tallest row in the band. It is gone. The dues sentence under the table still carries what dues bill and whether they cover the plan.

One state kept, and you can overrule it: a team with no dues schedule still sees “Not set yet · Set player dues” on this row. That one is not telling a coach what they know — it is the reason the Plan cell shows a dash, and it is the first thing a new team has to do. You cannot see it on this fixture, which has dues set.

Verdict
A5

The bottom line is the loudest thing on the table

Season net closes the report. It should measure exactly what Total revenue and Total expenses measure — set apart by the space around it, by its heavier rule, and by being last. Not by size.

Settled, and you ruled it twice. When you flagged this step, all three rows measured identically — and the step was wrong to claim otherwise. It said the net read “larger and bolder”; a note in the code claimed the subtotals wore a thinner rule. Neither was ever built. (The closing row had originally rendered smaller than its own subtotals; a fix lifted it to parity, and both descriptions went on claiming it had ended up louder.)

A size step up was then built and shown to you, and you ruled it back out on sight — “go back to making the font size the same as the revenue/expense rows. It keeps it in line with the other monthly statement and looks better.” That is what ships. A build check now pins the three rows as equal, which catches it growing (your ruling) and shrinking (the original defect) alike.

Verdict
A6

Months, unchanged except for its headings

Switch View to Months. This is the screen you told me was the best reading experience in the product, and it should be exactly as you left it — with one difference: its Revenue and Expenses headings now read as small olive capitals instead of plain dark text.

Three things I'm not sure about

Questions, not checks. Answer in the boxes — the answers come back with your findings.

Amber dash, or a plain one?
