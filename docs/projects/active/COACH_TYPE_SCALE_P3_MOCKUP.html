The owner approved Option A and Phase 3 shipped the same day. 01 — Before the options, a correction

The number I called the marquee case doesn't exist

The 32px "season record hero" is dead code delete, don't rule on it

When I told you the ladder needed an eighth step, the largest live example I gave was a 32px season-record hero on the Overview. It has no usages anywhere in the product. It and its phone override are leftovers from a card that was replaced, and Phase 3 should delete them rather than assign them a step.

That matters because it shrinks the question. Once the dead rule is out, only five things in the whole portal genuinely live above 24px — and they split cleanly into two different jobs, which is what makes the ruling easy.

The split that decides it

A card figure is a number you read while sitting at a desk. It lives inside a card and the card's title tells you what it means — the same job as $1,875.00 on the Money screen, which is already the ladder's 24px figure step.

A display number is one you read from the bench, from the stands, or across a room — the score, and the record at the end of a season. That is a genuinely different job, and it is the only thing that needs a step the ladder doesn't have.

04 — The ruling

Three ways to close the ladder

A · One display step at 32px

recommended

Add one step. The five display numbers go to 32px. The card figures come down to 24px, joining the figure they already are. Icons and the watermark become documented exemptions. The dead rule is deleted.

Ladder ends at 8 steps and governs everything. Nothing above 24px is left unmanaged.

Scores get bigger (+18% on the game-day console).
Card figures get smaller (−6% to −14%).
14 places move · 2 documented exemptions

B · Two steps, 28px and 36px

lowest risk

Add two steps so almost nothing has to move far. Card figures stay at 28px rather than dropping to 24px, and the biggest numbers round up to 36px.

Ladder ends at 9 steps. Safer, but 24px and 28px sit close enough that the pair does very little work — the same near-tie problem the whole exercise was about.

Almost nothing visibly moves (max ~12%).
But adds a step that earns little.
14 places move · 2 documented exemptions

C · No new step

not recommended

Leave the ladder at seven steps and treat everything above 24px as a per-surface exemption, each documented individually.

Nothing moves at all — but 12 sizes stay outside the system, which is precisely how the portal ended up with 62 of them. It preserves today's look at the cost of the thing the ladder exists to prevent.

Zero visible change.
12 sizes remain unmanaged.
0 places move · 12 exemptions

What the whole screen tells you that the specimen doesn't

The score card does not get taller — it has slack above and below the number, so the +18% is absorbed and the roster rows beside it do not move. The gain is that the score now clearly outranks everything else in the frame, which is what a game-day screen is for.
