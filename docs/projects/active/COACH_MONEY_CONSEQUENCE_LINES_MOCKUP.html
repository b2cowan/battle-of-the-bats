Coach money · a review project · nothing built

The most trusted sentence on the screen is the one nobody checks

Every money form promises the coach what will happen when they save. Those promises are factual claims written in prose, and no gate in this repo can check one — spelling, contrast, layout, tokens and the schema ratchets all pass happily on a sentence that is confidently false. Three have been found wrong. All three read perfectly.

01 — the evidence

Three that were wrong, and how each was found

Club tab · filing a billfixed 2026-09-01

Until it's filed, this bill doesn't appear on Budget vs. Actual at all.

False  The report deliberately counts unfiled club money under Not itemized. It always appeared — it just had no name. The sentence taught the coach the opposite of the design, on the screen where they would act on it, and would send them hunting for a problem that did not exist.

How it was found: someone traced what the report actually does. It had survived a build, a /simplify and a /review.

Record money · an arrivallive today

When you save: $200.00 comes in on Sep 2. Cash on hand goes up by $200.00.

When you save: $200.00 comes back in on Sep 2, and Umpires drops by $200.00 on Budget vs. Actual — it isn't counted as income, and nobody is owed anything.

Silent  Same form, same coach, money arriving both times. The income branch names cash on hand; the refund branch never mentions it — and a refund does raise cash on hand. Not false. Incomplete, and inconsistent with its own sibling three lines away.

How it was found: ten minutes of sizing this project.

Payables · the totals bandfixed 2026-08-25

still owing  $1,240.00 across 3 commitments

False  The figure summed a field meaning remaining on an unpaid piece and the full amount on a paid one. Ticking Paid counted settled instalments at face value under the words "still owing". The words went out with the bug — but they had shipped.

The point is not that three were wrong

It is that all three read perfectly, and several of these lines already carry review annotations in the code — they have had scrutiny. Reading a claim is not checking it. Nothing short of executing the path can tell the difference between a true sentence and a fluent one.

02 — anatomy

A consequence line is three different things at once

When you save: no team cash moves. $200.00 counts in the budget as usual, and the team owes Jenny Doyle's family $200.00 — saved as a credit you can put against their dues or pay out any time.

■ numeric claim — a figure a module can return ■ structural claim — a rule, no module returns it
Why the split decides everything below

A numeric claim can be made unbreakable: have the sentence report the figure the save is about to compute, instead of asserting one an author typed. A structural claim — "nobody is owed anything", "it isn't counted as income" — has no figure to report. No mechanism can hold it. It stays prose, and prose has to be audited.

So no option below removes the audit

The audit is the floor. The options differ only in whether the next sentence can go wrong the same way.

In scope: does the sentence match what the save actually does. Out of scope: tone, voice, length. A sentence that is accurate is finished — this is a truth audit, not a copy pass.

How each one gets checked

Execute the path; do not read the sentence. Reading is what let all three through. Put the form in that state, save, and look at the figures it named — cash on hand, the budget line, the month, the family credit, the register row.

⚠ Where a pure module owns the arithmetic, run the module rather than reading a screen: a screen can agree with a wrong sentence when both derive from the same mistake.

⚠ A fixture that cannot reach the state proves nothing. Several branches need a fixture in that exact shape. Anything unreachable gets recorded as unreachable, never as verified.

04 — four ways to stop the next one

The same sentence, drawn four ways

A · Audit only the floor

When you save: $200.00 comes back in on Sep 2. Cash on hand goes up by $200.00, and Umpires drops by $200.00 on Budget vs. Actual — it isn't counted as income, and nobody is owed anything.

Fix what is wrong, add the missing cash clause, change nothing structural.

What it costs: half a session. What it does not do: anything at all about the next sentence someone writes. The three found here were written by careful authors and reviewed.

It is under every other option, not an alternative to them — the structural claims can only ever be held this way.

B · The figures are reported, not asserted recommended, with A

When you save: $200.00 comes back in on Sep 2. Cash on hand goes up by $200.00, and Umpires drops by $200.00 on Budget vs. Actual — it isn't counted as income, and nobody is owed anything.

figures come from the same code the save runs

It looks identical, and that is the entire point. The coach sees no change. What changes is that the numbers in the sentence are computed by the module that is about to move the money — so the sentence cannot disagree with the outcome. A wrong figure becomes a failing test, not a fluent lie.

Precedent exists in this product: the dues credit preview was moved into the shared module precisely so the recording conversation draws the same preview the writer uses.

Honest limit: covers the numeric half only. "Nobody is owed anything" still rests on A.

C · Prose, plus a "what moves" strip your call

When you save: $200.00 comes back in on Sep 2 — it isn't counted as income, and nobody is owed anything.

Cash on hand ▲ $200.00 Umpires ▼ $200.00 Nobody owed

The figures leave the prose and become a rendered strip; the sentence keeps only what a strip cannot say. Scannable, and every number is visibly derived.

The trade: it is a second thing to read on a form that already asks a lot, and it makes every money form louder — including the eight quiet warning lines where a strip would be heavier than the warning.

D · Retire the prose not recommended
Cash on hand ▲ $200.00 Umpires ▼ $200.00 Income no change Owed none

Every claim becomes a rendered figure. Nothing can be false.

Why not: these lines earn their keep by explaining an invisible consequence in a coach's language — "it isn't counted as income, and nobody is owed anything" is the sentence that stops a treasurer double-counting a refund. A chip reading Income — no change states the same fact and teaches nothing. It also quietly deletes the structural claims rather than verifying them, which looks like safety and is the opposite.

05 — what I need from you

Where this lands

1 · Do A, and do B on top of it

A is not optional — it is the only thing that can hold a structural claim, and it is what fixes the three already found. B is the cheap durable half: the coach sees nothing change, and a wrong figure stops being a sentence nobody can check and becomes a failing test. Together they are roughly a session and a half.

2 · C is a real option and I am not pushing it

It is the most visible answer and it would look good on the record-money form. I have it below the line for one reason: it does not fit the eight quiet unplanned-item warnings, where a strip of chips would be heavier than the warning it decorates — so it splits the family into two shapes. If you want it on the big form only, say so and I will draw that.

3 · One scoping question

The in-app help articles restate many of these same claims in their own words. Sweeping them too roughly doubles the job. My recommendation is screens first — the screen is where the coach acts, and help has its own sweep — but it is your call, and a screen that is right beside a guide that is wrong is its own kind of defect.

06 — option C, drawn properly

You liked C. Drawing it across every state made a better case for it than I did

The argument I missed: a strip makes an OMISSION visible

My case for C was that it looks good and every figure is visibly derived. The stronger case is this: the refund defect could not have been written in C.

Today's refund sentence forgets to mention cash on hand, and nothing about a paragraph makes that absence noticeable — a sentence that omits something still reads as a complete sentence. A strip has a slot per figure. A missing chip is a hole you can see. C turns this whole class of defect from "a wrong sentence", which is invisible, into "a missing chip", which is not.

That is a durability argument, not a looks argument — and it is the one that moves C from "your call" to a real contender against B.

But C only works if the prose gives up the numbers

If the strip shows Umpires ▼ $200.00 and the sentence also says "Umpires drops by $200", the form says everything twice and gets longer, not clearer. C is a redistribution, not an addition: figures move to the strip, and the sentence keeps only what a chip cannot say — the rule, the reassurance, the thing a treasurer needs in words.

So the real cost of C is not the strip. It is rewriting thirteen sentences to stop naming numbers. That is the part to weigh.

the rule that falls out

A strip appears only where something moves

Three states move nothing at all. A strip of no change · no change · none is worse than a sentence saying "nothing moves" — it is chrome pretending to be information. Those keep prose and get no strip. This is also the answer to my own objection: the eight quiet unplanned-item warnings never get one.

Where I land now

C is a better idea than I gave it credit for, and it wants B underneath it. A strip of figures that an author still types by hand is a prettier way to be wrong — the chips only earn their promise if the numbers come from the code that does the saving. Built together, the strip shows what moves, B guarantees the figures are true, and the sentence is left holding exactly the claims that need a human: "a refund is not income, and nobody is owed anything."

That is the recommendation: A + B + C, C scoped to the seven states that actually move something. About two sessions, and the audit is still the first thing done — because it is what tells us which figures each strip should carry.

⚠ If two sessions is more than this is worth right now, A + B alone remains honest and invisible, and C can be added later without redoing it — B is the layer C would sit on either way. Nothing about doing A + B first forecloses C.
