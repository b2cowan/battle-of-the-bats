Does the page-title band earn its height on a phone?

It is the last large piece of chrome on all forty coach screens, and the 2026-08-24 Roster pass stopped short of it deliberately. Four options are drawn below, live and measured — every figure on this page is read off the drawing beside it, never asserted. It does earn it: the portal-wide prize is far smaller than it looks, and the biggest single number on the table belongs to one screen. Ruled 2026-08-25 — options 1 and 3 approved, 2 not built, 4 withdrawn.

What the measuring found

The band is a control row wearing a title’s name

At 390px the box is 44px because the help “?” is a 44×44 tap target, ruled portal-wide. Under that sits the 36px icon tile. The heading itself — Barlow Condensed 1.4rem at line-height 1.1 — is about 25px. The title text is responsible for roughly a quarter of the height it is being blamed for. Deleting words saves nothing; only moving controls does.

Overview is not the band’s fault, and it is the biggest number here

Every other screen is 60px. Overview is 116px, because its “Season setup 4/5” chip is too wide to share the title line, so it takes a second 44px row plus a 12px gap. That chip already carries a symbol — its progress ring — and the 2026-08-23 house rule says a phone turns words into symbols. Shrunk to the ring alone it fits the title-line corner beside the “?”, exactly as the Money hub’s Record “+” already does. Overview drops to 60px using a mechanism that shipped two days ago, reversing nothing. That one screen is worth more than the portal-wide alternative.

The right half of the phone masthead was not emptied because it was surplus. It was emptied because a short status string could not be made to fit there — two separate attempts to keep it by shrinking it both failed, and one ended in “6:30 p.m. · O…”.

Why folding the page name upstairs is the wrong trade

Option 2 is the one that saves real pixels, and the one I would not build. Switch to it at 390px and watch which word gives way: the team name ellipsizes to make room for the page name. An identity bar exists to say which team you are standing in; making the team’s name the compressible half inverts the bar’s purpose.

It also asks the page title to survive the phone masthead’s scroll collapse. Today’s band scrolls away honestly; a merged bar would make the page name a thing that vanishes and returns — precisely the borrowed space rejected on sight in August — unless the collapse ruling changes with it. And the plumbing is not free: the title is written by the page, the masthead by the team layout, so all forty screens must publish their name upward, with drill-ins, embedded hub tabs and the accessible heading each needing a separate answer.

The “?” has a better home, but not for the pixels — and it is a desktop win

Option 3 moves the help “?” into the masthead’s corner. It buys 8px on a phone and 0px on a desktop, where the band’s height is set by the 36px icon tile and the “?” is only 34px. Selling it as a space saving would be dishonest at either width. Unlike a “+”, a “?” is never ambiguous in an identity bar — it means “help with what I am looking at” everywhere in the product — which is exactly why the create button stays behind.

It moves at every width, not on phones only. The 2026-08-11 ruling makes the “?” chrome with “its own slot, always last, top-right at every width … one findable home”; a phone-only move would give it two homes. In the bar it is rendered last — after the status stack and the public-site link — so that clause stays literally true.

Not the top strip beside the account icon. That strip holds only leave-this-place doors, and the chat icon was removed from it in July 2026 for the two faults a “?” would repeat: it is not an exit, and it duplicated a door already reachable at the same width. The sidebar already carries a Help item, so a “?” up there would be a second desktop help door about 200px from the first.

And a correction to this option’s original pitch. It was sold on “help stops scrolling away” — true on a desktop, false on a phone. The desktop bar never collapses, so the “?” becomes permanently visible; the phone bar collapses to the bare team name and hides its whole right side, so the “?” folds away there regardless. It is ruled to fold with it rather than reopen the bare-name collapse. The lesson generalises: check whether a benefit survives the scroll state of the bar you are moving a control into, not just its width.

Why option 4 was withdrawn the day it was proposed

Option 4 was mine: make the fifth tab name the section you are standing in instead of always reading “More”. The owner’s reply was a question — would that make the More menu hard to find, and does any app do it well? — and the honest answer killed it.

The signal is already built, twice. The More tab already takes the full active treatment while you are inside a More section, and the sheet already highlights the row you are on. The rename was adding nothing but risk. And there is no effective precedent: iOS has auto-generated a “More” tab since 2008 for exactly this situation, and it keeps the word and the ••• glyph, putting the section’s name in a title bar at the top of the content — the same answer this review reaches from the other direction. Material’s guidance keeps bottom-bar labels static for the same reason: the bar trades on position and a fixed word. It would also have put a number on the wrong noun — that tab carries the unread-notification count, so renamed to “Money” the pip reads as two things waiting in Money.

The measurement that ruled it out as a replacement for the band still stands, and is worth keeping: the label is 9.6px uppercase in a tab about 74px wide, so Practice plans, Skills & Goals and Email families truncate. Switch to option 4 in the drawing to see it.

The ruling — 2026-08-25

Option 1, approved. Trim the bottom margin from 16px to 12px portal-wide, and give Overview’s setup chip its phone symbol so it joins the title line. Every other screen loses 4px; Overview loses 56px. Nothing is reversed, nothing needs new plumbing, and the band goes on naming the thirty-five screens that have no other name on screen.

Option 3, approved — as help placement, not as space, and at every width. The “?” moves into the team bar and is rendered last there. On a desktop it becomes permanently visible at zero pixel cost; on a phone it is worth 8px and one findable home. Not the top strip. The create “+” deliberately stays behind.

Option 2 not built. Option 4 withdrawn.

A measured “no change” was a live possibility here, and this is close to it. The band survives because the tap floor makes the portal-wide prize roughly 4–8px a screen, set against a plumbing change across forty screens, a scroll-collapse question, an accessibility question, and a 390px line that has already run out of horizontal room.
