The one edge, and its answer. A heading of yours with nothing under it has no side to appear under. It can only arise by abandoning the picker’s two-step after the first step, or by removing a heading’s last item. What was considered and set aside. A side on the item is already the rule; asking “in, out or both” per item would break it — a report reads a row’s side from what was filed, and “both” would leave it nothing to read. A side on the category was round 2’s Q6, now withdrawn; it would have removed the mixed shelf. Filtering the Category list to this side’s shelves only would forbid a mixed shelf of your own, so the list is ordered instead. A “+ Category” button on each band was round 3; set aside because a coach whose heading shows under the other band would read the band and make a twin — the heading is now chosen or made only inside the item form.

9 · Where this meets the two plans running beside it

“The category is the shelf” (built 2026-09-09, walk owed). Its first ruling — the category is the group on both sides, on every surface, items beneath, no shelf above the category — is what the bands and the shelf-under-each-band are. Its second — the forgetting index reads under the two direction headings in the form’s own words, money coming in first — is the same device this dialog uses, in the same order. Consistent by construction: a coach who has read the index reads this dialog the same way.

“One word, one line” (planning, mockup round 1). It collapses the plan to one line per item per season, on the premise that items are the shared vocabulary and the join between plan and real money — so one-off event words must not be minted as items. Four places the two touch:

  1. That premise is this dialog’s reason to exist. A vocabulary that carries every roll-up needs a place to be tidied — rename, fold into a shared item, remove — and needs adding to be deliberate. The add forms here say “saved to this team’s list only” and land under a side, which is the opposite of a generator of one-off words.
  2. The same fact from two sides. Their form says “already on this plan — add to it”; this dialog’s row says “1 budget line”. Once their change ships, an item can carry at most one line per season, and the sub-line can simply read “on this season’s plan”.
  3. Their open question, answered by the vocabulary. Their Phase 3 asks whether the join key needs a direction because “one item may appear as two rows”. An item carries exactly one side and has since the picker began filtering by it, so (season, team, item) already implies the side. Only lines filed before that rule could disagree; they should be counted in the same query as the twins.
  4. Sequence the nouns once. Their last phase rewrites the same help articles Q1 renames. Rule Q1 before that phase runs, so the help is rewritten once, in the final words.

Their plan also queues the standard vocabulary review (Insurance twice, Certification twice, and so on). That review is what makes this dialog’s “Everything” wall shorter, and the fold here is the coach-side of the same mechanism.
