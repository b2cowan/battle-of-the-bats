Support Console Stage 7

Stage 7 has one job, in your words: a growth person can see where every lead lands, gets metrics on historical conversions, and can input information to track and improve future growth opportunities. Today a lead is a row in a table, the conversion figure counts only the leads loaded on the page, and there is no history beyond the edit log. Nothing records where a lead came from apart from the web page the form sat on. The rule is the one Stages 3 and 5 used: the truth is shown when the screen opens, never remembered (R7).

Every drawing is true size, 1440 pixels wide; on a narrower window each scrolls sideways in its own box. Before is a capture from today's dev server. After is drawn on that same shell in the product's real warm and dark colours, flipped by the Warm / Dark switch at the top. Blue tags say what changes and why; each opens when you click it. Your eleven questions sit beside the drawing each one decides, each with my recommendation, and a paste-back at the end.

One example runs through every drawing. On Aug 4, Morgan Ellis, the registrar of a made-up club called Cedar Hollow Youth Sports, fills the early-access form on the League Plus page. Riley, a made-up growth staff member, works the lead through the summer and sends a pitch deck on Sep 3. On Oct 26 the club signs up for a League Plus trial with Morgan's address. Today, Oct 27, Riley confirms the match. Every name, club and example.com address is made up, and every figure is example data: dev has no leads at all. Anything marked Stage 6 or 8 is not part of this stage and is not drawn as if it were built.

READ THIS FIRST

Seven places where the code says something different from the prompt

1 · Today's "6 campaigns waiting" are six retired campaigns.

Today counts every marketing email ever written, including the six spotlight emails retired on Sep 7 and Oct 26. The Campaigns screen hides retired ones; Today's count was never told to. So every staff member who opens Today sees Campaigns past due 5 and Campaigns due soon 1, and none of them can be sent. It is a one-line fix to Today's count. Question 10 offers it to ride this stage, or to go now on its own.

2 · Purchases are recorded, but only since Oct 25, 2026. A club's sign-up date and a lead's whole life go back to May 20.

The prompt said billing events record no purchases. They do: "subscription started" and "upgraded" have been recorded since Oct 25, 2026 (two days ago), and are not rebuilt for anything earlier. Every lead, and every edit made to one, has been recorded since May 20, 2026, the day leads were built. So a live trend of leads and of "signed up" reaches back to the very first lead with no new job, but a trend of "started paying" starts on Oct 25. What a club pays now is always known. Drawing B says this on the screen, and question 3 turns on it.

3 · The three screens disagree because they use two definitions and three different row limits.

The Leads screen's band counts only the 100 leads on the page, and the page opens filtered to "New", so its Converted figure normally reads 0. It calls a lead converted if it has a club or a converted date. Business numbers reads the newest 5,000 and counts only leads with a club. The export stops at 1,000 and writes no audit entry. The fix is one definition, computed in one place, over every lead, and shown on every screen. Question 2 sets the definition.

4 · The same person can never be two leads; the same club can.

A second form from the same address updates the first lead and counts it ("filled the form 2 times"). So "filed twice" really means two people from one club, or one person with two addresses. Nothing ties those together today. Drawing G shows what to do, and an automatic match by email (question 2) must look past the owner, to an owner or admin who has joined the club.

5 · How often an automatic match would be right cannot be measured on dev, because dev has no leads.

The prompt asked what fraction of leads would match the account they became. Dev has zero leads, so there is nothing to measure. Production could answer it with a read-only count (no address printed), but that is a read of strangers' data, and it is yours to allow. The recommendation in question 2 does not depend on it: a match is offered to a person and never linked by itself, so a low match rate costs a little help, not a wrong record.

6 · The Audit log already shows every staff role a lead's name, email and words.

Each lead edit records the whole lead, before and after, in the Audit log, and the Audit log is open to all six staff roles, its export included. So Read only, Support and Billing can read strangers' details today, though they cannot open Leads. Refund entries already hide their details from roles that should not read them; lead entries never got that. Question 10 offers the fix: lead entries keep only which fields changed, and their details show only to the roles that may open Leads. It can go now, on its own.

7 · Anyone who knows a lead's email can rewrite that lead through the public form.

A second form with the same address replaces the lead's name, club, role and words, and its release-emails answer. That answer counts as "yes" unless it is turned off, and nobody confirms the address. So a stranger can turn someone's "no" back into "yes", or overwrite a lead staff have worked for weeks. Today's Copy emails also runs in the browser and ignores "Do not contact". Recommended (question 10): a new form from a known address never turns a "no" into a "yes" and never overwrites a worked lead. It lands on the timeline as "filled the form again", with its words. Copy emails moves to the server. No visitor sees a difference.

WHAT A GROWTH PERSON WILL SEE

Six changes, in plain words

  • Leads becomes a pipeline. One band shows how many leads sit at each step, from New to Signed up or Lost, over every lead rather than one page of them. The list opens on Needs you: new leads nobody has answered, follow-ups that have come due, leads stuck on a step for 30 days, and a club waiting to be confirmed.
  • Each lead gets its own page with a timeline. It shows every form they filled, every step change, every note, the club or portal it became, and when it started paying. The timeline is rebuilt from the edit log we have kept since May 20. Notes become dated entries instead of one box that gets overwritten.
  • A club that signs up with a lead's email is offered, never linked by itself. The lead says "a club signed up with this email", and a person links it or says "Not this club".
  • Numbers, beside the pipeline. Of the leads that arrived in a period: how many signed up as a club or portal, how many of those pay now, and how long it took. Broken down by the page they came from, the plan they wanted and the source staff recorded, with a month-by-month trend. The same figures appear on Today and in Business numbers.
  • Growth staff record what they learn: where a lead came from (a short list plus words), the next step, a follow-up date, notes, and why a lead was lost.
  • Pitch decks move onto the kit, unchanged in what they do. Campaigns keeps its name, and the pricing promos in Plans & pricing become "Promotions", so the two are never confused.

No visitor sees anything new unless you choose the public option in question 4. Deliberately not in Stage 7: the four catalogue screens beyond their names and menu places (question 1); emailing leads from Campaigns (question 5); counting deck opens (question 8, option only); a daily snapshot job (question 3, option only); club web addresses (Stage 6); staff, roles and the console's word pass (Stage 8).

Question 1 · What Stage 7 covers

The plan's Stage 7 row lists eight screens. Four of them serve this job: Leads, the numbers, Campaigns and Pitch decks. The other four (Plans & pricing, Approval queue, Email templates, Export registry) are the product catalogue's screens, read by Product and Billing. Growth cannot even open them today. Redrawing them here would roughly double the stage: four more captures and redraws, the catalogue's approval flow to re-walk, and a second reader (Product) with different jobs. None of that brings the growth objective closer.

Recommended: A. Stage 7 is the growth screens. The catalogue four stay as built; they get only their menu places and one rename (question 5's "Promotions"). If they need a redesign, they get a stage of their own with Product as its reader.

Question 2 · What "signed up" and "pays" mean

Signed up: today it is a club typed in by hand. Matching by email alone would link a stranger to someone else's club when two people share an address, or when an address is recycled. A missing link costs a figure; a wrong link puts a stranger's details on a club's record. So the match is offered and a person confirms it, as drawn in A. It looks at an owner or admin who has joined the club, signed in with an address they confirmed themselves; never an invited coach or volunteer, a staff account, a demo club or a club a staff address owns. The offer is worked out when staff open the lead, never while a visitor submits a form. "Not this club" dismisses it for good.

Pays: the club's own plan is not the free Tournament plan, its billing is active, and a real subscription stands behind it. A trial, free time and a Founding Season comp are not paying. That is the rule the billing records already use to date a club's first subscription. A Coaches Portal a club pays for counts with that club, not as a second club.

Recommended: an offered match a person confirms, and "pays" as above. Optionally, a one-off read-only count on production would tell us how often the offer will fire (no address printed). It is yours to allow.

Question 6 · A reason on a lead edit, and who may export every lead's email

The reason rule covers changes to a customer's club, person, plan, money or retention. A lead is not yet a customer, and the build's own reason check already exempts a lead edit ("a lead edit is the record itself"). The timeline and the edit log record who changed what. The export is different: it is a file of strangers' names and addresses that leaves the building, and today it writes no audit entry and stops silently at 1,000 rows.

Recommended: no reason on a lead edit, and no confirm window. The export stays with the roles that may open Leads (Super admin, Product, Growth). It writes an audit entry (who, when, how many rows, which filter), takes every row instead of stopping at 1,000, and never includes a lead marked "Asked not to be contacted" in its email column.

DRAWING H

What could go wrong, and what stops it

A threat list over the design, before any code. A lead is a stranger's name and address, given to us for one purpose; every line below starts from that. The build turns every rule into a guard or a test (the list below the table).

#ThreatWhat stops it

Tests the build must carry

  • One definition. The Leads band, Numbers, Today's rows and Business numbers' card all read one computation. A build check fails if a growth read cuts rows (no 100, 1,000 or 5,000 limit on a count). "Signed up" counts a lead with a club and no step, and vice versa, the same way everywhere.
  • Pays. A trial, free time (a running plan grant), a Founding Season comp and the free Tournament plan are not paying. An active subscription is. A Coaches Portal a club pays for counts with that club, once.
  • The offered match. An owner or admin who has joined, with an address they confirmed themselves, matches, whatever its case (one way of tidying an address on both sides; tested with accents, a dotted capital I and a +tag). An invited coach does not; an address confirmed by staff does not. A staff account, a demo club, an unconfirmed address and a dismissed pair are never offered. Two leads can be offered one club. Nothing is ever linked without a person's press. Unlinking puts the step back and keeps both timeline entries.
  • The timeline is rebuilt from a fixture of edit-log rows: step changes, source, follow-up, the club, in the right order, with the right names. A note is dated and signed and cannot be changed; a removed note leaves "a note was removed" and its text is gone.
  • The Audit log and the Story. A lead edit's entry holds field names and the step, never a lead's words or address; old entries' details show only to the growth power, in the page, the table and the export. A club's Story serialized for Support holds no lead number and no lead words.
  • The public form. A second form from a known address never turns a "no" into a "yes" and never overwrites a worked lead; it adds a timeline entry. A database error never reaches the visitor. Copy emails is a server call behind the growth power that leaves out "Asked not to be contacted", with an audit entry.
  • Lost needs a reason, refused by the server, not only the form. "Asked not to be contacted" never reaches Copy emails or an export's email column.
  • The export writes an audit entry (who, when, rows, filter), takes every row, and makes a cell starting with = + - @, a tab or a return safe for a spreadsheet, in every column (an address can start with =), in this export and the Audit log's. The audit entry is written before the file is sent, and a failed write stops the export. A ceiling of rows is set and said on the screen if ever reached.
  • Powers. Every new route asks the growth power before it reads anything. Support, Billing and Read only are refused by the server. Business numbers' card shows them counts with no door.
  • Today's campaign rows count live campaigns only: a retired campaign with a past date counts nothing (the regression for headline 1).
  • Words. The spelling gate learns the new words (Signed up, Pay now, Not set, Promotions). "Promotions" changes labels only: a test reads the stored values and addresses unchanged.

The security lens

A separate reviewer ran /review's security lens over this page and the code it stands on, before you saw it. It found 4 high, 5 medium and 4 low issues, every one now folded into the drawings, the threat list and the tests above. Two are live today rather than risks of the design, and lead the page (headlines 6 and 7): the Audit log shows every staff role a lead's name, email and words, and anyone who knows a lead's address can rewrite it through the public form, its "no" to release emails included. The others that changed the design: the offered match narrowed to an owner or admin who has joined, with an address they confirmed themselves; the offer worked out only when staff open a lead, so the public form never reveals who has an account; a club's Story filtered in the database, not on the screen; the export's audit entry written before the file goes, and spreadsheet-safe in every column; Copy emails moved to the server; a note a Super admin can remove; and the demo-club check that refuses when it cannot tell.

Question 11 · Where written rules meet (S7-D1 to S7-D6)

S7-D1 · A count in the scope picker. The toolbar rules (Oct 17) put "Needs you" in the scope picker. The waiting-count rule says a count is the amber pill, never "· 8" in a name. Recommended: the scope picker is rounded (it picks which list) and carries the amber pill, as drawn in A; it is the Teams division picker's shape, not a plain dropdown.

S7-D2 · Numbers has no Search. "Every view keeps the same line" (Oct 1), but there is nothing to search in a page of figures. Recommended: an exception for a figures view: the period picker on the left; Filter (plan, page, source) and Tools (export the numbers) on the right; no Search.

S7-D3 · Two pickers on the left. Leads' line carries the view switch and the scope picker, both "which list". Recommended: keep both, view switch first; the scope picker only exists in Pipeline.

S7-D4 · "Time to sign up" counts to the club's creation, not to the link. A late link would otherwise stretch the figure (Cedar Hollow signed up Oct 26 and was linked Oct 27). Recommended: the day the club or portal was created, as drawn.

S7-D5 · Export inside Tools. "Export is one button, Excel first" was ruled for screens where Export stands on the toolbar. Here it moves behind Tools. Recommended: Tools' Export item opens the same one export window, Excel first, so the rule holds one step later.

S7-D6 · Business numbers under Growth for every role. For Support, Billing and Read only, the Growth group then holds only Business numbers (drawn in G5). Recommended: keep the menu entry for every role, and keep Today's foot link too.
