Support Console Stage 3

Stage 3 is the owner's first job from the kickoff: "was this customer charged twice, and can you fix it?", answered inside the console, from the club's record, with no trip to Stripe's dashboard. The rule it builds to is R7: the money truth is shown, never remembered. Every drawing is at true size, 1440 pixels wide (the console is desktop only). On a narrower window each one scrolls sideways inside its own box. Before is a capture from today's dev server, in the look you walked on Oct 25. After is drawn on that same shell, in the product's real warm and dark colours. Blue tags say what changes and why; each one opens when you click it. The Warm / Dark switch at the top flips every drawing. Your questions sit beside the drawing each one decides, each with my recommendation.

Every amount, card and Stripe number on this page is an example, not a price. Prices live in the pricing facts and are /strategy's; nothing here states or changes one. The club names are dev's test clubs; no real customer's name or card digits appear. Anything marked Stage 5 or 6 is not part of this stage and is not drawn as if it were built.

READ THIS FIRST

Three places where the code says something different from the prompt

1 · M6 is not about the Coaches Portal. Any staff plan change on a paying club leaves Stripe charging the old price, and Stripe then puts the old plan back.

M6 comes from the archived operator walkthrough: "Setting a plan to Coaches Portal doesn't clear the old Stripe link (only the free Tournament downgrade does), could leave a paid sub attached to a near-free plan." The code today is wider than that line. When staff change a club's plan with the pencil, only our record changes; nothing is said to Stripe. The subscription keeps billing the old price. On its next renewal (or any change in Stripe), Stripe tells us the subscription's plan, and we write it back over the staff change, silently, while the Story and the audit log still say staff changed it. A move to the free Tournament plan clears our link, but the subscription in Stripe keeps charging, and the next renewal links it again.

Stage 4 already ruled the same thing for free time: no plan grant while Stripe charges (your Q3, Oct 25). The pencil was never brought under that rule. What I recommend: the pencil does not change the plan of a club with a live Stripe subscription. In its place, one line says why and what to do: the customer moves plan on their own billing page (which moves Stripe properly), or staff cancel the subscription first. Moving a customer's Stripe subscription from the console is a second money write (it bills or credits the difference), so I have not drawn it as the default. Question 9.

2 · We record no charges, so "what we recorded" beside each charge cannot be shown honestly

The end-state drawing put a We recorded column beside every Stripe charge. The code keeps no charge, invoice or payment of a club's subscription anywhere: only the customer and subscription numbers, the plan, the billing status and the renewal date. So the honest comparison (R7) is at the subscription: Stripe's plan, status, renewal date and subscription number beside ours, with every difference named. The charges are Stripe's alone, read when the tab opens, and the "charged twice" help works from Stripe's own invoices and periods (drawing B), not from a column that would always read "nothing".

And a refund is the console's first money write to Stripe. Today the audit writer does not stop a change when its own entry fails to save (it notes the failure in the server log and carries on). That is tolerable for a note. It is not for a refund. The design writes the refund's own record before Stripe is asked, so a refund can never exist in Stripe without a line on our side (drawing G).

3 · Two of the prompt's leads are already out of date, in the good direction

  • "Override revoke and the add-on toggle record no reason." They do now. Stage 2 made both ask why, and the reason is kept inside the entry. Only entries written before Stage 2 have none. The reason column is still worth its migration (the audit log screen shows reasons only as text inside raw values, and 49 of the 70 places that write the log keep none), but no action is missing one.
  • "No purchase or upgrade event exists." It does. Stripe's messages already record subscription started and moved up a plan for every club; the Story simply leaves them out. So purchases can join the Story with no database change and no change to how we receive Stripe's messages. Only the charges and refunds themselves are not kept on our side (question 4).
WHAT STAFF AND CUSTOMERS WILL SEE

Seven changes, in plain words

  • The Money tab reads Stripe. When it opens, it reads the club's subscription, card and charges from Stripe and sets the subscription beside our record. One line says "Stripe and our record agree", or names each difference. Nothing Stripe says is stored.
  • "Charged twice" is named for you. Two paid charges for the same month, two live subscriptions, a failed payment that Stripe tried again, a charge that is not the subscription's: each gets a plain line under the charge, with the evidence. Two charges on one day for different months is said to be fine.
  • Refund from the console. Open a charge, refund all or part of it, to the card it came from, with a reason. The window says in one sentence what will happen, that it cannot be undone, and that the club's plan does not change.
  • The Story gains its money. Purchases, plan moves, charges and refunds become Story entries. They are facts: never a Put it back.
  • A reason column in the audit log. Every staff change shows its reason in its own column; older entries say "Not recorded".
  • Find learns invoices and charges. A Stripe charge, invoice or payment number, or an invoice's own number, finds its club. Card digits are not searched (question 5).
  • The customer: nothing new on their screens. A refund reaches their card in Stripe's usual 5 to 10 business days, and whether they get a receipt email is question 3.

Deliberately not in Stage 3: credits and account balances (they stay in Stripe, Q7); moving a paying club's Stripe subscription to another plan from the console (question 9); families' payments to a club (that is the club's own money, on the club's own Stripe account); Status, the sent-email log and Feedback reply (Stage 5); club web addresses (Stage 6).

Question 10 · One spelling for the money words

"Canceled" and "Cancelled" both reach customers today, on two billing pages and in emails. The one-spelling rule makes that a product bug, and the spelling gate does not catch it yet. Canadian usage (the product's house language) is cancelled, like cheque and colour.

Recommended: "Cancelled" and "Trial" wherever a person reads them, console and customer pages and emails; one set of renewal labels and "Billing status" in the console; the gate learns "Canceled" so it cannot come back. Question 1 · Who may refund, and is there a cap? Who reads Stripe's side?

A refund can only return a customer's own money to the card it came from, and never more than they paid. So the risk is revenue given back without cause, not money taken. That makes the useful limits who, never your own club, and being seen, more than a dollar cap. Stage 4's caps were by time because free time has no natural ceiling; a refund's ceiling is the charge itself.

Recommended: Super admin and Billing refund; never a club you belong to in any way; no dollar cap per refund; a charge older than 6 months, or a sixth refund by one person in 24 hours, needs a Super admin (the Stage 4 shape: Billing limited, Super admin not). The daily brake is for a stolen or misused account: without it, one Billing login could refund every charge one at a time. "Belong to" is wider than Stage 4 checks today: a club member, a coach on one of its teams, an owner or billing owner of a Coaches Portal it pays for, a pending invite, or a staff email equal to the club's owner or Stripe customer email. Stage 4's free time adopts the same check. Reading Stripe's side (charges, card, refunds, and money entries in the Story) follows your A2: Super admin, Billing and Support. Support reads and hands a refund to Billing. Product, Growth and Read only see our record only.

Question 2 · Full only, or part too? To the original card only?

Stripe refunds only to the card a charge came from; there is no other destination to choose. Part refunds are the common case for a plan moved by mistake mid-month.

Recommended: all or part, as drawn; always to the original card (the only choice, stated in the sentence); several part refunds allowed up to the charge.

Question 3 · Does the customer hear about the refund?

Stripe emails a refund receipt only if a switch in our Stripe account's settings is on (Customer emails → Refunds), the customer has an email, and the charge was on a Stripe customer. It applies to refunds made from the console the same way. Whether that switch is on in production is not something the code can tell: it needs you to look. A notice from us would be a new customer-facing send, and account emails are not logged until Stage 5.

Recommended: Stripe's own receipt only, with the switch confirmed on in production before Stage 3 ships; no email from us. The sentence then ends "Stripe emails them a receipt." If the switch is off, the sentence says "The customer is not emailed."

Question 4 · Where do charges and refunds in the Story come from?

A · Read Stripe live when the Story opens (drawn). No database change, no change to how we receive Stripe's messages. Purchases, plan moves and console refunds come from what we keep, so they show even when Stripe is down; only charges, and refunds made in Stripe's dashboard, wait for Stripe. Cost: a Stripe read each time a Story opens (well inside Stripe's limits).

B · Keep them: Stripe sends us a message for every paid invoice and refund, and we keep each as an event. The Story then works fully with Stripe down. Cost: a migration, a change to how production receives Stripe's messages (a release step with /release), and subscribing production's Stripe connection to the refund messages in Stripe's settings (an owner step); history before the switch-on is missing unless back-filled.

Recommended: A now. The Story's own entries already cover what staff did; what waits on Stripe is only what Stripe did, which is also exactly what the Money tab cannot show without Stripe. B can follow if a Stripe outage ever costs a support call.

Question 5 · Find and card digits

Stripe can search charges by a card's last four (the end state said it could not). But the last four are shared by many customers; a search for them across every customer is a card-data search whose results need a second clue anyway; it runs about a minute behind; and it would put card digits in the console's search box, which A3 says is never recorded but is still typed and sent. Every real "charged twice" call starts with an email, a club name or a receipt, all of which Find already takes or takes now.

Recommended: whole Stripe charge, payment and invoice numbers and an invoice's printed number; no card-digit search; the panel says so in one line.

DRAWING H

The refund's threat list, and the tests the build must carry

A refund is a money write: designed, reviewed and tested as one. The threat list below is over the design, before any code; the build's own review runs over the code.

Who, how much, what is kept

  • Who refunds: Super admin and Billing (question 1), through a new refund power in the one rulebook. Support reads.
  • How much: never more than is still refundable on the charge, read from Stripe on the server at the moment of refunding. The browser sends a charge number and an amount; the server trusts neither until Stripe confirms both.
  • What is kept: who, when, the club, the charge number, the amount, the reason, Stripe's refund number and Stripe's answer, in the audit log (reason in its own column) and the Story. Never the card's number, expiry or name: the last four and brand are read from Stripe each time they are shown.

The order that stops a refund going missing

  • 1. Write our record of the refund as started, in a refunds record of its own (the audit log cannot hold a state or refuse a second start). Its number is the refund key. If this write fails, stop: nothing goes to Stripe (G 7).
  • 2. Ask Stripe once, with that key, the exact amount in cents and the remaining amount the person saw. "All of it" is sent as an amount, never left blank, so a refund made meanwhile in Stripe cannot change what this one does.
  • 3. Complete our record with Stripe's answer: done; refused only on Stripe's definite no; unknown on a timeout or a Stripe fault.
  • Recovery never asks Stripe to refund again. It lists the charge's refunds in Stripe and looks for our number: found, done; not found once the first request cannot still be running, closed as not made (G 6). Stripe forgets a refund key after about a day, so sending it again later could make a second refund.
  • One refund at a time per charge, enforced by the database, not by reading first (G 5). A start that is closed as not made releases the charge.
#ThreatWhat the design does

The tests the build must carry

  • A mocked-Stripe suite (no network): a retry with the same key makes one refund; a double submit makes one refund; Stripe's "already refunded", "disputed" and "more than the charge" each leave no money moved and a refused record; a lost answer more than a day old is recovered by listing the charge's refunds and sends no second refund request; a start still in flight keeps its hold, an abandoned one releases it; two starts for one charge at once, the database refuses the second.
  • Server rules: a role without the refund power is refused before anything is read; "your own club" refuses a club member, a team coach, a Coaches Portal owner or billing owner the club pays for, a pending invite, and a staff email equal to the owner's or the Stripe customer's; a demo club is refused; an amount above the refundable remainder is refused from Stripe's figure, not the browser's; "all of it" with a changed remainder is refused; amounts that are not whole cents (20.1, "20.10", empty, huge) are refused; a blank reason, or one holding what looks like a card number, is refused; the sixth refund by one person in 24 hours and a charge older than 6 months need a Super admin, judged from Stripe's own charge date.
  • Charge ownership on every read, not only on refund: a charge with no customer on a club with none is refused; a Stripe customer shared by two clubs is refused; a charge number for another club opened in this club's Money tab shows nothing; a test-mode charge on a live key (and the reverse) is refused.
  • Words that leave us: Stripe receives only our refund number, the club and the staff member, never the typed reason; Stripe's error text never reaches the browser or our records whole (fixed sentences from Stripe's error code; only its code and request number kept).
  • Find: a quote, OR, or anything not shaped like a Stripe or invoice number never builds a Stripe search.
  • Order: if our first write fails, Stripe is never called; the audit entry exists whether Stripe accepts or refuses.
  • No card data kept: a guard that fails if a refund or audit write carries a card number, expiry or holder name.
  • Reads: the Money tab and Story send Stripe's side only to roles with the Stripe-reading power; a Stripe outage renders our record and one line, not an error page; nothing Stripe returns is stored.
  • Test mode: the test-mode mark shows whenever the site holds a test key and never with a live one.
  • A window's foot sentence: a build check that every sentence in a console window's foot (the charge, refund, confirm and free time windows, and any new one) wears the kit's foot-note face, 12 px in the quiet ink, whether it arrives bare or with a class of its own; the console's own foot-note style is brought onto that face. The Oct 25 fix covered only a bare sentence.
  • Question 9: the shared plan-change rule refuses a club holding an uncancelled Stripe subscription, whatever its billing status (unpaid and incomplete included), through the pencil, Bulk changes, Put it back and the record; Bulk skips it with a reason line.
  • Reason column: every staff write passes one; the product's jobs pass none; old reasons are copied once and read back.
Question 8 · The double plan grant: fix it in this stage's migration?

Two staff giving a club a plan at the same instant can both succeed (Stage 4's finish review, Medium). Access stays sane (the newest wins), but the Story would show two. The fix is one database rule: at most one running plan grant per club. It costs one line in a migration Stage 3 is writing anyway, plus a check that no club already holds two (none should).

Recommended: ride this stage's migration.

The design pass

The design pass compared every frame with the real console's code and the written standards. It found eighteen defects. All are fixed above; the two places where written rules meet are listed below.

  • The audit log was drawn from memory. It had lost the Field, Old value and New value columns and invented a "Changed" one. It is redrawn from the real table, with Reason added after Action, and the real toolbar. Then, on your call (Oct 26), the raw values moved into the entry window, and Field with them (question 12).
  • One club told three histories. The Money tab, the duplicate drawing and the Story disagreed about what the club bought and when, and a refund in the Story was missing from the band. Drawings B to D now share one example payment history, with totals that add up. Drawing A is a second club with a clean history.
  • Invented parts were replaced with the kit's own. The agree line is the kit's callout, the refund's success line is the kit's good notice, "Test mode" is the product's existing chip and sentence, the danger button is the kit's tint and the window's sentence is the kit's olive box.
  • Answers after a click belong in the window (Stripe refused, more than is left, our record failed, no answer), as the kit's field error. Lines before a click stay as the refusal line the console already has.
  • Find was redrawn on its real words: "Stripe · 1", "Nothing matches “…”", and the foot "Card digits are not searched. Ask for the email on the receipt instead."
  • Small ones: "Refund this charge", not "Refund…"; whole Stripe numbers; the kit's flag icon, not a text glyph; Billing's frames no longer show "Add a note" (Billing lost it in Stage 2); "Refund the later one" is a door; the band says "from our record"; engineer words ("key", "a message we missed") rewritten.

Where two written rules meet (for you to rule, recommendation first)

  • D-1 · Red for refusals. The console's June 26 rule keeps red for what cannot be undone; Stage 4's D-3 made a refusal red when nobody here can get past it, and the kit's field error is red text. Refusals like "Stripe did not refund it" undo nothing. Recommended: the June 26 rule governs buttons and doors only; refusals and errors may be red lines. One sentence in the design log.
  • D-2 · A heading above every card, or the toolbar's count as its heading. Recommended: a table's toolbar count is enough for a card that holds one table; a card without a table (like the subscription comparison) gets a heading. Drawn that way.
Question 11 · Where two written rules meet (D-1 and D-2 above)

Recommended: both as drawn: red is for buttons and doors that cannot be undone, and refusal lines may be red; a single-table card needs no heading above it, any other card does.
