Walk · 2026-10-13

A Premium coach, a shared link, and a second team

What a coach who already has a team meets on a tournament’s registration page, in the order they meet it. Today’s screens are drawn from the code; the evidence is a read-only production lookup of the coach who reported it.

What happened

The coach owns one live Premium Coaches Portal, Milton Bats U13 Purple. Under every Premium team sits a free team that the registration form reads. His is the free team he made in June for last season’s Battle of the Bats, and it still carries that team’s name, Milton Bats U13 White.
Oct 13The coachThe product
9:45 p.m.Opens the 2027 link from WhatsApp, fills in the form, taps Review, then SubmitWhatsApp’s browser is signed out. The form tries to make an account, finds one, signs him in, and sends him back to the form with a team picker
Sees one team offered: Milton Bats U13 White · Coaches PortalThat is his Purple portal, labelled with the old free team’s name
9:46 p.m.Chooses New TeamMakes a second free team, “Milton Bats Purple - Jackson”, and links the entry to it

The organizer has already accepted the entry. The stray team is empty (no roster, schedule, fees or messages). His Purple portal does not show the 2027 tournament anywhere. The portal has five other staff, and none of them could have seen the team in that picker.

The stations

1The link arrives in a group chat

As built
Organizers share registration links in WhatsApp and Facebook groups. Those apps open links in their own browser, which does not share the phone’s sign-in. A coach who is signed in on Safari arrives signed out.
My read
This is the main road in, not an edge case. Any fix that only helps a signed-in coach misses most of them.

2The form opens as a new team

As built
Team Name first, then your name, email, division and “Create a Password — this sets up your free Coaches Portal”. The only hint for a returning coach is one grey line under the password box.
My read
Everything on this screen says “you are new here”. The coach typed a new team name into the first box before the product knew who he was.
See it drawn →

3Review, still anonymous

As built
The Review step (the coach’s screenshot) restates what he typed. The email is not checked until he taps Submit.
My read
By the time the product recognizes him, he has already decided this is a new team twice.

4Bounced back to a picker with the wrong name

As built
A notice says he’s been signed in but not registered. A picker offers Existing Team or New Team; the list holds one team, named after the free team underneath his portal.
My read
He chose correctly by what he could see: “White” is not the team he is entering. This is the defect that cost him, and the cheapest one to fix.
See it drawn →

5What is left behind

As built
The entry lives on a stray free team the Premium portal cannot see: no tournament on its schedule or Tournaments page, and roster submission reads the stray team’s empty roster. Staff on the Premium team never see the team on the form at all.
My read
One wrong tap leaves a permanent split that only a data fix can undo today.

Standing back

R1A coach who has an account is recognized before they type a team name, not after they press Submit.
R2A team is offered under the name its portal shows. The free team underneath is plumbing and never appears by its own name.
R3An empty list is never the only thing a coach sees. If a team exists that they can’t register, they see it and who can.

Product-manager brief

Register Your Existing Team — PM brief

What changes for the coach

A coach who already has a team on FieldLogicHQ registers that team for a tournament instead of making a new one. The registration form starts by asking for their email. If they have an account, they sign in right there and their team is offered first, under the name their Coaches Portal shows, already chosen. Making a separate team is still possible, and the form says plainly what that means. After they submit, the tournament shows up in their Coaches Portal.

On a Premium team, the head coach and the team manager can register the team. The head coach can give that to anyone else on staff from the Staff page, one switch per person, and whoever holds it also sees and looks after those entries on the team's Tournaments page. Staff without it see the team on the form with who to ask, rather than an empty list.

Why it matters

On 2026-10-13 a Premium coach registered for a tournament through a WhatsApp link and ended up with a second, separate team. The form offered his Premium team under an old name he didn't recognize, so he made a new one. His Premium portal now can't see that tournament: not on its schedule, not on its Tournaments page, and roster submission reads the wrong team.

The way in is the problem. Organizers share registration links in group chats, and chat apps open links in their own browser, signed out. Today's form treats every signed-out visitor as new, and only finds out who they are when they press Submit.

Customer impact

  • Every Premium coach who registers from a shared link. They are our paying coaches, and the tournament is the first thing their portal should light up with.
  • Every assistant coach and team manager on a Premium team. Today none of them can register their own team.
  • Organizers get entries linked to the coach's real team, so roster submission and the coach's schedule work.
  • Doing nothing means more stray teams, more Premium portals that don't show their own tournaments, and support fixes by hand.

Priority

High, and partly now. Two small fixes (the right team name on the form, and Sign in returning to the page) can ship ahead of the redraw, and the affected coach's entry can be moved today. The redraw follows once the drawing is ruled.

Success criteria

  • A Premium coach who opens a registration link signed out registers their existing team without being sent back to the form.
  • No new free team is created for a coach who has a Premium team, unless they choose "A different team".
  • A team manager on a Premium team can register the team with no change from the head coach.
  • Every new entry by a Premium coach appears in their portal's Tournaments page the moment they submit.

Stage 1b — the head coach comes from the portal (ruled 2026-10-15)

What changes. A coach who picks their Premium team types no names. The team's head coach comes from the portal's staff list, and the review step shows it ("From your Coaches Portal") along with who the organizer's emails will go to: the head coach. When a team has two head coaches, the person registering picks one. A signed-in coach never sees name boxes: no real account lacks a name (a production count, 2026-10-15), and every sign-up asks for one.

Why it matters. Today whoever presses Submit becomes the team's listed coach: on the organizer's list, in their emails, and on the tournament's public Teams page. Since Stage 1, a team manager can register, so a manager gets listed as the coach. The owner found the unexplained name boxes on the 2026-10-15 walk and asked why a Premium team doesn't send its own details.

Customer impact. Premium teams register with fewer fields and the organizer sees the right head coach every time. Free teams and brand-new coaches are unchanged, except that a signed-in coach never sees name boxes.

Priority. Medium-high: small, and it follows straight on from Stage 1. All three owner calls ruled 2026-10-15. Built 2026-10-15 on dev, after /design, /simplify and /review; the owner's walk is §314, on the hub's QA walk 1b tab. As built (plan §13): a team manager's registration is listed under the head coach and the organizer's emails go to the head coach; the person who registered gets the confirmation, addressed to them; every coach email says "Hi Coach" when no name is on file.

Success criteria.

  • A Premium team registered by its team manager is listed with its head coach's name, everywhere the organizer and the public see it.
  • No one who picks a Premium team with a named head coach sees a name box.
  • No signed-in coach is asked for their name.
  • When a team manager registers, the organizer's emails reach the head coach, addressed to the head coach.

F6 — Sign in loses the form. The public bottom bar's Sign in sends the coach to sign in and then to their own portal, not back to the registration they were on.
