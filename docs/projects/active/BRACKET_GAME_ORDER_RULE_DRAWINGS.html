1 · A schedule that is already out of order, when it opens

This is the case that started it. Semifinal 2 moved to 3:00 p.m. and the Final stayed at 2:15 p.m., so the Final starts while Semifinal 2 is still playing. Today nothing on this screen says so. The health score counts field clashes only, and the problem shows up only inside the bracket editor. In the drawing, the pair is drawn the way a double-booked pair already is: both games get the red outline and wear a red Out of order chip in the chip’s one place . The chip’s words tell it apart from a field clash, so there is no new fill and no legend to learn. The health button counts it, its window names both games, and the Filter’s Conflicts group gains an Out of order row. Nothing is locked: the organizer fixes it by moving either game .

2 · Moving a game on the timeline

The semifinals are at 11:30 a.m. and end at 1:00 p.m. The Final is at 3:00 p.m. Today, the check runs only after the drop, it compares start times only, and it lives only in the browser. A Final dropped at 12:30 p.m. is saved, even though both semifinals are still playing. A drop at 11:00 a.m. lands, then snaps back with a message window. In the drawing, a refused spot turns red while the organizer is still dragging, the way a taken field already does, so nothing lands and nothing snaps back . “Before” means before the earlier game ends .

A drop that slips past the screen (a second admin moved a game a moment ago, or the screen is stale) is refused by the server. The game snaps back, and a notice that stays until it is closed says why (tab 6). It replaces today’s message window, which the One Window ruling retires.

6 · Every refusal, in words

One rule, the same sentence at every door. Each sentence names both games, gives the time that matters, and offers the way out. Times are written the house way (3:00 p.m.). The game names are the admin’s round names (Final, Semifinal 2, 3rd Place). A window with no field to put the line under puts it beside its button, as the One Window ruling has every window do. A refusal outside any window becomes a notice that stays until it is closed. Both are drawn below the table .

Beside the button, in a window with no field for it

Drawn · the division’s game length changed to 120 min the line beside Save

The window’s foot holds the sentence at its left and the buttons at its right, the slot every window has. Save fades until the change works. The same shape serves the playoff generator’s stale draft and putting a cancelled game back.

Nothing is told to families when a save is refused. The refusal comes before anything is written, so no change notice goes out. A Final whose teams are still “Semifinal 1 winner” has nobody to tell anyway.

7 · Asks

Settled, not asks (owner ruling 2026-10-29, and what the investigation found):
  • Block, not warn, and on the server, so a second admin or a stale screen is refused too. Every door in the plan’s table goes through the one check.
  • The refusal names both games and the time that matters, the same sentence at every door (tab 6).
  • Moving a Semifinal later is blocked. “Also move the Final” is a separate, separately priced convenience, not in this stage.
  • A game and the game it waits for can’t start at the same time. Every existing check already refuses that, and option a of A1 keeps it.
  • The playoff generator already drafts in order, so a fresh draft is never refused. Only a draft made before someone else changed the schedule can be.
  • Round-robin-only tournaments see no change.
  • Two defects the investigation found are fixed by building this: the rain delay comparing across divisions, and the game window comparing across tiers (tab 5).

The plan’s five open questions are A1 (equal times and minimum gap together), A2 (existing bad data), A4 (refusing as you drag), A7 (pool play) and the settled list above (moving a Semifinal, auto-scheduling, published schedules).
