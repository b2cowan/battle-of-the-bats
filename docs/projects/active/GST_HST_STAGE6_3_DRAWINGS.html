What I need you to rule

Ten asks, each with a recommendation; "as recommended" answers them all. Rulings R1 to R10 and the §20 words are not re-opened. Once you've ruled, Chunks B and C are built to these drawings.

Approved 2026-11-09

Owner: "looks good, i agree with your recommendations". All ten as recommended: 1A, 2A, 3A, 4A, 5A, 6A, 7A, 8A, 9A, 10A. Chunks B and C are built to this page.

Ask 1 · Where the box sits on a sign-up card

  • A (recommended) — after the last field, straight above the main button.
  • B — under the main button, with the small print.
  • C — at the top of the form.

Why A: you agree before you act, and the box is the last thing your eye crosses on the way to the button. Under the button it reads as small print, which is what courts dislike. At the top, it is agreed to before the person knows what they're signing up for.

Ask 2 · A sign-up card's button before the box is ticked

  • A (recommended) — the button stays on; pressing it without the tick shows the refusal line and sends nothing.
  • B — the button is off until the box is ticked, as on the sign-in screen.

Why A: a sign-up card has five things to complete, and an off button doesn't say which one is missing (the house rule fades a disabled button and explains nothing). The sign-in screen has one thing to do, so off-until-ticked is clear there, and §20 rules it. The family page already keeps its button off until its boxes are ticked, and stays as it is.

Ask 3 · The mark on the sign-in screen

  • A (recommended) — FieldLogicHQ's lockup, in the portal's own colours.
  • B — the club's or league's name and crest.
  • C — no mark.

Why A: the agreement is with FieldLogicHQ. A club crest above "We've published our Terms" says the club wrote them. Every sign-in page already shows our mark, so it's the one the person just saw. With the nav gone, C leaves nothing that says whose page it is.

Ask 4 · The purchase window's title

  • A (recommended) — "Before you continue" (/legal's 5d).
  • B — the screen's own "We've published our Terms" / "We've updated our Terms".
  • C — "Agree to our Terms to continue".

Why A: the person was in the middle of a purchase; the title names the pause, and the first line says why and promises to carry on. B reads as an announcement in the middle of a purchase. C is the box's job, said twice.

Ask 5 · Does the window open over the move window, or replace it?

  • A (recommended) — over it, with the move window kept underneath.
  • B — the move window turns into the agreement step, then turns back.

Why A: the same window stops paid buttons that aren't in a window (the billing table's Upgrade, the organization create, the Coaches Portal start), so one window that opens over whatever is there works everywhere. Cancel then truly leaves things as they were. B would need each window to learn a new step.

Ask 6 · Where the paid-button line sits

  • A (recommended) — read before the button: at the end of a window's body, above the button on the account column, and under the table where each row has its own button.
  • B — under the button everywhere.
  • C — in a window's foot, at the left.

Why A: "By continuing, you agree" should be read before continuing. A window's foot holds one short sentence by the house rule; this is three.

Ask 7 · Sign out on the sign-in screen

  • A (recommended) — in the foot row under Continue, with "Signed in as {email}".
  • B — a text button in the top right corner.

Why A: it is where the column's foot links always are, and the email shows which account is agreeing, which matters for someone with a coach account and a parent account.

Ask 8 · When the cancel door is built

  • A (recommended) — in Chunk B, with the "We've updated our Terms" screen it belongs to. It shows nowhere until the Terms have a significant change.
  • B — later, before the first significant change.

Why A: it isn't needed at first publication, but the day the screen can say "updated", it hides the billing page, and §18's "if you do not agree, you can cancel" can't be kept without it. Built with its screen, it can't be forgotten. (/legal §20 (5e), note 3.)

Ask 9 · The legal foot line on the sign-in screen

  • A (recommended) — leave it off this screen.
  • B — keep it, as on every sign-in door.

Why A: the screen's line and its box already link both documents. A third pair under Sign out is noise on a screen that exists to be read.

Ask 10 · The box's size on the follow nudge

  • A (recommended) — 13.6 px, the size of the nudge's inputs, in a 44 px row.
  • B — 12.5 px, the size of the nudge's sub line.

Why A: it is the sentence that makes the agreement, and it holds the age line a teenage player has to read. It should not be the smallest text on the sheet.

Build notes for Chunks B and C (the build prompt points here)

  • One tick row: a shared piece built on the house checkbox in the label row the family page and billing address use (18 px box, 9.6 px gap, 44 px row), the sentence at the host's body size, links underlined, opening in a new tab. The family page uses its own check row and its "Required".
  • Sign-up cards: the row after the last field, above the main button; the form checks the box before it sends, and shows (5b) in the one account step's error callout.
  • The screen: the one account step's column, centred on the portal's paper, FieldLogicHQ's lockup above the head, no nav. Its callouts are the one account step's error and amber notice. Sign out and the email in the foot row. No legal foot line.
  • The window: the house question window (416 px, no ×), opened over whatever is there; its refusal line in the foot's left slot; Continue off until ticked; on success it closes and the original request is sent again unchanged.
  • The paid line: one piece. In a window, the end of the body; on a column, above the button at the column's fine-print size, left-aligned; on the billing page, under the table after the tax line, in secondary ink.
  • Everything renders only while the switch is on. Nothing here changes what production shows today.

What the /design review changed (round 1)

  • The cancel door's link sits in the middle of a sentence, so on a phone it was a 21 px tap. It now has a 44 px tall hit area without moving the line (the build should do the same).
  • Pins that hung off the column's right edge were clipped at phone width; they now sit just inside it.
  • The billing figures were made up in the first draft; the plan names and list prices are now read from the plan settings, and only the prorated amount, dates and team counts are examples.
  • The Coaches Portal start frame now switches to 390 px with the Device switch, like the others.

Checked and kept: the sentence links are underlined (colour alone fails inside running text); the paid line is a shade darker than the tax line beside it; contrast of the 12 px small print is above 6:1 in Warm and Dark; every row with a box is 44 px tall; the follow nudge and family page stay warm in Dark because that is how they ship (the family page's own code comment says otherwise — a finding for the build, not this round).
