Six screens a person meets when something is wrong or missing

Each screen is drawn whole, as it is today and as I propose it, in warm and in Dark where the screen follows the setting, on a desktop window and a phone. The proposal gives them one family look: a small line-icon tile, a plain headline, one sentence, one obvious next step, and the name at the foot. It is the offline page's existing look, carried to the others. Nothing is built. The wording was ruled by /marketing on 2026-10-23.

Every coloured chip under a drawing opens an explanation when you click it. Red is what reads as a terminal or is wrong. Green is kept or fixed. Blue is new. Amber is held for a decision that is not this session's. Drawings are true size. A desktop window is drawn 600 wide because these screens are a centred column. Open the page on a phone to judge the phone drawings at real size.

My recommendation

The direction is right: one family, warm everywhere a signed-out person can land, Dark only for a signed-in person who chose it. The trouble is not the look. Four of these screens say things that are not true, and one cannot recover the way its button says. A warm coat of paint over those would be the wrong job, so the proposal fixes the words and the two behaviours first and the styling second. They are listed below, each opens its evidence, and each has an ask.

The install banner off the sign-up steps (U7)

The build: the install prompt is mounted by the consumer layout, which the sign-up steps sit under, so it can appear over a half-finished sign-up. The build stops mounting it on those steps. Home and the signed-in app keep it.

Check: open the sign-up steps on a phone that has never dismissed the prompt and see no banner. Open Home on the same phone and see it.

The screen-reader pass on the shared account form

The build: a pass over the shared account form (sign-in, sign-up, forgot and reset) for labels, the error announcement, the password eye's name and focus order. It changes announcements, not the look.

Check: with a screen reader on, sign in with a wrong password and hear the error read out without moving to it; tab through the form in reading order.
