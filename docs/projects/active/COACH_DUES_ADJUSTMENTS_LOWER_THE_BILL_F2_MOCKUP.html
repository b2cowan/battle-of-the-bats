The Schedule's Own Total

Three screens already read the dues bill the same way — $11,291.30, net of what's been written off it. The Budget Plan page still reads $11,308.30, in two places. Nothing puts the two figures side by side today, so nobody's seen them disagree — but a coach reading both could.

When the "a bill lowered is not a collection" ruling shipped, it netted the dues figure everywhere it could find it — the Dues tile, the dues table and its export, and the Player dues row on Budget vs. Actual. The Budget Plan page was left alone on purpose: its Player installments figure is summed straight from the dues schedules, not from what's owed today, and answers a genuinely different question — what did you schedule, not what will actually arrive.

That's a real distinction. It's also one a coach has no way to notice from the screen — the figure just reads $17.00 higher, with nothing beside it to say why. Below is today's screen as it stands, then two ways it could go.

Planned costs ($9,850.00) and Expected funding ($1,120.00) are illustrative — a plausible team's numbers to show how the buffer moves. The dues figures ($11,308.30 / $11,291.30 / $17.00) are the real, walked fixture from §160.

For netting

One number for "what will this season's dues actually bring in," everywhere a coach reads it. A team that writes off bills stops looking better-funded on this one screen than it actually is.

For leaving it gross

The schedules are a coach's own record of what they billed — a write-off doesn't un-happen it. A treasurer reconciling against a printed schedule wants the number that matches the schedule, not the outcome.

Whichever way this goes, it has to move in both places. The summary band and the by-period grid read the same underlying figure today, and §160 itself was reopened by review three times for exactly this mistake — a number re-pointed on one surface with its twin left reading the old way.
