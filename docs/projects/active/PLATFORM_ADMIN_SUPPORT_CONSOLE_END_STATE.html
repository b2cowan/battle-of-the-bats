SCREEN 1

The menu by job, and Today as the front door

The console takes the product's navigation standard: the top strip with the lockup, the portal's name and Find in the middle, and a menu grouped by the work (Customers, Health, Growth, Product, Records, then your private Bookkeeping, where it sits today). Question 1 · Who does support, and should the menu follow the task, the role, or both?

Recommended: both. The menu follows the job, and each role trims it. Keep the six roles as they are, fix the one rulebook so the menu and the screens agree, and decide old issue M3 (whether Billing keeps Support's powers; I would say no). If you are the whole support team for the next year, nothing here costs you anything. The roles stay out of your way.

What rides on it: the menu groups, the Read marker, and whether Stage 8 rewrites the roles or only aligns them.

Question 4 · Is any support job done from a phone?

Recommended: desktop only, and say so. Every task in the walk is done at a desk with two windows open. The one exception worth a thought is glancing at Status from a phone at a tournament. I would make Status and Today readable on a phone and leave everything else desktop. Everything here is drawn desktop only.

SCREEN 2

Find, from anywhere

Press / or click the box in the strip. One search across people (email, name), clubs (name, web name, owner) and Stripe numbers (customer, subscription, charge). People and clubs come back in one list, grouped. A person shows their clubs underneath, so an email leads to its club in one step. A result opens a record the role may read, and nothing else.

Before

Two separate searches. Customer Users matches email, name and club, but only within the first 1,000 accounts, without saying so. Organizations matches a club's name and web name only. Nothing matches a Stripe number or card digits.

SCREEN 3

The customer record reads first: its story

A club's record opens on what has happened to it, not on a form. The chips say its plan and state once. The band holds the figures every task asks first. Story is one dated list of everything: plan changes, free time, Stripe charges and refunds, notes, staff actions, emails sent. Each entry has who did it and why. Edits sit behind each tab's pencil, and a plan or grant change offers Put it back from its story entry.

SCREEN 4

The Money tab: "I was charged twice"

Stripe's own record beside ours, read live when the tab opens. Every charge, refund and subscription event, with what our side recorded in the next column. The innocent pairs that look like doubles get named: a plan change with its partial-month charge, a renewal on the same day as a change, two clubs on one card. A likely duplicate (the same amount, minutes apart, with nothing on our side) gets a flag line under its name, and the callout above the table says so once. Opening a charge reads it first. Its refund is a second, small window, and either way the story records it.

Before

Plan, status and period end from our side only. Open Stripe goes to the customer in another window. The refund happens there and nothing comes back.

Question 7 · A refund or credit for a double billing: done from the console, or a deliberate hand-off to Stripe with a recorded note?

Recommended: refund from the console, full or part, with the reason required. Credits stay in Stripe. A refund is the one money action that comes up at a desk with a customer waiting. Doing it here means the story records it every time, instead of only when someone remembers. The drawn windows do exactly this. If you would rather keep every money write in Stripe, "Refund this charge…" becomes "Refund in Stripe…", and the console asks for the reason before it opens the charge there.

What rides on it: whether Stage 3 writes to Stripe at all (a refund is a real money write, so it would get its own adversarial review), and what the Billing role may do.

SCREEN 5

Give free time: one idea of "free for a while"

One window, on every customer record and on Free time. You choose what (the whole plan, or one section), which plan, until when, and why. A note in the window says what will happen, including that it ends by itself. The grant shows as a chip on the record and an entry on the story. Today lists it 14 days before it ends, and the customer's billing page says "Free until November 30". Founding Season becomes a named group of these grants, so the October 1, 2027 end is a real end, not a date somebody must act on.

Before

Four controls that each sound like the answer. A plan change is permanent. An override ends by itself but covers one section, sits behind an unconfirmed switch, and applies with no confirmation. "Comp Period" grants nothing. Founding Season has an end date that ends nothing.

Question 5 · Which open lines land inside this project?

Recommended: timed grants in (Stage 4, extended to a whole plan, with Founding Season folded in), and the tier trial with it. Observability and feedback in as screens (Stage 5). "Shown vs enforced" in as a rule each stage checks. The Stripe price guard (H8) out, handed to /billing, since it is a check on the customer's checkout.

Question 2 · Editing live customer data: what needs a confirmation, a reason, an undo?

Recommended: every change to a live customer opens a window whose foot says what changes. Every change asks why, and the reason shows on the story. Put it back is offered for plan, limit, section and free-time changes, and never for anything that reached Stripe or an inbox. Notes and lead updates stay light: no window, and they save as you type.

SCREEN 6

Status: is everything working?

One page that reads everything it can reach when you open it, and says when it read. It covers production and dev answering, each branch's latest build, the production database's vital signs, the scheduled jobs' last run, and errors in the last 24 hours against the week. Below that, whatever is failing right now: a route opens its error, the error names the people who hit it, and each person is a link to their record.

Errors live here. Up or down, builds and the database live in the morning check on your PC, or in the Amplify, Supabase and AWS log consoles. That is 5 to 6 places.

Question 6 · What does "the status of our dev servers" mean, and should the console show the morning check's result?

Ask F1 · Retire the console's written exceptions to the house look

The dark look is held in place on purpose by three lines: exception K-10 ("the platform admin uses the data face"), the design log's line that operator shells stay dark, and the navigation standard's "platform admin keeps its own look". The layout also pins dark. Recommended: retire all three, so the console takes the same kit, navigation and setting as every client screen. Keep the sign-in page dark only if you want staff to see at a glance that they are entering the console. I would not.

Design review: five places where the written rules disagree with each other

The design pass checked every frame against the written rules and found 25 things. Twenty were plain fixes and are already in the drawings above: the window frame, the field and chip recipes, the band recipe, the nav rows, the type ladder, the toolbar shape, name links, empty cells and more. These five are places where two written rules disagree. Each drawing follows the design pass's recommendation, and each needs your ruling before Stage 1 builds anything.

Two smaller notes from the same pass: the standard's badge row still says chips are in capitals, which the built kit and the Oct 19 ruling no longer do, so it wants correcting. And a band of non-money figures has no ruled recipe yet. The drawings reuse the money band so the two can't drift apart.

Question 3 · After the foundation, which job goes first: the one done most, or the one that hurts most?

Recommended: S2 first (Find and the record), then S4 (free time), then S3 (money). Every one of your three tasks starts with finding the customer and reading their record, so S2 pays off in all of them. Free time is the one that can quietly cost money today: a comp that never ends. Money is the most work, and a double billing is rare before customers pay at volume.

Found on the way: report now, or leave for its stage?

Four things the walk found are worth fixing before their stage comes round. (1) The bulk plan change skips the steps the single change runs. Moving clubs to Club in bulk would not start their trials. (2) The timed-grant switch on production is unconfirmed. (3) The new Bookkeeping page threw an error 4 times on dev (that project's session is building it). (4) Two old platform data routes have no callers. Recommended: (1) as a small fix now, (2) as your one-minute check, (3) passed to the bookkeeping session, (4) left for S7.
