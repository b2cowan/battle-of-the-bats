A Swapping a price link loses subscribers

Billing recognizes a customer’s plan by the price link alone. Replace the link and the old subscribers are no longer recognized. The system answers “ok” and stops updating them. No alert.

B A billing page shows the wrong price

A customer’s Plan & billing page shows the catalog price, not what they pay. After any price change it is wrong for everyone already subscribed.

C “Approved” does not mean “done”

The Approval queue applies a change the moment it is approved, even by the person who asked. A stored effective date is ignored. Promotions are recorded and do nothing at checkout.

D No plan charges sales tax

Nothing in the product adds GST/HST to a subscription. If your adviser says it must, adding it is a price change for every customer, with the same notice.

E One sign-in can do everything

With no second approver, the only control is that only you can act. Staff sign-in has one step and nothing asks again before a money action.

F Stripe’s updates are trusted as they arrive

Plan billing does not record what Stripe sent, can apply an older update over a newer one, and does not check live against test. Family payments already do all three.

Moving customers who already pay to a new price

Drawn to your Oct 30 rulings and the round 2 recommendations. You set two dates. On the notification date new customers switch to the new price, and once they have, the notices go out by themselves. Current customers move at their first bill on or after the release date, with no proration: each finishes the period they paid for. Between the two, you schedule the moves for customers whose notice was delivered. Each customer gets a reminder before their own first bill at the new price. One rule runs through every step: a failure leaves the customer on the price they have now. Built last, because nobody pays today.

Your decisions

Asks 1 to 10 are in dependency order; 11 to 19 are new in round 2, and 16 and 18 come before the rest. Ruled Oct 31: you walked round 2 and kept every recommended option. Business decisions 1 to 3, 6, 11 and 16 are logged with the strategy agent. The 30-day reminder and the 25% warning stay as drawn until build. For notice, invoice and tax wording, check Canadian consumer and tax rules with the person who advises you; I assert none.

Ask 1 · tab 3 · strategy
Where do new-customer prices live?
Ruled Oct 30: amounts stay in the app’s code, the console refuses to publish unless Stripe and the app agree, and checkout closes itself while they differ. Price changes are rare and nobody pays yet. It does not let you change a new-customer price by date inside the app.
Ask 2 · tab 4 · strategy
What do current customers keep, and when does a change reach them?
Ruled Oct 30: they finish the period they paid for and move at their first bill on or after the release date you set. No proration. You also set a notification date. Round 2 changes what happens on each date (Asks 11 and 12), not this rule. Founding holders and anyone with a chosen future price are held out until you rule on them.
Ask 3 · tab 4 · strategy and your adviser
Notice: how long before, what it says, who gets it, what happens when delivery fails?
Recommend: one email per subscription to the billing owner from our own system; a customer whose email fails is held out of the move. You set the notification and release dates, so you own the notice length; your adviser confirms the wording and the marketing agent writes the words.
Ask 4 · tabs 2 and 7
The Approval queue becomes “Changes”?
Recommend yes. With one admin, approving your own request is a step you do to yourself, and the queue already applies a change the moment it is approved. A Change has a preview, a reason and a history. The queue stays for planning records and the feature matrix.
Ask 5 · tab 7
Roles
Recommend: no new roles. Per-customer money actions reuse the existing money power. One new Super-admin-only power covers moving many customers. Support may read Plans & pricing to explain a price. Whether Billing may stop a move is your call; it is left unticked.
Ask 6 · strategy
Promotions today record an intention and do nothing at checkout.
Recommend: relabel the screen as a record so it stops looking like a control; make real discounts only when a concrete offer is planned. A one-customer discount is a separate tool (tab 9).
Ask 7 · tab 3
Create the new price in Stripe from the console, or paste it in?
Recommend paste and verify now. With all five checks blocking, a wrong paste cannot publish. Creating prices from the console is one more live write; add it after the first real price change shows pasting is the pain.
Ask 8 · tab 5
Trials: add a tool to move one customer’s next charge?
Recommend yes, for Billing and Super admin, beside the existing free-time door, with the preview shown. The default-length setting stays a plain edit.
Ask 9 · tab 6 · your adviser
Invoice document: name, tax lines, who may see it.
Recommend: one document per payment plus a date-range statement, owner-only at first, each carrying Stripe’s reference and any later refund or credit. Name and tax lines wait for your adviser (Ask 16). Customers’ own invoice list is a separate session.
Ask 10 · tab 1 and 8
Stage 0 and the add-a-plan checklist before any workflow?
Recommend yes. The Stage 0 list on tab 1 is defects that exist today; everything else stands on them. Ask 18 grows the list.
Ask 11 · tabs 3 and 4 · strategy · round 2
New customers switch to the new price on the notification date, and the notices wait for it?
Recommend yes. In round 1 current customers began paying the new price at the release date while checkout could still sell the old one. Switching new customers first removes that, removes sign-ups nobody told, and leaves nothing to do on the release date. This reopens one line of the Oct 30 ruling: a customer who switches to annual after the notice buys at the new annual price.
Ask 12 · tab 4 · round 2
The daily job sends emails only; you schedule the price moves after the notices are delivered?
Recommend yes. A job that changes many customers’ bills while nobody watches is the riskiest automation in the product. You press one button with the delivered list in front of you. If you never press it, nobody pays more.
Ask 13 · tab 4 · round 2
A reminder before each customer’s own first bill at the new price?
Recommend yes, 30 days before (a placeholder; you set it on each change). An annual customer can hear more than a year ahead otherwise. Same safeguards as the notice.
Ask 14 · tabs 3 and 4 · round 2
Catching a wrong number with one admin
Recommend: the size of the change shown per customer and in total, a warning above 25% (placeholder), you type the new amount to confirm, an email with the first real charge, and refund-the-difference on Stop.
Ask 15 · tab 4 · round 2
A customer’s own plan change meets a scheduled price move
Recommend: it carries the move forward or refuses in plain words, never drops it silently, and the row says “Changed by the customer”. Designed together with the Plans end at renewal work now being built, since that work is what creates the collisions.
Ask 16 · tabs 1 and 2 · strategy and your adviser · round 2
Sales tax decided before the first price notice or branded invoice?
Recommend yes. No plan charges GST/HST today. If it must, adding it changes every customer’s bill and needs the same notice, and every amount must say whether tax is included.
Ask 17 · tab 7 · round 2
Sign in again before money actions that reach many customers?
Recommend yes: before switching a live price, scheduling a many-customer move and stopping one, with an email to you after each. With no second approver, this is what keeps an open session from being enough.
Ask 18 · tab 1 · round 2
Stage 0 grows from four fixes to twelve?
Recommend yes. Items 5 to 12 on tab 1: Stripe’s updates recorded and in order, the morning check, live or test decided one way, the app owns Stripe’s customer billing page settings, chargebacks seen, Coaches Portal checkout, sales tax, sign in again.
Ask 19 · tabs 9 and 10 · round 2
Which Stripe dashboard jobs move into our admin first?
Recommend: billing details, failed payment, payments made outside Stripe, credits and discounts (tab 9); and the Stripe page with updates, the records check, settings and failed club payouts, with a monthly summary and an accountant export (tab 10). Pay by invoice and pausing for the off-season are strategy questions for later.
