01

Change 01 · the clock

A row and its heading stop disagreeing

This is the defect in your screenshot, and it is real — not the fixture being stale. 1d ago counts elapsed hours. Earlier counts calendar days. A notification posted Friday at 6:21 p.m. reads “1d ago” for the whole of Sunday morning while filing under Earlier, because by the calendar it is two days back.

It runs the other way too: at half past midnight, something posted an hour ago sits under Yesterday reading “1h ago”. The two can be a full day apart in both directions. The fix is to stop asking them the same question — the heading owns the day, the row owns the time inside that day.

02

Change 02 · the buckets

“Earlier” stops being a three-month drawer

In the QA fixture, Earlier holds 39 of the 61 activity rows — Wednesday’s champions sit in the same bucket as a weekly digest from 10 July. A coach opening the feed on a Sunday has no heading for “the back half of this week”, which is exactly the period they are most likely to be catching up on.

One heading between Yesterday and Earlier splits that pile roughly in half. “Earlier this week” rather than “This week”, because Today and Yesterday are already out of it — and it stays on the calendar clock, so it does not reintroduce the disagreement plate 01 just removed.

03

Change 03 · the filters

The pills go — the list already says this

They can be made to fit on one row. I don’t think they should be, because the row is buying nothing: the pills name Needs attention and Activity, and the list labels both, a finger below. The 3 on the pill repeats the 3 in the heading directly underneath it. On a five-item list, they filter to something already on screen.

Two rows of pills plus the read toggle cost 164 px. The toggle alone costs 60. That 104 px is one full notification row — and it is also the room the zone’s own explanation needs, which is why that sentence is switched off on phones today. Desktop keeps the pills: there the row fits, the list is longer, and filtering earns its place.

04

Change 04 · the decision · three options

The zone answers “looked at”, not “dealt with”

This is the one that isn’t a drawing problem. Needs attention holds the events that want a decision from you — a failed payment, an assistant-coach invite, a team marked no-show. Activity is everything that just happened: registrations, payments received, scores, the weekly digest.

But a row leaves the zone the moment you tap it. Open a failed payment on the bus, fix nothing, and the zone quietly reports you are clear. The heading promises “have you dealt with this?”; the list is answering “have you looked at it?”. Three ways to close that gap.

Opening a row no longer removes it — only Clear does, and Clear is its own tap target on the row’s meta line, so it costs no width and the body text never squeezes. A cleared row still sits in the day feed as history; nothing is deleted.

Cost One button and one flag. Works for every event type, present and future — no per-event wiring.

Watch “Mark all read” and “Clear” become two verbs a coach has to hold apart. Mark all read already leaves these rows alone by your ruling, so the split exists today — this names it rather than creating it.

The thing underneath all three

All three of the rows in your screenshot are club-admin decisions — billing, tournament check-in, rep-team admin — and every one of them links to an admin screen a coach may not be able to open. That is the recipient-scoping gap already ticketed from the redraw, and it is doing more damage to this zone than the read-state rule is. Whichever option you pick, a coach’s Needs attention should hold things a coach can actually do. I’d fix that before, or with, option C — otherwise C just makes an unactionable list stickier.

Recommendation

Ship 01–03 together; 04 is your call

Changes 01, 02 and 03 are corrections — the clock is a defect, the bucket is a gap, and the pills are chrome the list already replaced. They are small, they don’t touch the database, and they can go in one pass with the §138 walk still open.

Change 04 changes what a word on the screen promises, so it wants your ruling rather than my judgment. C is what I’d build: it works for every event type, it needs nothing the platform can’t already see, and it puts the decision with the person who has it. B is better where it works and impossible where it doesn’t — it could layer on later for the failed payment specifically, which is the one row that genuinely knows whether it is still true.
