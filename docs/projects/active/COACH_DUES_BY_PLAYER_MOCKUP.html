Round 3 · I was wrong twice, and you caught both

The cap is deliberate, and it is right

Round 1 claimed $850 of dues belonged to no player. Round 2 called it overpayment the Dues tab was hiding, and recommended the Dues tab stop capping. Both were inferred from totals rather than read from the records. I am withdrawing the round-2 recommendation as written: the cap is not hiding anything.

When a family sends more than their bill, the excess is automatically turned into a credit — your ruling, 2026-08-13, no prompt. The Paid column stops at the bill precisely so that money is not counted twice, once as paid and again as a credit. Avery's $550 is not lost. It is sitting inside their $1,128.15 of “credits”.

So nothing is missing. What is wrong is the word. “Credits” is one number carrying four different meanings, and one of them is the family's own cash.

Two changes, and they only work as a pair. Credits comes to mean money from someone other than this family, so the overpayment leaves it. Paid can then show what the family actually sent, because the double-count the cap existed to prevent is gone.

The Balance does not move. $700.00 − $578.15 − $1,250.00 = ($1,128.15) — exactly what the row says today. Nothing a coach has already acted on changes value; the $550 just stops being filed under the wrong word. Either change on its own breaks it: uncapping Paid while the overpayment is still a credit counts it twice.

And it settles D6 for free. The report would show Avery's dues as $1,250.00; the Dues tab would now say $1,250.00. One family, one number, two screens.

Knock-on — and it fixes a flaw in round 2

Only other people's credits come off the plan

Round 2 netted all credits off the planned figure. Wrong, for the same reason: an overpayment credit is the family's own cash, already counted in what arrived, so netting it would reduce the plan by money that also sits in the actual column. Only external credits — raised, reimbursed or contributed by someone else — come off.
