# FieldLogicHQ: an identity worth making familiar

Independent second opinion and concept study · 23 October 2026

**My recommendation is to refine 01, Joined FL, with 03, Open F, as the alternate. Let olive lead the identity and keep lime for action.** I would not adopt Claude’s pennant, tag or clipboard as drawn. I would also not approve a final logo from these new drawings alone: the next decision is which direction deserves refinement and a small recognition test.

Open the [interactive mockup](PUBLIC_FACE_WARM_W_CODEX_DRAWINGS.html). Choose any of the five directions, then switch between website/app, home screen, clothing/print, and email/sharing. Every setting updates together. The size comparison shows all five simultaneously. No application files or installed icons were changed by this study.

## Scope and review plan

This is an identity exploration, not an implementation plan for shipping a new brand. The work is to:

1. Inspect today’s assets and Claude’s drawings before choosing a direction.
2. Evaluate the whole identity across recognition, warmth, scale and reproduction.
3. Draw five conceptually different directions in the existing palette.
4. Apply each to the same settings, including clothing and printed material.
5. Check the review document’s behaviour and geometry; distinguish those checks from testing actual users, devices and manufacturing.

**PM summary:** You get a visual decision tool, rather than a logo pasted into one attractive hero. A selection updates the same name, symbol and placements throughout. It is easy to see whether a mark still works as a tiny tab, a quiet export credit or a shirt emblem. This work changes no customer experience or role permissions. Its success criterion is a well-informed choice of one direction to refine, with a credible alternate and explicit reasons to reject the others.

## The problem is wider than choosing a nicer pictogram

The product needs one recognisable identity across several existing representations: a command prompt in the tab and operator strips, disconnected F/L initials on the phone, and variations of the name in interfaces and emails. The current `public/favicon.svg`, `public/brand/logo-B.svg`, shared `BrandLockup.tsx`, and export mark helper support the mismatch described in the supplied brief. The export helper points to the PWA image; a change to the app tile therefore reaches printed/exported material too.

The warmth decision is sensible. But “look more like sport” should not become “choose a generic sports object.” A club’s own crest already carries its sporting identity. FieldLogicHQ needs to feel like the reliable platform behind the club, while being distinctive enough to find on a phone.

A symbol is warranted because there are app tiles, tabs and notifications. A separate pictogram is optional: an initial or monogram can do that job. The full name should lead at first contact. Expecting an unfamiliar symbol to communicate “community sports administration” immediately is too much to ask of any tiny mark.

Likewise, a warm identity does not require a pale app tile. An olive or ink square can belong to the same warm brand. The important changes are the geometry, type, palette hierarchy and consistent use.

### Claude’s three options

| Direction | Useful quality | Why I would not choose it as drawn |
|---|---|---|
| A · HQ tag | Direct, easy to pair with text, little conceptual baggage | HQ is the least specific part of the name. A tag can read as a plan badge. The small icon spends its limited space on two secondary letters. |
| B · Pennant | Simple silhouette and an immediate sports association | A common flag is not yet a distinctive brand. It also has plausible Report, golf and pennant associations. The brief identifies an existing flag-shaped Report action. Those associations do not disappear because we explain the story. |
| C · Clipboard | Fits the organizer’s work | Reads as forms, paste or a checklist. Describes an admin task rather than building a recognisable identity. I would not make that the central emblem on a shirt. |

These are design judgments, not measured user responses. Claude did useful application work, but the range was narrow: largely small UI pictograms beside a wordmark. I would not treat that range as the limits of the brief. Nor would I infer trademark availability from how simple or pictorial a drawing is.

## Common system used to judge the new directions

The five concepts deliberately share a restrained typographic and colour system so the symbol ideas can be compared fairly.

- **Name:** FieldLogicHQ, preserving its spelling and mixed case. Inter 700 for FieldLogic, Inter 500 for HQ, at the same size and baseline. No mini HQ, separate tag or spaced capitals. Direction 03 replaces the initial F with its own drawing.
- **Warm lockup:** ink `#241E15` for the name, olive `#57651E` for the emblem and HQ. The two inks are a useful option, not a requirement for recognition.
- **Primary app study:** paper `#F8F4ED` symbol on olive `#57651E`. The alternative is paper on ink. The paper-tile study is olive on paper. These are colour treatments of the same directions, not additional concepts.
- **Signed-in Dark:** paper on `#0A0A0A`. Lime can remain an action accent without being necessary to identify the logo. Marketing and signed-out samples remain warm.
- **Marketing:** no lime in the mark. The existing lime button owns that accent. Headings remain Barlow Condensed 700 and prose remains Inter. Only Google Fonts are requested; no font files are added.
- **One colour:** the same paths in black or white, with transparent holes. No background-colour patches are needed to fake a knockout.
- **Email:** live Arial/Helvetica text, FieldLogic at 700 and HQ at regular weight, ink/olive. No logo image or web font is required. The custom F becomes an ordinary Arial F. A consistent readable name is preferable to an empty image box.
- **Clear space for refinement:** start with at least 8 grid units around a freestanding symbol. This is a proposal to test, not a new canonical brand rule.

I would keep Inter for this first comparison. Adding five new typefaces would obscure whether a person prefers a symbol or just its typography. Final kerning, weight and the outlined wordmark remain refinement work.

## The five directions

### 01 · Joined FL — first choice

**Idea:** two initials become one connected emblem.

An F sits on the left; its middle arm connects to the stem and foot of an L on the right. It is deliberately a single filled shape. It connects to the actual name and gives the platform a useful small stamp without borrowing a ball, flag or generic toolbar icon.

The most important reservation is that the L will not be obvious to everyone. I can also see a bench or an engineered form. Recognising two letters is not required to recognise a brand, but “everyone immediately reads FL” would be an unjustified claim. Its angular character needs a warmth check with volunteers, particularly given the aim of moving away from a technical identity.

**Geometry:** on a 64-unit grid, the main shape occupies x12–52 and y12–52. The main stem and top bar are 10 units thick. The L has a 10-unit stem and a 22-unit foot. The upper-left corner has a 4-unit curve. A separate 16 px version aligns the main edges to the 4-unit pixel grid, using an 8-unit left stem and an 8-unit bottom opening. It does not add detail.

**Wordmark and colours:** the shared Inter lockup and olive-led system above. The mark sits beside the full name in the bars and alone on the app tile, badge and chest placement. It keeps the useful FL connection from today while dropping stacked letters, unreadable HQ, the command prompt and blueprint decoration.

**Where it earns its place:** compact, connected silhouette; a clean one-colour chest emblem; a mark derived from the name rather than a category icon. That is why I rank it first for refinement, not a claim that it is already a finished or uniquely ownable logo.

### 02 · Huddle — third

**Idea:** separate parts gather around a shared centre.

Three broad curved arms surround an open centre. This direction puts cooperation first. There are no dots as heads, no ball, no team formation or field diagram. It is the most explicitly communal of the abstract options.

**Geometry:** a broad quarter-ring with outer radius 24 and inner radius 12, centred at (32,32), has a short square terminal extending to x26 at the top. Repeat the same piece at 0°, 120° and 240°. All holes and spaces are genuine transparency. Keep the same geometry at small sizes.

**Wordmark and colours:** the shared Inter lockup and olive-led system. Use it with the full name until it becomes familiar. A white badge loses no essential colour information. It keeps the compact symbol/name arrangement but drops both old symbols and the initials.

**Risk:** recycling, aperture, industrial and wellness associations are credible. A pleasant communal story does not make it recognisably FieldLogicHQ. It is an alternative if warmth outweighs the more direct name connection of 01 and 03.

### 03 · Open F — second choice

**Idea:** the wordmark’s first letter becomes the app mark.

The F has a broad rounded shoulder, generous arms and a simple upright. In the wordmark it replaces the initial F rather than appearing again as a detached emblem. This is a wordmark-led identity, with a natural compact form for phones and tabs.

**Geometry:** on the 64-unit grid, a 12-unit stem runs from x16 to x28, with the top shoulder curving from (16,24) to (28,12). The top arm ends at x52, the middle arm at x46; the baseline is y52. The top-right corner has a 3-unit curve. For the wordmark, crop to `viewBox="16 12 36 40"` and scale to the text’s cap height. This is a conceptual join; final optical spacing is still required.

**Wordmark and colours:** the custom F followed by Inter 700 “ieldLogic” and Inter 500 “HQ,” with the same colours. Email uses a normal Arial F. The freestanding custom F is the icon and silhouette. It keeps the initial from the name while dropping the L and a separate pictogram.

**Risk:** an F is a crowded category, with obvious technology, fitness and social-product neighbours. This direction needs particularly careful visual comparison before adoption. It is my alternate because it is simpler to learn and visually softer than Joined FL, but gives up the two-initial connection.

### 04 · In Order — fourth

**Idea:** a busy season falls into place.

Four unequal rounded blocks create an ordered but nonuniform arrangement. This expresses the “logic” side of the name without reproducing a calendar, checklist or a particular sport’s field.

**Geometry:** rectangles at (10,12), 20×16; (36,12), 18×16; (10,34), 12×18; and (28,34), 26×18. All use a 4-unit corner radius. The narrowest gap is 6 units, or 1.5 pixels at 16 px. The arrangement is intentionally not a four-equal-square window.

**Wordmark and colours:** the shared system. The four cells work in one colour and may extend into supporting composition, but they should not be animated or rearranged within the logo. It keeps orderly geometry and drops all old literal marks.

**Risk:** it can still be a dashboard, productivity or window icon. It is useful evidence of the modular direction, but weaker as a distinctive company emblem than the first two recommendations.

### 05 · Clubhouse — fifth

**Idea:** borrow the familiarity of a sporting emblem, while keeping it simple.

A capital F is cut out of a solid shield. There are no stars, laurels, balls, dates or country cues. It is an especially natural shape for clothing.

**Geometry:** a shield spans x10–54, beginning at y10, with 4-unit top corners. Its sides descend to y31, then curve toward a point at (32,56). The transparent F spans x22–44 and y20–46, with an 8-unit stem. It is one even-odd filled path rather than an F painted on top.

**Wordmark and colours:** the shared system, paired with the name in interfaces and alone for the emblem. Reverse the exact transparent knockout for one-colour use. It retains the initial and a home-base association, while dropping the L and technical framing.

**Risk:** a platform crest can compete with clubs’ own crests. A shield also suggests security or insurance. Those are significant positioning costs. I would use this direction to pressure-test the desire for a sporting badge, rather than make it the default.

## Behaviour in the required settings

All five are drawn in each setting, not just the leading candidate.

| Setting | Treatment and what to inspect |
|---|---|

| 192 and 512 px app sources | Font-free vector geometry scales to each source size. Full-square, opaque backgrounds; corners are display masks, not built into the asset. The mockup shows crop studies and a 58 px home-screen placement. It can save a real 512 px PNG study. |
| Maskable app icon | Separate geometry placement at 84% scale about the centre, with a visible safe-circle overlay and circle/rounded-square crop studies. Essential artwork stays inside the centre circle of radius 40% of the icon width, following the [W3C manifest mask guidance](https://www.w3.org/TR/appmanifest/#icon-masks). The 84% transform applies to the already inset symbol, not a claim that an 84%-width square is safe. |
| Notification | White glyph on transparency at 72 px, plus a 16 px status-bar simulation. No tile background. The large notification picture follows the app tile unless the organization supplies its own logo. Device behaviour still needs verification; Android’s [notification documentation](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/notifications/create-notification) describes the separate small icon. |
| Marketing bar/footer | Warm lockup only. No logo lime competing with the lime action. Existing bar arrangement is represented, not redesigned. |
| Account, admin and coach bars | Warm and signed-in Dark studies. Same mark, name and proportions across shells. Signed-out pages inherit the warm treatment. |
| Email with images blocked | Full readable name in Arial text for every direction. Both image-allowed and image-blocked specimens intentionally remain identical. These are design simulations, not actual email-client tests. |
| Share card | A 1200×630 composition, scaled to the available review width. The selected symbol and full name accompany the existing headline. A final export must outline the approved lettering. |
| Export/print credit | Black on white, full lockup at 11 CSS px. The organization’s name leads the sample schedule. Also shown white on a club colour. |
| Clothing and promotional print | A small chest emblem, a larger one-colour back print and a poster. Native vector placements, not a photograph or manufacturing proof. |

## Why this ranking

I prioritised a connection to the name, a stable small silhouette, reproduction without colour tricks, warmth, and a supporting role beside club branding. These are reasoned judgments rather than fabricated numerical scores.

1. **Joined FL:** best balance of a name connection and a usable emblem. Main unresolved issue: does its angular construction feel too engineered?
2. **Open F:** the simpler, softer alternate. Main unresolved issue: how readily does it get confused with another F brand?
3. **Huddle:** strongest communal expression, with greater interpretive and recognition costs.
4. **In Order:** a coherent product metaphor with more generic software associations.
5. **Clubhouse:** good on apparel, weaker for the platform’s relationship to clubs and its wider product positioning.

I would not pursue five directions equally from here. Put 01 and 03 in front of people without their concept names, ranking or rationale. The review page leads with my recommendation because it is an owner decision tool; it would bias a neutral test if used as-is.

## The owner decisions and the next design pass

The material questions are:

1. Without reading the stories, which of 01 and 03 feels like the company you want to build?
2. Does Joined FL feel dependable and clear, or too engineered for the audience?
3. Is olive becoming the signature colour the right move, with lime retained for action?
4. Would you wear the small symbol, or do you want the full company name on apparel?

Once a direction is chosen, refine just that direction and one alternate: balance, corner treatment, wordmark spacing and one-colour minimum-size studies. Do not reopen the entire marketing layout.

Then run a small qualitative check with approximately 6–10 coaches, volunteer administrators and parents, including people unfamiliar with the product. Show the plain marks first; ask what they think they belong to, whether anything feels confusing or familiar, and which one feels most appropriate after seeing the full name and context. Include a delayed recognition task among unrelated app tiles. This is a way to surface strong misunderstandings, not a statistically representative vote. Do not make successful deciphering of both initials a condition for liking 01.

Before widespread adoption, complete visual collision/trademark checks and produce an actual paper and stitched sample. This study has not established availability or manufacturing performance. That is especially relevant before a large apparel order.

After approval of final artwork, the existing warm-public-face project can implement one coordinated identity package: shared lockup, icons, notification badge, email text line, export mark and share card. None of that rollout is performed here.

## Verification record

The standalone document was verified independently of the Next.js application on 23 October 2026:

- Playwright exercised all five directions, all three colour treatments and all four contexts at 1440, 390 and 320 px viewport widths: **180 combinations**, no JavaScript exceptions and no page-level horizontal overflow. The comparison table intentionally has its own horizontal scrolling area on small screens.
- Both intended Google Fonts loaded successfully with network access. Desktop shortlist, phone/crop, clothing, size-table and mobile website specimens were visually inspected. A restricted-network run also confirmed that the fallback-font warning appears.
- All **35 raster canvases** painted. The Joined FL SVG, opaque 512×512 PNG and notes-text downloads were checked. The PNG’s dimensions and full opacity were confirmed from its pixels.
- All five maskable glyphs were rasterized at 512 px and checked pixel by pixel: no nontransparent pixel fell outside the safe circle of radius 204.8 px. This checks the geometry, not a real Android installation.
- Keyboard arrow/Home/End navigation across context tabs and notes persistence after reload passed.

No Next.js build, application test suite or product layout sweep was run: the deliverables are static review documents and do not alter application behaviour. The supplied original drawings, brand canon, product source and installed icon assets were left intact. The repository’s required task-tracking entry is recorded in TODO.md.

The remaining limits are user recognition, actual installed iOS/Android behaviour, email-client rendering, final outlined lettering, trademark availability and physical reproduction. Rendering the study successfully does not settle those questions.
