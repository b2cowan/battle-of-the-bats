# PM Brief — Board Ready

> Plan: `BOARD_READY_PLAN.md` · Created 2026-11-10 · Status: In flight (trued up 2026-11-16)

**What it does:** It answers the eight things a club board asked for after the 2026-11-10 demo:
- an honest security pack, backed by fixes to the real gaps;
- friend requests that feed the house-league draft;
- group email to coaches;
- a register of the club's volunteers with their documents (any kind the club defines) and expiry reminders;
- custom sign-up forms;
- club events;
- sponsorships the sponsor fills in and pays for online;
- camps and clinics.

**Why it matters:** A board decides whether a club adopts us. Today they run screenings, friend requests and
sponsorships in spreadsheets and email, and they hesitate over trusting a small vendor with children's data. Both are
reasons to say no.

**Who benefits:**
- Club admins and registrars get friend matching, forms and camps.
- Boards and screening officers get volunteer compliance.
- Treasurers and sponsorship leads get sponsorships.
- Coaches and volunteers get one email from the club instead of many.
- Families get friend requests and camp sign-ups.

Plan gates are a `/strategy` call.

**Expected impact:**
- The registrar stops matching friends in Excel.
- The club always knows who is screened and who is due.
- A sponsor goes from "yes" to paid through one link.
- Camps and the banquet appear on the club site.
- The board gets a security brief it can read and believe.

**Priority:** High. These are the stated conditions of a real club's board. The security pack and friend requests come first. Sponsorship
is the owner's marked priority among the new modules.

**Success criteria:**
- The prospect board accepts the security pack without a follow-up question we can't answer.
- A season's friend requests are matched without a spreadsheet.
- Every volunteer's documents, and which are due or expired, show on one screen.
- A sponsor pays through the link with no admin re-keying.
- A camp fills from the club site.

**The two data pages (planned 2026-11-18).** Two public pages join the Terms and the Privacy Policy: the Data Terms for
organizations (part of the Terms, changing only with a new Terms version, nothing new to tick) and the list of companies
that handle a club's information (a living page with a dated change table; a new company is announced to every club's
owners by email 30 days ahead). They go live with the Payments Terms; if the page work isn't ready that day, the Payments
Terms go ahead and the data pages follow with the next Terms version. Success: a board member finds both from the footer,
and the list always matches the privacy policy (the build checks it).

**Club events, then camps and clinics (drawn and ruled 2026-11-24).** One drawing page covers both, and the owner ruled all
19 asks as recommended. A club gets an Events place in Programs for banquets, champs days, AGMs, clinics and camps; an
event can take open field time from a permit, shows on the club calendar as "Club events", and an event for everyone
gets its own page and a "Coming up" row on the club site. A free event can take sign-ups with no account. A camp adds
sessions with their own spots and prices, birth-year limits, a waitlist that holds a spot for a few days, payment as house
league does it, a phone roster for an instructor that shows a health answer only when the club allows it and only around
the session, a Camps and clinics heading in the books, and a "Receipt for camp fees" a family can use at tax time. Why it
matters: these are the club's own days and its second paid program, and today they live in spreadsheets and separate
sign-up tools. Success: a registrar runs a camp from sign-up to receipt without leaving the club's space, and an
instructor sees only their own children and only what the club chose to share. Nothing is built yet.

**Club events, the first part to build (prompt written 2026-11-27).** The Events place in the club admin: the list of the
club's own events and the one window that makes or changes an event, open to the owner, admins, the registrar and anyone
the club gives it to, on League Plus and both Club plans. Nothing public, no calendar, no sign-ups and no money yet. Its
first sitting draws what the earlier drawings leave open, such as an event's own page and cancelling an event, for the
owner to rule; the second builds. Success: a club keeps its banquet, AGM and meetings in one list.

**Team sign-ups for a club event (drawn and ruled 2026-11-25, all 16 asks as recommended).** An event can be signed up
for by each family, by each team, or both. A club offers an event, such as the year-end banquet, to some or all of its own
teams. The head coach lists who is coming (players from the roster, family contacts, staff, anyone else by name) and
sends it, which puts one Bill for event tickets on the team's Club tab, with the event's tax, paid the way team bills are
paid today. A club can also invite another club's team to a social event by its own link, with the event kept off the
website ("Only people with the link"). The invited team lists adults by full name and children by first name and last
initial, and pays once. Its names are deleted 60 days after the event. Outside teams coming to *play* get a tournament
with a new "Who can enter" choice, handed to the tournament side. Why it matters: a banquet with 26 people from one team
becomes one list and one bill instead of 26 sign-ups and a spreadsheet. Success: a coach sends the team's list in one
sitting and the club sees every team's answer, bill and payment on the event's Who's coming. It is built after paid events
(below). Nothing is built.

**Paid club events (drawn and ruled 2026-11-25, all 14 asks as recommended).** Any club event can have a price, one price
or one per kind of person (Adult $40.00, Child $15.00), and a camp is simply an event with a price, sessions and ages, so
there is one way to pay. Sign-up is first come: a club that takes cards is paid in full at sign-up; until then the
family's spots are held for a few days while it pays the club its usual way. A "Check your sign-up" step shows every line,
the total and the club's refund policy before paying. A full event says "Sold out" (no waitlist). After paying, a family
can ask to withdraw and the club decides; if the club cancels, one window refunds everyone. Prices include GST/HST,
dinners start taxable, the club's books get a Club events heading, and the family gets a "Receipt for event tickets".
Meal choices and food allergies are separate questions, and allergies reach only the caterer's logged list. Why it
matters: a banquet or hosted dinner no longer has to pretend to be a camp, and the club stops chasing e-transfers in a
spreadsheet. Success: a club sells its banquet, sees who has paid, and closes its books with the tickets and the tax
already split. It is built as Stage 5's fourth part, before camps. Nothing is built.
