# PM brief — Notifications that read at a glance

**What changes.** When someone opens a notification that carries several facts — a returned bank debit, a
disputed card payment, a bill from the club, a moved game, the hours the club gave house league — they see the
facts as labelled rows (Amount · Date · Team · What to do) instead of one long sentence. Lists that are crammed
into a sentence today (moved games, family answers, several children on one refund) become real lists. In the
notification list itself, each of these shows one short line.

**Why it matters.** Most of these are "needs attention" money notices for club admins and owners. The reader
opens them to find one thing — how much, by when, for whom — and today has to read the whole sentence to find
it. A notice you can scan is one you act on correctly the first time.

**Scope.** 28 of the 81 notification kinds: 6 that pack a list into a sentence and 22 that carry three or more
separate facts. The other 53 are one short fact, text a person typed, or never sent, and stay as they are. Along
the way: each notice gets one wording wherever it is sent from, and "Score submitted" says which game and score.

**Who sees it.** Club owners and admins (most of the money notices), league admins, and coaches (moved games,
family answers, practice plans, weekly insights). Families are not affected.

**Priority.** After the drawing is approved: the shared pattern and the money notices first (highest value,
most of the 28), then the lists, then the coach and league notices.

**Success looks like.** Opening any of the 28, the reader finds the amount, the date or the next step without
reading a sentence; the notification list stays one line per notice; no notice says the same thing two ways.
