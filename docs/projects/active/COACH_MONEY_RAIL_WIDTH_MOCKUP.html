Everything in Money, drawn one row per screen width

The rail’s rows are the right height for a thumb and the wrong shape for a 1200-pixel column. Same seven surfaces, same seven numbers, same order — laid out for the screen they’re actually being read on.

What this review does and does not touch

This morning’s ruling settled what the Overview contains: one shape all season, the four-step card stack gone, the rail as the single index at every stage. None of that reopens here. Row wording, row order, the four step markers, which surfaces appear, the tiles, the anchor card and the “money first, alert second” rule are all Unchanged.

On the table is one thing only: the rail’s layout when it has a wide card to sit in. Phones, tablets and the live operating dashboard’s narrow slot are all untouched, and it is the same component in every one of those places.

Typical laptop fold — 1440 × 900

Two of the four steps need a scroll, on a screen with two-thirds of its width empty

Three symptoms, one cause

1. The name and its number are ~930px apart. A row is one fact split across the full column, with a hairline as the only thing keeping your eye on the line. Nearness is what makes a row read as a single thing, and at this width there is none left.

2. Every row is floored at 44px tall — a thumb target — carrying 13-pixel text. On a phone the two ends of that row are 40px apart and this is exactly right. On a mouse-driven screen it is mostly air, multiplied by seven.

3. So the card runs ~465px tall and the last two steps drop below a normal laptop fold — while roughly 800px of horizontal space carries nothing at all. You are scrolling down a screen that has room to spare across.

Distances and heights here are computed from the shipped stylesheet (column width, row grid, row floor, step-marker leading), not eyeballed from a screenshot. The fold line is indicative — exact position depends on browser chrome, and gets confirmed in the rendered sweep.

Amended during the build: the rows keep their full height

The version you approved also tightened rows from 44px to 34px on mouse and trackpad. That is not built, for two reasons that only showed up on the way in.

A fine pointer does not mean no finger. A Windows touchscreen laptop reports a mouse as its primary pointer, so it would have been served 34-pixel rows to a hand — a real device class, quietly regressed, and no screen rule can separate it out.

And it was buying the last 15%. Two columns alone take the rail from ~465px to ~278px; the tighter rows would have added about 40px more. The “mostly air” problem was mostly a width problem — a 44px row inside a 567px column is an ordinary list row, not the same object it was at 1166px. It would also have added seven new sub-floor entries to the tap-target gate on a screen about to be QA’d. Easy to revisit later if the density is ever wanted.

Two things worth knowing before you say yes

The group hairlines have to go at desktop width. In two columns, “first group” stops meaning “top of the list”, so a rule above a column reads as a dangling line. The step marker plus the space around it is what actually communicates the grouping — the hairline was never doing that job. It stays on phones, where the list is still one column.

The step markers currently look like the card’s own label. “PLAN” and “EVERYTHING IN MONEY” are the same face, the same casing and the same grey, four hundredths of a rem apart — so they read as siblings rather than label-and-contents. Today the hairlines paper over it; once those go, it shows. The fix drawn above darkens the step markers one step so the structure of the content outranks the name of the card. If you’d rather leave that alone, say so and it stays as-is — it is the one piece here that is taste rather than geometry.

Considered

Just cap the width

Not recommended

The one-line version: hold the rows to a readable measure and left-align them. It does fix the pairing. It also leaves a visible hole in the right half of the card, and it saves no height at all — the scroll stays exactly where it is.

What else this touches

  1. A live operating season is NOT affected — correcting what I told you

    I said this would also change the operating dashboard’s rail. It does not, and the reason matters: on a season with money moving, that rail sits in the narrow right-hand slot beside the Next-30-days ledger — about 362px wide, where the rows are already the right shape and two columns would mean two 175px columns. It stays exactly as you QA’d it.

    So the trigger is the card’s own width, not the window’s. The same rail is wide on the setup Overview and narrow on the operating one, on the very same screen; only the wide one folds into two columns, and it works that out from the space it has been given rather than from a screen size.

  2. An archived season’s rail does pick it up

    A finished season drops the forward-looking ledger — it is a record, not an instrument — so its rail takes the full page column alone and has exactly the problem this fixes. Five rows, no step markers, three down the left and two down the right.

  3. Every other Money screen is untouched

    Budget plan, Player dues, Fundraisers, Expenses, Allocations, Payments and Budget vs. Actual are all their own tabs with their own tables. Nothing here reaches them.

  4. The in-app Money help needs no edit

    The help text describes what the Overview contains, not how many columns it is drawn in. Nothing to correct.
