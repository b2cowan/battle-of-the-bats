A sponsorship that names its payments

A parent and a coach agree that a sponsor's money should cover these payments — the December and February ones, say. Today the product has one team-wide answer to where any credit lands, and no family can be the exception. The proposal stores the agreement, not the dollars: the coach names the payments on that family's share of the sponsorship, the team default stays exactly as it is for everyone else, and the money still works itself out every time anyone looks — so a payment that arrives in cash, or a schedule that changes, can never leave a stale figure behind.

F01

One dial, every family

The only way a sponsor's money meets a family's bills is the team setting — last payment first, next payment first, or held to season's end. It is the right default and it should stay the default. What is missing is the exception: a parent and a coach agree something specific for one family, and the product has no place to write it down.

There is no honest workaround. Recording the sponsor's cheque as a payment on the December bill would put it in the right month but call a sponsor's money the parent's cash — which changes what the season reports as collected. Switching the whole team to "next payment first" moves every family for one.

F02

The drawer cannot explain a landing

The Note cell says Covered by fundraising — Riverdale Dental on whichever bill the direction reached. That is true, and it is not the sentence the parent needs: they asked for December, they see April, and nothing says why. Once an arrangement can exist, the drawer has to be able to say as arranged — and, when the money moved anyway, why it did.

D3

Cash still claims a bill before a credit does

This is the rule that makes the whole money model self-correcting today, and the arrangement lives inside it rather than over it. If Maya's parent pays the December bill in cash anyway, the sponsorship's $250.00 has no December to land on. Proposed: it goes to the next named payment, and anything still left follows the team default — so the family is never stranded, and the drawer says what happened (screen 5). The alternative is to hold it as money owed back to the family, which is stricter about the agreement and worse for the parent. Owner's call.

D5

On a "settle at season's end" team, an arrangement lands anyway

That setting keeps money-backed credits off bills until the season closes. An arrangement is an explicit, per-family decision to do otherwise — that is the point of making one — so it lands on its named payments. Whatever it cannot place follows the team default, which on such a team means it waits.

D4

Named by position, warned on change

An arrangement remembers which payments by their position in the schedule (#2, #3) and the date and amount each had when it was made. Nudging every due date a week, or changing an amount, keeps the arrangement on the same payments. Adding or removing a payment shifts positions — and the product cannot tell a shift that matters from one that does not. Proposed: keep applying by position, and show this cue on the sponsor's row and the family's drawer until the coach presses Keep these or changes it. A position that no longer exists (four payments became three, #4 was named) follows the team default. The alternative — drop to the team default the moment the schedule changes, until the coach confirms — is quieter for the coach and louder for the parent, whose December suddenly reads $250.00 again. Owner's call.

What does not change, and why that is the point

Every figure a family is asked to send — the reminder email, the statement, the export, the season-end settlement, the Money Overview — is read from one computed position per family. The arrangement is a rule fed into that computation, so every one of those surfaces is right without being touched. The only screens that change are the three drawn here: the pledge form, the sponsor's room, and this drawer.

D7

Built generic, opened on sponsorships only

The same "special arrangement" could be asked of a bottle-drive share or a written-off adjustment. The mechanism is built so any credit could carry one, but the door is drawn only on the sponsorship's credit families — where the parent-and-coach conversation actually happens. Nothing appears on the drive leaderboard or the Adjustment form until someone asks for it.

D8

The rule keeps its reason and changes one word

The money model's standing rule reads: where a credit lands is derived, never stored. Its reason — a stored landing goes stale the moment the schedule, a payment or a cheque changes — is fully honoured here: the dollars are still computed on every read. What is stored is the agreement. The rule's wording tightens to "no computed landing is stored; an arrangement may be", and that is an owner ruling to make explicitly, not a comment to edit in passing.

A sponsorship that names the family's payments it covers. Ruled 2026-09-21 (D1–D8 as drawn); built on dev the same day; owner QA walk owed.

What changes for the coach

When a coach credits a family on a sponsorship — logging the pledge, or later in the sponsor's room — each family's share carries one quiet line: Applies to the team default, with a Change door. Opening it shows that family's own dues payments as checkboxes; the coach ticks the ones the parent asked for and the line reads them back as dates. Nothing else on the form moves. A coach who never opens the door sees a form one line taller than today and otherwise identical.

In the family's drawer on Player Dues, the sponsorship lands on the named payments and the Note says as arranged. If the parent pays a named payment in cash anyway, the sponsor's money fills the next named payment and anything left follows the team default — and the drawer says so in a sentence, so an arrangement never looks ignored. If the coach re-runs the dues schedule after an arrangement was made, the sponsor's row and the drawer both ask for a look until the coach confirms or changes it.

Why it matters

Parents make arrangements with coaches — "put the sponsorship on the December and February payments, we're tight then." Today the product has one team-wide answer to where any credit lands and no family can be the exception. The honest workarounds do not exist: recording the sponsor's cheque as a payment misreports a sponsor's money as the parent's cash; changing the team setting moves every family for one. The coach either says no, or keeps the arrangement in their head and hopes the reminder emails do not contradict it.

Customer impact

Head coaches and treasurers on any team that credits sponsorships to families. Most sponsorships will never open the door — the team default is right for most families — so the cost to everyone else is one line of text. For the family that did make an arrangement, every reminder, statement and export agrees with what the coach promised, because they all read the same computed position.

Role-based access

Anyone who can write money on the team can make or change an arrangement (the same rule as the split itself). Read-only staff see the arrangement stated on the sponsor's row and in the drawer, without Change.

Tradeoffs

  • The pledge form learns a choice about landing. The owner retired landing arithmetic from this form on Aug 31. The proposal keeps that: no dollars, no bills, only the choice, and only when opened. The default path is unchanged to the eye.
  • The money model's rule changes one word. "Where a credit lands is derived, never stored" becomes "no computed landing is stored; an arrangement may be." Same reason, honoured the same way — the dollars are still computed on every read.
  • Two judgment calls are the owner's (D3, D4): what happens to the sponsor's money when a named payment is paid in cash first, and what happens when the schedule is re-run under an arrangement. Each has a recommended answer and a stricter alternative drawn beside it.

Priority

Medium. It is a real parent-facing gap with no workaround, but it affects the minority of sponsorships. It sits naturally after the current fundraising QA walks close and before the P4 money-book shelf, because it touches the same pledge and sponsor-room surfaces those walks cover.

Success criteria

  • A coach can name two payments on a family's sponsorship share in under a minute, from either door, and the family's drawer shows the money on those payments with "as arranged".
  • A cash payment on a named installment never strands the sponsor's money, and the drawer states where it went.
  • The reminder a family receives for a named month asks for the lowered figure — with no change to the reminder code.
  • A coach who never opens the door cannot tell the pledge form changed, beyond one line.

How to test it (once built)

  1. On a UAT team with a four-payment dues schedule, log a pledge crediting one family 50%, open Change on that family and tick payments #2 and #3. Save.
  2. Record the sponsor's cheque. Open that family in Player Dues: #2 covered, #3 part-covered, both Notes say as arranged; the credit row names the two dates.
  3. Record a cash payment on #2. Reopen the drawer: #3 now fully covered, the remainder on #4 with "followed the team default", the credit row's sentence explains it.
  4. Re-run the dues schedule with one more payment. The sponsor's row shows the "re-run after this was arranged" cue; Keep these clears it.
  5. Credit a family with no dues schedule: the line points at Player Dues and offers no Change.
Full assessment · 2026-09-21

Sponsorship Applies To — implementation plan

Source of record: docs/projects/active/COACH_SPONSORSHIP_APPLIES_TO_PLAN.md. This tab is the readable copy; the file carries the technical detail and the D-ledger.

Problem / context

Where a family's credits meet their bills is decided by one team-level setting (rep_program_years.credit_application: last payment first · next payment first · settle at season's end) and computed on every read by the pure credit engine (lib/dues-credits.ts · applyCreditsToBills). Nothing per family, per sponsorship or per credit exists. A sponsorship reaches a family as credit rows (one per cheque, sized by the family's agreed share in rep_fundraiser_credit_plan), and those rows are pooled with every other credit the family has and walked over the schedule in the team's direction.

The engine's header rule — "credit application is derived, never stored; no installment id ever lands on a credit row" — is load-bearing and stays. Its reason (a stored allocation goes stale when the schedule, a payment or a credit changes) is not violated by storing an arrangement, provided the dollars remain derived and the arrangement is keyed by something a schedule re-run does not recycle. Installment ids are recycled — replaceRepDuesInstallments deletes and reinserts — so an arrangement names positions (installment_number), which are stable through a same-count re-run.

F01 — One dial, every family

The team setting is the only decision point for credit landing. No per-family exception exists and no honest workaround does: a payment misreports the money's source (and changes dues-actual), and the team setting moves everyone.

F02 — The drawer cannot explain a landing

The Note cell prints "Covered by fundraising — {sponsor}" on whichever bill the direction reached. With arrangements possible, the drawer needs the clauses "as arranged" and "followed the team default", and the credit row needs to name its payments.

Scope

  • A per-(sponsor × family) arrangement: named payment positions, stored on the credit-plan share and carried onto each accrued credit row.
  • The engine: a "named payments first" pass, before the team-direction walk, after forgiveness. Leftover of an arranged credit joins the ordinary run.
  • Three surfaces: the pledge form's and the sponsor room's shared share editor (the line + the picker), and the Player Dues drawer (Note clauses, credit-row sentence, the re-run cue).
  • Help: the fundraising answer and the dues "how credits meet bills" answer gain a paragraph; search terms gain "arranged", "applies to", "which payment".
  • UAT: one lifecycle scenario covering arranged → cash-first → re-run.
Out of scope

Exposing the door on drive entries or the Adjustment form (D7). Undoing an arrangement's history. Any change to the team setting or its three modes. A per-family direction override (Option A in the investigation) — subsumed by naming payments.

Approach

Store the agreement, derive the dollars. The credit-plan share (rep_fundraiser_credit_plan) gains applies_to — an ordered set of installment positions with the due date and amount each had when arranged — and arranged_at. When arrivals accrue credits (lib/sponsor-arrivals.ts → rep_dues_credits), each credit row copies the share's applies_to, so every reader that already fetches credit rows has the arrangement in hand with no join. Editing the plan re-derives every arrival's credits from scratch (existing behaviour), so the copy cannot drift from the share.

Engine change (applyCreditsToBills): after the forgiveness pass and payout skimming, run arranged credits over their named positions only (team direction among them); then run everything remaining — unarranged credits plus arranged leftovers — by age over the remainders in the team direction. Named positions that do not exist in the current coverage are skipped. On keep_separate, an arranged credit is spendable on its named positions (D5); its leftover is not. Per-installment sources gain arranged: boolean and fallback: boolean so the Note can say which clause. Pinned by tests: the identity (issued = applied + paid out + owed back) holds; cash-first holds; forgiveness-first holds; a stale position degrades to the default.

Re-run cue: the dues schedule's last-replaced timestamp compared to arranged_at. "Keep these" restamps arranged_at (and re-snapshots date/amount per position). Shown on the sponsor's share row and on the family's credit row.

Beat: a per-family direction override (Option A) — cheaper, but cannot express "these payments", which is the question parents actually ask.
