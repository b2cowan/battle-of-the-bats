Sponsorships, Part 3b: the card

Part 3b lets a business pay its sponsorship by card, on its own page, and lets the club refund that card payment from the deal. A card payment writes itself into the deal and the club’s books. A refund goes back to the business’s card, comes with a credit note, and a refund of everything ends the deal.

Seven walks, 47 steps, one purpose: the card and its refunds. Nine owner calls sit at the foot, outside the count. Walk F is optional.

The two kinds of window

  • Owner window: your ordinary browser window, signed in as the club owner. It is the club’s side: Deals, the deal page, Accounting.
  • Private window: a private or incognito window, signed out. It is the business’s side. Keep it open for the whole walk and reload it as the steps say.
  • Steps say which window to use. “Reload” means reload that window’s page.

Before you start

These are owed to the building session, not to you. If one of them is not true when you start, stop and tell me; nothing in this list is a box.

  • The dev server is restarted on the finished code (new files and a new database table came with Part 3b), with the online-payments switch on and a Stripe test key, as in the earlier card walks.
  • One Stripe listen window is running (the card-payment messages from the club’s Stripe test account). First sign the Stripe tool in, once, in PowerShell: 
    C:\tools\stripe\stripe.exe login
    It opens the browser. Click Allow access on the test-mode account. (If the next command says “Permission denied” or 403, this sign-in is missing or has expired — run it again.) Then start the listen window: 
    C:\tools\stripe\stripe.exe listen --events-from '@accounts' --events "checkout.session.completed,checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded,checkout.session.async_payment_failed,checkout.session.expired,charge.dispute.created,charge.dispute.updated,charge.dispute.closed,charge.dispute.funds_withdrawn,charge.dispute.funds_reinstated,refund.created,refund.updated,refund.failed" --forward-to localhost:3000/api/payments/connect-webhook
    Each line it prints should end [200]. The signing secret it shows must be the one in .env.local.
  • Dev Club Org is already connected to a Stripe test account with a refund policy written on its Payouts card. That is how the card button is switched on for a club: payouts connected, a refund policy set, and the online-payments switch on. The Payouts card is read in step 1.
  • Dev Club Org’s Sponsorships are set up through the product’s own saves: mailing address 250 Lakeshore Road East, Oakville ON L6J 1H8; contact for sponsors Jordan Walker, sponsors@dev-club-org.local; e-transfer to pay@dev-club-org.local; cheques payable to Dev Club Org; due 30 days; registered for GST/HST with the number the paper prints as 81234 5678 RT0001; one package Walk card partner, $1,000.00, shown on the home page.
  • The tax in this walk turns HST on only to test the maths (13% for an Ontario address). It is not advice that a club should charge it.
  • Where the test money shows: the Ledger, http://localhost:3000/dev-club-org/admin/accounting/ledger, the club’s General book.

Left out of this walk

  • The invoice, recording and voiding a cheque, and the books for a cheque: walk §562 (Part 3a). Offering a deal by email: Part 2.
  • Switching the card off (the button and the refund route disappear) and the exact money in the books through a refund: proved on dev by a script, not walked by hand.
  • A business that is sitting on the card page when the club withdraws the deal, a refund made in the club’s own Stripe dashboard, and a refund that comes back from the card’s bank.
  • The club’s public home page: the deal has no logo, so there is no strip to see come down.
