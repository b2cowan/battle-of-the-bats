# One Board — PM brief

**What changes for the owner.** One page answers "what are we working on, in what order, and what is waiting on me?"
It is the morning brief he already has, grown into a board. At the top: the goals (Canadian launch, the board pack,
ongoing quality, then the US launch) with how many of their projects are done; the three projects being worked on now,
each with its next step and anything it waits on; and every step only he can take, in one list. Below, what comes next
and what waits until after launch.

**Why it matters.** Status had been typed in about eight places, so finishing something in one list left it open in
another, and a plan could say "nothing built" about work already walked. The board is the only place a project's status
is written; everything else links to it. Because it lives with the code, the work and its status change together.

**Customer impact.** None directly. Indirectly: the Canadian launch sequence is visible and its dependencies are named,
so the long-lead items (payments terms, live payouts, the Stripe clean-up) start early instead of surfacing last.

**Priority.** High — it orders everything else.

**Success criteria.**
- The owner can tell from the page alone which three projects are Now and why, and every step waiting on him.
- No project's status disagrees between the board and any other tracker (others only link).
- TODO.md shrinks to one line per project (Session 2).
- After a few weeks of use, a build check keeps a plan change and its board line together (only on the owner's word).
