    Running practices, end to end

    A coach who has never opened the product wants to run Tuesday’s practice. This is every screen between that wish and the field, read as built on 14 September 2026 — what each one shows, how it feels to use, and where I would change its shape. The plan maker gets the hardest look, because you asked for it and because it is where the evidence points. The walk is the record; the tabs above hold one proposal per stage, added as each stage is ruled.

    How to read this

    • Eleven stations, in the order a first-time coach meets them. Each one: what is on the screen as built, my assessment (what works, what gets in the way, what I’d change), and one or two shape questions with Keep · Reshape · Not yet. A notes box under each, and a paste-back summary at the end.
    • Then Standing back: five questions that cut across every station — the form-versus-document question, time as the spine, the three doors, drag, and vocabulary. Rule those and the stages fall out.
    • The 0 · Arrive tab is the first stage drawn as before/after, at true size, with the decisions under it. Later stages get their own tab once the one before is ruled. Two ladders, two numberings: the arc counts the eleven screens walked; the tabs count the stages built — each tab names the stations it covers (Arrive took 0, 2 and 6; The blank page takes 1 and 3).

    Arrive — the first screen, and where practices live

    Before any of the work: a coach signs in for the first time. Their next event is a practice. What on the first screen tells them a practice can be planned here, and where do they go to do it?

    Overview …/teams/{id} · the sidebar · then Practice plans …/practice on a team with no practices (read from the code). On the phone, Practice plans is under More.
    On the screen, as built

    Overview, fixture: a “Next event” card — “IN 6 DAYS · Sun, Sep 20, 3:31 p.m. · Practice review — next week · UAT Fields” — with the page’s one lime button, Take attendance →, and a quiet “Open schedule”. Six tiles: Dues, Budget, Record, Roster, This week (“1 practice · in the next 7 days”), Tournaments. The sidebar: Season → Schedule · Practice plans · Lineups · Tournaments; Progress → Skills & Goals · Insights. The word “practice” is on the screen twice; neither is a door to planning it, and nothing says whether Sunday has a plan (it does).

    Practice plans, fresh team (from the code): the empty state — eyebrow “Practice plans”, headline “Plan a practice once, run it from your phone”, a sentence on what a plan is, a payoff sentence about templates and helpers, then the blocker “A plan attaches to a real practice, so put one on your schedule first.” with Add a practice (which lands on the Schedule’s list) and “How practice plans work”.

    The library: Drills and Plan templates live under Skills & Goals, as two of that page’s four tiles. The Practice plans hub carries a foot link to Plan templates only.

    How it feels to use — my assessment

    What works

    • Practice plans has its own nav entry, directly under Schedule, and the phone mirrors it. A coach who reads the sidebar finds it.
    • The empty state says the one true thing — a plan lives on a practice, so make a practice first — and names what a plan unlocks (a template, a helper’s phone).

    What gets in the way

    • The first screen knows the next event is a practice and does nothing with it. The card’s lime is attendance, six days out. “This week · 1 practice” is a count, not a state. A coach whose whole reason for signing in is Sunday is not told Sunday has no plan — or has one.
    • The library is in the wrong room. Drills and templates are practice instruments, and they live under a page named for player development. A coach looking for “my drills” will not look there; the help even calls the room “Development” in one section and “Skills & Goals” in another. The Skills & Goals re-evaluation already ruled that Drills leaves that page — this walk is the receiving end.
    • “Add a practice” leaves the room. It lands on the Schedule’s list, where the form is two more clicks away (Add Event ▾ → Practice). The first thing a first-time coach does in this room is leave it.

    What I’d change

    • The Overview’s next-event card follows the practice’s state: no plan → Plan this practice is the lime; plan set → Open the plan; on the day → Run practice. Attendance stays as the quiet link it is for a game.
    • The hub carries the library — Templates and Drills as tabs beside the season’s list (ruled 14 September: Practice plans is the hub for all three) — and the empty state’s button opens the Add Practice form itself, not the list it sits behind.
    Your notes on this screen
    Q 0.1

    Should the Overview’s next-event card know whether the next practice has a plan?

    Today the card’s action is the same for every event type. For a game it is attendance, which is right. For a practice the thing a coach needs six days out is the plan, and the thing they need on the day is the field screen. The helper’s home page already does exactly this for a helper (“Tonight’s practice … Open my station”) — the head coach gets less than the volunteer.

    What rides on it: the Overview — One Thing ruling (one lime per screen — this keeps it, and changes what it is for a practice); the card’s “Take attendance” link stays as the secondary; the helper’s home is untouched.
    Q 0.2

    Should the practice library — drills and plan templates — live on the Practice plans hub?

    Skills & Goals stage 0 already ruled the Drills tile leaves that page and lands here; Plan templates has a foot link here and a tile there. One library, one room — as tabs beside the season’s list, so the practices stay the landing.

    What rides on it: the two Skills & Goals tiles (already ruled to go); the hub’s foot link; the help’s two spellings of the room (“Development” / “Skills & Goals”), which this retires by making the sentence untrue in both.
    Where I’d push on the shape

    Reshape, and cheaply: nothing under it changes. This is stage 0, drawn in the tab above — the hub opens on the next practice, counts only what is still to come, carries the library, and the Overview card learns one fact. The plan maker is stages 1–4; I have not let the cheap fixes here stand in for the expensive question there.

    1

    Put a practice on the calendar

    A plan attaches to a practice, so the practice comes first. How does the coach make one, and what does the calendar then say about its plan?

    Schedule …/schedule → Add Event ▾ → Practice → the “Add Practice” dialog; then the practice’s slide-over panel.
    On the screen, as built

    The dialog (“Add Practice”): an event-type pill row; When — a “Repeat weekly” checkbox first, then Starts*, Ends, and “Arrival / call time”; Where — Location with recent-location chips; a folded “Add details (optional)” holding Field / Diamond #, Address, Links (“e.g. Drill video”), Name (“Auto: Practice — leave blank to use the default name”) and Notes; footer Save Event (or “Add 12 practices” for a series).

    The slide-over for a practice, below its links: a Practice plan heading. No plan, head coach: “No plan yet — set out the blocks, stations and groups for this practice.” + Plan this practice →. With a plan: the summary (“3 blocks · 60 min planned · 1 rotation”), the goal, and two buttons — Run practice → and Open the plan →. No plan, not the head coach: the sentence “No plan yet.” and nothing else.

    How it feels to use — my assessment

    What works

    • A season of Tuesday practices is one dialog. The occurrence list with per-row remove is exactly what a coach with two long weekends needs.
    • The slide-over is the meeting point for a coach who lives in the Schedule, and “Plan this practice →” is a real door.

    What gets in the way

    • The form’s first question is the rare one. “Repeat weekly” comes before the date. Most practices a coach adds mid-season are one practice.
    • The plan is built on the practice’s length, and the length is optional. Ends is not required. Every clock the maker draws, and the “rest of practice” block, reads off the end time; a practice with no end has no length, and nothing on the form says so.
    • The plan panel is at the bottom of the slide-over, below links and notes. For a practice the plan is the content of the event; it reads as an afterthought.
    • “No plan yet.” for an assistant is a sentence without a reason. The plan page, one click away, explains (“Writing the plan comes with Schedule: View + edit — ask your head coach”); the calendar does not.

    What I’d change

    • Starts and Ends together, first, for a practice; “Repeat weekly” second. Ends required for a practice — it is the frame the plan is built in.
    • The plan panel moves up under when-and-where, and the assistant’s sentence gets the same reason the plan page gives.
    Your notes on this screen
    Q 1.1

    Should a practice require an end time, because the plan is built on it?

    The maker computes every block’s clock from the start; “rest of practice” and the unplanned-time figure I propose in stage 0 need the end. A practice without one is a plan with no frame.

    What rides on it: the Add Practice form (Ends becomes required for practices only); the recurring series (end time already carried per occurrence); nothing on games.
    Where I’d push on the shape

    Keep the shape. Two orders change — the date before the repeat, the plan panel above the links — and one field becomes required. It is the least important station in the walk and I would not spend a mockup on it; it rides along with whichever stage touches the Schedule’s slide-over.

    The list — the Practice plans hub with practices on it

    The practices exist. The coach taps Practice plans. What does the room say still needs doing, and what does it hand them first?

    Practice plans …/practice on the fixture — one upcoming practice (planned), seven recent (four planned, three not).
    On the screen, as built

    The header “Practice plans” with no action. A right-aligned chip “⚠ Needs a plan · 3”. Coming up: one row — 20 SEP · Practice review — next week · Sun · 3:31 p.m. · 1 block · 20 min planned · “Plan set” · Open the plan →. Recent practices: six rows, newest first — the probe practice (“3 blocks · 60 min planned · 1 rotation”), Team practice 5, then Team practice 4 · No plan · Plan this practice →, Team practice 3 the same, Practice review — written up, Team practice 2 the same. Foot: Plan templates →. No lime anywhere. Phone: identical, stacked.

    How it feels to use — my assessment

    What works

    • The chips are honest: “Plan set” means at least one block, so a goal typed and abandoned does not count as a plan.
    • Rows are doors, the date tile reads at a glance, and “Run practice” appears only around the practice itself.

    What gets in the way

    • The headline is wrong for the room’s job. “Needs a plan · 3” counts practices that happened in May. A coach in September is told three things need doing that cannot be done. The count is every practice without a block, past or future.
    • A past practice is offered “Plan this practice”. It is the same row and the same words as an upcoming one. Planning a practice that already happened is not a thing.
    • The page has no next move. The one lime is earned by the nearest upcoming unplanned practice; when that practice is planned (as here) there is no lime at all, and the page is a list. The next practice — the one thing a coach opens this room for — is one row among eight.
    • “20 min planned” of what? The row never says the practice’s length. A 20-minute plan on a 90-minute practice reads the same as a full one.
    • Half a library, at the foot. Plan templates is a foot link; Drills is not here at all.

    What I’d change

    • The hub opens on the next practice as a card carrying the room’s one lime (Plan this practice · Open the plan · Run practice, by state). Under it, the list.
    • “Needs a plan” counts upcoming practices only. A past practice with no plan reads “No plan written” and opens read-only; it is never asked to be planned.
    • The row says how the plan fits: “3 blocks · 60 of 90 min”.
    • Templates and Drills as tabs beside the list; Practices is the landing.
    Your notes on this screen
    Q 2.1

    Should the hub open on the next practice — one card with the room’s lime — rather than on a filter chip and a list?

    Money opens on the next move; the helper’s home opens on tonight’s practice. This room opens on a filter. The card is the same row, promoted, with its state made into the action.

    What rides on it: the “one lime, earned” rule (kept — the card owns it); the “Needs a plan” filter (kept, upcoming only); the demo’s Practice plans page, whose seeded plans sit on past practices — its headline would change from “Needs a plan · 3” to the next practice.
    Q 2.2

    Should a past practice ever be offered “Plan this practice”?

    I think not: the room is a live planner for what is still to come, and a record of what ran. The two halves want two vocabularies — “Plan this practice” above the line, “No plan written · Open” below it.

    What rides on it: the row action and chip for past rows; the “Needs a plan” count; nothing on the season-end shelf (“The practices you ran”), which already reads past plans read-only.
    Where I’d push on the shape

    Reshape — this is the stage 0 proposal, drawn in the tab. The count is a defect regardless of the shape ruling and I would fix it even on Keep.

    The blank page — the plan before a block

    The coach opens Tuesday’s practice for the first time. What is the first thing the page asks for, and does the page look like a practice plan?

    The plan page …/practice/{eventId} for Team practice 1 (Tue May 5, no plan), desktop and phone.
    On the screen, as built

    A back link to Schedule; the title Team practice 1; a meta line Tue, May 5, 2026 · 7:00 p.m. · View on schedule. A toolbar of two secondary buttons: Start this plan from… and Print the sheet (disabled). A card with three fields: TONIGHT’S GOAL — “What the whole practice is for”; WHAT THIS PRACTICE IS ABOUT — “Search your tags…” / “No tags yet — type a word to make your first one.”; EQUIPMENT — “Search your tags…” / “No equipment yet — type an item to add your first one.” Under the card, a secondary button + Add a block. To the right, a rail titled What everyone’s working on: twelve players, eleven of them “Nothing set yet”, one with a focus chip. Below everything, a box titled How it went — “For you and your staff. Families never see this.” — with a textarea, “What would you do differently next time?”. A “✓ Saved” pill docked at the foot.

    On the phone the same in one column: the three fields, Add a block, then the twelve-name rail, then How it went — 2,499px for a page with nothing on it.

    How it feels to use — my assessment

    What works

    • Autosave with three honest states (Saving… / Unsaved changes / Saved) and a bounded timeout. Nothing on this page needs a Save button and nothing pretends to.
    • “Start this plan from…” is one door with three sources (a template, a previous practice, a past season), each saying plainly that it copies and changes nothing.
    • The date and the schedule link are exactly where they should be.

    What gets in the way

    • The first three questions are about the practice, not the practice’s content. Goal, tags, equipment — a first-time coach is asked to classify a plan before they have written a minute of it. The tag pickers’ hints read as errors (“No tags yet — type a word…”), and the same sentence repeats on every block and every station below.
    • The page does not say how long the practice is. “7:00 p.m.” and nothing after it. The one number a plan is built against — sixty minutes? ninety? — is not on the page, so the coach cannot see what they are filling.
    • A third of the screen says nothing. On a team with no focus areas the rail is twelve lines of “Nothing set yet”. On the phone it sits after the blocks, so it is twelve lines between the plan and its foot.
    • “How it went” is asked before it went. The recap box renders on a practice that has not happened, with a question about next time.
    • No primary action. Everything is a secondary button. “Add a block” — the only thing a coach can usefully do here — is under a card, in the same weight as “Print the sheet”.

    What I’d change

    • The page opens on the plan: date · time · length as its first line, then an empty timeline with one ghost row — “Add the first block” — and, on desktop, the library beside it. The goal is one line above the timeline. Tags and equipment fold into a quiet “About this practice”.
    • The rail becomes a fold, shut, one line when nobody has a focus area (“No focus areas yet — Skills & Goals”).
    • “How it went” appears once the practice has started.
    Your notes on this screen
    Q 3.1

    Should the plan page open on the plan — a timeline with the first block — rather than on the practice’s metadata?

    Everything a coach types in the three top fields is useful later (the sheet prints them, the focus rail softens by tags). None of it is what they came to do. The question is order and weight, and it decides the shape of stage 1.

    What rides on it: the header card (goal · tags · equipment); the focus rail’s “Tonight: … — from the drills in this practice” line, which needs the tags to exist somewhere; the sheet’s PRACTICE / GOAL / EQUIPMENT lines (unchanged — they print whatever is set).
    Q 3.2

    Should the focus rail be a fold that stays shut until it has something to say?

    The rail is the reason this page beats a blank form once a team has focus areas. Before that it is the loudest empty in the portal. Dim-never-hide is about players, not about the panel; a shut fold with one line hides nobody.

    What rides on it: roster order and dim-never-hide inside the fold (kept); the phone, where the rail becomes a fold in the same place; the printed sheet’s focus section (unchanged).
    Where I’d push on the shape

    Reshape, and this is where the shape of the whole maker is decided — stage 1 draws it. Everything I say about blocks and stations below assumes the page is a timeline first; if this station is Keep, stations 4 and 5 become tidy-ups instead of a redraw.

    The first block

    The coach presses “Add a block”. What happens, and what does a block ask for?

    + Add a block on the blank page → the sheet → Write a block → the block card. Then the same on Practice review — written up, whose one block has content.
    On the screen, as built

    “Add a block” opens a dialog titled Add a block with two tabs — From your drills · Write one — a search box, tag chips, and the library’s rows (“Probe drill · Preview · Add”). On a team with no drills it opens on Write one: a paragraph (“You haven’t saved any drills yet. Write this one here — you can save it to your drills afterwards, and picking it next time takes four taps.”) and one button, Write a block. The dialog does not close on Escape.

    The block card: up/down arrows; a title field “What are we doing?” with the computed clock under it in small grey (“7:00 p.m.–7:15 p.m.”); collapse and delete icons. Then, open: MINUTES [15] and a “Rest of practice” checkbox; DESCRIPTION (“What happens, and how it’s set up”); GOAL (“What this is for”); STAFF (“Search your tags…” + “No staff yet — type a name to add your first one.”); PLAYERS (“Choose players”); COACHING POINTS (“+ Add a point”); STATIONS (“+ Add a station”). A written warm-up — title, 15 minutes, two sentences, six players, two coaching points — stands 743px tall.

    How it feels to use — my assessment

    What works

    • One kind of block. Rotation is a toggle that appears when a second station does, not a decision before typing.
    • The clock is derived — type minutes and every block after it moves. “Rest of practice” is the right answer to the last block.
    • A drill becomes a block in one pick, with its usual minutes.
    • Reorder with buttons works with gloves; the rule’s reason is real.

    What gets in the way

    • Two clicks and a dialog to get a blank block. The dialog exists to offer drills. On a fresh team it offers a paragraph about drills the coach does not have, and a button that does what the first button should have done.
    • A warm-up is eight questions. Minutes, rest-of-practice, description, goal, staff, players, coaching points, stations — every field open, every time, whether or not the block will ever have staff or points. The card is as tall written as it is empty.
    • Three of those questions are asked again one level down, under different names. A block has Description · Goal · Coaching points; a station has What you’re doing · What you’re watching for · Coaching points. Same three ideas, two vocabularies, one screen.
    • The block’s time is its smallest text. The one fact that orders a practice — when this happens — is grey, 12px, under the title. The plan has no visible timeline; the coach reads a stack of cards and adds the minutes in their head.
    • Blocks don’t fold. A written block stays fully open, so the second block starts a screen below the first, and the third a screen below that. Collapse exists as an icon nobody is told about, and it forgets on reload.

    What I’d change

    • “Add a block” adds a block — title focused, 15 minutes, no dialog. The library is a panel beside the plan on desktop, and a button on the block (“From your drills”) on the phone.
    • A block is a row on a timeline: time in a gutter on the left, title, minutes. It opens to one notes area (what we’re doing, what to watch for). Staff, players and coaching points are added on demand — “+ Coaching points”, “+ Staff”, “+ Players” — rather than five empty fields.
    • One vocabulary at both levels. Blocks fold to rows once the next one opens; quick durations (5 · 10 · 15 · 20 · 30) beside the minutes.
    • Escape closes every sheet on this page.
    Your notes on this screen
    Q 4.1

    Should a block be a row on a timeline that opens, rather than a card with every field open?

    This is the same question as 3.1 one level down, and it is where the height goes. A row shows the plan; a card shows the form. The model does not change — a block keeps every field it has — only which ones are on screen until asked for.

    What rides on it: the block card (rebuilt as a row + a drawer); the collapse state (a row is the collapsed state); “emptiness discards nothing” (kept — an on-demand field is still a field once added); the printed sheet and the run screen (unchanged — they already read the plan as a timeline).
    Q 4.2

    Should “Add a block” stop asking drills-or-write and just add one?

    The dialog earns its place only when there are drills to pick, and even then it is a sheet, a search, a row and a button. With a docked library (station 6) the pick is a drag or an Add on the row, and “Add a block” can mean what it says.

    What rides on it: the Add-a-block sheet (retired on desktop, kept as the phone’s “From your drills” sheet); the “four taps” promise in the help (becomes fewer).
    Where I’d push on the shape

    Reshape — stage 2. If 3.1 is Keep, the two cheap wins survive on their own: “Add a block” adds a block, and the fields that are usually empty become on-demand. Those alone take a written block from 743px to roughly a third of that.

    How it feels to use — my assessment

    What works

    • The grid is the best thing on the page. It answers the question a coach actually has — who is where at 3:41 — and it states its own arithmetic instead of tidying it (“3 rounds of 15 min. Everyone does everything.”).
    • The draw is honest about who was left out, and it never sorts anyone by ability.
    • People live at one level — a rotating block’s people are its groups, so a station cannot contradict the grid.
    • A drill-backed station renders as text, not greyed boxes; it looks like a different, settled thing.

    What gets in the way

    • The rotation comes before the stations it rotates. A coach reads “Groups move every 15 min”, two ways to make groups, three group cards and a grid full of station names — before they have seen a station. The grid names stations that are a screen further down.
    • Simultaneous on the field, sequential on the page. Three stations run at once; the page stacks them, 845px each, so the block that takes 45 minutes takes 4,127px. There is a help FAQ about why stations aren’t side by side, which is a sign someone asked.
    • The station repeats the block’s questionnaire and adds four more. Doing · watching for · setup · equipment · who runs it · starts with · coaching points · just for tonight · rotation note. For a ladder drill.
    • The link between a station and the grid is one chip in the middle of a card (“Starts with · Group A”). Column three of the grid and station three of the list are the same thing, drawn two screens apart.
    • The draw is a small form of its own — a select, a number, a button, a labelled alternative — for what is usually “three groups, go”.

    What I’d change

    • Stations as columns under their block on desktop — three stations, three columns, and the grid’s columns are the same columns, so “where everyone is” sits directly over the stations it names. Stacked on the phone.
    • The rotation strip — “every 15 min · 3 groups ⤮ Draw · or build them” — sits between the block row and the columns, one line.
    • A station card shows name · who runs it · tonight’s note, and opens for the rest; a drill dropped on a column fills it.
    Your notes on this screen
    Q 5.1

    Should stations be columns under their block, so the page looks like the field?

    The grid already draws the practice as columns. Drawing the stations the same way puts the plan and its arithmetic on one screen instead of three. The phone keeps a stack; it has no width to spend.

    What rides on it: the station card (opens in place); the rotation grid (kept, moved under the strip); the run screen’s station list (unchanged — it is a phone screen); the sheet (unchanged — it already prints the grid inside the block).
    Q 5.2

    Should the group draw be one control — “Draw 3 groups” — with “build them yourself” as the fallback?

    “How many groups” is almost always “one per station”, and the product already knows the station count. Players-per-group and hand-built groups stay, one click further in.

    What rides on it: the draw panel; “who was left out is named” (kept); the shuffle-and-deal rule (kept).
    Where I’d push on the shape

    Reshape — stage 3, and the stage with the most to gain: this is the block a coach builds most weeks, and it is the one that costs 4,000 pixels. If 3.1 and 4.1 are Keep, columns for stations are still worth doing on their own; the rotation strip is the cheaper half.
