03 / The useful constraints

Small is the honest test.

Actual raster drawings at 16 and 32 CSS pixels, alongside enlarged pixels. The badge is white artwork on transparency, with no coloured tile.

What this proves: the drawings can be rendered at the stated sizes, in one colour, and within the maskable safe circle. It does not establish whether someone recognises the brand after a week. That needs people, not a score invented by a designer.

Before final artwork: choose a direction, refine its spacing, outline the wordmark and check actual devices, paper and a stitched sample. The 16 px tab may use the explicitly labelled optical adjustment. No fine detail has to carry the identity.

04 / The second opinion

Why I would widen the brief.

A good brand mark does not have to illustrate a sport or an admin task. It has to become recognisable as FieldLogicHQ, then work wherever the name travels.

The old proposals above are redrawn from Claude’s supplied SVG geometry. These are visual assessments, not user research. I would also retire the command prompt, the blueprint border and the stacked F/L: retaining their colours is enough continuity.

05 / My recommendation

Take Joined FL
into refinement.

Own the initials.
Let olive lead.
Keep lime useful.

Keep Open F as the alternate. Show both without the explanation first. If the monogram feels too engineered to the people who will use it, the simpler letterform is the better direction.

  1. Joined FL — the best overall starting pointConnects to the actual name, gives the app one identifiable silhouette and works as a small one-colour emblem. Its angular construction needs a warmth check with volunteers.
  2. Open F — the strongest simpler alternativeA wordmark-led identity with a generous curved F. The same F works alone. Easier to read, but a lone F has more visual neighbours.
  3. Huddle — the strongest community expressionThree broad arcs around a shared centre. Warmer and less literal; more dependent on repeated pairing with the name. Watch the recycling, aperture and wellness associations.
  4. In Order — the clearest product metaphorUnequal blocks suggest fixtures falling into place. Calm and versatile, but also close to generic dashboard and productivity imagery.
  5. Clubhouse — the strongest apparel directionA compact F inside a shield. Familiar in community sport and easy to place on clothing, but too close to a club’s own crest and to security software for my first choice.

The colour decision

Keep the palette; change the hierarchy. Olive becomes the recognisable identity colour. Paper and ink make it warm. Lime stays the action colour and can remain an accent in signed-in Dark. The logo does not need to consume it.

An olive tile is my first choice; the ink tile is a useful alternate. A warm brand can still have a dark app tile. Colour alone is not the identity.

The owner decisions that matter

  • Do Joined FL and Open F still feel right without their stories?
  • Does Joined FL feel dependable or too engineered?
  • Should olive become the signature, with lime reserved for action?
  • Would you wear the small emblem, or prefer the full name?
