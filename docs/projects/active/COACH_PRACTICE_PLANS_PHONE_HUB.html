Standing back

  • S.1 The editor is nested, and nesting is the cost. Sheet → spine → card → field: each layer takes its padding and the writing column is the remainder. The fix is not thinner padding; it is taking the form out of the timeline on a phone.
  • S.2 A form that grows in the flow moves everything under it. A form on its own screen moves nothing — the list is where the coach left it when they come back.
  • S.3 A cut sentence is not information. The row carries the facts that fit (length · who · stations); the sheet carries the words.
  • S.4 What already fits a phone is left alone. The station sheet, the groups room, the run list and its stops were drawn for a phone and measure like it.
  • S.5 At the field the thumb is busy. The one control that moves the practice on sits where the thumb is, docked, never under the fold.

The four decisions

K1 · the list

On a phone, the plan’s blocks are a compact list

Three blocks measure 351px today and say two cut sentences each. The row keeps the facts that fit and the sheet keeps the words.

A · recommendedKeep the spine and the clock; the row is title + one line of facts

The clock column narrows from 92 to 66px (the reorder pair leaves it), the spine stays, a block is a 56px row: Warm-up · 15 min · UAT Coach · 6 players. The plan still reads as the document the desk shows; only the row’s sentences go. Drawn above.

BThe portal’s row list, the time as an inline lead

The Schedule’s phone shape: no spine, the time in the row’s lead cell. Slightly more room for the title (~290 vs 258) at the cost of the plan looking different from itself on a desk. Not drawn; say the word and it will be.

CKeep one truncated sentence on the row

“Dynamic warm-up, then partn…” as a second line. Costs ~20px a row and, measured, never completes a sentence at 390.

K2 · the block opens on its own screen

A block edits in a full-screen sheet, not inside the timeline

This is the load-bearing decision. It answers the margin, the narrow fields and the lost place with one change of container.

A · recommendedThe sheet — the “Add a station” shape

Full width, its own header, the list untouched behind it, a pinned foot with ‹ 2 of 3 › and Done. This is not a new idiom: the station form inside a block already opens exactly this way on a phone — 359px fields, “station 1 of 3” in its head, Delete · ‹ · Next in its foot (station D on the walk tab). Fields go from 197 to 358px (+82% characters a line), the title from 106 to 358, six player chips from six lines to two, and nothing under the block moves when it opens. The tradeoff is real: a coach cannot see the rest of the plan while writing one block; the eyebrow (“Block 2 of 3 · 11:00 p.m.”) and the ‹ › stepper are the answer to that, not a second column.

BStay in place; narrow the margin

The honest arithmetic of the first framing: with the ▲▼ pair moved and the column at 66px, the fields grow from 197 to ~223px (+13%), the title from 106 to ~132, and the block is still ~1,000px tall in the flow, still moving everything below it. A real gain, and a small one.

K3 · reorder

Where do Move up · Move down go once they leave the row?

The pair is the reason the clock column is 92px. On a phone it is used rarely and it is the only control on a shut row that is not “open me”.

A · recommendedOn the sheet’s head, beside Delete

Three 44px squares on the row that already carries ✕ and the eyebrow. Move keeps the sheet open on the same block and the eyebrow’s clock updates. The desk keeps its gutter pair untouched.

BA “Reorder” mode on the list with drag handles

The lineup builder’s phone idiom (the number as the handle). More build, a second mode to learn, and drag is ruled an addition — the buttons would still be needed somewhere.

K4 · adding a block

“+ Add a block” is one row; where do its two paths go?

Today the ghost row is a 130px paragraph — “Add a block · 15 min · or a drill from your library” — with two doors in it.

A · recommendedThe row opens a blank block’s sheet, with “Start from a drill ›” inside it

One tap to the common case (a new block with the cursor in the title); the library one more tap, as a quiet door under the title. The sheet opens on “Block 4 of 4 · 12:15 a.m.” with 15 minutes set, as the ghost row promises today.

BA chooser first — Write one · From your drills

The station sheet’s two tabs, one level up. Symmetric with stations; one more tap for every block a coach writes by hand, which is most of them.

The durable record: how the walk was read, what it found, the rules, the ladder, and what each stage owes at build. The markdown file is the source; this tab is the same text, readable.

0 · What this is

A phone-first re-evaluation of the two screens a coach builds and runs a practice on — the plan editor and the field screen — in the shape of the phone programme: a measured walk, shape questions for the owner, and a stage ladder drawn, ruled, built and walked one stage at a time. It picks up exactly where the phone programme’s stage 4 stopped (“the practice plan’s sheet itself” was named as not reopened there) and where the practices re-evaluation never went (every one of its screens was drawn on a desk).

1 · Method

23 September 2026, dev, signed in as the UAT head coach, the UAT probe practice (a written plan: a plain block, a rotating block with three stations and three drawn groups, a rest-of-practice block). A Playwright phone context at 390×844 and another at 360×780 drove the plan page into edit mode, opened each block, opened the station sheet and the groups room, then the run screen’s list, a rotating stop, a station and a plain stop. Per screen the browser’s own geometry was read: page height, every field’s width and the characters it holds, the open block’s height, how far the next block moved, controls under the 44px floor, pinned chrome, the position of the field’s buttons against the fold. Screenshots were taken only as reference for the drawings.

2 · Findings

On the “What the walk found” tab — the station table is the record. In one line: the writing column is 197 of 390px, a block opens to 1,013px inside the timeline and pushes the next block 895px down, the rotation grid scrolls sideways inside a 197px box, and a new station’s first field is four taps in behind a 31px button; the station form, the station sheet, the groups room and the run screen were built for a phone and measure like it — the station form is the very shape stage 1 proposes for the block.

3 · Standing rulings this plan works inside (not reopened)

The plan as a document (practices stage 1); “+ Stations makes two” (D13); the grid as a starting point (D14); drag as an addition, buttons everywhere (L2); no clock at the field (P10); the run list as the first screen (W1–W4); the record’s face (R2); the phone programme’s shell, the transient Saved pill, the field floor, and E1’s toolbar; the tap floor as a touch-only rule and the 641–768 band; the portal’s phone-panel conventions (drawers flush to the bar, the warm scrim, Escape/scrim/Back all close).

4 · The ladder

Stage 1 · The block on its own screen (K1–K4, drawn 23 Sep, ruling owed) · Stage 2 · Stations and the rotation (L1 the station row and the form one tap in; L2 the rotation by round on a phone; L3 hand-arrange as the pill’s tap menu) · Stage 3 · The field (M1 Back · Next · Rotate now docked; M2 the station screen; M3 swipe as an addition — recommended Not yet) · Stage 4 · The head of the page (N1 what sits above the first block on a phone).

The template editor inherits all of it. No migration, no new route, no year parameter.

Practice plans on a phone — the brief

For a product owner: what a coach sees and does differently, why it matters, what it costs.

The problem in one sentence

A coach writing a practice plan on their phone types into fields half the width of the screen, inside a block that is taller than two screens and pushes the rest of the plan out from under them every time they open it.

What a coach sees after stage 1

The plan is a short list of blocks — each one line of title and one line of facts, nothing cut off — with “+ Add a block” at the foot. Tap a block and it opens on its own screen with every field at full width and the minutes chips on one line; a bar at the foot walks to the previous or next block without going back; Done returns to the list exactly where it was, with the block you edited picked out. Stations, groups, equipment and the library doors are all in the sheet, where they are on the card today.

Who it affects

Every Premium coach and assistant who writes plans (Schedule: View + edit) on a phone or small tablet, and — read-only — anyone who opens a plan there. The desktop and larger tablets are unchanged. Plan templates get the same treatment because they are the same editor.

Why it matters

Practice plans are the Premium portal’s weekday tool and the one most often written in a car park or a kitchen, not at a desk. The phone programme made every other weekday screen fit a phone; this is the last one that does not.

Tradeoffs

A coach cannot see the rest of the plan while editing one block on a phone (the sheet’s eyebrow and ‹ › are the mitigation). The plan’s look diverges more between phone and desk than any screen so far (a list vs a timeline with the block open in place). Stage 1 leaves the rotation grid scrolling a little on a phone; stage 2 draws it by round.

Success criteria

At 390: a shut block ≤ 60px and never truncated; a writing field ≥ 350px wide; the minutes chips on one line; opening a block moves nothing else on the page; a three-block plan written from blank without returning to the list; the layout sweep green with the sheet open; the field’s advance button reachable without scrolling on every stop once stage 3 lands.
