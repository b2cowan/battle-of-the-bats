A

The Founding Season desk

One list, both account kinds. A comped Coaches Portal already carries the same free-season record an organization does — the workspace provisioning writes it — so this is one query over accounts, not two lists stitched together. The desk reads only: every action it implies (change a plan, cancel, send) already lives in Bulk Operations, the org page, or Email Campaigns, and the desk links to them. A read-only desk cannot convert the wrong account by accident.

C

A coach's billing page — the correction

The brief says coach workspaces need a card door built. Reading the code, the door already exists — and that is the problem. A comped Coaches Portal is a paid plan as far as the page is concerned, so it renders the ordinary "Billing tools" block with a button called "Payment method & invoices". That button opens a card-save session. It is unlabelled as such, available all year rather than only in the summer window, and the page never mentions the Founding Season at all — because the whole Founding Season block is suppressed for coach accounts.

So item 3 is not "build a door". It is gate it, name it, and let the page speak to the product the account is actually on.

D

What happens behind the choice button

The one thing that must be true: no path can take money before October 1, 2027. Three ways to build it, and the recommendation makes Stripe itself responsible for the date rather than a reminder in someone's calendar.

ApproachWhat it isWhy it wins or loses
A · A subscription whose first charge is pinned to the season's end
Recommended		 The customer's choice creates the 2028 subscription immediately, with its first charge set to October 1, 2027 and no charge before it. Wins. Stripe holds the date, not us. The choice is confirmed to the customer the moment they make it, the desk reads it back through a real event, and an account that has chosen needs no action at all in October — which is the entire point of the desk.
B · A schedule that starts in October Nothing exists until October 1, then a subscription begins. Loses. There is no subscription to read, so the desk cannot show the choice back from Stripe; a second kind of object to handle; and the October date still has to be right.
C · Record the choice here, create everything in October No Stripe object before the turn-off day. The safest against an early charge — but it moves the entire risk onto one manual day, and whether that day is hand-run is not decided until May 2027. The customer also gets no confirmation that anything happened.

Three rails on option A, and a test that fails if any of them slips. The first-charge date is the one Founding Season constant and nothing else — no arithmetic, no "days from now". No proration and no billing anchor, so no invoice can be raised at the moment of choosing. And the checkout refuses to build at all if the season's end date is already in the past, which is the one mistake that would charge someone immediately.

This is being built nine months before it can be used, and I think that is worth saying out loud. The choice cannot open until June 1, 2027 and cannot charge until October 1. Code that never runs rots quietly, and this repo has already been bitten once by Stripe moving a field. The plan books a re-verification pass in May 2027 — re-check the Stripe version, run the no-early-charge test, rehearse one sandbox choice, confirm no environment override has moved the dates — as part of the deliverable, not as a hope.

E

Four decisions I am not taking for you

Each has a recommendation. None of them is built until you rule.

D1 · The coach read-only window

What does a coach who chooses nothing actually keep?

An organization that lets its free season end drops to the free Tournament plan and keeps its data. A coach has no equivalent floor: the ruling says a Coaches Portal is cancelled. A read-only window was ratified in principle and has never been built — so until it exists, the copy on that last card can only promise what the product already does, which is keep Basic tournament records and retain the premium data for the restore window.

Recommended — build it in the spring, alongside Phase 3

The turn-off mechanism and the read-only window are the same conversation, and both are decided by the same cohort-size question in May 2027. Until then the coach copy stays on its honest fallback line and promises nothing extra.

Alternative — build it now, in this phase

Lets the summer copy promise a real read-only season. Costs a screen-by-screen ruling on what a lapsed coach can still open, which is a design conversation of its own — and it would be built nine months before anyone lapses.

D2 · When a choice takes effect

If someone chooses in June, do they get the new plan in June — or on October 1?

For almost everyone this is invisible: the realistic choice is the plan they are already on, because League and Club are not open for self-serve checkout. But if the menu ever widens, a June choice would hand over the bigger plan's features for free for four months, because the product flips the plan the moment the subscription exists.

Recommended — the plan changes on October 1, the choice is recorded in June

"Free season, then the plan you chose" is one sentence a customer can hold. It also keeps the free season identical for everyone, which matters if you ever have to explain it.

Alternative — the plan changes the moment they choose

A generous, simple story: choose early, get it early. But it makes the free season worth different amounts to different accounts, and it is a real entitlement change we would be giving away without deciding to.

D3 · The opt-out question

Does a "your free season is ending" notice honour the marketing opt-out?

Today the batch tool checks the marketing opt-out on every campaign and appends an unsubscribe footer; only transactional messages — welcome, billing, cancellation — skip that check. A founding account that unsubscribed from marketing in February 2027 would, under today's rules, never be told its free season was ending.

Recommended — send it as a service message, not a campaign

It is about the account's own billing state and has a deadline attached — the same category as a trial-ending notice, which already bypasses the opt-out. The customer opted out of being sold to, not out of being told what happens to their account. It should look like an account notice, not a campaign: no offer, no marketing footer, one fact and one link.

Alternative — honour the opt-out

Cleanest possible consent story, and no argument is ever possible. The cost is real: some accounts lose their season and never hear about it, which produces exactly the support conversation nobody wants.

D4 · The no-early-charge guarantee

Do you accept "nothing can charge before October 1, 2027" as designed?

The answer proposed is yes, on option A with its three rails and the test that enforces them. One thing sits outside that promise and should be said plainly: a founding account that deliberately buys a bigger plan during the free season is charged for it, on the ordinary checkout, as it is today. That is a purchase they chose to make, not the Founding Season turning over — but it happens on the same page, so it is worth ruling rather than assuming.

Recommended — accept, and leave the voluntary upgrade path alone

The promise is about the free season converting, not about blocking someone who wants to buy more. Nothing about that path changes in this phase.

Alternative — no charge of any kind before October 1

Absolute, easy to explain, and it would mean turning off in-season upgrades for founding accounts — refusing money from someone asking to pay us.

F

What is deliberately not here

  • The desk cannot act. No plan changes, no cancellations, no sends from this page. Those controls exist elsewhere and the desk links to them. The October turn-off mechanism is Phase 3's decision, due May 2027.
  • Nothing sends this autumn. The "no card yet" audience is built and counted; the words behind it are the parallel Phase 1 chat's work, and the reminder campaigns are hand-sent.
  • Only one new audience. "No 2028 choice yet" is a filter on the desk, not a second email audience — there is no copy for it and no decision about who it is for.
  • "Owner last seen", not "last activity". The product keeps no activity metric. The column is named for the fact we actually hold rather than implying one we do not.
  • The production backfill is presented, never run. Reading which existing accounts already have a card in Stripe is a read-only pass with a dry run, and its output comes to you before anything writes.

One fix rides along because it is drift in a surface this phase owns: the Organizations page badges any account whose comp expires after 2026 as "Founding", using a hand-typed date instead of the shared rule. A support comp granted into 2028 shows up in the founding cohort today.
