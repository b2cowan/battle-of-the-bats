Why moving between club pages jumps

The club's pages were built across Stages 4, 4b, 4c, 4f and 9, and each was drawn at its own width. Your August repair ruling says a club page's content starts directly under the club's logo, each page keeping its own reading width. Most pages do. About, a team's tryout page and the tryout sign-up form are centred instead, so on your screen their content starts 212 to 280 px to the right of the logo. Every page also stops at a different place. The eleven rules, read on a club page

The tournament standard (R1–R11) was written for a frame with a left menu. Where a rule fits a club page, the club page follows it. Where it doesn't, the proposal is to amend the rule rather than bend the page. The amendments go into the standard itself, which the tournament build writes in its first part. Numbers match the orange markers on the drawings.

The hop: a club home and its tournament

A family clicks the Fall Classic on the club home, or "Hosted by Lakeshore Lightning Softball" on the tournament, and back. Flip between the two pages below. Turn on the other page's lines to see, in purple, where the page you just left had its band, its logo and its content.

What stays different, and why. The club keeps its tabs in the band, and the tournament keeps its left menu. You ruled both (08-01: "a tab row at every width, never the tournament's left rail"; the menu came with the tournament redesign), for one reason: each site's navigation lines up with its own page. So on a wide screen the logo moves to the window's edge and the content moves 88 px to the right. The proposal makes everything else match: the same band height, the same content width, the same top, the same spacing, the same headings and rows.

    Answered without an ask

    • Menu on the left or tabs in the band. Each site keeps its own. You ruled the club's tabs on 08-01 ("a tab row at every width, never the tournament's left rail") and the tournament's menu in its redesign, and the reason is the same on both: each site's navigation lines up with its own page. Six to nine tournament tabs and a long name would not fit a band, and a club's three to five words do not need a 248 px column.
    • About, a tryout page and the tryout form move to the logo's edge. That is a repair under your 08-01 repair ruling ("a heading lands directly under the club's logo"), not a new choice. It happens whatever C2 says.
    • The word "door". The club's door (a card with the club's coloured edge and a button: tryouts open, the next season) keeps its name. The plain row with a chevron is the link row, as in the tournament standard.
    • Phone edge: 16 px on every club page. The house-league pages and a past tournament's record use 24 today.
    • Kept as you ruled them, and registered as exceptions to the standard: Born in stays the house pick-one pill (10-22, "not view pills"); on a phone, Subscribe, Directions and Copy stay bare symbols (10-20); a team's page keeps its calendar rows (10-19, 10-20); the "clearing" heading on the pay page keeps its blue information colour (Stage 12c).
    • Not drawn, standard applied on paper: a past tournament's record page, the in-club "not found" page, and the house league's Schedule and Standings pages (C7).
    • Found on the way, not layout (written into the plan): a failed Tournaments page shows the platform's error screen without the club's band; a team with no games yet leaves an empty 744 px column beside its tryout door (drawn fixed: one column); a code note in the shared form sheet says club forms use the tournament's 680 px form column, and they don't (they are 640); a brand note still says scores use the typewriter face on club pages, which the 10-19 ruling removed (for /design).
