The draft board at 120 players

The 9c board was drawn with five players in the pool, and with five it works. At a real division of 120 players and 10 teams, "Not on a team" becomes a column about 12,000 px tall. Three teams sit beside it and the other seven start under its last row. This drawing keeps every job the board does and changes its shape, so it behaves the same at 6 players and at 150.

Every frame is true size (desk 1440 × 900, phone 390 × 800) and uses the real players from the Draft Test 2027 seed. Ratings are examples, because the seed has none. The frames scroll like the real page: scroll inside one to feel today's 13,000 px of page, or to watch the proposed pool stay put. The numbered dots open their notes. Each note is tagged NEWRESTYLEDUNCHANGED.

What goes wrong today

The pool is one card in the teams' grid

The pool is simply the first card in the same wrapping grid as the teams, and as narrow as one. Its height becomes the height of the first row, so teams 4 to 10 start only after its last player.

A player is about 96 px tall

Each player has a name and then a sentence that wraps, "Age 10 · counts as a 3 · owes $165", plus a 54 px ⋯ button. The steps list above already says "the other 83 count as a 3", so each row repeats it.

Nothing helps you find anyone

There is no search, filter or sort. Families with more than one child, and the notes families wrote at sign-up, are not on the board at all.

The proposal

  • The pool becomes its own column, 344 px wide, on the left of the board. Once you scroll past the steps it stays at the top of the window, and its list scrolls inside it. Any team is in reach to drag onto.
  • The teams get the rest of the width in a grid of their own. This screen stops growing at 1200 px, so that is 2 across beside the pool at every window size. When everyone is placed, the pool folds to one line and the teams go 3 across (4 on a window wider than about 1,500 px).
  • One line per player, the same row in the pool and on a team: rating, name, small marks (family, note, owing), age, ⋯. It is 40 px on a desk, 44 px on a tablet and 52 px on a phone. A player's state (Locked, Moved by you, Asked to withdraw) drops to a second line under the name, only on the rows that have one.
  • The pool's toolbar matches the rest of the admin: Search, then Filter (a menu with ticks and counts), then a Sort pick. There is no row of chips.
  • Averages have one home, the top of each team card: "12 players · avg 3.1". The balance line keeps only "Moved by hand".
  • The steps become a drawer (your request, round 3). It starts open, and Hide steps folds it into one line ("Steps · 3 of 4 done · Next: publish the teams") with the next step's button. That saves about 240 px above the board. The steps themselves are unchanged.

        What 120 real players brought up beyond the layout

        The draft separates brothers and sisters. In this seed, 17 households have two or three children in the same division. The draft ignores family, so it splits most of them. Keeping siblings together is the request a house-league convenor hears most (one practice night, one carpool). Locking each child before the draft works today, but at 36 children that is 36 locks spread over a 120-row list. See ask A7. That one changes how the draft deals players, so it is a build of its own.

        Family notes aren't on the board. Notes like "wants to play with a friend from school" and "can only make Tuesdays in June" are the other thing a convenor drafts with, and an allergy note matters to the coach who gets the player. Today you'd leave the board to read them. See ask A8.
