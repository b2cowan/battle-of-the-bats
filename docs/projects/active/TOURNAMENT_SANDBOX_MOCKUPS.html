Shared machinery · one component, both sandboxes

The sandbox chrome

One banner and one chip rail, mounted by the org shell whenever the org being viewed is a demo org. It is org-agnostic on purpose — the coach sandbox mounts the same two pieces with its own chip list. It sits above everything the product already draws and never rearranges the page underneath it.

New
Sandbox banner. Full-bleed, sticky at the top of the viewport, above the product's own header. Lime eyebrow + plain-language promise + reset countdown + the one CTA. Never dismissible — the honesty claim has to stay visible.
New
Tour chips. 3–4 per side, numbered. Tapping one scrolls to the beat and puts a one-and-a-half-second lime ring on it, then marks the chip done with a check. Progress is remembered for the visit only.
New
Reset countdown. Live mm:ss to the next replay. It is the proof that the demo is running, not a recording. Decision D3 below — exact clock vs. "resets a few times a day".
Unchanged
Everything beneath the chrome. The fan page, the admin shell, the nav, the data — all the real product. The sandbox adds a hat, it does not build a replica.

Why one component, not two. The banner, chip rail, countdown, blocked-save nudge, and demo-session door are written once against "is this org a demo org?" — never against "is this a tournament?". The coach sandbox then costs a chip list and a seed, not a second build.

What the sandbox deliberately doesn't show New

Added 2026-08-02. A short list of admin areas is hidden in the sandbox rather than left to dead-end — for the demo's own quality, not for secrecy. This is the same rule already binding for archived coach seasons: hide the entry point rather than let it 404.

Hidden in the sandboxWhy it's bad demo real estate
Billing & subscription A comped demo org's billing page answers a question nobody is asking ninety seconds in.
Staff invitations Entirely outbound, so every control would be locked. A screen made only of locked buttons reads as a broken product, not a careful one.
Data tools & exports Exporting invented data is a dead end with nothing to learn from it.
Deep settings forms Long forms where a stranger can't tell what changed, or why it mattered.

Everything else stays reachable. The tournament module is meant to be shown deeply, not defensively — this list is four corners, not a curtain. The competitive question was asked and closed on 2026-08-02: the door itself is ungated, permanently, because a competitor already gets more by signing up free than the sandbox ever shows.

The moment · copy + placement rules

Every dead end in the sandbox is a sales moment, and every one of them tells the truth first. The rule: never say "error", never say "failed", never blame the visitor — the change didn't fail, it was never going to be kept, and we said so in the banner.

What happens when they press it

1Press "See it live" — no form, no modal, no email field.
2A single fixed demo organizer is signed in server-side. Nothing about the visitor's request chooses who that is — no account parameter, no redirect target, and the door is rate-limited.
3They land on the fan page — the friendlier half, and the one that proves it's live within seconds.
4Chip 3 (or the pill) flips them to the organizer's dashboard. The dual view is the beat that sells.
5Every dead end offers Start your own — free.

Shipping the doors is a separate decision. Phase 1 builds and verifies everything on dev. Creating the demo org on production and turning these two buttons on is a release step you and I do together — with a /strategy log entry, since it changes how the funnel works.

The decisions, as ratified

Approved by the owner on 2026-08-02 together with the screens above. The competitive-exposure question was raised and closed in the same session; the full reasoning is in the Business Decisions Log. The door is ungated, permanently — no email, no form, no lead capture — with a revisit trigger in January 2027, when Tournament Plus stops being free.

#DecisionRatifiedWhy / what it changes
D0 Gate the door? — whether "See it live" asks for anything first No. Ungated, permanently. No email, no form, no interstitial, no lead capture. Tournament is free with no card and Tournament Plus is free to everyone until Jan 2027 — a competitor gets more by signing up than the sandbox shows, so a gate protects nothing and only filters honest prospects. Accepted cost: no attribution — we won't know who visited unless they convert. Revisit Jan 2027.
D1 Naming — what the demo org and tournament are called Riverdale Minor Ball Association running the Riverdale Summer Classic, with Rapids / Cyclones / Marauders / Sharks. Follows the existing "Riverdale" fictional convention already used in the coach budget sample. Realistic enough to be believable, invented enough that nobody can mistake it for a real association. Also keeps both sandboxes speaking one fictional world.
D2 Plan tier for the demo org Comped Tournament Plus — show the full product. A free-tier demo undersells us and makes the sandbox argue against the upgrade. The brand rule still holds: anything Plus-only stays labelled as such, so nobody is misled about what the free plan includes.
D3 Reset cadence and how visibly we count it Replay every 2 hours, re-anchor dates nightly, with an exact mm:ss countdown in the banner. The visible clock is what makes "live" credible. Alternative if it feels gimmicky: "resets a few times a day" with no numbers — cheaper to trust, weaker as proof.
D4 Phase 2 moments dock (Registration week / Game day / The morning after) Later. Ship the one perfect moment first. Registration week needs a second seeded state with pending teams and payments. Worth doing — after we've watched real prospects use the first one.

Slice order I'd build in

S1The seed, standing on its own. A self-contained canonical tournament — no cloning the dev fixture, no inherited clutter, no test payment copy, invented contacts only. Deterministic results extended through the bracket so every replay lands identically. Nothing is visible yet; everything downstream depends on this being clean.
S2The safety machinery. Demo-org identification, the single chokepoint that refuses every change, and outbound silence. Built org-agnostic so the coach sandbox inherits it. Verified before the door exists — the block is proven before anyone can walk in.
S3The door. The one fixed demo organizer, signed in server-side, rate-limited, with nothing about the request able to influence who gets signed in.
S4The chrome. Banner, chips, countdown, the toast catch-all, and the locked outbound controls — public and admin.
S5The clock. Tick / replay / nightly re-anchor, each alerting loudly on failure. A frozen "live" demo is worse than no demo.
S6Hygiene. The demo org kept out of the public directory, out of our own metrics, out of error alerting — and out of search results, since its fan pages are genuine public tournament pages that could otherwise be indexed or mistaken for a real association. Plus the four hidden admin corners from section 3.
S7The doors, on dev only. Marketing buttons wired but not promoted to production until you say so.
1bThe drag beat. Fenced on purpose. If it fights, everything above still ships and this follows.

Standing guardrails these mockups are built to honour

  • One account, hardcoded. The door can sign in exactly one fixed demo organizer and nothing in the visitor's request can change that.
  • One chokepoint. Every change for the demo org is refused in a single place, not route by route — so a route built next year is blocked by default.
  • Silence, twice over. The demo org cannot email or notify, guaranteed independently at the notification and email paths. Every contact in the seed is invented.
  • No schema changes expected. If one turns out to be necessary, I stop and ask before touching the database.
  • Dev only. Nothing runs against production; the production demo org and the live marketing doors are a release step we do together.
  • Ungated door, curated room. Nothing is ever asked of a visitor before they walk in; four admin corners are hidden inside rather than allowed to dead-end.
  • No commits without your say-so, on the shared dev branch, staying clear of the coaches-portal and setup-wizard work other chats have open.
