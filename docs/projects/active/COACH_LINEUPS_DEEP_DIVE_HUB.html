What changes for the coach

  • Tap the Assign / Change / Keep one word beside a role and the candidates open as a dropdown floating over the panel — the money chooser's own list. The rows never move.
  • Each candidate is a name with a sub-line in the depth chart's own words: Pitcher P2 · 2 of 4 innings used · Best 1B, 3B, LF, Best RF · Never LF, or No positions rated yet.
  • A coloured word on the right only when it is about this role: Best 3 · Never · At cap · Doesn't pitch — plain coloured text in the build, the money chooser's live-hint slot.
  • Groups: Open · no decision yet, On the Bench, On the field · swap. Best first inside each.
  • Under the roles, every player who is NOT at a field position is a row with a door too: Decide for an undecided player (Bench this inning, or take a position — the holder sits), Move for a benched one.
  • A filled role's list ends with Leave open under an "Or" heading; a clash row's list offers Keep for each holder.

  • The native dropdown beside each row, and its "· Bench", "· Best 2", "· C" suffixes crammed into one line.
  • The in-place fold that was built between the two — it pushed the whole table down, and the owner rejected it.
  • The current position beside a swap candidate — it is on the grid behind.

Settled with the owner

  • The "On the field · swap" group stays, last and quiet ("looks good, go for it").
  • A dropdown, not a fold. The fold was built and rejected the same hour; the list now floats over the panel and opens upward from a row low on the screen.
  • On P for a team with a pitching chart the groups become Pitchers (innings left) · At cap · Not on the pitching chart, same rows.

What the coach does differently

  • Two tabs: Lineup (build it) and Playing time (read it). No separate first step to discover.
  • Reorder by the batting number — drag it on a laptop as today; press-and-hold it on a phone.
  • Remove with × on a laptop as today; on a phone, tap the number → Remove.
  • Nine-player ball: the Start checkbox promotes or benches, at every width, as today.

What goes, and what covers it

  • The Batting order tab and its vertical list.
  • Its “usual positions” line under each name — the depth chart keeps that; the grid shows where they are this game.
  • Its “drag above this line” bench cut — the Start checkbox already does this.
  • Its ↑ ↓ arrows — the same two moves live in the tap sheet on a phone; keyboard users keep arrow-key reorder on the handle.

The one thing to test on a real phone

  • Hold a number, drag it two rows, let go. Then swipe sideways across the innings starting from a cell. The first must lift, the second must scroll, every time.
  • If it ever lifts when you meant to scroll, the fix is the hold time, not a third tab — and the tap sheet already covers the move without dragging.
  • The phone toolbar is drawn as built today (a 2-column stack). Not part of this decision.
Product-manager brief

Coach Lineups Deep Dive

Outcome: keep the capable lineup engine, but give coaches an honest preparation state, readable tables, actionable coverage checks, and a review that supports real decisions.

What changes for the coach

The coach works through Batting order, Positions, and Playing time as one visible preparation flow. Auto-fill is the clear primary action and names its active mode. Blank decisions say Open, deliberate sits say Bench, and the table sits on a solid high-contrast ground.

Implemented core: started innings now show coverage, open roles are factual Draft checks with a direct jump to that inning, conflicts mark their exact controls, and Playing time names field, bench, pitching/cap usage, and attention.

Implemented addendum: Auto-fill now keeps each player’s pitching innings in one uninterrupted block; it will not generate P → Bench/field → P. Other positions keep their existing rotation behaviour.

The games list shows Not started, Draft, Ready, or Needs review. In the recommended model, any coach with lineup access can mark ready; the product records who did it, and a later edit returns the lineup to Draft.

Why it matters

Today a one-cell draft can be labelled “Lineup set,” and the attached warning guesses that a Never setting caused the hole without receiving eligibility or cap data. The visual treatment also lets the blueprint grid show through both tables and controls.

Customer impact

Every head or assistant coach with lineup access benefits, especially a first-time coach or someone editing on a tablet at the field. Access rules do not change.

Priority

P0 truth and legibility; P1 workflow and game-level analysis; P2 inning inspector and full Sport Pack vocabulary.

Success criteria

Partial work is never called ready; open-role copy never invents a cause; real conflicts point to exact cells; no page grid shows through the tables; and a coach can explain, build, review, and finish the lineup without needing Help.

Coach Lineups — findings and implementation sequence

P0 · Trust

F01 — Coverage is incomplete; the warning's cause and tone are wrong

Inning 7 lacks P and LF. Alex and Ashlyn are blank and both configured as pitchers, but neither blank assigns P. Both are still under cap and eligible. The product should say what is open, not guess Never.

P0 · Truth

F02 — Any one saved cell becomes “Lineup set”

Replace the boolean with Not started, Draft, Ready, and Needs review. Recommended: persist ready-by/time and return to Draft on edits.

P0 · Visual

F03/F04 — Tables and controls do not follow the approved surface/ink rules

Frames do not paint the card ground; headings use tertiary ink; controls use translucent grounds and faint borders. Align both tables with the standard and use Open instead of a hyphen.

P1 · UX

F05/F06 — Workflow and Auto-fill intent are hidden

Teach the existing three-view sequence, make Auto-fill primary, show its mode, and reveal concise placement rationale on demand.

P0/P1 · Rules

F07–F09 — Manual rule violations and exact correction targets are missing

Validate Never, pitching/rotation caps, minimum play, and conflicts without blocking coach overrides. Mark exact cells and add per-inning completion.

P1 · Analysis

F10/F11 — Playing time and blank-state language need decision support

Use explicit Field/Bench/Open figures, cap headroom, named attention states, and a clear legend. Keep season analytics in Insights.

P2 · Follow-on

F12/F13 — Add an inning lens and retire hard-coded diamond vocabulary

An inning inspector answers who is at every role. Role choices then come from the Sport Pack before another sport is enabled.

Build sequence

  1. Shared validation/readiness model and regression tests.
  2. Solid table/control treatment, exact-cell issues, and inning completion.
  3. Draft/Ready workflow across hub, Overview, Game day, print, and Help.
  4. Playing-time and Auto-fill rationale.
  5. Inning inspector and Sport Pack vocabulary.

Verification

Unit/API/component coverage; typecheck and `verify:changed`; rendered warm/dark checks at 1440, 768, 640, 390, and 361; owner browser walk from first build through ready, edit-back-to-draft, print, and Game day.

Gives assistants and head coaches a real handoff. Any edit returns the lineup to Draft.

D2 · Treat open roles as a neutral draft check?✅ Approved · built

Warnings are reserved for proven conflicts, cap/Never overrides, and a failed ready attempt. Open work stays visible without accusing the coach.

D3 · Make Auto-fill the primary action and show its mode?✅ Approved · built

Format and Innings now sit in a labelled Setup group; Reshuffle and Templates stay secondary. Polished on owner review 09-18: the Setup caption sits above its two fields rather than floating beside them, every control in the row shares one height, and the “Lineup · 12 hitters” mini-header above the toolbar is gone.

D4 · Show per-game pitcher cap headroom in Playing time?✅ Approved · built

Supports this game's decision. Season totals and rest history remain in Insights.

D5 · Defer inning inspector to Phase 4?✅ Approved · Phase 4 built 09-18

Deferred past the P0 trust and contrast fixes, then built the same day on the corrected shared truth model: an inning-first lens opened from any inning heading (and from "Review inning N"), listing every field role with its holder and a depth-chart fact, Bench / Open / Also lines, grouped Assign / Move / Keep / Leave-open controls, and a "Why this is a draft check" card that names only what the chart can prove (every idle player Never here; every idle pitcher at cap) — never a guessed cause. Two things the mockup drew were deliberately left out: the "Suggested resolution" line (a matching guess that is occasionally wrong costs more trust than it earns) and a confirm on a Never pick (F07's override marker — the grid has none either).
