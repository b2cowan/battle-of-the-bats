A coach joins a tournament: registers, signs up, pays and is refunded

Written 2026-10-28. One purpose: follow three coaches through a real tournament on the dev site, from the public registration form to the card payment and the refund, and check that every email and sign-up step behaves the way a coach and an organizer would expect. It is long on purpose; each part ends cleanly, so you can sign off a part at a time.

What this walk is for

This is the whole customer journey in one walk: a coach finds a tournament, registers a team, gets an account, hears from the organizer, pays by card, and (for some) is refunded. It uses a walk-only tournament, “Stripe Walk Invitational”, run by UAT Rep Club, so nothing here touches a real event. You play two sides at once: the organizer (signed in) and the coaches (signed out, in fresh private windows).

Two things get the closest look, because they are where customers get confused:

  • Sign-up. On the dev site a brand-new coach is not signed in after registering: the team goes to the organizer first, and the coach has to click a link in their email to open their portal. The walk checks that this works, and notes where the form still asks for things twice.
  • Every email. Ten kinds of message can go out in this journey. For each one the walk names who should receive it, its subject, and what it should say. Where the product sends nothing (a declined card, a move to the waitlist) the walk asks you to record that as an observation so you can rule on it.

These paths are covered: a new coach who gets accepted and pays (Parts A–C), a coach who fumbles the card first (D), a coach on the waitlist (E), and a returning coach with an account who registers a second team and is turned down (H–I). Reminders and the pay-link email are Part F, refunds are Part G, and Part J checks the email log and Stripe, then puts the tournament back.

The money is test money (Stripe sandbox), and the club passes the card processing fee on to the team, so a card total is always a little higher than the entry fee.

It passes when
  • Every coach who registers sees the right confirmation on screen, receives the right email at the right address, and the organizer receives “New Registration” each time.
  • A new coach can open their portal from the link in their email and sees their team as pending, with the fee shown and no Pay button there.
  • Accepting a team (one at a time, and in bulk) sends the acceptance email with a Pay now button that opens that team’s own pay page.
  • A card payment shows “Confirming your payment”, then a receipt; the same receipt number is on the pay page, in the receipt email and on the organizer’s record of the team; the organizer’s record and the coach’s portal both show the payment.
  • A declined card and a left-behind checkout record nothing and send nothing.
  • A waitlisted team cannot pay (the pay page says it has not been accepted); accepting it later sends the acceptance email.
  • A payment reminder and an “Email the pay link” both arrive, with working buttons.
  • A full refund and a part refund each send a refund email, update the organizer’s record, and agree with Stripe. The treasurer cannot refund.
  • Resend lists every email this walk should have caused, each delivered, and Stripe’s “Dev: club payments” destination shows only 200 responses.
  • Every observation step is ticked, so the gaps in the journey are written down.

Before you start

Setup, not checked. Keep this page private: it holds dev passwords.

Where
The dev site https://dev.fieldlogichq.ca. Keep three other tabs ready: the Resend dashboard › Emails, the Stripe dashboard in test mode, and your own inbox.
Windows (important)
You are two people at once. Use your normal window for the organizer and keep it signed in all walk long. For each coach, close every private window first and open a brand-new one: private windows share one sign-in, so a coach who is still signed in from the last step would be treated as the same person on the next form. The treasurer in Part G also gets a brand-new private window.
Your inbox
Use a real inbox that supports plus-addresses: yourname+coach1@gmail.com lands in yourname@gmail.com. Coach 1 is +coach1, coach 2 is +coach2, coach 3 is +coach3. Because they all land in one inbox, read the To line of every message: the organizer’s messages go to your plain address (the one you gave the seed command), the coaches’ go to their plus-address.
If an email is missing
Every email step says the same thing: if it is not in the inbox after 2 minutes, open Resend › Emails, find it by subject or recipient, and note whether it says Delivered, Bounced or is absent. Check the spam folder too. An email in Resend but not in the inbox is a mail-delivery problem; an email absent from Resend is a product problem.
Fixture
The walk-only tournament is created by node scripts/seed-stripe-walk-tournament.mjs --inbox <your address>. It makes “Stripe Walk Invitational” (slug stripe-walk-invitational) under UAT Rep Club, open for registration, starting about 45 days from today, with two divisions: Walk 13U (room for 12, empty) and Walk 15U (room for 1, already holding the accepted team “Walk Filler Team”, so the next team to register there lands on the waitlist). Its organizer contact email is your address. Part S checks it is there.
The money
From the seed: entry fee $300.00, deposit $100.00, so the balance is $200.00. The club has chosen that teams pay the card processing fee, so each card button shows more than the entry fee. These figures may change if the seed script is edited; the relationships (deposit then balance equals the entry fee, same receipt number everywhere) are what the walk checks.

Stop check

Stop check first (Part S). If the tournament, its two divisions or the club’s online payments are not there, stop and run the fixture command in “Before you start”; do not improvise.

What this walk leaves behind: teams, payments and refunds on the walk-only tournament, coach accounts, and emails in Resend. The put-back at the end of Part J clears the tournament’s side; Stripe sandbox payments, Resend history and the coach accounts stay.

Re-running: after the walk, the put-back step (last step of Part J) returns the tournament to this starting state, so the walk can be run again. Use fresh plus-addresses (+coach11, +coach12, +coach13) to be brand-new coaches again; the old ones stay as accounts.
