## 1. Outcome and scope

The sole proprietor can record a normal purchase, find its receipt, correct an error, understand recorded spending, and give an accountant the matching records without learning accounting-system terms. Start with expenses already supported by the tool. Add a full proprietor ledger only after the revenue source and owner-movement contracts are settled.

The tool is private to the authorized bookkeeping owner. It has its own AWS expense records and receipts; the club and coach accounting screens are interaction references, not a source of proprietor transactions.

### Baseline before Stages 1–3

- One page puts a fixture-only Stripe CSV preview first, then a long expense form, then an expense report and the expense list. An ordinary CAD purchase asks for both invoice total and CAD total. The payment source defaults to **Paid personally**. USD conversion and tax fields are visible even for an ordinary CAD purchase. Correcting a row scrolls to the top, where the preview sits ahead of the form. See `app/platform-admin/bookkeeping/BookkeepingClient.tsx`.
- The tool stores expense revisions and private receipts. Its report covers recorded expenses, purchase-tax status, categories, payment sources and missing evidence. Filtered CSV and receipt package exports already work. The owner reported the B2 expense-only walk passed 13/13 on 2026-10-23.
- It has no posted income, processor fees, payouts, owner transfers, bank reconciliation, or validated business cash balance. Its Stripe CSV control is a local fixture preview; it does not import or post rows. The [closure record](../archive/BOOKKEEPING_CLOSURE_RECORD.md) identifies the unfinished source and operating gates.
- The current `expense_date` field is labelled “Date paid or incurred,” mixing two events in one period filter. `tax_cad_cents = 0` with `tax_status = pending` cannot distinguish an unknown tax amount from a confirmed zero. Category suggestions come only from existing free-text entries.

## 2. Experience to build

| Place | First thing the owner sees | Main action |
|---|---|---|
| **Overview** | Recorded spend for a named period, receipts/details needing attention, and recent expenses | **Add expense** |
| **Expenses** | Searchable list with date, supplier, category, business amount, payment source and receipt state | Open a row to see its record |
| **Expense record** | The entered facts and original receipt, followed by change history | Correct; Void is the secondary action. A voided record shows no actions (R1) |
| **Reports** | A named month/year/custom period, recorded spend by category and supplier, personal payments, tax/evidence status and the matching entries | Open the entries behind a figure; export the visible report |
| **Sources** | The current fixture preview in an advanced area with clear “preview only” status | Review a source file when the Revenue contract is accepted; no posting control yet |

**Ordinary expense flow:** Add expense → enter the expense date (incurred), supplier, one amount, category, what it was for, how it was paid, and attach a receipt → review → Save → open the saved expense. The payment date defaults to the incurred date. A **Payment date is different from expense date** checkbox reveals the second date only when needed; toggling it off restores the incurred date. The form labels use words an owner uses. It asks for a payment source explicitly. For CAD, the same entered amount supplies the stored original and CAD totals. USD reveals the original amount, CAD amount, conversion source and date. Business-use share and purchase-tax details live in a clearly named **More details** section when they need attention. A missing receipt is allowed and appears in the review queue.

**Record flow:** The row opens one readable record with the receipt, all amounts, payment source and history. Correct opens the same focused form with existing values and asks why the record changed. Void is a separate question, and it is the only way a record leaves the totals: the history is never lost. **Permanent Delete is removed entirely** (owner ruling R1, 2026-10-25, reversing the 2026-10-23 decision that kept it). The bookkeeping database grants the app SELECT and INSERT only (verified read-only 2026-10-25), so the button could never succeed, and an append-only record is what an accountant needs. A voided record shows no actions.

**Reporting rule:** Until income, payouts and bank movements are accepted and reconciled, Overview and Reports describe **recorded expenses**. They do not display profit, cash on hand, a bank balance or recoverable tax. The owner accepted incurred date for expense reports and paid date for future payment/cash views on 2026-10-24. Neither view silently treats a legacy “paid or incurred” value as both dates. Each summary figure must trace to the exact entries it counts. Existing CSV/ZIP exports keep the visible filters and revision/receipt evidence.

## 4. Reuse from the existing app

- Both Ledgers' filtered list, whole-row record door and source-aware read window inspire the expense register and later proprietor ledger. They are UI patterns; the club/team ledger tables and balances stay separate.

## 5. Stage 0 owner rulings and remaining definitions

1. **D1 — dates (approved, 2026-10-24):** New entries carry incurred and paid dates. Paid defaults to incurred; a checkbox reveals a different payment date. Expense reports use incurred date; future payment/cash views use paid date. The existing `expense_date` is labelled “paid or incurred,” so historical rows cannot safely populate both new dates. Preserve its original value, mark both new dates unknown, show those entries in **Dates to review**, and export their date status. Inspect the actual development rows before migration.
2. **D2 — tax (approved, 2026-10-24):** Store unknown purchase tax separately from confirmed $0. The owner can record tax shown on a receipt without the product claiming a credit. The exact data migration remains in Stage 2.
3. **D3 — categories (approved, 2026-10-24):** Use these seven plain-language starting labels and a clear way to add or correct one: Software & online services; Advertising & marketing; Equipment & supplies; Professional services; Travel & meals; Bank charges; Other. Preserve original spellings in revision history. Categories are entry aids, not automatic tax classifications.
4. **D4 — receipt (approved, 2026-10-24):** Put upload beside the core entry fields, with a visible **Add it later** path and a missing-receipt follow-up item.
5. **D5 — reports (approved, 2026-10-24):** First answer spend in the selected period, purchases needing evidence, and personally paid purchases.

These are product/data definitions. The experience work can be designed immediately. Tax treatment and full ledger classifications remain with the accountant and Revenue workstream.

## 6. Acceptance and implementation discipline

- The owner can add a normal CAD expense with one amount entry; USD reveals only its necessary evidence; an absent receipt remains visible for follow-up.
- A saved expense is easy to find, open, correct and export. A correction keeps the previous revision and original receipt; void is the only way a record leaves the totals (R1).
- The selected period is named on every figure and export. Voided expenses are excluded from active expense totals, and filtered export contents match the visible entries.
- Phone controls meet the product's 44px tap floor; labels and refusals read in plain language; the form does not lose entered work on an incidental dismissal.
- Owner browser and visual verification follows the repo's `AGENCY_RULES.md`. Code changes to shared components, API/data contracts or config take their required focused/static gates and restart before handoff.

## 8. Stage 2 build and review path

Development inspection before the tax migration found 6 revisions across 5 expenses: one `none`/zero, four `pending`/nonzero and one `reviewed`/nonzero. The additive `scripts/bookkeeping-aws/003_tax_amount_known.sql` migration added `tax_amount_known` without changing row counts or historical amounts. A historical pending/zero row is interpreted as **Unknown**, while a historical nonzero amount or explicit no-tax/reviewed state is treated as known. New revisions store a deliberate boolean; corrections preserve earlier revisions. Production migration state belongs to the bookkeeping release record.

The Stage 2 interface provides a named period, category and supplier figures that open contributing expenses, a separate Dates to review group, and one Needs attention worklist for receipts, dates, unknown tax amounts and USD conversion evidence. ## 9. Stage 3 build and Revenue handoff

The owner authorised Stage 3 on 2026-10-24, with a group walkthrough after implementation. The source-independent draft is built: `bookkeeping.owner_movement_revisions` stores dated owner contributions, draws and expense reimbursements as immutable revisions; reimbursements link to an active personal expense and remain separate from its purchase amount. The Ledger screen combines active expenses and owner movements as readable dated activity, opens each expense or transfer, and keeps correction and void history. It does not calculate a bank balance or profit. The additive development migration `004_owner_movements.sql` preserved all six expense revisions and began with zero owner movement revisions. Backup archive format 2 includes these records and format 1 remains readable; restore proof remains a Stage 4 gate.

**Correction (2026-10-25):** this draft was described above as built for the group walkthrough, but it **could not save a transfer**. The save route's ID check was malformed (its fourth group lacked the `-xxxx` segment), so the server refused every owner transfer, and the same check refused the linked expense on every reimbursement. No owner-transfer row was ever written. The stand-in accountant's review found it; the fix is Part C1 of the [accountant-review fixes](BOOKKEEPING_ACCOUNTANT_FIXES_BUILD_PROMPT.md), with a unit test the route did not have.

Revenue's accepted Stripe source contract is still missing. The group needs actual source IDs and identity scope; seller and tax attribution; gross, fee, refund and adjustment relationships; payout-to-bank mapping; and real examples of normal payment, fee, refund, payout, duplicate delivery and interrupted recovery. ## 10. Stage 4 operating handoff and evidence

**Recovery rehearsal, 2026-10-24:** The encrypted development archive was created and authenticated, then replayed into isolated development DSQL tables and temporary S3 keys. Six expense revisions and four original receipts matched after replay; there were zero owner-transfer revisions to exercise. The working tables remained at six expense revisions and zero owner-transfer revisions. A post-drill read found zero recovery tables, zero recovery object versions and zero delete markers. The drill archive and its one-use passphrase were removed afterward. This proves the current development archive can recover those QA records and receipt bytes without clearing the working store. It does not prove a production restore, owner-transfer recovery with real rows, or a separately retained operating backup. The verifier was corrected to accept the safe legacy QA receipt key suffix as well as current UUID suffixes; it still rejects traversal and nested paths.

**Accountant package:** Reports now offers an Accountant package ZIP containing `expenses.csv`, `owner-transfers.csv`, original receipts, `receipt-index.csv` with receipt hashes, and `package-scope.txt`. Expense rows use incurred-date and expense filters. Owner transfers use transfer date; a narrowed expense view includes only reimbursements linked to the selected expenses. Each CSV includes revision history and void status. The scope note states that bank statements, Stripe income, fees, refunds, payouts and reconciliation are outside the package. The owner must supply those external documents and the accountant must review the classifications before this can represent a complete close.

**Proposed operating routine for BKX-4.1:** Create and verify an encrypted archive after each day with bookkeeping writes, before a schema or data migration, and at every month-end close. Keep the latest 30 daily archives and 12 monthly archives in a separate private location chosen by the owner; record archive SHA-256, timestamp, environment, revision/receipt counts, verification result, copy destination and the person who checked it. The bookkeeping owner holds the encryption passphrase in a password manager, separate from the archive, and names a second recovery custodian. These are proposed roles and intervals, not an enabled schedule or an accepted retention policy. The current local AWS login requires interactive renewal, so it cannot safely serve as an unattended backup identity. A future unattended path needs a scoped identity and its own recovery drill. A local `.bookkeeping-backups` file alone is not the separate retained copy. As of the 2026-10-24 AWS read, no AWS Backup plans were listed in `us-east-2`; the production receipt bucket reported versioning enabled. [AWS documents DSQL backups](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/aws-backup/latest/devguide/backup-aurora.html) and [S3 Object Lock/versioning](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonS3/latest/userguide/object-lock.html) as possible managed controls, but neither replaces the missing owner custody decision.

**Recovery procedure for BKX-4.2:** Verify archive authentication and receipt hashes before writing. Restore only into a fresh target with the required schema; never clear a working store to make room. Compare every revision and receipt byte with the archive, confirm owner-transfer links, record cleanup or promotion steps, and retain a nonsecret evidence log. The `restore-drill-dev` command provides the isolated rehearsal; `restore-dev` remains restricted to a truly empty development store. A production recovery path needs its own reviewed target, credentials and proof.

**Accountant review worksheet for BKX-4.3:** Record the period and accountant; provide the package plus bank/processor statements and any receipts supplied outside the app; review legacy dates, unknown purchase tax, USD conversion evidence, personal-use percentages, owner contributions/draws/reimbursements and void/correction history; list requested reclassifications and missing documents.
