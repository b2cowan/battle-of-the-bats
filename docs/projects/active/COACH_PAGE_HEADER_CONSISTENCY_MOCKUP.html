Coach portal page headers — the decided direction

Every page under a coach's team hub draws its own header by hand — forty pages, no shared pattern, and four different ideas of what the grey line under the title is for. The owner reviewed three options and ruled: nothing under the title, the masthead picks up the season and the coach's role, and actions always sit to the right of the title. This document shows today's state, the ruling, and the approved render.

2 · The ruling

Four rules, decided

The portal's existing "chrome rule" (2026-08-02: chrome may only say what the page hasn't already said) applied to all forty pages at once, with the season and the role handled at the source instead of guarded page-by-page.

1 · Nothing under the title

Page headers are a title and actions — no subtitle line exists anywhere in the portal. Live facts that subtitles used to carry ("12 active players") move into the body they describe; framing lines that prior rulings require ("Roster order — a coverage view, not a ranking") move into the card they frame. Relocated, never deleted.

2 · The masthead owns the season — and the role

The masthead is the identity surface: whose club, which team, which season, how it's going — and now who you are here. The role renders as a quiet tag beside the team name — "Head Coach" / "Assistant Coach" — the same chip vocabulary the portal already uses for identity and state (Premium, Complete). When an org names a season something richer than a bare year ("Fall Ball 2026"), the masthead shows that name; a plain year renders "2026 season" exactly as today. No page can repeat what only the masthead states.

3 · Actions right of the title — and only actions

One row: title left, actions right — the pattern Roster already had becomes the rule. A view switcher is not an action: it changes how you look at the same content, so it rides the body it switches (Roster's List/Depth-chart toggle already does; Schedule's List/Week/Month joins it). At phone width, secondary buttons go icon-only — the standing mobile rule from the admin side — while the one lime primary keeps its label. And the help "?" is chrome, not an action: it anchors the top-right corner beside the title on every page, at every width, so help lives in one findable place portal-wide. Only actions ever drop to a row beneath the title; that drop stays pinned right.

4 · One header, forty pages

One shared page-header component with fixed slots — icon · title · archive chip, then actions, with the help "?" anchored last in the top-right corner at every width. The portal already did exactly this for modals; pages never got the same treatment, and two pages have since forked the CSS (one fork already caused a real layout bug). The component retires both forks, settles the 20px/22px icon split, and makes the next inconsistency impossible to write.

Option A, amended — clean titles, a masthead that says who you are

Four screens plus the archive case, stacked under the one shared masthead. The dark pills on dashed lines are reviewer labels naming each screen — in the product, each header appears once on its own page.

And on the phone — the product's own compact rules, unchanged

Phone widths already have their own header metrics (smaller title, smaller icon tile), and the masthead already collapses to a slim bar once you scroll. This ruling changes nothing about that behavior — it just has less to collapse.

The trade acceptedHeader lines carry less prose; anything worth reading now has to live in the page body. That's the point — but it means empty states and body toolbars pick up the orientation job, and they must actually do it (part of the build, not a follow-up).
4 · Considered and declined

Two other options were on the table

B — "the earned line" kept a subtitle wherever it stated a live fact ("12 active players") — declined for the cleaner all-or-nothing rule; the facts move into the body instead. C — "facts as chips" pressed those facts into title-line chips — declined because chips mean state in this portal (Complete, Premium, Draft), and an archived roster collected four of them. Both renders are preserved in this artifact's version history.

Fidelity note. Frames commit to the warm coach-portal palette and the declared metrics from the product's stylesheets (title 1.75rem/900 display face, 48px icon tile, masthead mono metadata).
