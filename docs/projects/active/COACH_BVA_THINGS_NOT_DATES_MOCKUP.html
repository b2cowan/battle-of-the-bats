Things, not dates

Open Fundraising · Merchandise sales on the Statement and it unfolds to a single line reading Feb 2027 — against money that arrived on August 31st and September 10th. That is not a bug in one row. It is what happens when a report dates the plan on one basis and sweeps the money onto it on another, and it is the argument for taking dates off this report altogether.

A date on a record is a fact about that record. A date as a row is a claim about time — and that is the Months view's job.

Why the fold cannot be fixed, only removed. The sub-rows are the plan's dates. Actual money is then placed against them by a rule — each amount lands in the first planned slot on or after the day it moved, anything later lands in the last one. Merchandise sales has one planned slot, in February, so every dollar lands there whenever it arrived. Making that honest would mean dating both sides, which means a row per line per month on both halves of a report a treasurer is supposed to read in one screen. There is no version of this that is both truthful and short.

Where dates live today

Four places. Only one of them is making a claim it cannot support.

2 · Player dues folds to players, like every other category

Owner ruling, 2026-09-10. Player dues stops being a special row and becomes an ordinary category: it folds to one row per player, each carrying Budgeted, Actual and Variance in the report's own columns. No new panel is built — a modal listing what each family was billed and has paid would be the Player Dues page rebuilt inside a report.

The rule this settles, and it holds across the whole report: a door lives on an item number, never on a category number. Every category row on the Statement already has plain figures — Player dues, carrying a door on a category row, was the only violation. Folding it to players moves that door down to where doors belong.

What is still open

1 · The export is now the load-bearing decision, and it got sharper.
When the families were going to be a modal, keeping them off the exported file was a small divergence. As rows, they are in the table itself — and the Statement exports to a file that boards and parents read. Recommendation: the export keeps the single Player dues row it has today, and the per-family rows are screen-only. Drives and sponsors stay named in the export; they are businesses and events, not children.

⚠ This deliberately breaks the standing "export shape = screen shape" rule, so it has to be recorded as a named exception or a later session will "fix" it back. If you would rather the file match the screen, that is a coherent answer too — it just needs saying out loud, because it changes what a treasurer is handing round a table.
2 · Does the plan's own schedule survive inside the Budget door?
On an ordinary line, the Budget figure's panel lists the schedule — and it was deliberately made that way nine days ago, when one word came to carry one line and listing lines would have been one row restating the heading. It is dates, so it is in scope for your rule; but it is the plan's dates, making no claim about when money moved. Recommendation: it stays. If it goes, the Budget figure has nothing left behind it and should stop being a door at all — coherent (a plan is a number you set; an actual is a pile of records), but a bigger change, and it loses the line's note and its way back to the plan.

This does not affect a player row: a family's bill is one assessed figure with no records behind it, so it stays a plain number either way.
Settled by the 2026-09-10 ruling, recorded here so it is not reopened: Player dues folds to players with the report's own three columns; no billed/in/owing panel is built, because that is the Player Dues page; the composition panel moves from the category figure down onto each player's Actual; and the category row's figures go plain, because doors live on item numbers, not category numbers.
