The ceiling is the bill

An Adjustment lowers the dues. A bill can be lowered to zero and no further — what the family has paid so far has nothing to do with it.

Coach money · guard ruling

The hand-back wrinkle

The ceiling is one rule for every team. But on a "settle at season's end" team, credits don't touch the installments until then — so the whole write-off reads as "owed back" the moment it's saved, and the mid-season Pay out door is bounded only by credits held, not by what the family paid. A $900.00 write-off on a family who's paid nothing would let a coach hand them $900.00 in cash. That is the hole yesterday's zero-ceiling was closing.

Recommended

(i) Let it lower the bill right away — like forgiveness already does there

On a hand-back team, an Adjustment lands on what's owed immediately, exactly as a forgiven amount already does in that mode. Only the leftover is owed back.

  • One rule everywhere, no second guardrail — the roll-forward arithmetic already does the right thing.
  • The product already groups "Adjustments & forgiveness" as one kind of thing; this makes them behave as one.
  • A coach who says "the bill is $883.00" stops having the system chase $900.00.
  • Changes one thing on hand-back teams: a mid-season write-off lowers the next installment instead of being refunded at season's end.

(ii) Keep it held until season's end, but cap the refund

The write-off stays a credit until settlement, as the mode intends — and what's refundable from it is capped at what the family has actually paid in.

  • Preserves the mode's philosophy: families pay the schedule, differences settle at the end.
  • Adds a rule on the payout side that only Adjustments need — every other credit is money-backed.
  • The Dues figure reads $883.00 while the installments still total $900.00 until settlement.
  • Two rules answering two questions, where (i) needs one.

This replaces the earlier "hand-back teams only vs. any family" question, which assumed the ceiling itself was mode-specific. It isn't — the bill is the bill.

Product-manager brief

An Adjustment is limited by the bill, not the balance

What changes for the coach

An Adjustment lowers a family's bill, and the only limit on it is the bill itself: a $900.00 bill can be written down by up to $900.00, whether the family has paid none of it, half of it, or all of it. Today the limit is what's still owed, so a family who has paid $500.00 can only have $400.00 written off, and a family who has paid in full can't have anything written off at all — even though the write-off is about the bill, not the balance. When a write-off is bigger than what's still owed, the form says so before the coach saves: "This is more than is currently owed — $17.00 becomes a credit this team can hand back to the family." Nothing else about adding an Adjustment changes — same form, same fields, same place it shows up afterward.

Why it matters

The coach hits the wall at exactly the moment a write-off is most needed — after money has come in and something turns out not to have cost what was billed. The two workarounds (paying back directly, or editing the schedule total) both move the same money, but one leaves no reason anywhere on the record and the other replaces the coach's reason with a fixed system sentence and quietly rewrites what the family was "always billed."

Customer impact

Every team, any time a bill is lowered after a payment has landed. Most visible on hand-back ("settle at season's end") teams, where today an Adjustment is refused outright — but a roll-forward team with a family half-paid hits the same ceiling.

Priority

Small and self-contained: one guard formula, one new sentence, no migration. One open question on hand-back teams (below) decides whether it's one change or two.

Success criteria

A coach can write off any part of a family's bill with a real reason, regardless of what's been paid, and it shows up in Adjustments & forgiveness exactly where every other write-off lives. Write-offs never exceed the bill from either direction — an Adjustment can't push past it, and a schedule edit can't pull the bill beneath it; the Dues figure never reads negative. Nothing is ever handed back to a family that they didn't actually send — including on hand-back teams.

Full assessment

The Adjustment ceiling is the bill — implementation plan

Problem / context

An Adjustment is the one credit a coach types by hand with no arrived money behind it. The ceiling in lib/dues-credit-guards.ts (adjustmentCeiling, owner ruling 2026-09-11) exists so a hand-typed number can never turn into "the team owes this family cash nobody sent." That goal is right. The formula chosen for it — what's still owed — is the wrong quantity: an Adjustment is a reduction of the bill, and it lowers the Dues figure in every mode, so its ceiling is the bill. Owner correction 2026-09-12: "the adjustment is to the total dues, so we shouldn't be able to total adjustments more than the total dues … regardless of how much they have paid." The invented-money goal survives that correction on roll-forward teams unchanged (the arithmetic already caps what's owed back at what was sent); on hand-back teams it needs one decision (F03).

F01 — The ceiling measures the wrong thing

What the code does today: adjustmentCeiling() returns leftToSend — what's still owed after credits — floored at zero, and returns 0 outright on a keep_separate team. So a family who paid $500.00 of a $900.00 bill can have at most $400.00 written off; a family who paid all $900.00 can have nothing written off. Yet the Dues figure (splitDuesLadder → duesActual) subtracts standing other + forgiven credits in every mode — an Adjustment lowers the bill regardless. The ceiling is measuring the balance when the act is a reduction of the bill.

What the proposal does: the ceiling becomes what's left of the bill: original charges − (standing other + forgiven credits), for every team, every mode. Payments and money-backed credits (fundraiser, sponsor, reimbursement) don't enter into it. Downstream, nothing new is needed on roll-forward teams: applyCreditsToBills lands the write-off on what's owed first and the leftover flows to owedBack — which by construction is ≤ what the family sent, because the bill can't go below zero. Note this is not the schedule editor's path (strandedExcess mints an overpayment credit against the gross schedule); the Adjustment credit itself carries the owed-back amount, which is why it keeps its own description.

F02 — A reason that doesn't survive

What the code does today: lowering the bill through Edit schedule does create a payable-back credit, but it's labelled with a fixed system sentence ("Overpayment (dues changed)") no coach can edit, and it silently rewrites what the family was "always billed" on every season report that reads the schedule's total.

What the proposal does: the Adjustment keeps the coach's own written reason, shown in Adjustments & forgiveness exactly where every other write-off lives — the original $900.00 charge is never rewritten.

F03 — The hand-back payout hole

What the code does today: on keep_separate, applyCreditsToBills filters the spendable queue to forgiven only (lib/dues-credits.ts ~420–423) — so an other credit never touches an installment there and its whole amount lands in owedBack. payoutCeiling counts every standing non-forgiven credit and knows nothing about unpaid installments. Under a bill-sized ceiling, a $900.00 Adjustment on a hand-back family who paid $0.00 is offered in full by the Pay out sheet. This — not the ceiling — is the "money nobody ever sent" hole the 2026-09-11 zero was closing.

What the proposal does (recommended, (i)): include other alongside forgiven in keep_separate's spendable set — an Adjustment is a bill reduction, not a held credit, and forgiveness already gets this treatment in that mode. Then the roll-forward arithmetic closes the hole with no extra rule. Alternative (ii): leave the spendable filter alone and cap an Adjustment's refundable portion at the family's payments in on hand-back teams — a second rule, on the payout side, that only this credit type needs.

F04 — The bill can be lowered below its write-offs (today)

What the code does today: the per-player schedule POST (dues/route.ts ~452–465) and the roster re-run (generate-installments/route.ts ~271–329) read credits only inside if (payouts.length > 0) and only to feed projectScheduleTotalChange → payoutFloorViolation, whose docstring says "a LOWER total only grows the credit, so only a rise can reach the floor" — true of overpayment credits, untrue of write-offs. Nothing compares the new total to standing other + forgiven. splitDuesLadder clamps loweredC against credits, not the bill, and has no floor on dues — $300.00 bill / $600.00 other prints Dues −$300.00. In applyCreditsToBills an other credit is indistinguishable from a money-backed one: $300.00 lands, owedBack = $300.00, and payoutCeiling (excludes forgiven only) offers it. planRosterDuesRun has no creditType branch — a $0-paid family with a $600.00 Adjustment gets no exception row. No unit test pins this scenario (nearest: coach-dues-band.test.ts:98 asserts balance −450 without asserting Dues). Reachable today under the 09-11 ceiling: record an Adjustment ≤ what's owed, then lower the schedule.

What the proposal does: a new pre-flight in lib/dues-credit-guards.ts — writeOffCeilingViolation(newScheduleTotal, standingWriteOffs) — asked by both schedule doors before the write, in the payout floor's idiom: refuse with one sentence naming the figure and the door that fixes it. The roster-wide run refuses only the affected families (its existing per-family pattern) and planRosterDuesRun emits them as blocked rows. splitDuesLadder additionally floors dues at zero as belt-and-braces for any pre-guard data. Allow-and-clamp was rejected: it leaves a wrong record and tells the coach nothing.

Scope

One formula change in adjustmentCeiling() (every mode, one credit type). The consequence sentence in the Add Adjustment form when the amount exceeds what's still owed. The refusal sentence names what's left of the bill instead of what's owed. The mirror guard on both schedule doors (F04). Plus whichever of F03 (i)/(ii) is ruled.

Out of scope

Forgiveness (credit_type: 'forgiven') is untouched as a credit type. No installment picker — a credit has never been tied to one installment in this system (that landing is derived at read time, never stored), and picking one wouldn't change the question. No schema change.

Approach

Ceiling = original charges − standing write-offs (other + forgiven). Existing test at dues-credit-guards.test.ts:82 already pins "an existing other credit reduces the ceiling" (600 → 450) — that case is unchanged. Three cases flip: :49 (fundraiser 200 → ceiling 400) becomes 600, because a money-backed credit isn't a write-off; :59 (600 paid → 0) becomes 600; :67 (keep_separate → 0) becomes 600. The two adjustmentCeilingViolation cases at :102/:110 hold; :117 (0.01 on keep_separate refused) flips to allowed.

Open questions

F03 — (i) or (ii)? Screen 5. Recommendation is (i). Move to Decisions once ruled.

Exact wording of the consequence sentence in the form, the refusal sentence, and the owed-back note in the drawer — the mockup proposes language; it is not final.

Rollout

No migration. Unit coverage: the four flipped cases above plus a partial-payment case ($500.00 of $900.00, $900.00 written off → $400.00 lands, $500.00 owed back); under (i), a keep_separate allocation test proving other now lands like forgiven; for F04, both doors refusing $300.00 against $600.00 written off (per-player 409 with the sentence; roster-wide refusing that family by name and completing the rest), planRosterDuesRun emitting the blocked row, and splitDuesLadder never returning a negative dues. The Exceptions-First preview gains one blocked-row kind, so its help sentence about blocked rows widens by a clause. Help copy for the Adjustment ceiling needs rewriting either way. The coach demo's money narration doesn't describe the ceiling, so no dock/tour line moves. /review required — it touches money guards on three doors and (under (i)) the allocation engine.
