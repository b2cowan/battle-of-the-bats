Walk B: a person arrives with a job

Your three tasks come first, then nine more the walk turned up. Each station has the same parts: what the person knows when they arrive, the path as built, the counts, my verdict, and the proposed path with its click count before and after. Red flags are things that can go wrong. Amber flags are things that slow a person down. Click any flag for its detail.

Owner-named

1 · "I was charged twice"

The intent

A customer emails support. They give an email address, maybe a club name, maybe the last four digits of a card. Done means: the customer is found, every charge sits beside what our side recorded, a real duplicate is told apart from two legitimate charges (a plan change, a renewal plus a partial-month charge, two clubs on one card), the money is put right, and a record says what was done and why.

Path as built
  1. Customer Users, type the email, press Search.
  2. Click the club in that person's row to open its record.
  3. The record shows our plan, status and period end. It shows no charge, invoice or payment.
  4. Press Open Stripe. You leave the console.
  5. In Stripe: find the payments list, compare the two charges, work out why, refund there.
  6. Come back. Open the Support tab, type a note by hand, press Add Note.
3 + Stripescreens here, then Stripe's
~14clicks, 6 here and about 8 in Stripe
1trip out to Stripe, for the actual job
0trace on our side unless someone types a note
Verdict

The path makes sense only to someone who already knows the money lives in Stripe. A first-time support person would look for charges on the record, find none, and either guess or ask. The console cannot answer the question it was asked. It can only hand you over.

Proposed path (drawn: End State › The customer record, Money)

Type the email in Find, which sits on every page. Open the customer, then the Money tab: Stripe's charges, refunds and subscription events listed beside what we recorded, with the usual innocent pairs labelled (a plan change, a renewal and a partial month, two clubs on one card). Refund or hand off to Stripe (your question 7). Either way the console writes the record, with the reason, onto the customer's story.

~14 clicks and 1 trip out → 5 clicks, and a record written for you

Owner-named

2 · Activate a customer without charging them, for a while

The intent

A club gets a free month, or a coach gets a comp season. Done means: it is granted, everyone can see when it ends, it ends by itself, and later the customer's record shows who granted it and why.

Path as built: three different things, and none does the job whole
  1. Organizations, search, View, then the Billing & Access tab.
  2. Way 1, change the plan: pick the plan, give a reason, Review, Confirm. It is permanent. The screen calls it a "free comp". Nothing ends it, and nothing reminds anyone.
  3. Way 2, add an override: pick a kind, sections, an end date, a reason, Apply (no confirmation step). It ends by itself, but only for one section or a forced status, never a whole plan. It only works where a background switch is on, and nobody has confirmed that switch is on in production.
  4. Way 3, the "Comp Period" override: the screen itself warns that it grants nothing. It is a label.
  5. Way 4, Founding Season: set at checkout, with an end date of October 1, 2027. The desk says outright that nothing in the product turns a comp off by itself.
6–8clicks, depending on which way
Permanentthe only whole-plan option
Remembersomeone must undo it by hand
Unseenthe customer never sees an override's end date
Verdict

This is your hunch, confirmed in the code. "Free for a month" today means a plan change that someone has to remember to undo. The timed tool exists but only covers single sections, sits behind a switch nobody has confirmed, and applies without a confirmation step. A first-time person meets four controls that all sound like the answer, and one of them is a label that does nothing. On your question of one idea of "free for a while" or two: today it is four, and it should be one (see the End State).

Proposed path (drawn: End State › Give free time)

On the customer, press Give free time. A window asks what (the whole plan, or one section), which plan, until when, and why. The foot sentence says what happens: "Lakeshore Lynx gets Club free until November 30. It goes back to Tournament by itself." The record then wears a "Free until Nov 30" chip. The Today page lists every grant ending in the next 14 days, and the customer's own billing page says "Free until November 30". Founding Season becomes a named group of these same grants, not a second idea.

6–8 clicks, permanent → 5 clicks, ends by itself, seen by everyone

Owner-named

3 · Are the servers up, and how is the API handling errors?

The intent

You sit down and want one answer: is everything up, are the recent builds green, are errors rising, which route is failing, and what happened to the person who hit it. ("Dev servers" is your question 6. I have read it as the deployed sites and their builds plus the databases, not your own PC.)

Path as built: where each answer lives today
  1. Errors rising, which route: Observability. It reads only errors the app caught itself.
  2. What happened to the person: open the issue, read the email in a sample event, then retype it into Customer Users. It is not a link.
  3. Is the database healthy: the morning check on your PC, or the Supabase console.
  4. Are builds green: the morning check, or the Amplify console.
  5. Raw logs: the AWS log console.
  6. First-time alerts: the admin email inbox.
5–6places to look
1of them inside the console
On your PCthe morning check is a local file
Twoerror rates that measure different things
Verdict

The error triage is good: severity, route, count, affected orgs, a 14-day trend, and feedback joined to the error. What is missing is the first question, "is it up?". The best answer you have, the morning check, runs on your own PC and writes to a folder there, so the deployed console cannot read it. The other answers sit in three AWS and Supabase consoles. A support person who isn't you has no way to see any of it.

Proposed path (drawn: End State › Status)

A Status page under Health reads everything it can when you open it: production and the dev site answering, the last builds of each branch, the database's vital signs, errors in the last 24 hours against the week, and the scheduled jobs' last run. A failing route opens its issue, the issue names the person, and the person is a link to their record. Showing the morning check's own result here means changing that check so it also saves its summary where the console can read it. That is a change to shared automation, so it is your question 6, not something I would wire.

5–6 places → 1 page, then 3 clicks to the person

5 · A club's whole subscription story on one screen

The record holds the pieces across five tabs. Notes are on Support, the plan and grants on Billing & Access, sections on Entitlements, people on People & Tournaments, and the last 8 audit rows on Activity. Stripe's own history is not there at all. To tell the story you visit every tab, then Stripe, and keep the order in your head. The record opens on an editable identity form and a notes box, so the first thing a support person sees is a place to change something.

The record reads first: a money band (plan · status · next charge · free time), then Story, one dated list of everything that happened to this customer: plan changes, grants, Stripe charges and refunds, notes, staff actions, each with who and why. Edits sit behind the pencil.

6 · Change a plan mid-season

The plan form says plainly that it "changes access only, not billing". That is honest, and it means the person must then go to Stripe and change what the customer pays, by hand, or the two disagree. The bulk screen's plan change has drifted from the single-club one. It skips the follow-on steps the single change runs: starting a Club trial, removing a Founding comp, restoring a reactivated club. The same request gives a different result depending on which screen you used.

The plan window's foot sentence says both halves: what changes in access now, and what the customer pays (or "Stripe is not changed, so open it and change the price"), with the Stripe step recorded on the story. Bulk and single share one rule.

Added

7 · A customer who can't sign in

Customer Users, find them, then the row's Actions menu: Reset Password, Confirm Email, Revoke sessions, Edit, Ban. Reset Password sends a link. Neither the product nor the console records that the email was sent, so you can't tell whether it went or bounced. There is no way to see the errors this person hit, and no "send a sign-in link".

The person's record shows how they sign in, when they last did, the emails we sent them with each one's result, and the errors they hit. The recovery actions sit on that record.

Added

8 · A club whose own web address stopped working

Club Tier's own web address feature is built so that support never touches a club's address. The club connects it, a check runs every 15 minutes, and only the club owner is emailed. That is right for the club, and it leaves us blind. No console screen lists connected addresses, their state or their last check. The hosting allows 5 addresses and the request for 500 is still open with AWS. The product has a refusal for "we've hit our own ceiling" that nothing ever produces. When a club calls, today's path is the AWS console or a DNS lookup.

Club web addresses under Health: every connected address, its state, its last check and padlock date, and a quota readout ("3 of 5 used · request for 500 open"). It reads only, and it shows the same state the club sees.

Added

9 · Answer an in-app feedback message

Feedback lists messages with status, links to the club and to a matching error, and an escalate step. There is no reply. The sender's email is plain text, so you copy it into your own mail client, and the answer is recorded nowhere. Changing the status tells the customer nothing.

Open a message as a record, write the reply in a window that shows exactly what the customer will receive, and send. The reply is kept on the message and on the customer's story. Sending to a customer asks before it goes, per the standing rule.

Added

10 · Find a marketing resource to send a prospect

Pitch Deck Studio is a composer of about 12,300 pixels with 98 buttons: every slide, with up, down and remove arrows. A saved deck gets a private link, and the only route to a PDF is printing that page. To send a prospect a deck, you scroll the composer to find the saved decks.

Pitch decks opens on a list of the saved decks, each with Copy link. The composer sits behind each deck.

a long scroll → 2 clicks to a link

11 · "I never got the email"

Account emails (password resets, invites, notices) go out and are recorded nowhere. Campaign emails are recorded per person, but "sent" only means our mail service accepted the message. Nothing tells us whether it was delivered, bounced or marked as spam. Support cannot answer this question at all today.

A Sent email log for every email the product sends, shown on the person's record. Its "delivered or bounced" half needs the mail service to call us back, which is a new incoming route. It runs nothing on a schedule.

What the role walk found

The fit, in one line: the six roles draw good permission lines, but they do nothing to shape the menu. Every role meets the same seven groups, sorted by what the system stores. A role trims entries out of a menu built around data, not jobs. The recommendation (question 1) is a menu built from the jobs, with each role trimming it, and one rulebook that both the menu and the screens read.

Standing back: rules that cut across both walks

Each of these showed up at three or more stations. They become the rules every stage builds to.

R1

One place to find a customer

Every task starts with "who is this?", and today the answer depends on which list you guessed. Find sits on every page and searches people, clubs and Stripe numbers together. Nothing is capped without saying so.

R2

A customer record reads first, and edits whole

The record opens on editable forms today. It should open on the story, with changes behind the pencil, like every client screen since the portal ruling.

R3

A change to a live customer confirms in one sentence

The plan change confirms. A grant, a section switch and an add-on switch don't. Every change to a live customer gets a window whose foot sentence says what changes and what doesn't ("Stripe is not changed").

R4

Every change records who and why

The reason gets its own place in the audit log and shows on the customer's story. Today some writes record a reason inside the stored value, and some record none.

R5

Undo is "put it back", from the story

A plan, a limit, a grant or a section change shows its previous value on the story, with Put it back (which asks for a reason too). There is no undo for anything that reached Stripe or a customer's inbox (question 2).

R6

A section you may only read shows as a record, without controls

Today a read-only role sees locked forms. It should see the same record without the pencil, and the menu marks the entry. Absent beats disabled.

R7

The money truth is shown, never remembered

Whatever happened in Stripe is shown beside our record, so nobody keeps it in their head between two windows.

R8

Plain words, the product's look

No all-caps mono menu, no "9999 for unlimited", no raw keys like "module_families". The console takes the same kit and Warm / Dark setting as every client screen (see the formatting check on the End State).
