“is there a better way to show this step, this feels weird to me, like these are predetermined groups but they actually are just suggestions.”

Why it reads that way

  • It has the shape of a list you choose from. Nine rows, a box on each, column headings, and a total at the foot. Nothing on the screen says the nine are examples.
  • “Teams it holds” is empty on all nine rows. It is a heading over nothing until you tick.
  • Competitive and Recreational say “Ages 18 and up”. Those are levels of play, and the app decided they are adult. That is the kind of grouping you asked us to stop making.
  • Naming your own comes last. Add a division sits after all nine. In your screenshot it is half under the window’s foot.
  • Your words at round 2’s build were “we can display those as examples”. The build made them the list itself.

Recommended

  • The list holds only this tournament’s divisions. It starts empty, every row in it is real, and so is the count at its foot.
  • The nine common divisions become suggestions under the name field (Ask 1). Pressing “+ U11” adds U11 to the list, and the button leaves the row.
  • You name your own in the same place (Ask 3). Type “U11 Girls” and press Add or Enter. The field and the suggestions sit above the list, so they never move as it grows.
  • A division leaves from its own fields (Ask 2). “Remove this division” ends them, under its arrow at a desk and in its window on a phone. Rows carry no box.
  • Open, Competitive and Recreational arrive as “Any age” (Ask 4). A “U” name keeps “Up to age 11”, because the name says it.

Why A

  • A box on a row says “choose”. When every row is in, three ticked boxes say nothing, and the step starts to look like a list to choose from again.
  • B’s strength is the mis-tap. Press + U17 by mistake and B undoes it with one untick. A takes two taps at a desk (the arrow, then Remove) and three on a phone (the row, its window, Remove). Nothing is saved until Create the draft, so neither loses anything, though a name you typed and then removed has to be typed again.
  • Both follow a written rule. B is the form table as the standard writes it today: a tick says in or out. A needs one new sentence in the standard (under “What else changes”), and follows the rare-door ruling: a record’s rare door ends its body.
Asks 3 and 4

Naming your own, and what the three adult words mean

Ask 3 · The name field (recommended) or today’s window

  • Today: Add a division opens New division over the setup window, with the name, ages, teams and pools, and an Add division button. Round 2 drew it to answer “what is that workflow like?” when the app sorted divisions under Youth and Adult and needed the ages to do it.
  • Drawn: the headings are gone, so a division needs only its name to land. A name and Enter puts it in the list; its arrow or its window holds the rest, the same as for a suggestion. The field sits above the list, with the suggestions under it, and the list ends with its total.
  • The cost: a division’s ages come from its name until you open it, so “Varsity” arrives as Any age.

Ask 4 · Open, Competitive and Recreational

  • Today they arrive as “Ages 18 and up”. A U13 Competitive or a Recreational coed league is not adult, and the app does not know which one you mean.
  • Drawn: “Any age”, the same as any name that does not start with an age group. Open the division to set ages if you mean them.
  • A “U” name keeps its age. U11 means up to age 11 in its own words, so the app is reading the name, not guessing.
History

Round 1 drew chips, and the review replaced them

  • What round 1 drew (2026-10-11): the nine as chips you toggled on and off, above a list of cards, each with an ✕ and a pencil. The design review replaced it with today’s ticked list for two reasons: chips were a new pattern, and the Venues step in the same window ticks rows. You ruled with the review.
  • The Venues reason does not carry over. The Venues step ticks venues your past events used, which are real records. The Divisions step ticks names that do not exist yet, which is the feeling you named. It is the same control with a different meaning.
  • The new-pattern reason is weaker than it was. The club scheduler now draws the same pill (its day buttons, built 2026-10-11), and the coaches portal has both the pill and the drop-down. Ask 1 chooses between two shapes the product has.
  • What this round keeps from the review: no toggles (a suggestion adds and leaves; it is never on or off), no ✕ on a row, no pencil, the total in the table’s closing row.
