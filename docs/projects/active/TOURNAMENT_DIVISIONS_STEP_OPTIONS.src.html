Recommended

  1. The step becomes a list you tick. It uses the house’s form table, the one the redesign shares for create forms; the Venues step ticks venues the same way. The nine common divisions are its rows under Youth and Adult. Ticking the Youth heading ticks all six, which is today’s “Start from: Youth” in one tap.
  2. Nothing is ticked to start. A one-division event is one tick, and nobody unticks divisions we guessed.
  3. The count is the table’s closing row at a desk and the frame’s band on a phone: “3 divisions · 24 team spots”.
  4. On a phone, a division reads as one line and opens its own window (your round 2). The line under the name says “8 teams · up to age 11”. Tapping the division opens a window with its fields, the same kind of window “A new venue” opens in this setup window. At a desk, Teams it holds sits in the row and the rest opens under the division’s arrow.
  5. Add a division asks for the whole division, in that same window. It asks the name, ages, teams and pools, and the ages decide the heading: an oldest age under 18 sits under Youth in age order, a youngest age of 18 or more sits under Adult, and anything else sits under Other. Change the ages later and it moves when its window closes.
  6. Not keyed on the plan. The free tier review decides what a free tournament is. This start gives a small event its one division without deciding that here.

Why not one division for a free tournament

  • The free tier review owns that question. Its first question is “Exhibition only, or small? How small (teams, divisions, days)?”, and it is asked after that review’s findings. A plan-keyed start would answer it here, quietly.
  • How many divisions is a fact about the event, not the bill. A paying club running a one-division showcase wants the same short start. A free organizer running three age groups is allowed today.
  • A already gives every small event its one division in one tick. If the review rules free = Exhibition only, nothing on this step changes.

Ask 3 · The desk: in place, or the phone’s window?

Recommended: keep it in place at a desk. The desk has the room, the table stays in view while you change a division, and this is the form table’s written way to open a row. The window is the phone’s answer because the phone’s problem is room. The cost is two ways to change a division, one per width, so the help says “tap a division” for the phone and “its arrow” for the desk. The other answer is one way everywhere: at a desk the row opens the window drawn below, at 640 instead of full screen.

The first draft drew chips

Round 1 added divisions with toggle chips above a separate list, each row with a pencil and a remove ×. The design review turned that down: chips that add many are a new pattern, a remove on every row breaks “delete is never a row action”, and the window would then pick divisions one way and venues another. Chips stay as Ask 2’s other answer.

Adding a division · round 2

Add a division asks for the whole division, and its ages decide its heading

What happens today, and in round 1’s drawing

  • The field asks a name and nothing else. The division arrives holding 8 teams, with ages read from the name only when the name is a bare “U12” or “12U”.
  • Anything else (“U12 Girls”, “Varsity”) arrives with no ages at all, and you open it to add them. Round 1 put it under a “Your own” heading, so a U12 sat apart from U11 and U13.

Drawn

  1. Add a division opens the division window, empty, with the same fields as the window above. Typing a name like “U12” fills Oldest with 12 while Oldest is untouched.
  2. Add division puts it in the list, ticked, under the heading its ages decide. An oldest age under 18 puts it under Youth, in age order, so U12 Girls lands between U11 and U13. A youngest age of 18 or more puts it under Adult. With no ages it goes under Other.
  3. It follows its ages. Change them later and, when its window (or its arrow at a desk) closes, it moves under the heading they decide, with the same wash. It never moves while you are typing in it.
  4. A name already listed stops with today’s words: “U11” is listed twice. Give each division its own name.

Formatting check

What the reviews changed

Round 1 · the design review, 2026-10-11
  1. Chips and a separate list became one ticked list (the form table). Chips that add many were a new pattern, and the Venues step in the same window already ticks rows.
  2. The remove × on every row is gone. Delete is never a row action (the table standard). Unticking leaves a division out.
  3. The pencil became the form table’s down arrow. The pencil ↔ ✓ standard is for a saved record. Nothing here saves until Create.
  4. The count moved into the table’s closing row. Its words match the preview beside the window (“Divisions”, “Team Spots”), and on a phone it is the frame’s band.
  5. The review step’s line is corrected. It reads “3 divisions” over “U11 · U13 · U15”; the first draft added “8 teams each”, which it does not say.
  6. The spelling ask is scoped to tournament divisions. Club team names say “13U AAA”, so “U12 everywhere” was not true of the product.
  7. Sizes follow the kit. The Add button is 40px, the frame’s radius and lines are the form table’s, and the cell input is New allocation’s 32px figure field.
Round 2 · your direction, and what it changed
  1. The phone row carries its summary under the name (“8 teams · up to age 11”), and the figure at the row’s end is gone. The form table lets a form leave that figure out.
  2. On a phone a division opens its own window, full screen with ← at the top left. You asked for a sheet from the bottom; a form you type into covers the screen on a phone (the drawer-layers ruling), and a half-height sheet is the shape for a menu.
  3. Add a division is a button that opens the same window, empty, and the ages decide the heading.
  4. The phone window’s head is corrected: ← at the top left and no ×, as today’s window has. Round 1 drew an × at the top right.
Round 2 · the design review
  1. It confirmed full screen, not a half sheet, and named a second source: the table standard’s §3.10.8 says a row that opens a form opens it full screen. It also confirmed the desk’s add window matches “A new venue”, and the ← head with no ×.
  2. “An exception” became “two written rules that disagree” (Ask 6). The form table’s phone sentence says in place; §3.10.8 says full screen. Built, it is an option of the one shared form table, never markup beside it.
  3. A heading now follows its ages. The first round-2 draft said an added division never moves, which would leave a U12 under Youth after its ages became 20 and over. It now moves when its window or arrow closes, with the same wash.
  4. The edit window lost its Done. It did what ← does. ← holds the window when the name is cleared, so a nameless division is never dropped silently.
  5. One phrase for ages: “Up to age 11” at a desk and “8 teams · up to age 11” on a phone. The heading hint now covers every case (“anything else is Other”), and the › is the house row chevron (16px, tertiary ink, 0.8rem from the edge).
  6. Not taken: a 22px tick box like the Venues step’s rows. The form table’s own box is 18px everywhere it is used; changing it is a change to the shared table, New allocation included, and not part of this ask.
