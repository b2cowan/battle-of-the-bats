T1

When do US clubs come in?

What you need to know

  • On Oct 23 you ruled the launch Canada only. US subscriptions were moved to "expansion".
  • Selling a subscription to a US club is the easy case. They pay 0% Canadian tax, as long as we keep proof they're American (T7). US state sales tax only starts once sales into one state pass about US$100,000 a year.
  • US clubs taking online payments is the hard case. It needs US payout accounts in US dollars, a decision on who files the US year-end payment forms (1099-K), and a professional opinion on state "marketplace" rules.
  • The address step at checkout is the same work for either country. Adding US states later means rebuilding that step.
  • US clubs would pay in Canadian dollars at first; their card converts. US-dollar prices are a separate later decision.
Example · how far away US sales tax is

At about US$0.72 per Canadian dollar, US$100,000 in one state is roughly 30 clubs on Club · Association ($3,790/yr), or 350 clubs on Tournament Plus annual ($390/yr), all in the same state.

Your choice

Why A: it costs almost nothing extra now, and it opens a market where no US tax is owed at our size. The risky US parts stay out until they get their own review.
T2

The online-payment fee: tax on top, or inside?

What you need to know

  • On Oct 26 you decided no tax on the fee, because FieldLogicHQ was "pre-revenue and below the $30,000 threshold". That reason is gone now that you're registered.
  • Whether the fee is taxable at all is still open. It could count as an exempt financial service or a taxable platform service, and CRA hasn't addressed a fee like ours. The lawyer and accountant settle it. Plan as if it's taxable; if it turns out exempt, nothing changes for customers.
  • Today's fee: card 3.5% + $1.50, bank debit 2.5% + $1.00. US clubs would pay 0% tax on it.
  • A club registered for GST/HST gets tax it pays back as a credit. Most small non-profit clubs aren't registered, so for them it's a real cost.

T3

Who is the fee legally charged to?

What you need to know

  • Each club picks "family pays" (the default, where the fee appears as its own line at the family's checkout) or "club pays" (it comes out of the club's money).
  • Tax follows the person we charge. If our fee is a charge to the club, the club's address decides the tax every time. If it's a charge to the family, each family's location decides it.
  • Charging families would mean finding out where every family is, and the tax would change from one payment to the next.
  • This choice is written into the terms. What families see on screen can stay the same.

T5

Let Stripe work out the tax, or set the rates ourselves?

What you need to know

  • Stripe Tax reads the buyer's address, picks the right GST, HST or provincial rate, shows it at checkout, and warns you when a province's or US state's threshold is coming. It doesn't register you anywhere; you still do that.
  • It costs 0.5% of sales where you're registered. Stripe lists that price in US dollars; Canadian pricing isn't confirmed.
  • Setting rates ourselves means entering each province's rate in Stripe and having our code pick the rate from the province the buyer chose. It's free, but rate changes (Nova Scotia's in 2025), provincial thresholds and PST/QST rules all become ours to track.
  • Neither option handles tax on the payment fee. That needs its own monthly statement either way.

T7

What proof do we keep before charging a US club 0%?

What you need to know

  • To charge 0%, CRA expects you to be satisfied that the buyer lives outside Canada and isn't registered for GST/HST, and to keep the evidence. If the evidence doesn't hold up, the tax is yours to pay.
  • CRA's guidance accepts a buyer's own statement plus a full address, backed by one independent sign such as the card's country.
  • A club counts as Canadian if the people running it live in Canada, even with a US mailing address.
  • A US address alone, or the visitor's internet location, isn't enough.
The proposed rule: all three must line up

1. A US street address (not a PO box only).
2. A required tick-box: "Our organization is run from outside Canada and is not registered for Canadian GST/HST." (A coach buying for themself ticks "I live outside Canada.")
3. A card issued outside Canada, or a payout account Stripe verified in the US.
