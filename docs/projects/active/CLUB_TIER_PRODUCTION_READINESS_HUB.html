The club shell

Stage 1

Board roles cannot be assigned from the screen; a volunteer who coaches elsewhere cannot be invited; there is no way to buy Club even when the gate opens; coming back after cancelling restores nothing.

Rep teams & the coach bridge

Stage 2

Club teams have no season rollover and next season cannot exist while this one runs; adopting a standalone coach's portal into the club orphans most of that team's data. The franchise contract itself holds.

Club money

Stage 3

A treasurer cannot finish the allocation loop; every board figure has a confirmed way to be wrong; money moves on one click with no confirmation, no reversal and a race. No build gate guards any club number.

The public face

Stage 4

A rep-only club's page is an empty tournament page: teams are invisible, there is no teams index, and unticking "Listed on /discover" takes down the whole site.

Families

Stage 5

A merge undoes itself, a changed guardian email strands the child, the family page's money disagrees with the Money hub, and the export claims "everything" while holding a fraction.

Venues, scheduling, permits

Stage 6 · permits Stage 10

Three venue books that never check each other, so a rep practice, a league game and a tournament game can share a diamond unwarned. No club calendar. The admin cannot add a game — by ruling.

Tournaments in a club

Stage 7

A club's own team enters its own event in two manual steps; tournament fees never reach the books; a "first tournament setup" banner shows forever for a club that runs none.

Tests, help, demo, design

Stage 8

The club fixture has an owner and an admin and nothing else; the June walk artifacts are the only record; the rep-teams cluster predates the admin design system; marketing promises features that do not exist.

House league

Stage 9 · last

Five of the six June trust defects are still open: messages that email nobody and report success, the vanishing season, dishonest schedules, no payment loop, unescaped emails. Its standalone plan already exists (League Plus).

Product-manager brief

Club Tier Production Readiness

What it is

A staged programme that takes the Club plan from "built but never tested" to "a rep club can buy it, set it up, staff it, run a season on it and trust the numbers it shows the board." Each part of the offer is tackled, walked and signed off one at a time, in the order a real club meets them. House league is deliberately last so Club can be released to rep clubs that run no house league.

Why it matters

  • Club is the top of the pricing ladder and has never been validated against one real club. Every June pricing decision is still a hypothesis.
  • The club-admin side has been effectively static since June while the Coaches Portal got three months of intensive work. The two halves now disagree about money, seasons, venues and who can do what.
  • The June audit of the club president's journey found the oversight layer "part broken and part unguarded". Today's re-verification: of its 50 findings, 7 fixed, 6 partly, 4 superseded, 33 still open. Of the 27 staff-admin findings, 19 still open.
  • Every coach in a club is on a free Premium portal until September 2027. A club pays $219 a month only for what the club side gives it. That is exactly the side that has never been QA'd.

What the club sees and does differently when this is done

Buying and setting up. A club owner is provisioned (or buys) onto Club or Club · Association, sees the right price and trial, is walked through a club-shaped setup, and lands on a hub that leads with the club's own programs. Cancelling and coming back restores everything.

Staffing the board. The owner invites an admin who can see the club's modules, a treasurer and a registrar, from the Members screen, with roles that mean what the screen says. A volunteer who also coaches elsewhere can be invited.

Teams and coaches. The owner creates teams and program years, names coaches, and each coach lands in a populated Premium portal at no per-team charge. Starting and closing a season means the same thing on both sides. The president gets one oversight view of every team.

Money the board can repeat. The treasurer plans a budget for the club's year, allocates to teams, approves coach requests with a confirmation and a way back, and reads one board summary whose every figure is true by construction and guarded by a build check. Team money and club money are two clearly separated things; the club reads the coach's records rather than keeping a shadow copy.

A public face that leads with the rep program. Families find the teams, open tryouts and the club's colours. "Tryouts are open" takes them to the tryout. Unlisting from the directory no longer takes down the website.

Families. Household records read the same money the Money hub does, a changed guardian email follows the child, a merge stays merged, and the export says what it holds.

One venue book. Rep, house-league and tournament games read the same venues, and a diamond double-booked across modules is flagged.

Tested and documented. A Club fixture and browser suite, admin screens in the layout sweep, help that describes today's screens, marketing that promises only what exists.

Who benefits

Club owners and presidents (the buyer), treasurers, registrars and admins (the roles the plan sells but the screen cannot assign today), coaches in a club (their portal stays; the club side stops contradicting it), and families. Standalone Premium coaches are unaffected.

Trade-offs

  • Fix the club side to read the coach's records rather than rebuilding both money models — the coach side has three months of hardening and build gates; the club's copy has neither.
  • Permits and a club-wide calendar are new features, not readiness items. They sit in Stage 6 behind an explicit decision on whether they gate the first release.
  • Families P3 is not release-gating; its four serious defects are.
  • House league last, and no new plan needs inventing — League Plus already is the standalone plan.

Success criteria

  1. One real rep club (or the fixture standing in) completes the nine-point exit definition with the owner watching; every stage's QA walk is on the ledger as passed.
  2. Every board-facing figure has a build gate, and the same number cannot be computed two ways on two screens.
  3. Every June audit finding routed to Club is fixed or explicitly superseded — none open.
  4. Club and Club · Association can be bought in-product, the gate flip is one decision away, and the marketing surfaces promise only what exists.
Full plan — condensed

Ten stages, one exit definition

The full ledger lives in the plan file

CLUB_TIER_PRODUCTION_READINESS_PLAN.md carries every finding with its code anchor (sections 4A–4I), the eleven rulings (§5), sequencing (§7), build gates (§8), the release checklist (§9) and the June-audit register (§11). This tab is the reading version.

Release-ready for a rep club means

  1. Buy or be provisioned onto Club with the right price, trial and comp state; cancel and come back with everything restored.
  2. Staff the board — owner, an admin who sees the modules, a treasurer, a registrar — including volunteers who coach elsewhere.
  3. Create teams and program years, name coaches, every coach lands in a populated Premium portal; a season starts and closes the same way on both sides.
  4. Plan a budget for a club year, allocate, receive requests, mark money received, and read one board summary that is true by construction and guarded.
  5. Show a public face that leads with the rep program.
  6. Know its families — same money as the Money hub, merges that hold, emails that follow the child.
  7. Keep one venue book every module reads.
  8. Coexist with tournaments — the club's own team enters its own event, the mirror keeps the coach's schedule true.
  9. Be tested: fixture, browser suite, layout sweep, current help, truthful marketing.
  10. Hold its permits (ruled in 2026-09-25): the club records the field, diamond or ice permits it holds, every module's scheduling reads them, and a permit's cost sits in the budget. Stage 10, and the release waits for it.

Go/no-go on the nine points

A Club browser suite on the fixture; admin club routes in the layout sweep; every club help article re-read; a demo decision; pricing and marketing drift check; the gate flip runbook and checkout verified; migrations on prod in order with functions verified; support posture and rollback runbook; the release and cohort shape logged.

Stage 9 — House league (its own release track)

Its own plan when it opens · D11 · may run alongside Stage 10

The trust-plan defects re-verified and closed; the standalone-plan coupling check; the ledger seam under D1's pattern; venues under Stage 6's book; confirms on one-click season changes; League Plus un-parked as the standalone plan.

Stage 10 — Permits & bookings (ruled in 2026-09-25; the release waits for it)

New feature · its own plan, PM brief and mockup session · walk §K

A permit is a record the club holds: venue, date range, weekly slots, cost and payee, the issuer's reference and document, and who it is for. Scheduling reads it — the clash check gains "is this slot inside a permit we hold?", the league generator and club calendar can offer permitted slots, a coach's event at a club venue shows the permit it sits in. Money reads it — a permit's cost can be cited by a budget line and allocated to teams. A per-permit view of used vs unused slots. Out of the first cut: requesting or paying for permits through the platform, municipal integrations, public booking.

Sequencing

Stage 1 before everything — no other walk is honest while admins and treasurers are tournament-only users. Stage 2 before 3 — the allocation loop needs teams, program years and the season rule. Stages 4, 5, 6, 7 are independent of each other and of 3. Stage 10 needs Stage 6's venue book and Stage 3's allocations, and the release waits for it, so Stage 8's go/no-go comes after Stages 3, 6 and 10 at the earliest. Stage 9 is its own release track.

Findings referenced on the map

A01 — Anyone but the owner is a tournament-only user

Admin and staff have no module permissions by default; the hub decides "tournament-only" from the member, not the plan, and redirects them into tournaments or an owner-only setup page that bounces them back. Stage 1, ruling D8.

A02 — The board cannot be staffed from the screen

Treasurer, registrar and league admin exist in the product but not in the invite or role pickers; saving another role quietly turns it into staff; help still tells owners to pick Treasurer. Stage 1.

A03 — A coach who coaches elsewhere cannot join a club's board

The membership policy counts the plumbing rows every coaching-staff invite writes, so the invite, the acceptance and a reinstatement all refuse. Contradicts the July account-model decision. Stage 1.

A05 — No way to buy Club

The billing shelf only opens an article; only Tournament Plus can be upgraded to; Club · Association appears on no customer surface; the team-cap error tells a club to upgrade with nowhere to go. Stage 1, ruling D7.

A07 — Coming back after cancelling restores nothing

Resubscribing restores only a plan-downgrade retention; the site stays hidden, tournaments stay archived, and the retention job later emails a paying customer that their window expired. Stage 1.

A08 — The Founding Season comp leaks onto Club

Every signup gets the comp; moving the org to Club does not revoke it; the billing page then says Club is free through 2027 beside a $219 card. Stage 1, ruling D6.

A09 — "Listed on /discover" is the master switch for the whole site

The checkbox writes the public flag, which hides the org home, the league pages and every link back to the org. The real directory flag has no control. Stage 1.

B01 — A new head coach must be invited as staff or admin

No Coach role exists to invite; the Coaches page assigns only existing active members; no email tells the coach they were assigned; between seasons a coach cannot be added at all. Stage 2, ruling D10.

B03 — Club teams have no season rollover

"Start next season" is standalone-only; the club's new program year is blank; a new year is blocked while one is active, so next season's tryouts cannot open until this season is marked complete — which locks every coach out. Stage 2.

B04 — Adopting a standalone portal orphans the team

The transfer routine was written when the team had 17 tables and never revised; it leaves behind the coach's access record and roughly 48 tables added since. The customer UI calls the rest "platform-assisted". Stage 2.

Team cards answer roster, pending and families only; three different roster-count rules and two record rules across the club screens. Stage 2.
