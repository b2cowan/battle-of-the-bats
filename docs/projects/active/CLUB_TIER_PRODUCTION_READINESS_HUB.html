Ask 1 · When does a club team's season change, and who presses it?

The plan recommends two things for club seasons. The first is that the club admin gets the standalone coach's Start next season. The second is that a Draft year may run beside the Active one, so next season's tryouts can happen while this season is still going. The first half fits everything built since August. The second half does not do what it hopes, and the code shows why. Both shapes are drawn small below; every specimen after this one is drawn to shape (a).

Findings behind this ask:

What the code says (read 2026-09-28)

  1. The coach's portal has two season states, and it always shows the newest live year. A team is live while it holds a Draft or an Active year. Draft and Active are the same thing to the portal. When a team holds two, the portal shows the newer one. lib/coach-season-view.ts:14-17, 49-82 · lib/db.ts:4419-4431 (getActiveRepProgramYear: newest of draft|active)
  2. All twelve of the coach's tryout tools act on that one year. They cover sessions, candidates, evaluators, the scoreboard, decisions, accept, the report, the rubric and the rest. None takes a year. app/api/coaches/[orgSlug]/teams/[teamId]/tryout-*/route.ts — each resolves getActiveRepProgramYear(teamId)
  3. A standalone team runs next year's tryout after the roll. Start next season creates the new season already live, with tryouts closed, and closes the old one. The portal's own help tells the coach to do it in that order: “Next year's tryout needs a season to live in. If you run tryouts in the summer for the autumn, start the new season first — everything else follows from it.” lib/rep-season-rollover.ts:183-192, 422 · lib/help-content/coaches.tsx:1401-1402 · COACH_SEASON_CLOSE_AND_ARCHIVE_PLAN.md §5 risk 2 (“Start next season is the first real step of the year”)
  4. The two lines that looked contradictory agree. CLAUDE.md says next year's tryout happens after the last game. It does not say before the season closes, and the roll is what closes it. The help line “turn the tryout you just ran into the start of their season” is about each new player's development starting point. It says nothing about when the roll happens. CLAUDE.md (season ruling) · lib/help-content/coaches.tsx:381
  5. One help line is stale. It says a finished season's tryout “stays readable under Tryouts … the whole period between seasons”. Since 2026-08-18 a team between seasons has one page, and Tryouts is not on it. The Stage 2 build's help pass carries this line. lib/help-content/coaches.tsx:4733 · finding S2-03
  6. The club side today: a new year is refused while any year is Active. The year that is created is a blank Draft, with the staff copied and nothing else. Drafts aren't counted, so two can pile up. app/api/admin/rep-teams/teams/[teamId]/program-years/route.ts:64-100

The two shapes, side by side

(a) One model for both kinds of team Recommended

The club admin holds the three doors a standalone head coach holds: Start next season, Close the season and Reopen. They work the same way, carry the same five things, and give the same warning about unsettled money, which never blocks. Tryouts run on the working season.

  • Aug 16Last game. The 2026 Season stays live while the coach settles money, gives awards, collects documents and emails families.
  • Sep 21The club presses Start the 2027 Season. 2026 becomes a record. The roster, budget plan, fee plan, opening balance and each player's history come along.
  • Sep 21 →Tryouts open on 2027. The coach runs them in the portal, and the club sees the same list.
  • OctoberAccepted players join the 2027 roster.

What it costs: a club whose tryouts come before its last game has to choose. It can start the new season early, and the games the old season still has left can then no longer be scored. Or it can hold the tryout until after the last game. A standalone team faces the same choice today. If that is a real problem for clubs, it is a real problem for standalone coaches too, and the fix should be one change for both kinds of team, not a separate club model.

(b) The plan's draft year beside the live one

The club opens next season as a Draft while this season is Active and runs next season's tryout on the Draft. Draft would be redefined as a “prep” state that the portal treats as not yet live.

  • July2026 is Active, with games still to play.
  • JulyThe club creates 2027 as a Draft and opens tryouts on it.
  • Portal today2027 is now the newest live year, so the coach's whole portal moves to it. The roster, schedule and money read an empty 2027, and the 2026 games still to play have nowhere to go. 2026 isn't closed either, so it isn't on the closed-season page.
  • Draft = prepThe portal stays on 2026, so the coach can't see the 2027 tryout at all. Reaching it needs a year picker in the coach's tryout tools, which the working-season rule forbids and its build guard exists to stop.

Recommended: option B, one Accounting page with tabs. Ruled as recommended, 2026-09-30.

  1. Accounting is one book, not a set of separate jobs. Every one of its pages reads and writes the same club money. Recording an allocation received writes a ledger line, approving a request writes one, and the budget allocates to the teams. A treasurer crosses between them in one sitting. The coach's Money is one page with tabs for exactly that reason; its own design notes put it as "a panel here is a tab, not a page".
  2. The phone. A treasurer approves a request or records an e-Transfer from her phone. In A she reaches a sibling page only through Accounting's first page. In B every page is one tap from every other.
  3. One idea, one shape. The coach's Ledger took the word "Ledger" from the club's Accounting on purpose, so that a treasurer who is also a coach meets one word for one thing on both sides. The frame around the word should match too.
  4. The rail stays short and still. One row that never opens, with its count, instead of five or six rows that appear and disappear as you move.

What it costs. Accounting becomes the only admin program with tabs, which is drift unless the reason is written down. Proposed rule for the formatting standard (the Stage 8 nav review): a program whose pages are views of one body of records is one page with tabs; a program whose pages are separate jobs lists them in the rail. The coaches portal already follows it without saying so: Schedule, Roster and Practice plans are rail entries, while Money and Insights are one page each, with tabs. In the admin, Rep Teams (teams, assistant coaches, documents, past seasons) stays in the rail, and no other program changes.

When A would be right. At a club where different people keep the books and run the team billing, each person would live on their own page, and A reads better. The clubs this tier is built for have one treasurer doing both.

Product-manager brief

Club Tier Production Readiness

What it is

A staged programme that takes the Club plan from "built but never tested" to "a rep club can buy it, set it up, staff it, run a season on it and trust the numbers it shows the board." Each part of the offer is tackled, walked and signed off one at a time, in the order a real club meets them. House league is deliberately last so Club can be released to rep clubs that run no house league.

Why it matters

  • Club is the top of the pricing ladder and has never been validated against one real club. Every June pricing decision is still a hypothesis.
  • The club-admin side has been effectively static since June while the Coaches Portal got three months of intensive work. The two halves now disagree about money, seasons, venues and who can do what.
  • The June audit of the club president's journey found the oversight layer "part broken and part unguarded". Today's re-verification: of its 50 findings, 7 fixed, 6 partly, 4 superseded, 33 still open. Of the 27 staff-admin findings, 19 still open.
  • Every coach in a club is on a free Premium portal until September 2027. A club pays $219 a month only for what the club side gives it. That is exactly the side that has never been QA'd.

What the club sees and does differently when this is done

Buying and setting up. A club owner is provisioned (or buys) onto Club or Club · Association, sees the right price and trial, is walked through a club-shaped setup, and lands on a hub that leads with the club's own programs. Cancelling and coming back restores everything.

Staffing the board. The owner invites an admin who can see the club's modules, a treasurer and a registrar, from the Members screen, with roles that mean what the screen says. A volunteer who also coaches elsewhere can be invited.

Teams and coaches. The owner creates teams and program years, names coaches, and each coach lands in a populated Premium portal at no per-team charge. Starting and closing a season means the same thing on both sides. The president gets one oversight view of every team.

Money the board can repeat. The treasurer plans a budget for the club's year, allocates to teams, approves coach requests with a confirmation and a way back, and reads one board summary whose every figure is true by construction and guarded by a build check. Team money and club money are two clearly separated things; the club reads the coach's records rather than keeping a shadow copy.

A public face that leads with the rep program. Families find the teams, open tryouts and the club's colours. "Tryouts are open" takes them to the tryout. Unlisting from the directory no longer takes down the website.

Families. Household records read the same money the Money hub does, a changed guardian email follows the child, a merge stays merged, and the export says what it holds.

One venue book. Rep, house-league and tournament games read the same venues, and a diamond double-booked across modules is flagged.

Tested and documented. A Club fixture and browser suite, admin screens in the layout sweep, help that describes today's screens, marketing that promises only what exists.

Who benefits

Club owners and presidents (the buyer), treasurers, registrars and admins (the roles the plan sells but the screen cannot assign today), coaches in a club (their portal stays; the club side stops contradicting it), and families. Standalone Premium coaches are unaffected.

Trade-offs

  • Fix the club side to read the coach's records rather than rebuilding both money models — the coach side has three months of hardening and build gates; the club's copy has neither.
  • Permits and a club-wide calendar are new features, not readiness items. They sit in Stage 6 behind an explicit decision on whether they gate the first release.
  • Families P3 is not release-gating; its four serious defects are.
  • House league last, and no new plan needs inventing — League Plus already is the standalone plan.

Success criteria

  1. One real rep club (or the fixture standing in) completes the nine-point exit definition with the owner watching; every stage's QA walk is on the ledger as passed.
  2. Every board-facing figure has a build gate, and the same number cannot be computed two ways on two screens.
  3. Every June audit finding routed to Club is fixed or explicitly superseded — none open.
  4. Club and Club · Association can be bought in-product, the gate flip is one decision away, and the marketing surfaces promise only what exists.
