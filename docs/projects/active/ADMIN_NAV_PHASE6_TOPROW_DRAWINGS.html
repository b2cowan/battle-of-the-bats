n1Why the “?” sits in the strip (the reason the drawing rests on)

The 2026-07-31 boundary held the strip to leave-this-place doors and said a “?” is not an exit. The owner reversed it on purpose for the Coaches Portal on 2026-11-12 (4b R3, revised). The reason recorded in the plan is that most pages have no heading to hang a “?” on: tabbed pages open on tiles or lists, Email families on a paragraph, and every gate and first load has none. Anchoring it to the page would give a different place on every page. The strip is the one place that is the same on every page. Phase 5 F3 added the phone half: help in the fixed top chrome is the computer’s arrangement exactly. The tournament and club follow for the 11-12 entry’s reason, one grammar on all three portals. This reason is in the plan but not in the design log; the build writes it there.

n2What the “?” opens, everywhere

One panel, already shared by the Coaches Portal and the tournament admin: it slides in from the right (420px), headed “Help · on this page” and the page’s name, with the page’s own sections, an “I want to…” list where the page has one, and “Open the full guide” at its foot. The account menu’s Help row stays the full guide in a new tab. A page with no help topic shows no “?” rather than a “?” that opens an empty panel. That is the Coaches Portal’s rule today (the player page), and the drawing keeps it for all three portals.

n3No topic, no “?”

The Coaches Portal holds the “?”’s width for a moment while the page loads (so the bell does not slide), then gives it back if the page has no topic. The phone bar does the same. The tournament admin and the club take the same behaviour. A “?” that opens an empty panel is a door that dead-ends; the full guide stays one tap away in the account menu (computer) and the More sheet (phone).

n4The account menu’s Help row

Unchanged in all three: it opens the full guide in a new tab, at the program you are in (club: House league, Rep teams, Accounting or Organization; tournament: the tournaments guide). So every screen keeps two help doors with two jobs: the “?” for this page, the menu row for the whole guide. That is the Coaches Portal’s pair today.

The asks (first option = the recommendation)

T1The “?” position in the strip

  1. (A) Recommended: between the bell and the account button, in all three portals. That is where the Coaches Portal’s already sits (walked §517 and §519). The admin order becomes View site · bell · “?” · account · Workspaces. It costs 40px; at 1280 the tournament keeps 408px of free room, the club 472, the Coaches Portal 362, so nothing gives way at 1280, 1366 or 1440 on any page measured. If a page ever runs short, the shipped order holds: the facts’ quiet words go first, then the dates; the page’s name, the chip, the “?” and the doors never do. On a phone the “?” is the bar’s last control in all three, with the admin’s View site globe just left of it. In the demos the “?” shows (the Coaches Portal’s already does): it opens in place and is no way out of the demo. On a cancelled account’s one page there is none, as there is no Help today.
  2. (B) First of the doors, before View site. That would reorder the Coaches Portal strip the owner has already walked.
  3. (C) The far right corner. The corner belongs to the Workspaces pill for people with more than one place and to the account button for everyone else, so the “?” would move by person.

T2The page’s buttons: one rule for three portals

  1. (A) Recommended: the buttons stay in the page under the strip; a page with no buttons has no row. This is the Coaches Portal’s 4b ruling, so all three end on the same answer. On a tabbed page the band holds the tabs only and the buttons sit below it (4b R1/R2). Nothing to build for the buttons themselves: once the “?” leaves, a tournament row with nothing else on it already disappears. What A leaves: a page with one button keeps a full row for it (Divisions, Communications, House league, a rep team’s page), about 50px on a computer. None of B or C removes those rows without the costs below, so A is the answer, and the room it leaves is the price of one rule.
  2. (B) The buttons ride the strip’s right end. At 1280 the Teams buttons need about 442px (measured) and 408 are left; on a phone there is no room, so buttons would sit in two places depending on width, and the strip would turn into whatever page is open’s toolbar.
  3. (C) The buttons ride the band. Only tabbed pages have a band, so most pages still need a row, and 4b R2 ruled the band holds tabs only.

T3The club: where a “?” shows

  1. (A) Recommended: a “?” on every club page that has help to show, and the build gives every club place and tab a topic, so in practice every page has one. Same rule as the other two: no topic, no “?”. Matched in tab 6: of 44 club screens, 37 have a clear section today and 7 a partial one (Assistant coaches, a rep team’s page and its schedule, a season’s Overview, The board, PDF settings, the club Overview). The help pass points those 7 at the nearest section or writes a short one; no place’s tabs end up half covered (the tournament’s Settings & access shows what that looks like, and the build fixes it there too). The phone bar gets it too, last, after the globe. The risk sits here: in the club this is an addition, and it is only as good as the matching. A “?” that opens the wrong section is worse than today’s program guide in the menu, so the help pass is part of the build, not a later tidy-up.
  2. (B) Only the pages whose section already matches by name, with no new matching. Quicker, but the “?” would come and go across a place’s own tabs.
  3. (C) No “?” in the club; keep the account menu’s Help. That leaves help in a different place in one of the three portals, against the direction.

T4The rows still left: “?”-only, the lone “+”, the second row

  • “?”-only rows: gone everywhere. That is the move itself: the five tournament screens lose a 50px row on a computer and 52px on a phone, and the Coaches Portal’s off-team pages lose theirs with T6·1.
  • The lone “+” on a phone (both portals: Coaches Portal Money today; the tournament’s Divisions and Communications once the “?” leaves; drawn in tab 5): (A) Recommended: keep it. It is the page’s own add, where every page’s actions are. Each other home puts it in the chrome (the band, ruled tabs only; the bar, already holding name, globe and “?”) or invents a floating button the product does not have. It costs about 60px on a phone, only on pages with an add. (B) Into the bar beside the “?”: the tournament door would shrink to 214px. (C) A floating button: a new pattern, one more thing that covers content.
  • The second row on a computer: Recommended: join Roster’s two rows (Add Player and Import beside Export on the List / Depth chart row, −50px, fits at every computer width). Leave the tournament Schedule and Teams as they are: Schedule fits only at about 1400 and wider, and Teams does not fit at all. This is a fix to one page, not a rule.

T5Order of the build

  1. (A) Recommended: one phase in two parts, one walk at the end. Part 1 (the tournament admin): make the help panel reachable from the admin’s strip and bar (today it is not, which is why this is first); move the tournament’s 13 “?”s into the strip and bar; point the 5 event screens without one (Check-in, Communications, Staff kit, Your notifications, Payouts) at their tournaments-guide sections, a small help task. Part 2 (the club): the club’s help matching with the help agent (44 screens, 7 partial), then the club’s “?” at both widths; the three club screens the tournament reuses (Members, Audit log, PDF settings) get theirs here. Part 2 needs Part 1’s slot; Part 1’s help work is five pointers, so it does not wait on the club’s matching. Under T2 A there is no button work, so there is no “buttons second” step. The Coaches Portal changes (its off-team pages, T6·1; Roster’s row, T4) ride Part 1 only if you rule them in; they touch no admin code and can be left out without rework.
  2. (B) Strip first, buttons second. Under T2 A the second part would be empty.
  3. (C) One part. The club’s matching is content work that would hold up the tournament’s mechanical move.

Rework to avoid: the Phase 6 closing walk comes before this phase. It should not check where the tournament “?” sits, because this phase moves it; say so in the walk’s open list.

T6What the drawing surfaced

No disagreement with the ruling. The direction is right, and the code agrees: the panel is already shared, and the tournament’s empty rows already disappear on their own. The risk is in the club’s help matching (T3). Four smaller things:

  1. A separate small ask: the Coaches Portal’s own pages outside a team (notifications, billing, link a club) still draw a “?” on the page row. After this phase they would be the only pages in the product that do. Keeping them there was a deliberate line, not an oversight: the 4b build hosts the strip “?” on team pages only, and its code says a page whose “?” is handed to nothing has no help at all. (A) Recommended: they move into the strip too, crossing that line on purpose (the shell gives those pages the same host; the free portal stays out, E13). (B) Leave them: three low-traffic pages keep the old shape.
  2. The help panel has to be reachable from the admin’s chrome. Today the tournament admin’s panel sits inside the page area. A “?” drawn in the strip would show and do nothing, with no error. This is a build step, not a ruling; the walk’s first check is that every “?” opens its panel.
  3. The admin phone bar loses 44px of line. Recommended, one rule for the admin bar: the line’s words give way (cut with “…”), the chip and the chevron stay whole. For a tournament event that cuts the event’s name sooner. The tightest bar measured is a club house-league season (back arrow, season name, “House league”, its stage chip): 258 → 214px, so “House league” is cut and the chip stays. Both are drawn in Edge cases.
  4. Records to fix on the way: the prompts index row for 4b still says the phone’s help goes into the More sheet (Phase 5 F3 replaced that); the design log has no entry giving the reason for the strip “?” (n1). The build writes both.
