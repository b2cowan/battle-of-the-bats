§01

Design A — the season dial comes out of the portal

what changes on every page

Today the sidebar carries a season selector; picking a finished year swaps the whole portal into an archive with its own ten-door menu. In Design A that dial and menu are gone. You are always on your team, in its current chapter — history arrives inside the tools that use it (§03–§05).

§02

A · Staff becomes one list, and Remove means removed

the access half, on screen

Staff stops being “staff of the season you're looking at.” It is the team's staff, one screen, no year attached. Removal takes everything away at once — and says so. Past seasons keep every name as a record; re-adding someone restores access because nothing was destroyed.

§03

already shipped — it just becomes the only door

§04

§05

A · Money gets last year's book, read-only

v1 · chosen

The treasurer's moment — building this season's budget against what last season actually cost, or answering “what did we raise last year?” — gets a closed book inside Money. Stated widening, out loud: whoever holds money access today can read the closed books, including years before they held the role. That follows the “current permissions, everywhere” ruling.

§06

Design B — one Season File per finished year

the consolidated alternative

Same access layer as A, same deletion of the dial — but instead of per-tool shelves, each closed season gets one read-only page holding everything anyone might look up: results, the roster's names, awards with reprint, attendance, and a money summary for money-permitted staff.

New

Reached from the compare list in §03 (“Open file”). Certificate reprint — the one real moment Design A leaves homeless — survives here. Nothing on this page can be edited from it.

For B

  • One build answers “what happened in 20XX?” forever — no future drip of shelf decisions and releases.
  • Reprint survives, and so does “who was on that team,” without re-opening a whole portal.
  • Dead-simple mental model: every season is one page.

Against B

  • It builds a browsing surface no moment has asked for — the instinct the owner ruled against.
  • It re-creates the drift force. The last “deliberate, opt-in” container grew to ten doors in a month; every future feature will ask “shouldn't the Season File show this too?”
  • History gets two homes — the File and Season's End/Wrapped overlap and will disagree eventually.
  • Working-moment pieces still end up in the tools anyway (copying a plan belongs in Practice plans), so B tends toward “A plus one more page.”
§07

Design C — keep the place, fix access only

the honest baseline

Membership still moves (the revocation defect is real under any design), but the season dial, the ten-door archive, and the frozen-season copy of the portal all stay. Cheapest today. The mock is simply the left half of §01 — that is the design.

For C

  • Smallest immediate build — access fix plus nothing.
  • Whole-season browsing and certificate reprint keep working exactly as today.
  • No demo re-scripting; the sandbox's “every screen still opens” promise stays true.

Against C

  • Every future feature pays the archive tax forever — the three-question checklist, the allow-list edit, the one-level-down audit that produced Chunk F's most expensive defects.
  • Nothing automated can see it. The rendered test fixture has no completed season; §36/§37-class owner QA recurs with every door.
  • ~2,400 lines of season-toggle machinery stay live to protect a surface whose audience has never been named.
  • The opt-in contract's own history — ten doors, thirty-one routes — shows where this road keeps going.
§08

M1 — membership lives on the team

recommended · the §0.2 answer

One membership card per person per team: role, permissions, active-or-revoked. Seasons become chapters that snapshot who served — written automatically, never managed. Access questions have exactly one answer in exactly one place.

§09

M2 — membership derived from season rows

the plan as written, priced honestly

Keep the per-season staff rows as the only real thing; define “current staff” as whoever holds a row on the newest season. Cheaper to reach — no migration — but belonging stays a side-effect of season paperwork, and three patches are needed to make it safe.

Season rows — the source of truth

2024 · rows: JB, RO, ST 2025 · rows: JB, RO, ST 2026 · rows: JB, RO, ST ← “current staff” 2027 · created blank → staff = nobody
“The newest column is the team” — until a newer, emptier column exists.
EventWhat happens under M2
Head coach removes RileyTwo mechanisms. Flag the newest row revoked and sweep every older row so nothing else re-admits them. The sweep is the kind of shadow rule that gets missed.
Riley is re-added next springWorks — new row on the newest season.
Season rolls overPermissions still reset silently — new rows are minted with defaults. Needs its own separate fix to copy grants forward, forever, on both rollover paths.
Club admin creates next year early (blank)The whole staff is locked out the moment the blank year exists, until each person is re-added by hand. Needs a special-case patch (“newest season with staff”), which is a rule nobody will remember.
Season ends, next not startedWorks — the completed year is still the newest, so its rows still count.
PriceNo migration, smaller day-one diff — plus three permanent patches (revocation sweep, rollover grant-copy, blank-year exception) and staff management stays season-shaped, which is the mental model the owner already called not useful. The cheapness is front-loaded; the complexity is annual.

Recommendation

Build A on M1 — history in place, membership on the team.

  • The pattern is already proven in this product, six times, with zero wrong-season defects — while the toggle model needed a build-time contract, a 700-line smoke test, and still shipped the defects §36/§37 exist to catch.
  • M1 is the only membership home that kills all five defect classes at the root — revocation, the yearly permission reset, the blank-year lockout, the between-seasons dead screens, and the archive's “as-you-were” access maze.
  • “Start from nothing” stays honest: v1 ships only the two moments already named (plans §04, money §05); everything else waits for a person and a task. Wrapped and the compare list absorb the browsing questions at zero marginal cost.
  • Named losses, day one: browsing a past roster/schedule/attendance/money screen; reprinting an old certificate (decide: accept for now, or seat a small awards shelf later); any screen listing who staffed a given year (the data is kept, the screen isn't).
  • What would flip me: three or more new reference requests in the first month of real use — that's B's consolidation winning on evidence. Or a real customer moment for whole-season browsing that Wrapped can't answer — that's C's toggle earning its audience for the first time.
