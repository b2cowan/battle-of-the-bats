02

Three things the inventory turned up

1 · Money does not line up anywhere in the list tables

The shared table has no number treatment at all — figures inherit left alignment from text. Five surfaces are affected, including the two a treasurer reads most. The portal's own design principles already say numeric columns use the data face; none of these do. This is the single change that makes the biggest visible difference for the least work.

2 · Two tables scroll sideways on a phone with no warning

Inside the Player Dues drawer, one player's payment schedule and the season-end refund preview sit in a frame that scrolls horizontally with nothing telling the coach it can. Every other table in the hub either stacks into cards or shows a swipe hint. These two were missed.

3 · Three lists are built entirely from hand-written styling

Fundraisers, Payables and Payments carry no shared class between them — every border, size and colour is written at the call site. They look similar today by coincidence, not by contract, and the next edit to any one of them moves it away from the other two.

03

Three jobs, three treatments

Consistency here does not mean one table. It means no more than one treatment per job — and there are exactly three jobs in this hub. Five treatments collapse to three, and every surface can name which one it is and why.

Job one

The list

Many records of the same kind. One row each. Read down a column, act on a row. Eight surfaces.

Dues · Expenses · Payables · Payment schedule · Fundraisers · Payments · Allocation instalments · one player's schedule

Job two

The outline

A hierarchy — categories holding lines holding instalments — with a figure at every level. Read down, and expand. Two surfaces.

Budget Plan · List  ·  Budget vs. Actual · Categories

Job three

The grid

A comparison read across — time, or plan against actual. Never a list. Never cards. Two surfaces.

Budget Plan · By period  ·  Budget vs. Actual · Months

What the three share

One number cell — right-aligned, tabular figures, the data face, one size. One column-heading recipe — uppercase, letter-spaced, one muted ink, one tint, with a documented denser size for grids that carry twelve columns. One category bar. One row rule and one hover. That set is the liftable piece — it would serve roster, schedule, attendance and results unchanged — but it lands inside Money and nowhere else in this pass.

05

What is deliberately left different

Three surfaces depart from the list treatment, and each departure is load-bearing. Naming them is the point of the exercise — an unnamed exception is what drift looks like from the inside.

Keep
Budget Plan stays a card stack

It is an editable outline with nested instalments and per-row actions, not a report. A flat table would make every line's period breakdown a second table inside a cell. It joins the shared chrome; it does not become a list.

Keep
The month grid scrolls sideways on a phone

A standing ruling. It is a comparison read across time; card-stacking would destroy the only thing it is for. It scrolls inside its own frame with the first column pinned and a visible hint, and the page around it never scrolls.

Keep
Card-stacking on phones, with a label per cell

A desktop table that becomes an unreadable phone table is a regression, not a tidy-up. Every list surface keeps stacking, and every stacked cell keeps its own label.

Watch
Payables carries a deposit and a balance

The one list row with genuine nesting. It becomes a list row whose chevron opens the two instalments underneath — the same expand-in-place the outline already uses — rather than a card that prints both inline. The Payment schedule sub-tab next door already tabulates exactly this, which is the argument for it.

06

How it lands

Four passes, each independently shippable, each verified by a rendered sweep across the Money hub before the next begins. Ordered so the highest-visibility change is also the smallest and safest.

Pass 1
Money lines up

One number cell and one column heading, shared. Applies to all five list tables and both grids at once. No layout moves, no controls move — figures right-align, digits become tabular, headings match. Roughly thirty hand-written styles disappear into two classes.

Pass 2
One outline

Budget Plan and Budget vs. Actual's category view render from the same frame, category bar and row. Two forks become one. Nothing a coach does changes.

Pass 3
The three card lists become lists

Fundraisers, Payables and Payments. The most visible change of the four, and the only one where a coach will notice a different shape on a desktop — a heading row instead of repeated labels. Phones look much as they do now.

Pass 4
The two grids agree, and the phone gaps close

Budget Plan's By period grid adopts the month grid's heading and type. The two Dues tables that scroll sideways in silence get the same phone treatment as everything else. The in-app Money guide and the demo tour narration are checked in the same unit of work.

Three calls to make before any code

  1. Is the three-job model the right cut? It is what makes "consistent" mean something specific rather than "make everything the grid". If you would rather see two treatments, or four, that changes the whole pass.
  2. Do Fundraisers, Payables and Payments become tables? This is the only genuinely visible desktop change, and the one worth seeing rendered before committing. The argument for is the repeated-label rule you already ratified; the argument against is that a fundraiser has a description that reads better in a card.
  3. Is Pass 1 alone enough for now? Right-aligning the money is most of the perceived inconsistency for a fraction of the work. Passes 2–4 are the part that stops it coming back.
