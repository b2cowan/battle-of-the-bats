    Define what the team measures

    Before the season, and again the day a test turns out to be wrong: decide what this team measures and how.

    Skills & Goals → Metrics …/development?section=metrics · open 60-yd sprint, then Throw speed
    On the screen, as built

    One table: 60-yd sprint (seconds · lower is the aim) · Throw speed (km/h · higher is the aim · method not recorded) · Changeup speed (mph · aim: 62–68 mph) · Sets feet before throwing (Observed skill · 3 descriptors); “Retired definitions (1)” folds Shuttle run away. Change the unit or a written method and the product starts a new definition; old results stay under the retired one. Writing a method where none was recorded keeps the series.

    A session can still add a test by name and unit alone (“+ New test…”) and sends the coach to Metrics for the rest.

    How it feels to use — my assessment

    What works

    • The Metrics table says in one line what a record means — “seconds · lower is the aim”, “aim: 62–68 mph”, “3 descriptors”. A coach reads the team’s whole measurement policy in five rows.
    • The definition page is the best form in the lifecycle: the fields on the left, a live example on the right (8.05 seconds · Best of 2 attempts · 8.12 · 8.05), and the consequence of a change written exactly where the change is made.

    What gets in the way

    • A stale sentence under the table: “An observed skill is defined here now; recording an observation against it comes in a later release.” It shipped. A coach reading this today would not try the skill chip.
    • The preview promises a word the reports never write: “‘Faster’ is said only because lower is the stated aim.” Phase 3 ruled “faster” out; the definition page and the report now disagree in front of the coach.
    • “Measured test” three times in the Kind column carries nothing — the kind matters once, for the skill.

    What I’d change

    • Retire the stale sentence. Align the preview’s last line with the report (“arithmetic in the unit; the aim is stated, never ‘faster’”). Let the Kind column carry only the exception.
    Your notes on this screen
    Q 1.1

    Is a full definition the right amount of ceremony before a coach can record anything?

    A test needs six facts before its results read honestly downstream — that is why the chart can say “same method throughout” and the coverage cell can say “best of 2”. The price is that the quick road (name + unit on a session) produces a test that reads “method not recorded” everywhere, forever, until someone finishes it. Two tiers, or one?

    Ruled and walked: Phase 1 passed 16/16 (§178), the successor rule and “keep the series when writing a first method” as built. Nothing open here — this station is the ground the rest stands on.
    Q 1.2

    Measured test and observed skill — is that the split coaches think in?

    Everything downstream forks on it: a test gets attempts, a headline, a chart and a change sentence; a skill gets descriptors, an observation, a timeline and no line. It is honest — a descriptor is not a number — but the coach meets the fork on the definition page, on the session grid, in the player’s views and in the Metric dropdown.

    What rides on it: the whole record model (tests vs. observations, mig 293/295); the Observed-skill chip on the grid (station 3); Observations as a view (station 5).
    Where I’d push on the shape

    I think this station is right and I would not spend the re-evaluation here. The two-tier definition is the honest answer to “a coach on the field will not fill in six fields”, and the split between a number and a descriptor is what lets the reports refuse to draw a line through “With a reminder”. The risk is not the model; it is vocabulary — the coach meets “metric”, “test”, “observed skill”, “measurable”, “result” and “reading” for two kinds of thing. That is Standing back, question 1.

    Your notes on this screen
    Q 2.1 · the question that paused the walk, widened

    What is a session — a testing day of its own, or something that happens at a practice?

    Today a session is its own record: a date, an optional event, a note, a scope. A practice has its own record of what happened: the plan and the recap. Two records can describe the same evening, and the link between them is one optional field that pre-fills a date. If a session is a facet of a practice, “Taken at” is its identity and the date locks by definition — but every testing day must then be an event on the calendar. If a session is its own thing, the date stays free and the two records stay two, with a visible link both ways at most.

    What rides on it: the “Taken at” rule (§10.2 ruling 1, twice implemented); whether a practice’s page should list the readings taken at it; whether Practice review and the Sessions list are two lists of one evening (station 7).
    Q 2.2

    Is the scope worth a dialog between the coach and the first field?

    The scope exists so that a blank can mean something: “Not recorded” is a player who was in scope and got no reading; without a scope the count can only run against the whole roster. The alternative is to infer the scope from whoever got a reading or a not-assessed mark — no dialog, and no “Not recorded” state.

    What rides on it: D-call1 (the scope stored as two id lists; “not assessed” its own record) · Part D steps 1–2, 10–11 · Coverage’s “Not assessed” dated by its session (station 7).
    Under this screen
    D-call1 The scope lives on the session as two id lists; “not assessed” is its own record

    A snapshot of intent, never queried by member; the route proves every id is this team’s. The alternative shape on the hub: a participants list with a present/absent state per player.

    ruled “Taken at” pre-fills the date and never locks it — §10.2 ruling 1, unchanged by Phase 2

    The behaviour that raised the pause. It is exactly as designed and as the code comment says; no defect is recorded against it. Q 2.1 is the question underneath it.

    Where I’d push on the shape

    I’d keep the scope and I’d keep the session as its own thing — and I’d say why, because the code sides that way for a reason: recording happens outside practices (a testing day, a combine-style morning), a recap is prose about the practice while a session is figures about players, and folding one into the other forces every testing day onto the calendar. What I would not defend is the link being invisible: a session taken at a practice should show on that practice, and the practice on the session. That is an item, and it waits — but it is the honest answer to the question that paused the walk: the date did not need to lock; the two records needed to see each other.
