    The whole workflow, read end to end

    Phase 2’s walk paused on a small question — should picking an event lock a session’s date? — and you said the real problem was wider: you needed the whole workflow in front of you before ruling on any of its parts. Phase 3 is built and committed, so the whole workflow now exists. This page walks it the way a head coach would, one screen at a time, on the real product with the UAT fixture. At each screen it puts a question to you about the shape — not whether a label is right, but whether this is the right screen, in the right place, asking the right thing.

    How to use it

    • Open the product on a second screen and walk the nine stations in order — it is the coach’s sequence, from defining a test to handing a family a sheet of paper.

    • The build calls that sit under each screen are listed there so you can see what rides on your answer — but nothing here asks you to rule on them. Those are Part D (nine) and Part E (eight) on the hub’s QA Walk tab, and they follow the shape.
    • “Where I’d push” is my own reading, argued from what the code does. Disagree with it freely; it is there so you have something to argue with.

    1

    Define what the team measures

    Before the season, and again the day a test turns out to be wrong: decide what this team measures and how.

    Skills & Goals → Metrics …/development?section=metrics · open 60-yd sprint, then Throw speed
    On the screen, as built

    One table: 60-yd sprint (seconds · lower is the aim) · Throw speed (km/h · higher is the aim · method not recorded) · Changeup speed (mph · aim: 62–68 mph) · Sets feet before throwing (Observed skill · 3 descriptors); “Retired definitions (1)” folds Shuttle run away. Open the sprint and a definition is a page: kind, attempts per session, which attempt leads, unit, aim, and the written method — with a live preview of how a result will read (Best of 2 attempts · 0.35 seconds lower). Change the unit or a written method and the product starts a new definition; old results stay under the retired one. Writing a method where none was recorded keeps the series.

    A session can still add a test by name and unit alone (“+ New test…”) and sends the coach to Metrics for the rest.

    Q 1.1

    Is a full definition the right amount of ceremony before a coach can record anything?

    A test needs six facts before its results read honestly downstream — that is why the chart can say “same method throughout” and the coverage cell can say “best of 2”. The price is that the quick road (name + unit on a session) produces a test that reads “method not recorded” everywhere, forever, until someone finishes it. Two tiers, or one?

    Ruled and walked: Phase 1 passed 16/16 (§178), the successor rule and “keep the series when writing a first method” as built. Nothing open here — this station is the ground the rest stands on.
    Q 1.2

    Measured test and observed skill — is that the split coaches think in?

    Everything downstream forks on it: a test gets attempts, a headline, a chart and a change sentence; a skill gets descriptors, an observation, a timeline and no line. It is honest — a descriptor is not a number — but the coach meets the fork on the definition page, on the session grid, in the player’s views and in the Metric dropdown.

    What rides on it: the whole record model (tests vs. observations, mig 293/295); the Observed-skill chip on the grid (station 3); Observations as a view (station 5).
    Where I’d push

    I think this station is right and I would not spend the re-evaluation here. The two-tier definition is the honest answer to “a coach on the field will not fill in six fields”, and the split between a number and a descriptor is what lets the reports refuse to draw a line through “With a reminder”. The risk is not the model; it is vocabulary — the coach meets “metric”, “test”, “observed skill”, “measurable”, “result” and “reading” for two kinds of thing. That is Standing back, question 1.

    Q 2.1 · the question that paused the walk, widened

    What is a session — a testing day of its own, or something that happens at a practice?

    Today a session is its own record: a date, an optional event, a note, a scope. A practice has its own record of what happened: the plan and the recap. Two records can describe the same evening, and the link between them is one optional field that pre-fills a date. If a session is a facet of a practice, “Taken at” is its identity and the date locks by definition — but every testing day must then be an event on the calendar. If a session is its own thing, the date stays free and the two records stay two, with a visible link both ways at most.

    What rides on it: the “Taken at” rule (§10.2 ruling 1, twice implemented); whether a practice’s page should list the readings taken at it; whether Practice review and the Sessions list are two lists of one evening (station 7).
    Q 2.2

    Is the scope worth a dialog between the coach and the first field?

    The scope exists so that a blank can mean something: “Not recorded” is a player who was in scope and got no reading; without a scope the count can only run against the whole roster. The alternative is to infer the scope from whoever got a reading or a not-assessed mark — no dialog, and no “Not recorded” state.

    What rides on it: D-call1 (the scope stored as two id lists; “not assessed” its own record) · Part D steps 1–2, 10–11 · Coverage’s “Not assessed” dated by its session (station 7).
    Under this screen
    D-call1 The scope lives on the session as two id lists; “not assessed” is its own record

    A snapshot of intent, never queried by member; the route proves every id is this team’s. The alternative shape on the hub: a participants list with a present/absent state per player.

    ruled “Taken at” pre-fills the date and never locks it — §10.2 ruling 1, unchanged by Phase 2

    The behaviour that raised the pause. It is exactly as designed and as the code comment says; no defect is recorded against it. Q 2.1 is the question underneath it.

    Where I’d push

    I’d keep the scope and I’d keep the session as its own thing — and I’d say why, because the code sides that way for a reason: recording happens outside practices (a testing day, a combine-style morning), a recap is prose about the practice while a session is figures about players, and folding one into the other forces every testing day onto the calendar. What I would not defend is the link being invisible: a session taken at a practice should show on that practice, and the practice on the session. That is an item, and it waits — but it is the honest answer to the question that paused the walk: the date did not need to lock; the two records needed to see each other.

    On the screen, as built

    A chip per metric in scope. One row per player, one field per attempt (the sprint: two; the changeup: three).     Q 3.1

    Is the grid the right instrument on the field?

    One row per player, one field per attempt, Enter to move on. It was built for a laptop at the fence or a phone held sideways. The other shapes a coach might want: one player at a time (a card you swipe through, phone-first), or one attempt at a time (“next up”). The grid wins on a roster of twelve; it is the wrong shape if the real moment is one player, one stopwatch, one thumb.

    What rides on it: D-call4 (a correction keeps the original; no history table) · D-call6 (“entered by” is the display name) · D-opencall2 (“Mark not assessed” a link on blank rows only) · Part D steps 3–7.
    Q 3.2

    Does an observed skill belong on the same grid as a stopwatch?

    The skill chip records a note-like thing in a spreadsheet-like place. That is the coach’s moment if observations are made in the same drill as the timing. It is the wrong moment if an observation is something written later, on the player, from the bench — which the product also allows, from two more doors (station 5). One record, three places to write it.

    What rides on it: D-call2 (an observation needs a descriptor or what was seen) · Part D step 8 · the Observations view and the Notes timeline (station 5).
    Q 3.3

    Are two kinds of blank one too many for the field?

    “Not recorded” is in scope with nothing typed; “Not assessed” is the coach saying so — absent, hurt, skipped — with a reason. Both honest, both shown, both counted apart. Coverage later reads “Not assessed” with the date of the session that said it. Is that the distinction that makes Coverage trustworthy, or a finer line than a coach at the fence will draw?

    What rides on it: Coverage’s third cell state (station 7) and the count line’s three numbers.

    On the screen, as built

    Q 4.1

    Is a review that stores nothing a review?

    The build prompt asked for “a review state” and “never marks complete” in adjacent lines; storing a state would have been the mark, so none is stored. A session therefore never says “reviewed”, and a Sessions list of ten gives no sign which were finished. Is a session’s doneness something the team needs to see later, or are the counts themselves the only truth worth keeping?

    What rides on it: D-call5 (“Review session” stores nothing) · Part D step 9.
    Under this screen
    D-call5 “Review session” stores nothing; no review state on the session

    Disagreed before building, then built as read-back only. The alternative on the hub: a session carries a “reviewed” mark.

    Where I’d push

    Keep it storing nothing, and name the risk: the Sessions list cannot tell a finished session from an abandoned one. If that bites, the fix is a derived label — a session is “complete” when every in-scope row has a state — not a stored mark that can be true while the rows are not.

    Review a goal

    Sit with the player, say how it is going, date it, and say when you will look again.

    Goals → the goal → “Review goal” · the status pill on the list · “+ Add goal” → “More — success, review date, tag” · add nothing you do not remove
    On the screen, as built

    “Review goal” asks for a status (required), a date (today), a note, a next review date and evidence (the linked observations), and appends one dated event to “How this goal has developed” — set → observed → reviewed, never overwritten, each with who wrote it. The status pill on the list still cycles (Working on it → Achieved → Parked) and each tap appends a dated status-only review. A goal carries five facts beyond its focus: the success sentence, the review date, the origin, the tag, the status.

    Q 6.1

    Is a status change a review?

    Every tap on the pill is a review with no words. That keeps the history complete — nothing changes without a dated row — and it makes the history noisy the first time a coach cycles the pill to find the status they meant. The alternatives: retire the pill and route every change through the dialog; or keep the pill and fold status-only rows into the history quietly.

    What rides on it: D-opencall1 (a review requires a status, never prose; the pill appends) · Part D step 14 · the handout’s “Save this next step as a review →” (E-call2), which is a door to this dialog.
    Q 6.2

    Is set → observed → reviewed the lifecycle coaches want, or is a goal simpler than that?

    The mockup approved a goal with a success sentence, a review date, an origin and a tag, and a history behind it. The question the fixture cannot answer is whether coaches fill those in. A goal that is only ever “focus + status” is fine here — the extras are optional and fold away — but the reports and the handout lean on them (“Next review: 24 Jun”, “Success looks like…”). Is the richer goal the shape, or the ceiling?

    What rides on it: the goal’s history in Player progress (station 7) · the handout’s “What we’re working on” lines (station 8) · Part D steps 13, 15.
    Under this screen
    D-opencall1 A goal review requires a status, never prose; the pill still works and adds a status-only review

    Carried at its recommendation. The alternative: a review must carry a note.

    /review accepted Two coaches reviewing one goal within the same second can leave its status one event behind

    Self-correcting on the next review; a database-side transaction if it is ever seen. Recorded, not fixed.

    Where I’d push

    Keep the append-only history — it is the one thing that makes “how this goal has developed” true rather than a status with a date on it. The pill I would watch rather than remove: it is the cheap path, so it will be the common one, and if the history fills with status-only rows the fix is to show them collapsed, not to take the pill away and make every change a dialog.

    The line is defensible and it was ruled; I re-ask it for one reason only — the handout.     Standing back

    Five questions that cut across every station. They are the ones a single walk never surfaces, because each screen is right on its own.

    S 1

    One record, how many names?

    The coach meets “Skills & Goals” (the sidebar, the hub, the player’s tab), “Development” (the Insights tab, the grant, the handout, the help), and for the things inside: metric, test, observed skill, measurable, result, reading, attempt, headline, observation, goal, focus area, review. Some of that is real distinction and some is drift across four phases and two sessions. Should the coach meet one word for the door, and how many for what is behind it?

    S 2

    How many doors to one figure?

    Devon’s latest sprint headline can be read on the session grid, on Skills & Goals → Players, on his Results view, in Coverage, in Player progress and on the handout — six readings of one number, all from one reader, all correct. Which of those are homes, and which are doors that should say so?

    S 3

    Is the session the right spine?

    Everything dated hangs off a session — results, not-assessed marks, observations — except a goal review (dated on its own) and a single reading recorded on the player (“+ Record a result”, outside any session). Two ways to record a result: in a session on the field, and on the player from the bench. Is that the right pair, or should every result have a session, even a session of one?

    S 4

    Reading it top-down, is a screen missing?

    The four phases built the model from the bottom — trust the records, define, record, explain. Read as a coach on a Tuesday, the workflow has no “this week”: nothing says which goals are due for review, who has not been measured since May, which sessions were left half-recorded. Coverage answers one metric at a time; the goal’s review date is on the goal. Is that view Phase 4, or is it the screen the whole lifecycle was missing?

    S 5

    What the product refuses to say — is the refusal right for every team on it?

    No ranking, no sort by result, no team average beside a child, no percentile, no “on track”, no projected date, no “faster”. Right for a youth product, and the plan says so in as many words. The teams this portal now serves include ones whose coaches will ask for exactly those things. Is the refusal a product principle, a default a team can loosen, or something in between?
