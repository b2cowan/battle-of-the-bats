Game-day ticker: four ways to carry today's scores

Stage 1 restyles the scores strip that runs under the tab row on game day. You asked for better versions before it is built (Ask 17), "maybe like a soft flashing scoreboard". Below is today's strip as it now draws on the new frame, then three options at true size: a phone at 390 px and a desk at 1280 px. Which pages carry the strip is Stage 2's and 3's call (the Overview drops it; Schedule and a game's page drop it), so these show it on the Teams page.

The scores in options B and C change every few seconds so you can see how a goal or a run arrives. With reduced motion on, they change with no movement at all.

Today: every game of the day crawls past in one line, live games first, each one a link. Hover or a held finger pauses it; × shrinks it to a "Live scores" button.

Phone · 390 px

What a family meets

  • The strip never stops moving, so reading one score means waiting for it to come round. One lap always takes 48 seconds, so the more games a day has, the faster they race past.
  • "LIVE" and "FINAL" are 9.6 px capital chips in heavy 12.5 px lettering: the old scoreboard style the frame retires (Ask 2: sentence case, 12 px minimum). The typewriter face is already gone behind the switch.
  • Your own team's game is wherever the order puts it.
  • A score that changes simply swaps its number on the next pass. Nobody sees it change.
  • It already respects reduced motion (it stops and scrolls sideways by finger) and pauses under a held finger. Every option keeps that.

A · Calm marquee: the same strip, easier to read as it passes. Half the speed, a fixed "Today" label so a family knows what it is, the state in plain words, and a soft fade at the edge.

Phone · 390 px

What changes

  • Half today's speed, and a steady speed: a busy day makes a longer lap, not a faster one.
  • "Live now" in the live red with its dot, "Final" and the start time in sentence case: the frame's state words (Ask 8).
  • Team names in the text face; scores in tabular figures, so they do not jiggle as they pass.
  • 44 px (today's is 40, under the tap floor), same tap-to-open, same pause and same ×.

What it does not fix

  • It still moves, and a score still has to come round to be read. A changed score is still not noticed.

Smallest change restyle only, no new behaviour.

B · Soft scoreboard: the scores stand still. Live games first in their own tiles, your team's tile first of all; when a score moves, that tile glows soft green once and fades back over two seconds, so a parent looking up sees what changed.

Phone · 390 px · Riverdale Rapids followed

How it behaves

  • Fixed tiles, no crawl. One and most of a second fit on a phone, so the cut edge says "swipe". Swiping snaps tile by tile. Four and a half fit on a desk. Tiles are wide enough for a full team name (a 21-letter name fits), because a name cut short can turn "Silver Creek Storm" and "Silver Creek Sharks" into the same team.
  • Order: your followed team's game today (outlined in the organizer's colour; its live game, or its next one), then live games, then the next to start, then today's finals. The last tile opens the full schedule.
  • A changed score, once. The new number settles in (0.6 s) and the tile's tint fades from soft green to white over 2.4 s. One change, no blinking: well inside the three-flashes rule. With reduced motion nothing moves or fades; the new number is underlined for ten seconds instead.
  • Each tile opens its game. "No score yet" (Stage 0) shows on a live game before its first score.

What it costs

  • It is taller: 75 px against today's 40. Pinned on a phone with the tab row that is 123 px of 667 (18%), still inside the Nav Unification budget (26%).
  • It shows fewer games at once on a phone (one and a half, against a crawl that eventually shows all). Swiping or the last tile reaches the rest.

C · My team line: one still line with the one game a parent came for, their team's score or next start, and a count of what else is live. Tap the game to open it; tap the count for the live games.

Phone · 390 px · Riverdale Rapids followed

How it behaves

  • Following a team: "Live" in red and its score; between games, its next start ("Next · 6:30 p.m. · Diamond 1"). On a phone a long opponent's name is cut at the end ("Cedar Hollow Cy…"); the desk has room for the field too.
  • Following nobody: "2 games live now · Follow a team to keep its score here ›", which opens the Teams list.
  • "2 live" on the right opens the Schedule at its live games.
  • A changed score settles in (0.6 s), no tint: the line is small enough that the number is the news. With reduced motion it is underlined for ten seconds.
  • 44 px tall, the whole game part one tap.

What it gives up

  • Other games' scores leave the strip; a family sees only how many are live. The Schedule and the Overview's "Live now" carry them.
  • The dock already shows the followed team on a phone (My Team dock). C repeats it unless the dock goes, which is Stage 2's call.

My recommendation: B, the soft scoreboard

The strip's job on game day is "what is the score now", and a family reads it standing at a fence with a phone. B is the only option where a score can be read without waiting and where a change is noticed without anyone watching the strip. It also keeps the other games, which C drops, and puts the parent's own team first, which is C's best idea. A is a fine restyle, but it keeps the thing that makes the strip hard to read: it moves.

The cost is about 35 px more pinned height on a phone than today (123 px against 88), still inside the budget. The trade is fewer games in view at once, with swiping and the full schedule one tap away.
