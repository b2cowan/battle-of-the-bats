Game-day scoring: seven ways to carry today's scores

The question is how to keep scores useful without a moving ticker or a tall phone strip. Compare today's ticker and options A–E at a 390 px phone width. Options D and E came from a second opinion (Codex): D keeps one quiet 44 px score line; E removes the strip and puts a live count on the Schedule tab. F is your idea of 10-28: today's results fading in one at a time. Claude's review of D and E, and the recommendations, are on the last tab.

The score changes in B–D are only a demonstration. In a real experience, update only when the score changes. Push alerts are optional and belong in the installed app; the public tournament page stays a website, with no browser alert prompt.

Today: every game of the day crawls past in one line, live games first, each one a link. Hover or a held finger pauses it; × shrinks it to a "Live scores" button.

Phone · 390 px

What a family meets

  • The strip never stops moving, so reading one score means waiting for it to come round. One lap always takes 48 seconds, so the more games a day has, the faster they race past.
  • "LIVE" and "FINAL" are 9.6 px capital chips in heavy 12.5 px lettering: the old scoreboard style the frame retires (Ask 2: sentence case, 12 px minimum). The typewriter face is already gone behind the switch.
  • Your own team's game is wherever the order puts it.
  • A score that changes simply swaps its number on the next pass. Nobody sees it change.
  • It already respects reduced motion (it stops and scrolls sideways by finger) and pauses under a held finger. Every option keeps that.

What changes

Desk · 1280 px (scrolls sideways)

B · Soft scoreboard: the scores stand still. Live games first in their own tiles, your team's tile first of all; when a score moves, that tile glows soft green once and fades back over two seconds, so a parent looking up sees what changed.

Phone · 390 px · Riverdale Rapids followed

How it behaves

  • Fixed tiles, no crawl. One and most of a second fit on a phone, so the cut edge says "swipe". Swiping snaps tile by tile. Four and a half fit on a desk. Tiles are wide enough for a full team name (a 21-letter name fits), because a name cut short can turn "Silver Creek Storm" and "Silver Creek Sharks" into the same team.
  • Order: your followed team's game today (outlined in the organizer's colour; its live game, or its next one), then live games, then the next to start, then today's finals. The last tile opens the full schedule.
  • A changed score, once. The new number settles in (0.6 s) and the tile's tint fades from soft green to white over 2.4 s. One change, no blinking: well inside the three-flashes rule. With reduced motion nothing moves or fades; the new number is underlined for ten seconds instead.
  • Each tile opens its game. "No score yet" (Stage 0) shows on a live game before its first score.

What it costs

  • It is taller: 75 px against today's 40. At the top of a page (band, tabs and strip) that is 195 px of a 667 px phone (29%); once the page scrolls the band goes and 123 px stay pinned (18%). Both sit inside the Nav Unification budget (39% at rest, 26% pinned), but it is the tallest option here.
  • It shows fewer games at once on a phone (one and a half, against a crawl that eventually shows all). Swiping or the last tile reaches the rest.

C · My team line: one still line with the one game a parent came for, their team's score or next start, and a count of what else is live. Tap the game to open it; tap the count for the live games.

How it behaves

  • Following a team: "Live" in red and its score; between games, its next start ("Next · 6:30 p.m. · Diamond 1"). On a phone a long opponent's name is cut at the end ("Cedar Hollow Cy…"); the desk has room for the field too.
  • Following nobody: "2 games live now · Follow a team to keep its score here ›", which opens the Teams list.
  • "2 live" on the right opens the Schedule at its live games.
  • A changed score settles in (0.6 s), no tint: the line is small enough that the number is the news. With reduced motion it is underlined for ten seconds.
  • 44 px tall, the whole game part one tap.

What it gives up

  • Other games' scores leave the strip; a family sees only how many are live. The Schedule and the Overview's "Live now" carry them.
  • The dock already shows the followed team on a phone (My Team dock). C repeats it unless the dock goes, which is Stage 2's call.

How it behaves

How it behaves

  • Zero extra vertical space. The “2 live” count is part of the existing Schedule tab and opens the full live scores list.
  • Your team's score without a tap comes from the My Team dock, which already ships: on a phone it sits at the foot of every page on game day (live score, or the countdown to the next game); on a desk it is the rail card. Drawn here on light.
  • Alerts are a choice. In the installed app, a family can follow a team and turn on score alerts. Keep alerts off until requested; let people choose teams and avoid sending an alert for every routine refresh.
  • The public tournament page remains a website and does not ask for browser notification permission. Notifications complement the app’s Scores/Following views; they do not replace the in-page route to scores.

Trade-off

  • While reading another page, a family that follows no team sees only the live count, not the scores. They tap Schedule, or follow a team (the dock then carries its score).

Smallest footprint no score strip; app alerts stay opt-in.

F · Today's results, one at a time (your idea, 10-28): one still line. A result fades in, holds for five seconds, fades out, and the next fades in. It goes through the day once when the page opens, shows a new result when one lands, and otherwise rests on the newest. Your own team stays in the My Team dock at the foot, so the line carries everyone else's.

Phone · 390 px · Riverdale Rapids followed (the dock)

How it behaves

  • Made for results entered after games. Most scores arrive when a game is over, so the line is a reel of today's results, newest first, then games on now (no score, as there usually is none), then the next to start. A score entered during a game simply shows on that game's turn.
  • Moves only when there is news. One pass when the page opens (six games here, about 35 seconds), then it rests on the newest result. A new result fades in marked "New", holds, and rests there. Nothing moves the rest of the time.
  • Each result holds still while read: a 0.4 s fade, five seconds held. No sliding, no blinking.
  • You can stop it. A finger or pointer on the line pauses it; the button at the right pauses it, and when it rests the same button plays today's results again. Tapping the line opens the game shown.
  • Reduced motion: no reel at all; the newest result stays, and the button steps to the next one by hand.
  • Screen readers are not told about every turn (that would be noise); the line is read as a link to the game shown, and the desk carries "All results".
  • A busy day keeps the latest eight results in the reel; the rest are on the Schedule.

What it costs

  • 56 px on a phone (two short lines, so a long pair of names fits without cutting a team short): 176 px at the top of a page (26%), 104 px pinned (16%). A desk keeps one 44 px line.
  • A family wanting one particular game may wait for its turn; the Schedule has them all, one tap away.

Second opinion (Codex)

D for the tournament page; E for the installed app

Your two concerns point away from B as the default: its strip takes 75 px, and the score tint still calls attention to itself. D keeps the most useful part—your followed team’s current score—in a still 44 px line. It saves 31 px versus B and replaces the crawl with one predictable tap to all live games. If even that row feels too much, E removes it completely and leaves a live count on the existing Schedule tab.

When the app is open, prefer a quiet in-page score update; push alerts are mainly useful when the app is in the background. Keep them optional and team-specific, and avoid browser notification prompts on public tournament pages. A live-count badge is quieter, but it cannot say which score changed until someone opens the Schedule.

Claude's review of the second opinion

Where Codex is right, and where the evidence goes further

Agreed

  • The strip should not ask for attention on every page; calm beats clever. B's tint is the part of B most worth doubting.
  • Score alerts belong to the installed app, never a browser prompt. That is already your direction of 10-27 (alerts only inside the installed app).
  • E, no strip at all, is a real option I had not drawn.

Not agreed

  • D's job is already done. "Your followed team's current score without a tap" is what the My Team dock (phone) and its rail card (desk) do today, on every tournament page on game day. D (and my C) would put the same score at the top while the dock shows it at the foot.
  • D is C without the score settling in, and its short names ("Rapids", "Cyclones") need a short-name field the product does not have.
  • The height case against B was overstated. On a phone the band scrolls away; what stays is the tabs and the strip (B: 123 px, 18%). At the top of a page B is 29%, inside the 39% budget. B is still the tallest; it is not over budget.
  • E's count was 11 px on a "Scores" tab; tournament pages have no Scores tab, and the floor is 12 px. Both fixed on the E tab (the Schedule tab, 12 px, the live red).

Claude's recommendation, revised

F, your results reel, with the dock that already ships

Your point that scores are mostly entered after games changes the job: the strip is not a live scoreboard, it is "what just finished, and what is next". F does exactly that, holding each result still while it is read, and moves only when there is news: one pass when the page opens, then a new result when one lands. Your own team stays in the My Team dock (phone) and rail card (desk), where it already is, so the reel carries everyone else and nothing shows twice. Alerts stay an opt-in inside the installed app.

E (no strip, a count on the Schedule tab) is the fallback if even one line is too much; B only if seeing several games at once on every page is worth 75 px and a tint.

What I need from you

  • If F, the timing: five seconds per result and a 0.4 s fade, as drawn? And one pass on opening, then rest (as drawn), or keep cycling?
  • If E: removing the strip from every page is a bigger call than Stage 1’s restyle (which pages carry it was Stages 2–3). Say so and Part 4 removes it behind the switch and adds the Schedule count.
