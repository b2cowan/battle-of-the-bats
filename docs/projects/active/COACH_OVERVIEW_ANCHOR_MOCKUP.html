The missing icon, and the card that eats the fold

Two things you spotted on the Overview after Pass 2 shipped. The first has a definite answer that isn't the one I expected. The second is measurably real, and worst on a phone — every render below is drawn at its true pixel height so the argument is visible rather than asserted.

Built 2026-08-12 — and one thing below was wrong

Both recommendations are built. Measured after: the everyday next-event card is 144px on a desktop (was 228) and 199px on a phone (was 267); on a real coach's 390px phone the first tile row moved from 40px below the fold to 14px above it.

The deviation: Option B below proposes dropping the date from the card because the team bar already shows it. I checked that after you approved it, and it's wrong — the bar's right-hand slot is hidden the moment the phone header collapses on scroll, so the “duplicate” disappears exactly when a scrolling coach would need it. The card keeps its full date. The height saving comes entirely from removing two stacked rows, not from removing information — which is the better trade anyway.

Question 1

Why doesn't Overview have an icon?

Because the approved mockup drew it that way — deliberately. Every sibling screen in that mockup carries an icon tile; Overview is rendered title-only, and the shared header component records the exception in as many words. So this is a decision that shipped, not a page I missed.

But the plan contradicted itself, and nobody noticed. Its own inventory lists Overview among the “7 pages with no icon tile”, and the Pass 2 punch list says to put the section icon on those seven pages. I gave six of them an icon and left Overview alone, because the mockup and the component both said to. Your eye landed on exactly the seam.

My recommendation — give it the icon

The case for leaving it off is that an icon is a section marker, and the front door has no sibling to be distinguished from. That reasoning holds if you see the screens side by side. A coach never does — they see them one after another, and in sequence the thing that registers is the title sliding 44px left and back again. Consistency was the entire point of the ruling, and one exception in forty reads as an oversight rather than a decision. Yours did.

Question 2

The next-event card is too tall

Agreed, and it's measurable. That card is “the One Thing” — a ruled feature that picks exactly one anchor per visit and answers what should I do now. The feature is right. Its height is the problem, and the height comes from stacking five rows — kicker, headline, place, button, secondary link — to carry one fact and one action.

Measured on the running app, on the same team as your screenshot. The phone saves less than the desktop because at 390px the headline and the button don't fit on one line, so the button wraps to its own full-width row — which is the right fallback: a squeezed primary is worse than a wrapped one.

My recommendation — Option B

It recovers the fold on a phone, which is the actual complaint, without flattening the one card the portal deliberately makes unmissable. It keeps the accent rail, the kicker and the display headline — the things that make it read as an instruction — and removes only the stacking: the action moves up onto the headline row instead of sitting under it, and the place and the schedule link share one quiet line.

It also fixes the duplication rather than tolerating it: the team bar keeps when, so the card leads with what and prints the day once, in the kicker where “in 2 days” already is.

If you pick these

What I'd change, and what I wouldn't touch

  • The icon is a one-line change plus a note in the ruling explaining why the original exception was dropped, so it doesn't get “restored” later as a regression.
  • The anchor is a layout change to one shared card. It has six situations — welcome, game day, next event, a season question, a quiet week, pre-season — so all six need walking, not just the one in your screenshot. Game day carries a score and a lineup-ready chip and will legitimately be taller; that's the shape earning its height.
  • Not touching: which anchor appears and when. That resolver is a tested, ruled piece of logic and none of this changes its answer — only how much room the answer takes.

Both are presentation-only: no new data, no route changes, nothing that touches what a finished season shows.
