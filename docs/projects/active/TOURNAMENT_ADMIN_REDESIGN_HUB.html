Tournaments · every screen an organizer works in · a deep dive

The tournament admin, walked end to end, on a phone and at a desk

On 28 September every tournament screen took the coaches portal's look, Warm and Dark, with the same layouts and words. This walk reads the screens as they now stand, in the order an organizer meets them: create, set up, teams, the schedule, game day, the volunteers, after the event. For each station you get what's on the screen and its numbers, how it feels to use, and a shape question. Stage 1 (game day) is drawn at true size on its own tab. You read both against the product and tell me what to change or add.

How to read this

  • Thirteen stations. Each has as built (captures and browser-measured numbers), my read (what works, what gets in the way, what I'd change) and one or two shape questions with Keep · Reshape · Not yet and a notes box.
  • Then standing back (nine rules across the stations), the stages, the asks (A1–A10, a checkbox per option) and your rulings to paste back.
  • Every red flag, green chip and NEW / RESTYLED / UNCHANGED tag opens its explanation when clicked. A a guess tag marks a claim that rests on how a director will behave, not on a measurement.
  • The Warm / Dark switch at the top turns every drawn screen and, where one exists, every capture.

Where I walked

  • A read-only browser probe at 390×844 and 360×780 (phones, touch), 768×1024 (tablet, touch) and 1440×900 (desk), Warm and Dark. It opened windows and menus and closed them; every write was refused.
  • Numbers come from the browser's own geometry. Captures are the browser's own pictures of the same pages.
  • The June persona walks were re-checked against today's code. A June row counts as open only where the code still does it: of 76 organizer rows, 26 are fixed, 9 partly, 6 changed and 32 open; of 23 volunteer rows, 14 fixed, 4 partly, 1 changed, 3 open.

Creating a tournament: two wizards, and only the first asks about diamonds

An organizer creates their event, then next year's.

New tournament · the rail's + or Tournaments → New Tournament · and the first-run setup a new organization sees once
On the screen, as built

From the Tournaments list, New Tournament opens a window. An organization that has run an event first sees Start blank or Reuse a previous tournament setup; then five steps: the tournament (name, dates, style, address), divisions, a welcome message, venues, review. Nothing saves until Save setup on the last step, which is the house rule for creating. At a desk a live phone-shaped preview of the public page sits beside it.

The first-run setup a brand-new organization walks through asks, for each park, how many fields or diamonds it has, and creates Diamond 1…N so the schedule has lanes. That was June's J1-028 fix. The New Tournament wizard, the path every event after the first takes, asks for a street address only. Neither asks for fees; that was ruled optional on 16 June, and the launch check agrees.

create, blank: 5 clicks (Next ×4, Save)+1 when past events exist (Start blank)phone window 366×384 at the pre-stepPlus notice: no styling at all

How it feels to use: my read

What works

  • Create asks, and nothing is half-made. One save at the end, a preview at a desk, every step skippable.
  • Reuse is offered at the moment it matters, when an organizer starts their second event.

What gets in the way

  • The second event is set up worse than the first. A three-diamond park created here becomes one scheduling lane until the organizer finds Venues & facilities.
  • A Tournament-plan organizer's "reuse needs Plus" line has no box, colour or edge: its styling is defined nowhere, so it reads as stray text.

What I'd change

  • One set of creation questions for both wizards, diamonds included (Stage 5). The notice's styling is a defect for the list below (A8).
Q 1.1 · Stage 5

Should the New Tournament wizard ask the first-run setup's question about fields and diamonds?

It is the one creation answer that changes the schedule the generator builds.

Recommendation: Reshape, in Stage 5: both wizards ask the same questions.

What rides on it: the Venues screen stays where facilities are managed; the wizard only seeds Diamond 1…N, as the first-run setup already does.

Divisions, venues and rules: a sold-out division painted as a failure

An organizer adds divisions, parks with their diamonds, and the rulebook.

Setup → Divisions · Venues & facilities · Rules & resources
On the screen, as built

Divisions is a card per division with a capacity gauge; a full one reads FULL in the danger colour, and every card carries twin Edit and Delete icons whether or not games are scheduled. Adding one is two clicks (Add, Save). Venues & facilities is where a park's diamonds are managed; it has no help link. Rules & resources saves each section on its own, adds a point instantly, and guards against leaving with a three-way dialog.

add a division: 2 clicksEdit / Delete: 35×25 each, side by sideRules' Browse samples / Add section: 36×26Venues' export: 32×32

How it feels to use: my read

What works

  • Deleting is careful: a division with games asks again with real counts; a venue with a played game can only be renamed.

What gets in the way

  • Full is good news, drawn as bad news, and Delete sits beside Edit at equal weight on a live event's cards; errors come in the browser's own alert box.
  • Four setup screens, four ways of saving: an organizer can't learn one "did that stick?".

What I'd change

  • Stage 5: full reads as good news; Delete moves into the division's own editor (delete is never a row action); one save model, Event settings'.
Q 3.1 · Stage 5

Should the setup screens move to one save model, autosave with the fading "Saved", and put Delete inside each record's editor?

Recommendation: Reshape, in Stage 5. a guess on how much Rules' leave-guard is used; a first director would tell us.

What rides on it: the "edit autosaves, create asks" rule already says it; adding a division or a venue still asks.

G6 · Game day stays current by itself

The board refreshes every 30 seconds while it is on screen. Results and Check-in fetch once, so a scorekeeper's submission or a second gate volunteer's check-in appears only after a reload, while Results' empty state promises "no refresh needed". After: both refresh the same way the board does, and the false line goes. Nothing new is drawn: no "live" badge and no "updated" clock; the page simply stays right. An open score editor is never refreshed out from under the organizer.

4. Standing back — rules across the stations

  1. Name the event once. The pinned event header is the event's identity; a page names only itself.
  2. One status, one rule, one word. A phase and a game state are computed once and spelled once.
  3. A row opens its own record. A game opens that game; a team opens that team. Never a list you search again.
  4. Open on what needs you. A working screen opens on the work (unscored, waiting, not arrived), in every division, and one tap shows the rest.
  5. Game day stays current by itself. Every screen an organizer watches on the weekend refreshes on its own, the way the dashboard does.
  6. The control you use most is the biggest. The daily job gets the full row or a worded 44px button; a rare or destructive one moves into the record's sheet.
  7. A phone row fits the phone. Nothing ends past the right edge; a row that can't fit becomes a card.
  8. One way to do one thing. One creation question set, one reuse flow, one archive sentence, one name per tool.
  9. Say the true thing. No restore that isn't there, no "no refresh needed", no "email will be sent" when it won't be.
