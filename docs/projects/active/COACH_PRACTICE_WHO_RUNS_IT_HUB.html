Rulings

Decisions A–K

Each has a recommendation. Press the answer, add a note if the shape should differ, then "Build the paste-back" and paste it into chat. Answers stay in this browser only.

A · Audience

Do helpers belong in "Coaches and helpers"?

The product defines a helper as "runs a station at practice. Sees the plan and the players in front of them." A practice plan is the one thing a helper is for.

Recommendation: yes. If no: the option reads "Coaches" (head + assistants) and a helper is reached only by "Named in this plan" or "Everyone on staff".
B · Default

Which audience is selected when the sheet opens?

"Named in this plan" whenever it reaches at least one person; otherwise "Coaches and helpers". After the first send, the sheet remembers the team's last choice.

What rides on it: a Tuesday habit is one tap. Alternative: always open on "Coaches and helpers" — simpler, but it makes the personalized message the second choice.
C · Merge

When a linked word is merged into an unlinked one, does the link carry across?

The coach merged "Jen O." into "Jen" because they are one person. Losing the identity on the way would silently break "that's you" on every plan that used the loser. The reverse merge (unlinked into linked) keeps the winner's link either way.

Recommendation: carry it. Edge: two linked words for two different people merged together — refuse with a sentence ("Jen and Craig are different people"), never pick one.
D · Channels

Bell on, push on by default, email off by default?

Time-sensitive, so push defaults on (like chat mentions and score updates). Email stays off unless the person turns it on in Account › Notifications — a practice plan two hours out is a phone thing.

Caveat: Android push on production has an open diagnosis; the bell + deep link is the floor. Re-test the device before ship.
E · Fallback

Delete the name match, or keep it as a fallback for unlinked words?

Keeping it means "that's you" keeps working for the teams where the spelling happens to line up — and nobody on those teams ever learns the link exists. Deleting it means the honest path is the only path: the "a name only" line and the manager's link control.

Recommendation: delete it.
F · Order

Build the link first, then the strip, then Send — all after the Stage 5 commit?

Send's best audience needs the link. The five files this lands on are the five Stage 5 is rewriting; the row component it introduces is what the "you" mark rides on.

Alternative: ship Send alone first with two audiences (no "Named") — faster, but it ships the message without the sentence that makes it worth sending.
G · Placement

"You're on …" — under the where-line, above the sheet, only when the viewer is named?

One support-size line, absent for anyone not named, never an empty strip, never on paper. The sheet stays the page (screen 1, before/after).

Alternatives weighed: a mark on the rows alone (no strip — but then a phone reader still scrolls to find them); a strip for everyone with "You're not on a station" (noise for the manager).
H · Changed since

Show "Changed since you sent it" on the toolbar?

The practice has no plan-only "last edited" stamp; its general one moves on any schedule edit (moving the start time would light the line). A true version needs a plan-only stamp — small, but a second stamp to keep honest.

Recommendation: defer. Re-send is the answer; revisit if coaches ask for it.
I · The stamp

Store the last send on the practice (four columns), not a history table?

When, by whom, which audience, how many — the same idiom as "shared with families at / by". Nothing reads a history of sends; the toolbar reads the last one.

If a history is wanted later (who was sent what, when — for a dispute), it is a small table then; nothing here prevents it.
J · Email

“Also email them” sends the coach’s email regardless of the person’s notification settings and a paused account?

The owner’s ask (2026-09-17): an explicit action by the coach, so it bypasses opt-outs. The ground it stands on already exists twice: a dues reminder skips the family opt-out because a bill is transactional (owner ruling 2026-08-18), and an @mention pierces the master pause because a person addressed you. The bell and push keep following each person’s settings — only the email the coach chose to send is exempt.

Recommendation: yes, with two boundaries drawn in: the email names the coach as the sender and its footer says why it arrived; and it is off by default, remembered with the audience, so it is always a choice the coach made this time or last time. What it is not: a way for the platform to email staff — only this button, only this audience.
K · The email’s body

Does the email carry the plan’s outline, or only the message?

The outline — times, blocks, stations, who — with the reader’s rows marked, then a button to the whole plan. An assistant on a train reads the shape of the evening without signing in; the drills, coaching points and groups stay behind the button (they are the sheet, and the sheet is the app).

Alternative: the message and the button only — simpler, and it makes the email a doorbell rather than a document. Not proposed: the whole sheet in the email — it is the printed page, and it names children.

Rulings this project stands on

Product-manager brief

A station knows who runs it

Drawn 2026-09-17, awaiting rulings A–I. Builds after the Stage 5 practices commit lands.

What a coach can do that they couldn't before

  • Tag a station with a person, not just a word. The Staff picker on a block or station opens with the team's own people first — head coach, assistants, helpers, manager, treasurer, each with their word — and picking one links the tag to their account. The tag still reads as the coach's own label ("Craig"); outside instructors stay a plain word, exactly as today.
  • Fix the old names once. In the staff tag manager, "Jen" can be linked to Jen with one control; every practice already written that says "Jen" then knows who that is.
  • Send the plan to the right people. A Send to staff button on a finished plan offers three audiences — Named in this plan · Coaches and helpers · Everyone on staff — each showing who it reaches by name, with a preview of the message. Managers and treasurers stop receiving practice plans unless the coach chooses "Everyone". The toolbar then says when and to whom it went; sending again is always allowed.
  • Email them too, when it matters. A tick on the same sheet sends the coach’s own email — the message, the outline with their rows marked, a button to the plan — to everyone in the audience, whatever their notification settings say. It is the coach’s explicit act, on the same footing as a dues reminder; the email names the coach and says why it arrived.

What an assistant sees that they didn't before

  • A phone notification the moment the coach sends it — "Tuesday's practice plan is ready — you're on Footwork ladder and Close control. Arrive by 5:45 p.m. for 6:00 p.m." — that opens the plan.
  • On the plan, a quiet "You're on …" line under the title listing their blocks and stations in order, each a jump; those rows wear a small you mark. Nobody else's plan page changes.
  • On the field, "that's you" / "You're running" fires reliably on every station and block that is theirs. Today it works only when the tag's spelling exactly equals a member-list field that is often blank — it fails silently for most teams and nobody knows.

Why it matters

The practice plan is the one document a whole staff shares in the hour before a practice. Today the only way an assistant learns a plan exists is to go looking, and the only way they learn which station is theirs is to read for their own name. The notification closes the first gap; the identity link closes the second — and makes the notification worth sending, because it can name their stations.

What was deliberately not proposed, and why

  • An automatic reminder before every practice. A scheduled send is a second decision (a cron, a quiet-hours policy, what "before" means); the button is the one the coach controls.
  • Marking "you" on the printed sheet. Paper is for everyone at the table.
  • Any change to what a helper or assistant can open. A tag is a label, never a grant — the 2026-07-31 ruling holds. Linking gives a person nothing they couldn't already read.
  • Keeping the old name-matching as a fallback. It would keep working for some teams and hide from everyone that the link was missing.

Customer impact

Every team with more than one adult on staff, every practice. The coach's cost is one button and, once, linking a few tags. The assistant's benefit is the difference between "there's a plan somewhere" and "I'm on Footwork ladder at 6:10, here's the sheet".

Priority

After the Stage 5 build commits (it rewrites the same screens); before the next practices-area work. The link is the first half to build — the send's best audience depends on it.

Success criteria

  • A linked assistant opens a plan they are named on and sees the "You're on …" line without hunting; on the field every one of their stations says "that's you" — by identity, with a blank member name.
  • "Named in this plan" reaches exactly the linked people on the plan and lists the unlinked words as not sent; a manager receives nothing unless "Everyone" was chosen.
  • The push arrives on the owner's Android device (the open diagnosis re-tested before ship).
Full assessment

A station knows who runs it — plan

Read-only walk of the code on 2026-09-17. The file docs/projects/active/COACH_PRACTICE_WHO_RUNS_IT_PLAN.md is the record; this tab is the same content, readable.

Build gate. The Stage 5 (Paper & the field) build must be committed first — this lands on the same five files it is rewriting: the plan page, the run page, the station view, the editor, and the plan module. Written against Stage 5's ruled shape (P7's one list keyed by station), not the tree on 2026-09-17.

1 · Assessment

Two asks on 2026-09-17: notify the staff when a plan is written so they can prepare before the start time; and link the staff tags on stations to real portal users so an assistant on a phone sees the whole practice and which blocks and stations are theirs. Widened on the second exchange to three audiences — everyone on staff · all coaches · everyone named in the plan.

Read from the code, they are one project with a fixed order: the identity link is the load-bearing half. "That's you" on the run screen is a name match against an optional field, so it already fails silently for the most natural inputs; D12's "team coaches offered automatically" was never built. Without the link, "named in the plan" would have to guess by name, and the notification could only say "go read it".

2 · Scope

In: staff tags can be people (one person per tag per team); the picker offers the season's staff first and mints a linked tag on pick; the manager links/unlinks a word; the plan and run screens decide "yours" by identity; a "You're on …" strip for a named viewer; Send to staff with three audiences, a sent stamp, and a new notification type (bell + push, personalized).

Out: a scheduled reminder (a cron, not a button); "you" on paper; links on org-shared staff tags; any change to a helper's access (a tag is a label, never a grant — D12); chat as the channel.

4 · Findings

F01 — "That's you" is a label match against an optional field. The run page compares each resolved staff name with the viewer's member display name, whole string, case-insensitively. That field is nullable and UI-only. "Craig" never equals "Craig Whitfield"; a blank display name can never match. The route's own comment: "a MATCH ON A LABEL, not an identity claim … this can miss."

F02 — D12's "team coaches offered automatically" was never built. The staff library is a plain word list; the picker knows nothing about the team's staff. The plan read already loads the staff list (for the viewer's name and the head coach's) and never hands it to the picker.

F03 — The plan page — the reading surface — has no "yours" marking at all. Only the run screen and its station view mark the viewer's station. The plan page prints every staff line identically. Stage 5 P7 marks every rotation row on the run screen, which is right for the field; the phone-before-practice read happens on the plan page.

F04 — There is no "done" moment; a notification must be an explicit act. The plan autosaves about a second after the last change. Nothing distinguishes "paused" from "finished". The send is a button; it can never be inferred from a save.

F05 — The notification system can already reach exactly these people. Explicit-recipient dispatch resolves through the account, not org membership — the same path "assistant coach joined" uses — so a standalone coach portal and a non-member assistant are reachable. The catalogue is typed as total records, so a new type fails typecheck until every list carries it. Demo orgs are silenced inside dispatch.

F06 — Five staff words exist, ruled "a label that gates nothing." Head coach, assistant coach, team manager, team treasurer, helper (stored since the staff-access pass; derived for older rows). Addressing a notification by the word is not access control — a manager left off the list can still open the plan — so it sits inside the ruling; but it is the first place the word decides anything beyond the row. Said here so it is a decision, not a drift.

F08 — Email today is either a preference or a suppression list; a coach’s addressed email is a third thing. The notification pipeline’s email channel is per-person, per-event, default off. Family email runs through one guarded door that honours an opt-out list and appends an unsubscribe — and dues reminders deliberately stay outside it because a bill is transactional (owner ruling 2026-08-18). A coach pressing Send is neither: a colleague addressing colleagues about tonight. It goes through the plain sender, names the coach, skips the preference and the pause on purpose, and is recorded as such.

F07 — Push to Android on production has an open diagnosis. Bell works; OS push unproven on the owner's installed app; the self-serve device test is on prod. Re-run it before this ships.

5 · The proposal

5.1 The link

  • A staff tag may carry one person (team tags only; never org-shared). The name stays the coach's word; the identity is the id. Still a label, never a grant.
  • Picker: "People on this team" first — every staff member this season with their word — then the team's other words, then "+ Create". Picking a person selects (or mints) their linked tag. Finishes D12.
  • Manager: each row shows who it is or "a name only"; "Link to a person…" / "Unlink". Merge carries the link (C).
  • "Mine" by identity, server-side: the plan read says which blocks and stations carry a tag linked to the viewer. The run screen and station view read that; the name match is deleted (E).
