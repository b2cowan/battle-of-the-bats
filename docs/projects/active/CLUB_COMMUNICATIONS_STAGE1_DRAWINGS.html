Which rows a league admin or registrar sees

  • Every email to a house-league season, and to house-league coaches.
  • Every email to Every family and to All coaches, because house-league people were in it.
  • A pick of coaches made only from house-league coaches.
  • A message to one family sent from a season, as the season’s own list shows today.
  • A board email, only if they sent it or it went to them (tab 8).
  • Never: rep teams’ families, rep coaches, a pick that includes a rep coach, a Families list, or a message to one family sent from Families.

Why names are held back on the wider rows

The registrar is entitled to know the email went out and what it said. That is what stops the double send. They are not entitled to learn which rep family opted out of club email, or the names of rep staff they don’t work with. The family record can’t tell which of its named families are house-league ones, so a wider family email shows a registrar no names at all. The coach record can, so it names their own coaches.

What the owner and admins see

Every row and every name, as the shipped record and the coach record show them. An admin without the Families book sees up to three family names in the Opted out and Didn’t go lines, as a book holder does today, and no household record. The legal check called this low risk and left the narrower choice to you.

Finishing a stopped email

“Send to the ones it missed” is offered to anyone who may send to that same group now, whether or not they sent the first one. A reader who may not, like a registrar looking at a stopped email to every family, sees a plain line in its place: “Morgan Ellis or an admin can send it to the ones it missed.”

Build notes

Visibility is decided from the kind of group an email went to. The names are left out by the server for that reader, never hidden on the screen. The Last sent line in each window follows the same rule. A resend goes back down the path its email came from: the club’s, a season’s, or the coach path.

The Communications switch

You ruled that the switch in a member’s access window becomes this place’s on and off. Two things follow, and both need your yes.

  • It is on by default for league admins and league registrars, as it already is for the owner and admins. Without that they could not open the place your ruling gives them.
  • Off means no group email from anywhere: not from Communications, not from a season’s Email families, not from a list’s button. Those buttons are absent for that person. Otherwise the switch would hide the list and leave the sending. Automatic emails that an action causes still go.
  • The member’s access window says what the switch covers in one line, so turning it off never surprises the owner.
  • If every board seat may email the board (ask 16), the treasurer’s switch is on by default as well.

The “not yours” words, by seat

  • An admin, league admin or registrar whose switch is off: “…Morgan Ellis (owner) can give an admin access.”, naming their role.
  • A staff member or volunteer: “…Morgan Ellis (owner) and the people on the club’s board open it.” The switch can’t give them the place, because they have nobody to write to.
  • The lime button goes back to that person’s own home: Overview, Accounting or House league.

What no seat gains

  • No list or record shows an email address. They carry names only, and a family with no name on file reads “A family”.
  • A league admin or registrar can write to exactly who they can today, plus house-league coaches as the coach email drawing proposed.
  • The one widening is the one you ruled: an admin can email rep teams’ families and every family without the Families book.

A club on a smaller plan

A choice the plan doesn’t carry is not drawn. A club with family email and no coach email has one choice, so its button reads Email families and opens the window at once (tab 2). The plans themselves don’t change in this work.
