Bookkeeping, as the accountant who has to close from it

A stand-in review for FieldLogicHQ's own books. I walked every journey on the real screens as the platform's test admin (desktop first, then a phone), read the plans, and checked the claims that mattered against what the product actually does.

Verdict

Usable with fixes for recording expenses. Not yet usable as the company's books.

On the things an auditor cares about, the expense side is stronger than most small-business tools. Every correction keeps the earlier version and its receipt. A void keeps the record. The database itself refuses to delete or overwrite anything. The accountant package ships every receipt with a fingerprint. Recording a purchase is quick, and I could always find what I'd entered by supplier or date. But I could not record a single owner contribution, draw or reimbursement: every save is refused, whatever you type. For a sole proprietor with no business bank account, that ledger is how every expense gets paid for. Beyond that, there is no GST/HST figure anyone could file from, no way to close a period, and no income. The only working store is the development one that test sessions (including this one) write into. Fix the owner ledger, decide where the real books live, and start capturing GST/HST properly before the May-to-October backfill. Do those, and I would close October from it.

Stop-the-line
  • Owner transfers cannot be saved at all (contribution, draw, reimbursement).
  • "Permanently delete" is on every record but can never succeed, and it contradicts the audit trail.
  • No production home for the real books yet; the dev store is the only live one.
Genuinely good
  • Corrections and voids keep full history, receipts included.
  • Receipt files are checked for real type; a fake PDF is refused.
  • Double-clicking Save creates one entry, not two.
  • The package states plainly what it does not contain.

The journeys, walked

J1–J8 follow the order of the brief. Seven test purchases were created, each starting "ACCT-REVIEW", and every one is voided. The full list is at the end.

J1

Record a purchase

Works · tax is weak

All four kinds saved first time: a $113.00 subscription with $13.00 HST and a PDF receipt; a US$25.00 invoice entered as C$34.75 with its conversion source; a domain renewal with no receipt; and a toner purchase on a personal card, paid two days after it was bought. The saved record reads cleanly, showing the amount, both dates, how it was paid, the receipt, the tax and a version list.

Worked
  • One amount for CAD. Picking USD reveals the CAD amount and the conversion source, and nothing else.
  • "Payment date is different" is a tidy way to keep incurred and paid dates apart.
  • "You can save now and add the receipt later": the missing receipt goes straight onto the attention list.
  • The phone's own date picker on mobile; the form fits a 390-pixel screen with no sideways scroll.
Didn't, or made me think
  • Tax sits behind "More details" and defaults to "unknown". The fast path leaves tax blank on every entry.
  • There is one tax box. You can't tell GST/HST (claimable) from PST or QST (not claimable), and there's nowhere for the supplier's GST/HST number.
  • The USD conversion note must contain a date written 2026-10-16 and a "specific" word. You find that out from a generic error.
  • The category list still offers the old test labels "B2 QA" and "QA verification", and "+ Add a category" turns any typo into a permanent category.
  • "Business bank" and "Business card" are offered, though the revenue plan records that no business bank account exists.
J2

Find a receipt later from a vendor name or a rough date

Works · no amount search
  • "datastack" (lower case) and "toner" (a word from the purpose) each found the right purchase. So did a 13–17 October date range.
  • The receipt opens straight from the record. Each version keeps its own receipt link.
  • "Needs attention" listed the missing receipt. Attaching it through "Correct expense" kept both versions.
  • Searching "34.75" or "113" finds nothing. A card-statement line is an amount and a date, so that is the question I ask most.
  • The date filter always includes the older entries whose dates are "to review", whatever range you pick.
  • Attaching a late receipt is a full correction with a typed reason. That's fine for the audit trail, but heavy for the commonest follow-up there is.
J3

Correct a mistake, then void

Works · history kept

One correction changed the amount (113.00 → 124.30), date, category and tax (13.00 → 14.30), with a reason. The record then showed versions 1 and 2, each with its receipt. Voiding needed a reason (a blank one was blocked) and left a version 3 marked "Voided" that stays in the list. The totals dropped it at once, and no further correction is offered. My test script swapped the two dates on the personal-card purchase; fixing that through the screen made a good second test.

  • The version list shows only amount, date and reason. That the category went from Software to Professional services, and the tax from 13.00 to 14.30, is visible in the export but not on screen.
  • After a void, the one button left is Permanently delete. I confirmed read-only that the bookkeeping database grants the app no right to delete or overwrite. The button can therefore never succeed; at best it shows "Unable to delete expense". I did not press it.
J4

Owner contribution, draw, reimbursement

Broken
No owner transfer can be saved.

I tried a $500.00 contribution, a $200.00 draw and a $56.49 reimbursement linked to the personal-card purchase. All three were refused with "Check the date, amount and required movement details". I captured what the screen sent each time: a valid date, amount and type. The server's check on the transfer's internal reference rejects every reference the screen creates; I reproduced that check on its own. So nothing in this journey (record, correct, void, over-reimbursement guard) can be walked. It also explains why the recovery proof still "needs a real owner-transfer row".

  • The error appears at the top of the page, not beside Save, and nothing scrolls to it. You press Save and appear to get no response. The expense form behaves the same way.
  • The Ledger is a list of expenses and transfers. It shows no balance, so nothing answers "how much does the business owe me?"
  • The reimbursement picker offers only purchases paid personally, which is the right design.

J6

The accountant package, read as the filing accountant

Complete, not self-explaining

It takes four clicks: Reports, period, then Accountant package. The download held an expense file, an owner-transfer file (empty), a receipt index with a fingerprint per file, all eight receipt files, and a scope note. The note says honestly that bank statements, Stripe income, fees, refunds, payouts and reconciliation are not included. The package refuses to build if a receipt is missing, which is right.

  • The expense file holds every version of every purchase (14 rows for 7 purchases). The accountant has to know to keep only "current" rows, and nothing says so on the first line.
  • Receipt files are named with random ID strings rather than "2026-10-15 Datastack 34.75", so every lookup goes through the index.
  • It contains columns an outsider can't use: a receipt link into our admin site, and "created by" as an internal ID rather than a name.
  • There is no summary page (totals by category, tax by type, personally paid, open items), which is exactly what I'd read first.
J7

Month-end close

Stops after the export

As far as the module allows: clear the attention list, set the period to custom dates, check the report, then download the package. Then it stops. Nowhere on screen does the word close, lock or reconcile appear. There is no "closed through" date, no opening or closing balance and no income. Any purchase in a month I've filed can be corrected next week, and the next package will quietly differ from the one the accountant used. The history shows the change, but nothing flags it.

The six questions

1. Is the flow what I expected?

Mostly yes for expenses. Several things match how I work: a record reads first and you correct the whole thing with a reason; a void is a separate, reasoned act; the receipt sits next to the entry; one attention list for loose ends; and an export that states its own scope. Four places made me stop and think:

  • Tax is treated as an optional detail rather than the second most important number on a receipt.
  • "Tax review status" (Pending review, No tax recorded, Reviewed): who reviews, and against what?
  • "Ledger" means something specific to an accountant: accounts with balances. This one is an activity list.
  • "Sources" is a developer preview labelled "B3 · fixture import preview" with a "synthetic preview file". The owner should never see that.

2. Is it enough for my daily needs?

I could
  • Record any purchase, CAD or USD, with or without a receipt
  • Attach a receipt later and find it by name or date
  • Correct and void with a full trail
  • See what is missing
  • Export a period with its receipts
I couldn't
  • Record any owner movement
  • Get a GST/HST figure
  • Find a purchase by amount
  • See on screen what a correction changed
  • Close a month, or reconcile to a statement
  • See any income

What I'd get wrong on a tired Friday afternoon: skip "More details" and leave a month of tax as unknown. Paste "1,200.00" from a statement and get a message that doesn't say why. Type a conversion note without a date in the right shape. Re-key a subscription I already entered. Press Save on a transfer and decide it worked because nothing happened. Pick a stale "QA verification" category. And sooner or later, press "Permanently delete".

4. Are the outstanding items the right direction?

Mostly the right items, in the wrong order. The full verdicts are in their own table. The short version: fix the owner ledger and bring the accountant in before the walks and before Stripe. And don't let the first close wait for Stripe.

5. Is the reporting adequate?

For expenses by category and missing receipts, yes. For tax, owner movements and income, no. Details are in the report table.

6. What else do I want?

The wish list is sorted into three groups. Most of it is small. The two big items are a usable GST/HST picture and the Stripe income side, and only the first is urgent.

Recommended order, and where I'd push back

  1. This week, all small: fix transfer saving and show errors where Save is; take "Permanently delete" off records; hide the Sources preview; add last-month and last-quarter periods.
  2. Bring in the outside accountant (owner-movement review plus the accountant's review), with the questions below, before building any tax fields.
  3. Decide the business account and where the real books live. Set up the production store. Start backups with a copy kept somewhere separate, and prove a restore from that copy.
  4. Build what the accountant's answers call for: GST/HST capture, the package summary page, the owner balance, copy-as-new, the duplicate warning, and search by amount.
  5. One owner walk covering the five journeys, Stage 1, Stage 2 and the owner ledger, in one sitting.
  6. Backfill May–October, then the first real close (October, expenses and owner money only) with a "closed through" date. Then the production recovery proof, then S9a.
  7. Stripe: the contract, import, reconciliation and case walk, plus the GST/HST return worksheet, all before the first live charge. Then S9b.
  8. The later list.

Push-back on the owner's own plan

  • The 23 October ruling to keep Permanent Delete. The database was built append-only on purpose, and that is the right call; the button can't work against it. Keep Void, and drop Delete or fence it hard.
  • The first close waiting for Stripe. There is no income yet. Making the first close depend on Stripe import and reconciliation ties your books to Club payments going live.
  • Tax as a note only. That's reasonable while you're unregistered, but you're about to key six months of receipts. Capture the split now and calculate claims later.
  • "Bank reconciliation" assumes a business bank account that, by the revenue plan's own record, doesn't exist yet. Decide the account first.
  • The plan calls the Stage 3 transfer draft "built for the group walkthrough". It can't save a transfer. The plan says more than the code delivers, so the walk should be the check, not the status line.
  • Walks before fixes. Walking the expense journeys now is fine, but don't schedule the ledger walk until a transfer can be saved.

Questions for the real outside accountant

  1. GST/HST: will you ask for the backdated 1 May 2026 effective date, and if granted, are tax credits on purchases from then claimable? Annual or quarterly filing? Regular method or the Quick Method? The Quick Method changes what we need to capture.
  2. US software suppliers: which of ours charge GST/HST because they're registered under the simplified regime? Should we give them our GST/HST number once we're registered? How do you want tax handled when such a supplier charges it?
  3. Which province are we filing in? Do you need PST or QST tracked separately from GST/HST?
  4. For USD purchases, is the CAD amount on the card statement acceptable evidence, or do you want a Bank of Canada rate?
  5. With no business bank account, what arrangement do you recommend? Should personal-card purchases be recorded as owner contributions or as amounts to be reimbursed?
  6. Cash or accrual for the T2125? How should annual plans paid upfront by clubs be treated at year-end?
  7. Above what amount should a purchase be treated as capital rather than expensed? Do you want a capital cost schedule from us?
  8. What file do you want: CSV, Excel, or an import format for your own software? What belongs on the summary page?
  9. Records: how long do we keep them? Are digital-only receipts fine? Is storage in a US region plus a readable copy in Canada acceptable? (The plan already lists the location as open.)
  10. How often should we close (monthly or quarterly)? Once you've used a period, how do you want later corrections handled?
  11. Stripe: is our income the application fee only, and how is GST/HST charged on that fee? Do Stripe's own fees carry tax we can claim?
  12. Home office, phone and internet: do you want us to record a business-use share during the year, or will you do it at year-end?

What I could not verify

  • Production. It has no bookkeeping store wired up, so everything here is the development site and store. A production deploy may behave differently, especially access and storage.
  • Real Stripe data. Only the fixture preview exists. Nothing about income, fees, refunds or payouts was exercised.
  • A real filing. Whether the package satisfies the actual outside accountant is a guess until they read one. I'm standing in for them.
  • The owner ledger beyond the save. Correcting, voiding, the reimbursement limit and the owner balance are all untested, because nothing could be saved.
  • Permanent delete. I checked the database rights read-only and did not press the button. The exact message you'd see is inferred from the code.
  • Backup and restore. Not run. I relied on the plan's record of the 24 October development rehearsal.
  • The "Close without saving?" question. My script's Cancel discarded without showing it. That may be my script, so check it in your walk.
  • Devices. One desktop browser engine and a phone-sized emulation, not a real phone or a screen reader.
  • Tax rules. The points about the simplified regime, the Quick Method and records location are an accountant's working knowledge. They're phrased as questions for that reason.
  • A moving target. The Bookkeeping work under review is largely uncommitted and shared with other sessions, so what I walked may change.
