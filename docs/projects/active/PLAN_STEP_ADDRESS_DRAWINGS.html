Plan step: the billing address as a step, not a window

Today a club owner who picks a paid plan on first-time setup gets a Billing address window laid over the plan screen. The coach sign-up page asks the same six questions as a step on the page. Same questions, same purpose, two different looks, and nothing in the records says why. This page draws the plan screen asking in a step, the way the coach sign-up does, and keeps the window for the screens where a window is the right tool. Nothing about the fields, the wording, the tax or the price changes. All names and addresses are made-up examples. Everything is a proposal until you rule on the asks in the last tab.

One rule: a page that runs in steps asks in a step; a screen you are working on asks in a window

Both halves of that rule already exist in the product. The window is right when you are in the middle of something (Plan & billing, an upgrade drawer, a plan move) and a full page change would throw the screen away. A step is right when the page is already a short walk through numbered steps. First-time setup, Run a tournament step 3, is exactly that walk, and it is the one place that breaks the rule.

The button and the fold

One of the first-run rules says the main button is in view on a 390 × 844 phone and a 1366 × 768 laptop. Six fields plus the heading, step line and a two-line explanation add up to roughly 780px on a computer and a little over 900px on a phone, so Continue to checkout sits below the fold on both. The coach sign-up’s Address step has the same height and was ruled that way, so this drawing matches it. Whether the rule gives way for a six-field form is Ask 3.

Asks

Three decisions. The first sets the rule. The other two are details the design review found: how the step line treats the new screen, and whether a six-field form has to keep its button above the fold.

Ask 1Where does the billing address appear?

Drawn in tabs 1 and 2.

Ask 2What does the step line say on the new screen?

Drawn in tabs 2 and 3. The design review changed this recommendation: the shipped three-label line already fills a 390 phone, so a fourth label does not fit on one line.

Ask 3Does the button have to be in view on a six-field screen?

Shown in tab 4. The first-run rule says the main button is in view on a 390 × 844 phone and a 1366 × 768 laptop. This screen cannot meet it, and neither can the coach sign-up’s Address step.
