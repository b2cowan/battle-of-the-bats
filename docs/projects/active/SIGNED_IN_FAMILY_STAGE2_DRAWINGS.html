Signed-in family accounts, Stage 2. A tournament coach keeps their account at registration, unchanged. These drawings tidy what surrounds it: the emails a new coach gets, the screen they finish on, the address that rides inside portal links, a limit on the form, and an old claim link that goes. Every pin opens the ask it belongs to; every tag explains itself.

View

A new coach gets one email, and it tells the truth

Today a coach registering for the first time gets two emails within seconds. The first says their portal account “is ready” when it cannot be opened yet. The second, from FieldLogicHQ, asks them to confirm their email. Stage 2 makes them one email: the registration, with the confirm button inside it.

What the code says, against the plan

  1. The confirm email is ours, not the sign-in service’s. It is “Confirm your FieldLogicHQ email”, sent only on the live site. Both emails are ours to merge.
  2. The registration email is sent before the account exists. The organizer gets the entry first, on purpose, so a failed account never loses a registration. The account is made a moment later, which is why “your account is ready” is never true when it arrives. The build keeps the organizer-first order and sends the coach’s one email after the account step.
  3. “Your account is ready” goes to every coach, not only new ones. A coach who already has an account, and a manager registering for someone else’s team, get the same sentence (tab 2).
  4. The confirm button works for one hour. After that it opens sign-in with “That link has expired or was already used. Sign in, or send yourself a new one.” and an “Email me a new link” button. So a keepsake email with an old button does not dead-end.

After · a coach who already has an account

The finish screen says what’s really next

This is the screen a new coach sees after pressing Register. It already tells them to open the link we sent. What it gets wrong is the big button, “Create Coaches Portal Account”, for the account they made one step earlier, and a row asking them to “Set a password”. Pressed before confirming, that button leads to a sign-in that says “Confirm your email before signing in”.

Settled by the code, so not asked

  • A coach who is already signed in never sees this screen. Register takes them straight to the team’s tournament page in their portal, with its welcome.
  • An invite-only event has no form. Its Register address goes to the tournament’s home page.
  • The promise is true. On the live site a new coach cannot sign in until they confirm. The registration stands either way: it is with the organizer before the account is made.
  • No other project redraws this screen. Public tournament pages Stage 1 only recoloured it for the light ground. The card above the form (closing date, spots taken) belongs to that project and is not drawn here. This stage owns the finish screen.

No email address inside a portal link

Every tournament coach email that links to the portal writes the coach’s address into the web address. That covers the registration email, the waitlist email, the accepted email, the schedule email, the night-before reminder and the organizer’s invitation. A forwarded email, a shared screenshot or a browser history shows it. Stage 2 takes it out of all of them, not only the registration email.

Nothing on this tab or the next two is marked unchanged, so the view switch is hidden there.

What the address does today

  1. It fills in the email box on the join page and on sign-in. A typing saving.
  2. It lets the join page tell a signed-out visitor “You’re already set up. Sign in to see Lakeshore Lightning.” The page only answers when the address matches the registration, so the address is the proof. Without it, the page cannot know which screen to show.
  3. It names the invited address when someone opens the link signed in as somebody else. “This invite is for sam.rivera@example.com, but you’re signed in as…”

A limit on the form, and an old door that goes

The tournament Register form is the only public form with no limit on how often it can be sent. Each send puts a team in the organizer’s list and emails the organizer. Stage 2 adds the same limit the family forms have, with the same words.

Goes, with nothing to draw · the old tournament-team claim link

  • A link that let a tournament coach claim a paid team page. No email or screen has sent one for months. Only a developer tool can make one.
  • What goes: the claim page, its old address, the developer tool and its seed, and the claim branch inside team checkout. The organizer’s “invitation” for a team they add by hand is a different, live thing and stays.
  • No database change. The table of past claims stays, because Tournament Chat reads it to work out which coaches are in a tournament’s chat. Nothing else points at it. This corrects the plan, which expected a column to be dropped.

Your calls

Ruled 2026-11-01: all five A, as recommended. The build follows the A answers below.

Five calls. Pick an answer for each, add a note if you like, then copy and paste them back. Already ruled and not asked again: a tournament coach keeps their account at registration; tournaments stay public; the team pay link stays as a sent link; no other link is sent to a family.

1 · The one email: what leads

Merging the two emails means one of them leads. The coach keeps this email as their record of the registration; the confirm button inside it stops working after an hour and then offers a new link.

2 · What replaces the address in portal links

The address does a real job on the join page: it decides whether a signed-out visitor is asked to sign in or to create an account.

3 · The finish screen for a new coach

Whether the screen keeps a big button, and what the title says.

4 · The form’s limit: shared with the family forms, or its own?

The family forms share one budget on purpose: one visitor gets 30 sends an hour across all of them. They also share a ceiling of 150 sends every five minutes across everyone.

5 · The team pay link: raised, not drawn

The journeys page says a team’s pay link “ends when the tournament ends”, that “the organizer can send a new one, the same as the families’ link”, and that “the coach can also pay from the portal”. The plan’s Stage 2 does not include any of it. Public tournament pages Stage 7 rebuilt the team pay page and is waiting on its walk; it kept the pay block, its words and the link as they were. In the code: once a tournament is over the page already refuses payment and says so. The coach can already pay from the portal: its tournament page has Pay now, which opens the same page. What is missing is a way to stop one team’s old link: the organizer’s “Email the pay link” sends the identical link again. And one thing nobody listed: the team pay page fills in the coach’s full email for the receipt, so anyone holding a forwarded link sees it. Stage 1 masked the parent’s address on the family page for exactly this reason.

What the design check changed (round 2)

  1. The tournament buttons wear the event site’s real style: the title face at 16, squarer corners, white Rules and Home buttons, an outlined Follow the Schedule.
  2. The check-your-email block keeps its shipped sentence, “Verification email sent to …”, its shipped sizes, and “Already confirmed? Sign in” at its foot, as the shipped block places it.
  3. The event band matches the site: the state is plain text after the dates, with a gold rule under the band, Share and Sign in on the right. The tab row is in its real order.
  4. The “Watch your email” row no longer claims the registration email holds the link. It is only true in some cases. The already-has-an-account frame keeps today’s words.
  5. The join page wears the app’s top bar: the name with its mark, Home, Scores and a Sign in pill. Its sizes and its olive foot link match the account pages.
  6. The emails’ panels, button and spacing match the shipped card, and the “before” words use the shipped punctuation.
