The club picks who pays the card fee on sponsorships, and the business sees it before it pays

Ruled Nov 22: a second choice on the Payouts card, for sponsorships only, “The club pays it” by default. Here Riverdale has switched it to “The sponsor pays it”. The fee is 3.5% + $1.50 on what the business owes, the same rate as every card payment. The club’s HST applies to it, because the deal charges HST.

Processing fee on sponsorships
Card networks limit what a business may add to a card payment (2.4% in Canada, and none in Québec). This fee can be more than that. Choosing it is your decision and your responsibility. The rules this tab keeps

  • The fee shows before the business pays (the Nov 22 ruling): the card button names the total, and the note under it names the fee and its tax.
  • Choosing a cheque or e-transfer issues the invoice at once (ask 4, ruled), with no fee on it. An invoice already issued never gains a fee line afterwards.
  • A card payment issues the invoice when it is paid, so its invoice has the fee line from the start (tab 2).
  • Until card payment is on in production, the card button isn’t there, and this choice has nothing to decide. The Payouts card itself only exists once payouts are set up.
  • The words on the new Payouts row and the two buttons are drafts. /marketing confirms them at the build.

What the row gives, and what it doesn’t

  • Defaults: on for the owner, admins and the treasurer (ask 12, ruled). Off for the league admin, the registrar, staff and volunteers until the club turns it on. On a plan without Sponsorships the row isn’t there at all, as with every program.
  • Turned on, it opens all four tabs: Deals, Packages, Sponsors, Setup. The person can offer a deal, record a cheque and tick deliverables. Their entries land in the club’s books as the treasurer’s do.
  • Except the invoice details (ask 4): the club’s mailing address, GST/HST number and payment instructions are read-only for them. Those are on every invoice, every offer email’s legal footer, and Stage 7 camps read the same registration. Without this, one access would let a volunteer change the club’s tax registration.
  • Every offer keeps who sent it (from /legal), beside the reason and the date round 1 already keeps. The name is kept as it was, so it survives the person leaving the club. Under the anti-spam law the sender has to prove the reason, and now a volunteer can send.
  • The Payouts card’s fee choice stays the owner’s, as every Payouts change is today.
  • “What each role can open” (the role guide) gets a Sponsorships row: owner, admin and treasurer “yes”, the others “if you turn it on”.
  • Round 1 said a sponsorship volunteer gets in through one of the three seats until Stage 3’s people groups exist. This row replaces that.

/legal’s questions

  • A registered charity (ask 7). /legal treats the offer as a commercial message for every club and doesn’t rely on a charity exception, so the email is the same either way. What a charity changes is the tax side: GST/HST on a sponsorship. The invoice already avoids “donation”, “gift” and “tax receipt”, which is right either way. The product can’t see whether a club is a charity, so a re-check happens only when you tell us one is using it.
  • Our mailing address in the email foot (ask 8). The offer email’s foot has the club’s mailing address, an unsubscribe and “Sent through FieldLogicHQ”. The law asks for the sender’s address, and the club is the sender. Whether FieldLogicHQ also counts as a sender is one of the lawyer’s-eye items (no gate). FieldLogicHQ has no mailing address today (you ruled email only on Nov 17), so adding one means getting one first: a PO box or mail-forwarding address, never a home address. Our own marketing emails will need one before any promotional send anyway.
  • The same cap line on the registration row (ask 9). The new sponsorship row tells the club the card networks’ limit is its own responsibility. The registration row above it has families paying the fee by default, and families are consumers, so the same line matters more there. It would be a hint only. It doesn’t reopen the ruling.
