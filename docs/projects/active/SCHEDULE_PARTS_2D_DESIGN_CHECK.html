What to look at

Two lines where the ruling said one. Round 1 Ask 9 A: "one line names unused time a final schedule holds". With two schedules the window prints two sentences that open the same way.

No gap under the line. The quiet line has no bottom margin and the rows card has no top margin, so the last line sits on the card's border.

The band's note slot. The shared band already has a place for one note under it, with 1 rem below. Use that slot.

The schedule names. They stay plain text in the note. The links to the schedules stay in the line at the foot of the window.

New schedule, step 2 (KitDialog xwide, 1024 px). The schedule's hours include Field 1, where the draft Fall house league already gives practices on some Sundays. Draft 1 puts 4 slots there, draft 2 puts 6, and draft 3 leaves that time open. The cards column is 15 rem (240 px) wide.

Before 2D: only a row brought in by Find the time could warn. A typed row on the same ground said nothing. The line was short.

As built: every row can warn, which is right. The line is now two sentences, about 145 characters, and wraps to six lines in a 240 px card. The second sentence is the same on every card.

Recommended: the card keeps the count and the draft's name on one or two lines. The rule ("whichever is finalized first keeps it") appears once, in the step's warning notice above the cards. That notice uses the same style as the step's other notices.

What to look at

Six lines in a 240 px card, on every card that has the slots. The other lines on a card are short facts. This one also explains a rule, and the rule is the same on every card.

The cards column grows about 90 px per card that warns. On a 768 px tall laptop window, Three more drafts and the third card drop below the fold.

The fact stays on the card and the rule is said once. The rule goes in the step's warning notice, the place the step already uses for "too few slots" and gaps.

The card is a button that holds a list. This was already true before 2D. Opens the note.

Find the time inside New schedule (xwide, 1024 px; computer-only by its stylesheet). Field 1 is in two sides. The draft Fall house league holds side A on 4 of the 13 Sundays, so the whole of Field 1 is free on 9. Side B is free on all 13.

Before 2D: a booking on side A held the whole field. Field 1 showed 9 days free and nothing else, so side B's 4 Sundays were never offered.

As built: "Field 1 side B" is a row right after "Field 1". Ticking it unticks Field 1. Nothing in the two names says that one is the whole field and the other a part of it. The count line counts the side as a fourth facility and adds its hours to Field 1's.

Recommended: the rows use the words of the tick list New schedule already ships for the same choice (its Facilities chips, round 2 tab 3): "Field 1 (whole)" and "Field 1, side B", flat, with no indent. The tick moving between them is that list's ruled behaviour. The count line counts facilities, not rows.

What to look at

The two rows don't say whole and part. The tick list New schedule ships for the same choice says "Field 1 (whole)" and "Field 1, side B". Without "(whole)", ticking side B and seeing Field 1 untick looks like a mistake.

The count line adds the side as a facility and adds its hours. 36 h of Field 1 and 52 h of side B overlap on the same 9 Sundays.

Use the shipped tick list's words, flat. Round 2 left the indent out of that list on purpose: an indented parent with its own tick can't tell "all of it" from "some of it".

The list is a table drawn with divs. This was already true before 2D. Opens the note.

390 px. Find the time is computer-only (its stylesheet says so, and its three columns have no phone rule), so it has no phone frame here. The permit window and the drafts step do open on a phone. The Screen switch applies here too.
