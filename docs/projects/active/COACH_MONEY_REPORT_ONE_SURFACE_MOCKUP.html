Money Reports on One Surface

A fresh look at the three list-shaped money reports, measured against the one you already like. The frames below are drawn at the real width of your screen (1,500 px of content) so density can be judged by eye, and each "before" is a redraw of what the build paints today, not a caricature. Figures are the UAT team's, carried across so the comparison is like for like.

What the screens are doing today

Months works because it is a single table: one white surface on the page, hairlines between rows, and exactly two tints, both meaning structure. The other three are drawn from a different recipe, and every complaint in your note traces back to that choice.

01Two recipes for one hub

Statement, By activity and the Budget list draw every category as its own bordered, rounded card with a 12 px gap under it, sitting directly on the paper ground. Months draws nothing but rows. The card recipe was a deliberate choice for the list views in August; a month of use has shown the table is the better reading experience, and nothing about these reports needs a card.

02The row colour is a status pretending to be a structure

An amber row means "nobody planned this". In By activity a planned line sits bare on the paper and an unplanned one is amber, so the colour splits a group in two instead of holding it together. The dues row is a third tint (a tan card) and the section band a fourth (white). Four grounds on one screen, and none of them means "these belong together".

03A name and its figures are a screen-width apart, with nothing to walk across

On your monitor "Concession revenue" and its $400.00 sit about 1,200 px apart, and a row on the paper has no rule under it, so the eye has nothing to follow between them. Months has the same distance from a name to its Total column and nobody notices, because a hairline under every row carries the eye across. The distance was never the problem; the missing rule was.

04The two shapes put their totals in different places

The Statement carries a category's total on the category row, as Months does. By activity carries it in a closing "netted" row at the foot of the block, under a band, an inner label and its lines. Same report, two grammars, and the second one costs three extra rows per activity, which is where most of the height goes.

The proposal, in one sentence

Give the three list reports the Months recipe: one white table spanning the page, hairline rows, tints only for structure, and the total on the category row. Nothing about what the reports say changes; only how the same rows are drawn. The numbered badges on the "after" frames map to this list.

  • 1One surface. The whole report is a single white table with a hairline between rows. No per-category frames, no rounded corners inside, no gaps.
  • 2Tints mean structure only. Olive for a band heading (Revenue / Expenses), tan for a category row. Nothing else gets a ground.
  • 3Status stops wearing a row colour. A line nobody planned shows an amber dash in the Plan column and nothing else. The amber row ground goes.
  • 4Full width, like everything above it. The table spans the page as the band, the toolbar and Months do. The hairline under every row is what carries the eye from a name to its figures, exactly as it does on Months.
  • 5Months' rhythm and headings. Condensed uppercase column headings, 36–38 px rows, tabular figures, the heading row sticky as you scroll.
  • 6An activity is a category row that carries its own net. The "netted" closing row goes; Revenue and Costs become quiet sub-labels inside it.
  • 7Player dues is an ordinary category row with its caption under the name. It no longer has a card of its own.
  • 8The Budget list takes the same recipe. The When column and the pencil stay where they are; only the card stack goes.

Three details, up close

Four grounds become one surface and two tints

Every ground a row can wear today, against the two that survive. A tint that means structure has to be the only kind of tint on the screen, or the eye cannot tell which one it is reading.

A status is not a colour of row

The amber ground was kept last week because the dash already said the words. It does say them; the ground is what makes two neighbouring rows look like they belong to different groups. Proposed: the dash, in amber, and no ground.

What does not change

  • Every figure that opens a panel today still opens it. The budget figure shows its plan lines, the actual shows its payments.
  • A report row still folds on a tap; a plan row still opens its editor. The two screens keep their different jobs.
  • Compare (Whole season / To date), Expand all, the exports and the footnotes under the table are untouched.
  • Months is the reference and is not redrawn.
  • The table spans the full width, as the band, the toolbar and Months do.
  • On a phone the table scrolls sideways with the first column pinned, which is the same behaviour Months already has, so the phone gets simpler rather than harder.
  • Variance still says the word, "under" and "over", and colour never carries the meaning on its own.
  • Screen-reader sentences for an unplanned row stay; only the visible ground goes.

One call settled, one that is yours

Width: full, settled

Round one capped the table at a document width and it looked odd under a full-width band. Round two spans it. If the right-hand cluster ever wants company, the honest way to fill the width is a column that means something, a "Season plan" beside "Plan to date" under the To-date comparison. That is a product change and not needed for this pass.

Any cue at all for an unplanned line?

The frames keep the amber dash in the Plan column. Dropping the colour and leaving a plain dash is also defensible, since the Off-plan tile at the top already totals what nobody budgeted. What I would not do is keep the row ground; it is the thing your note is describing.
