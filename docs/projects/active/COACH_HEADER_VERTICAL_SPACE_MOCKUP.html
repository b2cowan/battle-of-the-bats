Two header rows, and what they actually cost

Measured on the running dev build, not estimated. What follows is the real pixel budget above a coach's first line of content, an audit of what the two rows genuinely share, and five directions with their tradeoffs — including the one that reverses a decision made two days ago.

Measured 1440×900 and 390×844 UAT fixture · club org · head coach B+C+E on dev · A reverted 08-19 · QA §59 owed
The short answer

The two rows share nothing. The duplication was already removed.

Finding

The FieldLogicHQ strip carries the wordmark, the words “Coaches Portal”, and three personal doors. The team bar carries the club name, the team name, your role, the season, the record and today's live status. There is not one word in common. The org name was printed twice — in the sidebar and in the team bar — and the shell slimdown deleted the sidebar copy two days ago. The vertical space is not being spent on repetition; it is being spent on three separate jobs stacked on top of each other.

So

There is still real space to reclaim — 234 pixels sit above the first line of content on a desktop screen — but it comes from making each row denser and letting the team bar shrink while you scroll, not from merging identity into the top strip. That merge was proposed and rejected in August, and doing it now would cost the live status line and the phone.

Corrections

Three estimates on this page came out differently, and the measured numbers above are the ones to trust. The phone's page title barely moved — its height is set by the finger-friendly minimum on the help button, not by the icon tile, so shrinking the tile bought 4 pixels rather than 28. The finished-season phone bar is 82px, not the ~72 predicted — the extra line direction B removed is genuinely gone, but the test team's unusually long name wraps its role badge onto a second line. And direction A's own share is 18px, not 34, because B had already taken the bar from 74 to 58 before A collapsed it; together they still deliver the 34 pixels of pinned chrome the page promised.

Unproven

The test team has no game this week, so the slim desktop bar had no status to display. The mechanism is in place and verified; whether the status line reads well at 40px needs eyes on a team with a game coming up.

Review

A three-lens adversarial review on 2026-08-19 produced two fixes and one owner ruling. The biggest finding was not in this change at all: the smaller heading used on drill-in screens (Money → Fundraisers → one fundraiser) has never once rendered smaller — the rule that shrinks it was written the day that shape shipped but placed where it could not take effect, so a drill-in heading always matched its hub exactly. Measured: both at 22.4px, weight 900, 36×36 tile. Now 17.6px / 800 / 28×28 against the hub's 22.4 / 900 / 36 — parent and child, for the first time.

Also fixed

A keyboard user focused on the public-site link or the reminder's dismiss button, who then scrolled, lost their place entirely when the bar slimmed under them — focus fell to the top of the document with no indication. It now lands on the bar. This had been live on phones since August without being noticed.

Ruled

Direction C plus direction A together mean an assistant's role is stated nowhere while scrolled on a desktop. Owner ruling: no change — "users know what role they have and don't need to be reminded every day." Desktop now matches the phone.

Method note

Two reviewers disagreed on the heading hierarchy — one read a code comment claiming the sizes were fine and believed it. Rendering the page and reading the computed styles settled it. The comment was the thing that was wrong.

D stays open

The owner declined to close direction D: "don't close D forever, I am reconsidering… not opposed to revisiting moving to the single header." The August "do not re-propose" is lifted, pending how this build feels in use. Any return still has to answer the five costs listed above — and it now has to beat 88 pixels, not 122. Worth carrying forward: direction A has proved team identity fits in 40px, so a merged bar at that height is a genuinely different proposal from the one rejected in August, and probably the version to mock up first.

Reverted the next morning

The bar stays put — space comes from smaller, not from vanishing

Ruling

"I want to revert one thing, I like the size changes we made but you can leave this header as is when scrolling." — owner, 2026-08-19, on sight. Direction A is reverted. The team bar no longer slims as you scroll on a computer; it stays full height at every scroll position. Phones are untouched and still slim to the bare team name, as they have since August. B, C and E stand unchanged.

The rule

Space taken by making a thing smaller is kept. Space taken by making a thing disappear and come back is borrowed. The collapse was the second kind — and it was the one change that made a bar whose entire job is to sit still start moving. Direction A is now data, not a proposal: built, measured at −34px of pinned chrome, reviewed, and rejected anyway. Do not bring it back as a space saving.

Five directions

What could change, and what it costs

A

The team bar shrinks as you scroll — on desktop too

REVERTED 08-19 — data, not a proposal

At the top of a page nothing changes: the full bar, club name, team, role, season, record and today's status. Once the coach scrolls past the first screenful it slims to a single line — team name on the left, today's status on the right — and stays pinned there. This is exactly what the phone already does, extended to the desktop and made slightly more generous: where the phone drops to the bare team name, the desktop keeps the status, because there is room.

  • Reclaims 34px of permanently pinned chrome — while scrolling, which is when the space is actually wanted.
  • Costs nothing at rest. A coach arriving on a page sees exactly what they see today.
  • Status survives in both states. A finished season shows the team name and “Complete”.
  • Phone unchanged — it has done this since August.

B

Two lines instead of three

Built · −16px, and 98px phone bar → 82px

Today the bar stacks club name, then team name, then the season line. Fold the club name down into the season line: team name and role on top, club · season · record underneath. Nothing is removed — the club is still named on every team page, which is the promise made when it left the sidebar.

  • Reclaims 11px on every screen, at rest and on both devices.
  • Fixes the 98px closed-season phone bar — the tallest bar in the product comes back to normal because “Complete” and the final record stop being pushed onto a third line.
  • Standalone team workspaces see no change — they never had a club eyebrow.

C

Stop saying the role twice

Built

An assistant coach currently reads “Assistant Coach” in the team bar and again in the sidebar, two inches apart, on all forty screens. The bar's chip is the better home — it is always visible and sits beside the team the role belongs to — so the sidebar label goes. A head coach is unaffected either way.

  • No vertical gain — this is honesty, not space. Worth doing because the duplicate is real, worth separating from A and B because it changes nothing about layout.
  • The alternative — dropping the chip and keeping the sidebar label — is worse: the chip is the only thing that tells an assistant why some doors are missing, and the sidebar label disappears on a phone entirely.
D

Team identity moves into the FieldLogicHQ strip

Parked, NOT closed — owner reconsidering

The direction the session opened with: one bar carrying wordmark, club, team, role, season and status, with the separate team bar deleted on desktop. It is the biggest single number — 74px reclaimed — and it is the one direction with a rejection already written against it.

  • Reclaims 74px on desktop. Zero on a phone — the strip does not exist below 900px, so the team bar has to stay there anyway.
  • Two builds of one thing. A strip on desktop and a bar on phone means every future change to team identity is made twice, and the rule that this chrome must read the same on every device gets harder to keep, not easier.
  • The status line is the expensive part. Today it is worked out once when the coach enters a team and rides down with the page for free. The strip sits above the team, where that information isn't available — so it becomes either a fetch on every single page or a new piece of plumbing to carry it up.
  • Two things have nowhere to go in a 48px strip: the game-week scouting reminder that attaches under the bar, and the game-day link into the bench console.
  • Pages with no team — the team picker, notifications — would leave the strip half-empty or, worse, showing the last team you were in.

E

Put the page-title band on a diet

Built · 80px → 52px desktop, −4px phone

The largest band above the content is not either header row — it is the 80 pixels the page title takes: a 48px icon tile, a heading at the largest size in the product, and a 32px gap beneath it, to say one word the highlighted sidebar item has already said. Shrinking the tile and the heading a step, and tightening the gap, takes it to roughly 52px without touching the rule that all forty screens open the same way — every slot stays, every screen stays identical to every other, they are simply less loud.

  • Reclaims ~28px on desktop and a similar amount on a phone.
  • Nothing disappears — the icon, the heading, the actions and the “?” all keep their slots and their positions.
  • Touches all forty screens at once, so it wants its own review pass rather than riding along with a header change.

Recommend

A + B now, C alongside it, E as its own decision afterwards. Together A and B give a coach 45 fewer pixels of chrome while they read and cost them nothing when they arrive — more than half of what direction D would buy, on both devices instead of one, with the live status line and the finished-season variant untouched and no separate phone build to maintain. E is worth more than either on its own, but it lands on all forty screens and deserves its own look rather than being smuggled in beside a header change.
