Where the club’s email should live

An evaluation of your question: should ad hoc group email have one permissioned home in the club portal, where today it has two places and the coach email proposal adds a third? Read from the code on 2026-11-16 and from the coach email drawings. The frames are sketches to judge the idea, drawn at true size on the club’s own look. They are not build drawings. Coach counts are the seeded Harbourtown counts from the coach email drawings. Family counts, messages and history rows are examples.

View
Dims what the owner and the registrar both have on this tab.

1 · Yes to one home. Keep the buttons where people already work.

Your instinct is right, and the code makes the case more strongly than your note does. I would not build the coach email as a “Group email” tab on Organization. I would give the club one place called Communications, with one list of what went out and one window to write in, and build the coach email as its first new piece.

What I recommend

  • One home. A Communications place in the club sidebar. It holds the club’s sent record and a New email button.
  • One window. The same send window the club already uses. Its Who section lists every group this person may write to, families and coaches together, and more than one can be ticked. Each person gets one copy.
  • One record. Every email the club sent appears in one list. A president sees all of it. A league registrar sees the part that reached house-league people.
  • Many doors. “Email these families” on a list of families who owe, the ticked rows on Registrations, a season’s Email families: they stay. They open the same window with the people already filled in, and what they send lands in the same record.
  • Automatic and one-off emails stay where they fire, exactly as you said. The record lists them so the picture is complete.

Where I would go further than your note

  • The split is in the keys as much as the places. Today the club-wide family email and its sent list open only for someone who holds the Families book. An admin does not hold it unless the owner grants it, and granting it also opens every household’s contact details and each child’s medical note. So a vice-president who should be able to tell every family the fields are closed either can’t, or gets far more access than the job needs. A hub fixes this only if sending is separated from reading family records.
  • Nobody can see everything that went out, the owner included. The owner’s fullest list is Families › Emails. It has no tournament announcements, none of the emails sent to coaches, and none of the emails a coach sends to their own team’s families.
  • Coaches’ team emails are invisible to the club. A rep coach can email their team’s families from the Coaches Portal and no club screen shows it. If “see all communications” is the goal, this is the largest gap, and it needs your call.

Where I would hold back

  • The hub should not be the only door. Emailing the people you are looking at is the easiest version of this job. Taking those buttons away to make the hub “the” place would make the common case slower.
  • “All coaches and parents” is one message but two mailings. Family email carries an unsubscribe line and a daily allowance. Coach email carries neither, by the legal review. One window can write both. The product sends them down their own paths and shows them as one row. This needs a legal check before it is drawn for build.
  • Tournament announcements stay in the tournament. They reach visiting clubs, post to the public site and push to fans. They belong in the record as rows, later, and their composer stays put.
  • No saved lists, templates, attachments or send-later in this work. The hub gives each of them an obvious home when you want them.

Four facts from the code that shaped this

  • The machinery is already one thing. Families email and house-league email use the same window, the same sender and the same sent tables. The coach email is drawn on the same window parts. What is separate is the doors, who may open them, and the lists. So this is mostly moving and merging.
  • The product already sends people elsewhere mid-task. The Families window says: “To email a division, a team or a status, use the season’s own Email families.”
  • Members already has an access switch named “Communications”. The owner and admins hold it by default, it can be turned on or off per person, and no screen reads it.
  • You asked this question once already. On October 22 you noted the Club notice strip belongs under a Communications home, and it was parked because the club has no such place. This gives it one.

What a volunteer runs into

  • “Where do I go to email people?” has three answers, and the right one depends on who the people are.
  • “What did we send last week?” has three lists. A registrar can’t see that the president already told every family about a closure, so the season’s families hear it twice.
  • A message for two kinds of people is written twice. A message for one season plus two rep teams can’t be sent as one.

Why the Group email tab is the weak part of the coach proposal

  • The name describes the mechanism. Every email on all three screens is a group email. You said so yourself.
  • Organization is the set-up area. The tab would sit between Members and Audit log, beside Settings and PDF settings. Sending the club’s mail is weekly work.
  • It was placed there to work around a permission. The drawing rejects Families › Emails because it needs the Families grant. That is a reason to fix the grant, and a hub does.
  • Everything else in the proposal holds up. Live groups, the recount at Send, colleague mail, names and never addresses, the record naming who it reached. All of it carries over unchanged.

Why B over C

C is the tidiest diagram and the worst afternoon for a registrar. Standing on a list of 19 families who owe a fee, the natural act is “email these”. C makes them leave, open Communications, and describe those 19 families again. The buttons cost nothing to keep because they open the same window.

Why B over D

D gives the president the list and leaves everything else as it is. It is the cheapest fix for “see all communications”, and worth choosing only if the aim is nothing more than that. It does not answer where to go, the overlapping groups, or the Families grant.

What B costs

  • A new place in the sidebar, which changes the navigation standard and needs your yes.
  • The Emails tab leaves Families. Anyone used to it finds it under Communications.
  • One rule for who sees which rows (tab 6), where today each list simply follows its screen’s key.

One message, two mailings

The legal review of the coach email set a firm rule: family email and coach email are two different kinds of mail and are never mixed. A single window can still write both. The product splits the send and the record shows it as one row.

The family mailing

  • The club’s family email, with its unsubscribe line
  • Families who opted out are skipped and counted
  • Counts toward the 3 emails in 24 hours
  • One email per household

The coach mailing

  • Colleague mail, with no unsubscribe
  • A line saying why they got it, and how to be taken off
  • No daily allowance, only the just-sent check
  • One email per person
  • A coach who is also a parent gets the family copy only. The product already does this when a family email copies the head coaches.
  • The product already sends one message down both paths. “Copy head coaches” on today’s family email is exactly this, in a small way.
  • One open legal point. A message that promotes something (registration is open, apparel is on sale) is fine for families, who can unsubscribe. The same words to coaches with no unsubscribe is the risk the legal review named. Before this is drawn for build, the legal check should say what the window does when both kinds are ticked.
  • The daily allowance needs one rule for an email that spans the club and a season. Simplest: it counts toward the club’s.

A button on another screen fills Who in

“Email these families” on the 19 families who owe opens this window with those 19 as the only Who row, already ticked. The sender writes the message and sends. Nothing about those buttons changes for the person using them.

Later groups are more rows

Board, Volunteers and “Needs a new document” from the Board Ready plan arrive under Coaches and staff with their live counts. No new screen and no new list.

What I would leave out

Saved lists, as the coach proposal already recommends. A hand-kept list goes wrong the day someone joins. “Send again to these people” from a record covers the repeat case.

Sketch limits

  • Tick rows are the shipped kind: the window already ticks rep teams this way. A row inside a wider ticked group loses its box and says why, as a row the send won’t reach does today.
  • The registrar’s season choices are drawn as one field to keep the frame short. The shipped season window offers them as rows, and the drawing round decides which.
  • “Send to 221” counts emails: 214 households plus 7 coaches who aren’t already in one.

Why sending shouldn’t need the Families book

The Families book is locked down for a good reason: it shows contact details, balances and each child’s medical note. Sending an email shows none of that. The sender sees a count and, in the record, family names. The house-league desk already works this way: a registrar emails a whole season without the Families grant.

So the only real change is that an admin can reach rep-team families club-wide, where today they can reach house-league families only.

The “Communications” switch that does nothing

The window where an owner sets a member’s access already lists “Communications”. It is on by default for the owner and admins, and no screen checks it. Either it becomes the hub’s on/off for a person, or it should be removed. I would make it real: it gives an owner a way to keep one admin out of the club’s mail without changing their role.

What nobody gains

  • No seat sees an email address it couldn’t see before. The lists carry names only.
  • No seat can write to a group it couldn’t reach before, except the admin change above.
  • A seat with no group to write to gets no Communications place, never an empty one.

The season tab: keep it

A registrar lives in the season, and its Email families tab is shipped and walked. Under the hub it reads the same record, narrowed to that season, so nothing is kept twice. One honest counterpoint: a season’s tab row already runs off a phone screen, and removing this tab would shorten it. That belongs to the navigation review, and the hub makes it possible later without losing anything.

Coaches’ team emails: I would list them

They go to the club’s families under the club’s name, and a board that hears a complaint has nothing to look at today. I would list a club team’s emails in the record, with the team and coach named, and say so plainly in the coach’s own window. This is a change in what the club can see of a coach’s work, so it is yours to rule and needs a legal check.

Why “Communications”

  • It is the word you used, and the tournament side already uses it for the same job.
  • It still fits when the Club notice moves in, and when families sign in and a message can also show in their app.
  • “Messages” is spoken for by the chat and inbox direction. “Email” would be wrong the day a second channel arrives. “Group email” names the mechanism.

9 · Asks

Ruled 2026-11-16: all 12 as recommended. Asks 7 and 8 keep the legal check their recommendations name. Next: the plan, then the stage 1 drawing round.

The asks as they were put, each with the recommendation that is now the ruling.

1 · The direction: one home, many doors?

Recommend: yes, option B. One Communications place with one record and one window. The buttons on other screens stay and feed it. The alternatives are on tab 3: three homes as proposed, one home with no other doors, or three homes plus a read-only list.

2 · The name: Communications?

Recommend: yes. It is your word and the tournament side’s word, and it still fits when the Club notice and the family app arrive. Two naming points ride with it. The Coaches Portal’s group is headed “Communication”, without the s. And a tournament’s announcements page is already called Communications on a desk and Messages on its phone bar, which the navigation standard records as an open question. A second Communications is the moment to settle both.

3 · Its place: the Club group of the sidebar, after Families?

Recommend: yes. It changes the navigation standard, so it needs your yes. On a phone it sits in the More sheet with the rest of the Club group. Families loses its Emails tab.

4 · Sending no longer needs the Families book?

Recommend: yes. An admin can email every family from Communications, seeing counts and family names only. The Families grant keeps guarding household records. Today an admin without the grant can email house-league families and not rep-team families, which is hard to explain to a president.

5 · Who sees which rows?

Recommend: you see every email that reached people in the programs you run, whoever sent it. Owner and admins see everything. So a registrar sees a club-wide email that included house-league families, and never one that went only to rep coaches. The stricter choice is “only what you sent yourself”, which loses the double-send protection.

6 · A season keeps its Email families tab?

Recommend: keep it, as the season’s slice of the same record. Removing it is a navigation-review question for later, and the hub makes that possible without losing anything.

7 · One message to families and coaches together?

Recommend: yes, as one message and two mailings, each person once, after a legal check. The check should settle what the window says or refuses when a promotional message has both kinds ticked, and which daily allowance a mixed email counts toward.

8 · Does the club’s record list the emails coaches send their own team’s families?

Recommend: yes for the club’s own teams, in a later stage, with a plain line in the coach’s window that the club can see them. The record would show the team, the coach, the subject, the date and how many families it reached, and the message. A coach who bought the portal for a team outside any club is not affected. This widens what a club sees of a coach’s work, so it needs your ruling and a legal check.

9 · Tournament announcements?

Recommend: they stay in the tournament, and each becomes a row in the record that opens it, in a later stage. The coach proposal reached the same answer for sending (its ask 5). This adds only the row.

10 · The Club notice moves to Communications?

Recommend: yes, in a later stage, with a pointer left in the Public site editor. This answers the question you noted on October 22. The counter-weight recorded then still stands: the notice is seen on the site, so the editor’s preview should keep showing it and link to where it is edited.

11 · The “Communications” access switch in Members?

Recommend: make it the hub’s on/off for a person. Today it does nothing. If you would rather keep access purely by role, it should be removed so the window doesn’t offer a switch with no effect.

12 · The order: the coach email is built inside the new home, as stage 1?

Recommend: yes. The coach proposal’s asks 6, 7 and 13 are replaced by this page. Its other ten asks stand as written and still need your answers. If you would rather not touch navigation before the coach email ships, option D is the fallback, and it carries the rework described on tab 3.
