What this page is for

4c-1 gave the club its own editor. 4c-2 is the same idea from the team's side: a family sees a team's birth years, a parent with a tryout question writes to the person running that team's tryouts, and the club says in its own words what a tryout costs and who may come.

Four pieces were ruled but never drawn. They are drawn here, each with today's screen first, then the proposal at true size on a phone and a desk, then the empty, filled and refused states. Nothing is built. The words in the frames are drafts: they go through the copy pass after you rule, before any code.

Push back first

You asked for disagreement before the work. These change what gets built; each has an ask.

  1. 1 · The coach's address is a carve-out from Ask 2. Say what it costs.

    Ask 2 keeps every email off the team page, for two reasons: robots collect any address printed on a public page, and a family writes to someone who left. A coach-typed address on the tryout page brings both back on one page. The drawing keeps the carve-out narrow and shows every safeguard. It is opt-in per team and blank by default. The sign-in address is refused, and the coach is told the address is public. The club sees it and can put its own back, and it ends by itself when the coach stops being head coach.

    The other reading of your ask is "a different contact per team", not "the coach's own inbox". That would be the club typing a per-team address (a registrar, a team inbox, or the coach's, if the coach agrees). Both are drawn as options in Ask 1. I still recommend the coach's own: the coach is who a hesitant parent needs, and an address tied to a role cannot go stale the way a club-kept one can.

  2. 2 · Birth years belong to a season, not to a team. This widens the question.

    A 13U team's birth years change every September. A field on the team is right for one year and then wrong. That is the same stale-words failure the past-year hint exists to catch, built into a field. And during the autumn, when a team can have two seasons open, a team-level field cannot say which season it describes. The drawing puts the years on the team's season. It labels the field with the season's name, and Start next season carries them forward one year later. A team that keeps its players together year after year, named by birth year, changes them back once. Ask 4 ·

  3. 3 · Do not suggest birth years from the team's label.

    The label does not carry the age reliably. Lakeshore's divisions read "Provincial AAA", so its ages live only in the team names. "13U" (13 and under) and "U13" (under 13) can mean different birth years, and the date the age is counted on differs by sport and by club. The Stage 4 drawings disagree with themselves: Riverbend's U13 reads 2015–2016, while a 13U team counted at the end of 2027 would be 2014–2015. With the carry-forward above, a club types each team's years once, ever. A suggestion would save that one entry, at the cost of a confident wrong default that nobody checks. Ask 6

    Round 2, ruled: you raised clubs whose cut-off is not January 1 ("anyone born before September 1, 2014"). The club now sets when birthdays count from, per sport (1b). That settles the date, but not whether "13U" means one birth year or two, so Ask 6 stands.

  4. 4 · The past-year hint would fire on every honest tryout blurb.

    The ruled rule is "any four-digit year before this calendar year, a founding year skipped". A birth year is always a past year, so "Open to players born 2012 and 2013" reads Mentions 2012 and 2013 — still true? every time. I checked the hint's own rule; it has no exception for that. A hint that fires on every correct blurb teaches the club to ignore it. The fix is to skip a year that follows "born" or "birth", the way a founding year is skipped. Ask 11 ·

  5. 5 · The blurb's problem is when it shows, not how big it is.

    Plain text does answer what a parent asks: what it costs, who may come, what to bring and when they will hear back. Who may come is now also a field the page shows by itself (the birth years), so a fee field is still not needed. What is missing is timing. The blurb shows only while sign-ups are open. In September, though, the club posts the page and parents first read it while it says "Not open yet", so they see nothing about cost or eligibility. Ask 9 ·

  6. 6 · Two smaller things the frames exposed

    A house-league "division" is not always an age: its window suggests "Division A, East". Years per division still work, and nothing changes in the ruling (). And a team can have more than one head coach, so "the head coach's address" needs a rule for whose (): one per team, and it ends with the role of whoever set it.

Flow check · the head coach's tryout address

The UX flow review (who, where, when it ends), run on 2026-10-22 before the frames were finished. Its recommendations are already drawn; they stay proposals until you rule.

Per person

Head coach

  1. Opens Tryouts › Set up and sees "Questions from families · Optional", saying questions go to the club today.
  2. Types an address. It saves when it is complete and acceptable and they leave the field, never part-typed. The row's status then reads "… · on your tryout page".
  3. They can remove it at any time ("Remove and use the club's address"). It also comes off by itself when they stop being a head coach of the team.

Club admin

  1. Sees, on the team's Tryouts page, which address the page shows and who set it.
  2. Can put the club's back ("Use the club's address", asked once). The coach's row then says the club changed it, and when.

Family

  1. On the team's own tryout page, open or not open yet, reads "Questions about these tryouts? Email the head coach" with Copy.
  2. Everywhere else (footer, the club's Tryouts page, the team page, a finished or closed page, a share card) sees the club's address, or no address at all.
Completeness
Happy path
Complete: set, shown, changed, removed.
Empty
Blank is the default and the safe state: the club's line. The row says so in words.
Loading
The field is not editable until the team's tryout read returns, so an empty box is never mistaken for "nothing set".
Error
Two refusals at the field (sign-in address; not an address). A failed save keeps the pill's error with Retry. The page keeps what it showed before.
Recovery
Remove is reversible by typing again. The club's override is reversible by the coach, and the coach is told about it.
Confirmation
Only the club's override asks, because it changes another person's choice. The coach's own Remove does not ask (it is theirs, and it is one field).
Role access
Head coaches set it. An assistant with tryouts access reads it. Other assistants do not see the row. The club sees it and can clear it.

What the review found

  1. The ruled spot is the wrong room. Sharing is a "once a year" settings card that any coach who can configure the team sees. The address is part of setting up a tryout, which only head coaches (or assistants granted tryouts) see. Medium → tryout setup row (Ask 2 A).
  2. "The head coach" is not one person. A team can have several. Without a rule, two head coaches overwrite each other silently. Medium → one per team, "set by …" shown, any head may change it, it ends with the role of whoever set it.
  3. A part-typed address would go live. Autosave as you type would publish "dana.tryouts@gm" for a second. High → saves on leaving the field, and only when it passes both checks.
  4. A silent revert leaves two people guessing. If the club clears the address with no trace, the coach sees it gone and types it again. Medium → the coach's row says "The club put its own address back on Oct 22."
  5. The name must not follow the address. Showing "Email Dana Whitfield" because Dana typed an address would bypass the name switch (Ask 2). Medium → "the head coach" unless the name switch is on.
  6. The open page's address can't be copied. It is the page with the sign-up button, and today it is the one state without Copy. Medium → Copy on every state (C2-06).
  7. The sign-up form's closed message says "Questions? Contact us". A family that follows an old sign-up link to a closed form reaches the club, and that is right. Low → unchanged; the form keeps the club's address.
  8. Stale copies cannot be recalled. A family that copied Dana's address keeps it after Dana leaves. Nothing in the product can fix that. Low → the cost is named in Ask 1, and the role-address nudge is the mitigation.
