How the ticks read

  • Tick Field 1 (whole): the whole field, one team at a time, as today.
  • Tick all four quarters: Field 1 in four quarters, a team on each.
  • Tick some quarters: those quarters here; the others are left for something else.
  • With halves: tick quarters A–B for one team on that half, and quarters C and D for a team on each.
  • A place and a place inside it are never both ticked: ticking a quarter unticks the whole field and the half it is in; ticking a half unticks the whole field and its two quarters. This is the round 1 build note for the shared tick list, widened.

Still flat. A field in quarters offers five plain options: the whole field and quarters A to D. No indent and no half-ticked parent box, which round 1's design check ruled out because it can't tell "all" from "some". A field without a split keeps its plain name.

With halves, the two halves are the fixed pairs A–B and C–D: the dividers stand in a row, so A–B is one end of the field and C–D the other. Halves across the field (A with C) are not offered. A quartered field then offers seven options, and the box reads "Field 1 quarters A–B, C and D": one team on that half, one on each of the other two quarters, three places at each start.

What the club's rulings mean in house league

  • R3, same place: the same start in the same building, each on its own field or part. On a dome in sides that can be the two sides of one field (round 1, Ask 7 A).
  • R4, no extra time: every team in a division gets the same number of practices, as today. The rule only picks which start.
  • R7, a hand move: does not arise. A house-league admin never moves a club-time practice by hand ("Only the club can move or remove this practice."); handing out again is the only change, and its question already counts what changes.
  • R8, sharing: house league has no sharing rule. Teams at once is a setting for the whole division, not a rule that can fail.
  • The rank (round 1, Ask 10 A): below a team twice at once and the division's time, above the early-and-late turns.

Why a comma, not a second dash. The club calendar reads a house-league game's facility back out of its stored place, splitting at the last " — ". "Milton Sports Dome — Field 1, side A" splits into the venue and "Field 1, side A". A second dash ("— Side A") would split into "Milton Sports Dome — Field 1" and "Side A". The same holds for a quarter: "Field 1, quarter C".

A part change is a move. The game's change record compares its place; it learns the part, so moving a game from side A to side B is sent to families as a move, with the full Was line, as a field change is today.

Ask 1 · How far parts reach in house league (round 1’s 14 C read with its 5)

Ask 2 · A field in four quarters, also used as halves and whole

Ask 4 · The words for a quarter

  • A · The word says the size (drawn): "side A" on a field in two sides, "quarter C" on a field in four; a half of a quartered field is "quarters A–B". The box: "Field 1 in four quarters".
  • B · One word for every part: "section A", "sections A–B", "Field 1 in four sections". One word to learn, but it doesn't say how big the space is.
  • C · Letters only: "Field 1 A", "Field 1 A–B". Shortest, but it reads like a field named "1A".

Recommend A. A family that reads "quarter C" knows how much field their child has. Working words either way, for the marketing pass.

Recommend A. B makes a second way to get a side, decided in house league rather than where the club splits its time, and C shares a part, which round 1’s 6 A ruled out.
