READ THIS FIRST

Four places where I think the prompt asks the wrong thing, or the code answers differently

1 · "Convert the coach's free portal to Premium." A free coach has no portal record for support to open.

A free (Basic) coach is a person with a team list. They have no organization, no billing page and no entry in Clubs & portals: Frankie's record in the console today is a person record that says "0 clubs" and does not mention the team at all. A Premium portal is a separate thing with its own organization, billing, members and entitlements, and it only exists once someone pays or is comped. So "giving free time" is not a change to a record support can open. It is what the coach's own upgrade does, minus the payment: make the Premium portal, copy the team across once, and mark when it stops. Support therefore meets the coach where the coach already is, on the person's record (drawing A), and the portal's own record takes over from the moment it exists (drawing B). I drew the door there, not on a portal record that does not exist yet.

2 · "Going back must not compromise the coach's data." The code has no way back. It has a forward copy and nothing else.

The upgrade copies the free team's roster, games and fees into Premium once and never again; nothing copies Premium work back, and most of what Premium holds (practice plans, the money book, awards, tryouts, attendance, lineups, notes) has no home on the free portal at all. So the only way to "go back without losing anything" is to keep the Premium portal exactly as it is, hidden, and show the free portal as it was. Premium keeps everything; the free side shows the team as it stood on the day of the upgrade. The price of that safety is a visible one: a player Frankie added on Premium does not appear on the free roster, and drawing C draws that moment honestly. A copy back would make the free roster current, and it would also be the riskiest write in the stage (duplicates, overwrites, a second copy when the coach returns). Question 1 asks which you want; I recommend keeping it whole, and say so on the free portal.

3 · "One reader, and nothing runs at the end." Access can end that way. The mail that goes out by itself cannot, and Stage 4's door alone is not enough.

A portal is live or not by three separate records (the organization, the workspace, and the team's entitlement), asked in many places. The entitlement already carries an end that is checked at the moment of reading by the gate every coach page passes through. The sharper problem, which the safety review found in the code: the scheduled jobs that email families about fees (dues reminders, the weekly digest) decide whether a team is still served from the portal's organization status, and nothing changes that status at the end. So a coach whose time is up would stop seeing Premium while families kept getting payment reminders under the team's name.

The design that closes it without any job: store the portal as ended, and let a timed billing-status grant hold it open until the last free day. Stage 4's door already turns an expired grant back into the stored state for every reader that goes through it: the sign-in rail, the portal list, the family layer, chat, and the scheduled sweeps. The entitlement's own end does the same for the per-team gate. The few readers that read the workspace's status directly (the free team's redirect, the free team list, the registration picker, the one-live-portal check and its callers, the people list) learn one question, "is this portal in force right now?". A build check, Stage 4's census widened to those three families, fails when new code reads a portal's status around them. No schedule, no job, nothing flipped on a first visit. This is a build call, and I say it out loud because you asked for "one reader or a grant plus a read-time revert": it is the second, with the stored state being the ended one.

4 · Today a free team that has a live Premium twin can still be written to by anyone who calls the free team's API. Free time makes that common.

When a free team is upgraded, the pages for it redirect to Premium. The free team's save routes (roster, schedule, fees, announcements) do not check that and keep accepting writes, into a copy nobody sees. One of them sends real emails to the old roster's contacts. Until now a live Premium twin was rare because a coach paid. With free time it is routine, and the same goes for the "Upgrade" button on the free portal, which makes a brand-new empty portal when the coach already has an ended one. These are two defects the build must fix first, and they are on the list at the end (question 11).

WHAT STAFF AND COACHES WILL SEE

Six changes, in plain words

  • Give free time, from the person. On a coach's person record a new Coaches Portal tab shows the coach's free team (what it holds, which tournament it is in) and a Give free time door. One window: which team, the last free day, why, and a sentence saying exactly what happens.
  • Premium is really on. The coach's Premium portal is made, the team's roster, games and fees are copied across as they would be on a paid upgrade, and the Premium tools work. Nothing is pretend.
  • The record shows it. The portal appears in Clubs & portals like any other, with a Free time chip, a band figure, the Free time list and a Story that says what was given, when it ended and what put it back, on the same rules and windows as a club.
  • It ends by itself, safely. The morning after the last free day the coach lands on their free team, which reads as it did before. Nothing is deleted and no retention clock starts. What they made on Premium is kept and returns if Premium does.
  • The coach is told in the product. A "Free through" line on their Plan & billing page, a band on their home screen, a calmer band in the last 14 days, and a plain one on the free portal the day after. No email in this stage unless you rule one (question 7).
  • One queue. Today's "Free time ending" counts a coach's portal beside the clubs, and the Free time list shows it with its kind. No second queue. (Today is drawn; the list and the Clubs & portals list reuse their existing screens with one more kind, and are described, not drawn.)

Deliberately not in Stage 4b: a coach paying for Premium (unchanged), Founding Season's turn-off (it shares the portal's billing record; drawing F and question 6 sit them side by side), Stripe reads and refunds (Stage 3, built), Status and the sent-email log (Stage 5, built), and any change to a price, a plan name or a gate.

Question 2 · Lengths and the longest period

You said "30, 60 or 90 days". Stage 4 ruled the opposite shape for clubs (any last free day through the shared date field, no quick picks, because a quick pick would be a new part), with Billing capped at 6 months and Super admin at 12, measured from today each time. A Premium Coaches Portal is a small plan; a 6-month grant of it is a very different sum from a 6-month grant of Club.

Recommended: the same date field, with a lower limit for a portal: 90 days for Support and Billing, 6 months for Super admin. It reads "30, 60 or 90" because the owner picks a day; the window greys everything past the limit. A fixed 30/60/90 row is the other shape, and it is a new part the club window does not have. Stage 4's 6/12 months fits a Club plan, not this one.

Question 3 · Who may give it

Your words were "Support says: I'll convert your free portal". Stage 4's rulebook gives free time to Super admin and Billing only and sends Support to ask Billing, which was right for a Club plan. I think that is the wrong shape for this job: the person talking to the coach is Support, the sum at stake is a month or three of one small plan, and "ask Billing" on every try-it call is exactly the friction you described not wanting. The risk that remains is a staff member giving a friend free Premium, and the answer to that is the same as for clubs: every give is seen (Story, Free time list, Today before it ends, audit log), capped, and never to your own account.

Recommended: a new power for this one job, held by Super admin, Billing and Support; Support and Billing up to 90 days, Super admin up to 6 months; Product, Growth and Read only see it and cannot give it. It is a new line in the one rulebook (the way Stage 5 added two), not a change to the club rule. The other answer keeps it to Super admin and Billing, as for clubs.

Question 4 · A coach who already pays, is on a Founding comp, was cancelled, or has had free time

Drawn above as lines 1 to 4. Paying: Stripe rewrites a portal's status on every subscription event, and the coach would keep paying while using free time. Founding: it already runs to Sep 30, 2027, and changing its end is the Founding decision, not this one. Cancelled: reopening a cancelled portal restores what the retention clock holds, and it is a different job with its own steps. A second give: Stage 4's rule only measures the cap from today, so repeated gives could chain.

Recommended: refuse all four, the door absent with one line. The second give for a coach who has had free time is allowed to a Super admin only (line 4). Allowing a paying coach "free time on top" would be a refund by another name.

Question 5 · Giving more time: change the running end, and a second time later

Stage 4 ruled it for clubs: change the running grant's end (one row, one end, the Story says "was … now …"). A club's limit is measured from today each time, so changes can chain past the limit, and nothing sums them. For a portal I would close that: measure from the day free time was first given, which is why the Change window above reads "you can go through January 25, 2027". After the time has ended, a fresh give for the same coach reuses the same Premium portal (nothing is copied again) and is a Super admin's job (question 4).

Recommended: change the running end, measured from the first give; a give after it has ended reuses the portal and needs a Super admin. Nothing is stored per extension beyond the Story entry.

How it ends with nothing running (the build's shape, for the record)

Free time writes three things when it is given, in this order, so a stop halfway never leaves an open-ended portal: (1) the Stage 4 free-time record, which is a billing-status grant that holds the portal open until the last free day (the Story, Today, the Free time list, Put it back, the limit and the reason all work as they do for a club; a record with no portal is harmless, so it goes first); (2) the portal, made by the same upgrade a paid coach runs, stored as ended (organization cancelled, billing mode "platform override") with the entitlement's end set in the same insert; (3) the copy. When the end passes nothing runs and nothing is written; every reader fails closed:

  • Everything that reads the organization's status through Stage 4's door (the sign-in rail, the portal list, the family layer, chat, and the scheduled sweeps that email families) sees "active" while the grant runs and the stored ended state after. Families stop getting reminders the minute the coach stops seeing Premium.
  • The per-team gate every coach page passes through reads the entitlement's end, as it already does.
  • The readers of the workspace's own status (the free team's redirect into Premium, the free team list, the registration picker, the one-live-portal check and its callers, the people list) read it raw, so each asks the one question "is this portal in force right now?". Without it the coach is sent to a "Coaches Portal not ready" page, or told they already have a portal.
  • The wall's words change: a coach whose free time ended must not read "subscription ended".

The build starts with a census of all three families of reader, as Stage 4's did; that list is the allowlist of a build check that fails when new code reads a portal's status around them. One risk to prove at the gate: a shadow organization stored as cancelled must not appear as a churned customer in Retention, the lead book, the metrics or any cancelled-club list.

Two alternatives I did not draw as the answer. (a) Tidy the three records on the first visit after the end: it makes a read into a write of three records Stripe's events also write, it misses a coach who never visits, and the sweeps would keep emailing families. (b) Leave the organization "active" and let only the entitlement end: that is the version where families keep getting payment reminders for a team that no longer has the product. Both dropped; say if you want either argued.

MONEY AND SAFETY

Giving Premium away is a money-adjacent write, and it moves a coach's data

A give is revenue not collected, and it also makes a portal and copies a coach's roster, games and fees into it. So it is designed and reviewed as both: the way a refund would be, and the way a data migration would be. Below: who may give it, the longest period, what is kept, what stops someone giving free Premium to themselves or a friend, and the security review's threat list run over the design. Data loss (T15 to T19) and double copy (T20 to T22) are listed beside the usual ones.

Who, how long, what is kept

  • Who gives it: a new power for portal free time, held by Super admin, Billing and Support (question 3). Product, Growth and Read only see it and do not give it.
  • How long: it always ends. 90 days for Support and Billing, 6 months for Super admin, measured from the day it was first given, so changes cannot chain past it (questions 2 and 5).
  • What is kept: who, when, the coach, the team, through when, the reason, every change to the end, what was brought across, and how it ended: each a Story entry on the portal and an audit entry. The reason is required.
  • What is never done at the end: archive, delete, tag for retention, suspend, close pages, touch Stripe, send mail. The only change at the end is whether the portal is in force.

What stops free Premium for yourself or a friend

  • Yourself: the server refuses a give to your own account, and to any team in a club you belong to (drawing A, lines 5 and 6).
  • A friend: nothing technical can know who your friends are. What it can do is make every give seen: on the portal's Story with your name and reason, in the Free time list's "Given by staff" view, in Today's queue before it ends, and in the audit log, which Super admin reads. The 90-day limit caps one bad give at about three months of one small plan.
  • Serial gives: one first give per coach; a second needs a Super admin (question 4). As refunds do, a giver's sixth give inside a week needs a Super admin too (recommended in question 3; say if you would drop it).
  • No one gives quietly: no bulk give for portals (question 9), no give without an end, no give over a paying coach.
