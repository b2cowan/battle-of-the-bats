One word, one line

Twenty steps in five parts. A budget word now carries exactly one line on a plan; picking a word already there adds to it. Part A reads what the one-time join left behind, Part B is the new form, and Part E is four calls that are yours. Nothing here should have moved a total — if one has, that is the defect worth finding.

Part A

What the one-time join left behind

Before today this plan carried two lines on Entry Fees and two on Fundraising drive. The migration joined each pair. These three steps read the result — on dev it rewrote six lines, and on production it will rewrite one pair.

A1

Two lines became one — and the half with no date did not vanish

Budget → List. Open the Tournaments category and find Entry Fees. Open the row.

Entry FeesMay · $1,600.00 no date$2,500.00
May 1$900.00
No date yet$1,600.00
Expected: ONE Entry Fees row at $2,500.00, with a pencil of its own, opening onto its payment dates — not onto two sub-lines. The two amounts under it add to the row's own total; that is the pass condition, not the individual figures.
Why the "No date yet" row is the interesting one. The line this word used to carry as Spring classic entry had no dates at all; its twin had May. Joining them left money that is genuinely unscheduled — and every save on this screen insists a split adds up to its total, so leaving that gap implicit would have made your next edit of this line impossible to save, with an error about money you never mis-entered. It is a real period now, wearing the same words the months view uses.
A2

Two notes became one, joined rather than dropped

Open the Fundraising section and find Fundraising drive. Open its edit form (the pencil) and read the Notes field.

Fundraising driveNotes: Expected team share; test drive$1,810.00
Expected: both notes survive, joined with a semicolon, in the order they were written. The alternative was keeping one and silently losing the other — words a coach typed are the one thing a join cannot reconstruct.
If the joined note reads badly on your own plan, edit it here — it is an ordinary note now. Say in the box whether the join is acceptable or whether it needed a different rule.
A3

Run your eye down the table and tell me what kind of thing each row is

Open every category on the List. Do not read the figures — read the rows. Say out loud what each one is.

Expected: every row is the same kind of thing — a line, wearing its word, with a When answer, an amount and a pencil. Before today some rows at the same indent were summaries with no edit door and others were real lines, and nothing on screen said which was which. There should be no row you cannot edit, and no row that opens onto other rows rather than onto dates.
The one legitimate exception is a Not itemized row, if this plan has one: lines written before a budget word was required have no word to be joined on, so that bucket can still hold several. If you see one, it should be the only one.

Part B

Adding to a word that is already there

The whole point of the change. Seven steps, all in one pass of the Add Line form — and the last one is the way out if you meant to correct rather than add.

B1

The form tells you before you type, not on the way out

Budget → Add Line. Leave the first question on Money the team spends. In Category & Item pick Tournaments → Entry Fees. Stop there and look.

Entry Fees is already on this plan$2,500.00 planned · May
Open that line instead
Expected: the panel appears the moment the word is picked — before you have typed an amount. This is the step the whole design turns on: the same fact delivered at save time is a surprise.
B2

The amount field changes its question

Read the label above the amount box, then type 250.

Expected: it reads Amount to add, not Amount. The figure you are typing is no longer the line's total — it joins one — and a label still saying "Amount" beside a panel reading "$2,500.00 planned" is the ambiguity that makes a coach type 2750.
B3

The date question names the money it is about

Read the next question, then answer it: One month → May. Pick May deliberately — the line already has a May payment, and Part E asks you about what that produces.

Expected: the question reads "When does this $250.00 happen?" — the dates you are giving belong to the money being added, not to the joined total. Before an amount is typed it falls back to the plain "When does this money move?".
B4

Notes is Notes again — and the old question is nowhere

Scroll to the Notes field.

Expected: the label reads Notes, and the field is pre-filled with the word's existing note (the joined one from A2). One word carries one note, so a join that blanked it would lose words you wrote from a control that only says "pick a category".
What must NOT be there: "What makes this line different?", its placeholder, or a sentence explaining the pre-filled note. The question is retired; and a caption explaining something you can plainly see is the over-explaining your own §133 ruling banned.
B5

The arithmetic, where you are about to commit it

Look at the two quiet lines just above the buttons, then read the button itself.

$2,500.00 planned  +  $250.00$2,750.00
ScheduledMay
Expected: the running total, the joined schedule, and a button reading Add to Entry Fees. The button matters — a save that creates no line must not say "Add Line".
B6

Save — and count the Entry Fees rows

Save. Then look at the Tournaments category and open the Entry Fees row.

Expected: still ONE Entry Fees row, now $2,750.00. No second row anywhere on the plan. Open it and the schedule carries the new money alongside the old: two May amounts ($900.00 and $250.00) and the undated $1,600.00, adding to $2,750.00.
The two May rows are deliberate and Part E3 asks you to rule on them. Your $250.00 kept its own date rather than being folded into the existing May payment — folding would have destroyed whichever label the two chunks carried. Look at it now while it is in front of you.
B7

The way out, for when you meant to correct it

Open Add Line again, pick Tournaments → Entry Fees, and this time click Open that line instead.

Expected: the form becomes the edit form for that line — its title changes, the amount shows the line's full total rather than an amount to add, and Delete line appears at the foot.
This door is the undo story for the whole change. Before today, a coach who added a second line by mistake deleted it in one click. There is no second line to delete now, so the mistake has to be preventable instead — which is what this is.

Part C

The doors that are not the Add form

Three more ways a duplicate could have got in, and the file the plan leaves by.

C1

Re-filing a line onto a word that is taken

Open a different line for editing — Facilities → Diamond Permits is a good one. Change its Category & Item to Tournaments → Entry Fees and press Save.

Expected: refused, and the line in the way is named: "Entry Fees is already on this plan. Open that line to change it, or pick a different word." The category picker is flagged and the counter at the foot says there is something to fix.
Then put it back — set the word to Facilities → Diamond Permits again, or cancel the form.
Why refused rather than joined. Merging two lines you had already saved would destroy one of them — its schedule, and anything pointing at it — from a control that only says "change the word". The database refuses it too, so this message is the polite half of a rule that holds either way.
C2

The exported plan has nothing underneath a word

Export the budget plan (the hub header's export door) and open the file.

Expected: under each category, one row per word — and no indented rows beneath any of them. The bands (COSTS / FUNDING), the category rows, Planned costs, Planned funding and the closing ladder are all still there, exactly as §156 built them.
This is a deliberate difference from the mockup you approved, and Part E2 asks you to confirm it. The mockup's "after" file drew a bare flat table with Category and Item columns and no subtotals — which would have deleted the ladder you asked for. Only the nesting came out.
C3

The by-period grid, which was right all along

Switch the Budget tab from List to By period.

Expected: nothing has changed here, and that is the point — this view has always shown one row per word, and it is the view the rest of the plan has now been brought up to. Entry Fees should show its money in May and in the No date yet column, totalling the same figure the List shows.

Part D

The report, and the phone

Where the change shows up away from the Budget tab — and the one measurement no automated check made.

D1

What the planned figure opens onto

Money → Budget vs. Actual → Statement. Find the Entry Fees row and click its Budget figure.

Entry Fees$2,750.00 planned
May 1$900.00
May$250.00
No date yet$1,600.00
Expected: the panel lists the schedule — what this figure is made of — with the word's note beneath it, and a footer that is a working link back to the line on your plan. It used to list the budget lines behind the figure and explain why they were shown as one row; there is nothing left to explain.
The footer must be clickable. This panel dead-ended for everybody until yesterday — that is the regression to watch for, and it is why the door moved into the footer when the rows stopped being links.
D2

No total moved

Still on Budget vs. Actual. Look at the Tournaments category total, the season's Total expenses and Season net. Then go back to the Budget tab and read Planned costs, Planned funding and Player installments.

Expected: the only figure anywhere that changed is the $250.00 you added in Part B. Everything else reads exactly as it did before this release. The join summed what it merged; it never dropped or invented a dollar.
This is the step that matters most. Twenty rows can look right while a total is quietly wrong — and a migration that rewrites money is exactly where that happens. If any figure other than Entry Fees has moved, stop and say so.
D3

On a phone — and this is the only measurement it gets

Open the Budget tab on a real phone, or at 390px wide. Read the plan list, then open Add Line and pick a word already on the plan.

Expected: the plan rows keep their When answer under the name; the "already on this plan" panel, its Open that line instead link and the two preview rows are all readable and comfortably tappable; nothing scrolls sideways.
⚠ check:layout was NOT run for this change — the dev server is shared with other sessions today. And it cannot open a modal in any case, so the new panel, the new labels and the preview rows are measured by this step and nothing else. Do not let the skip read as a pass.

Part E

Four calls, and one thing to know before the release

Tick the option you want — the tick is the answer, so it travels in the paste-back. The notes box is for why.

E1

The spreadsheet import skips a duplicate word rather than adding to it

A sheet holding two rows that name one word — after the importer snaps them to your library's spelling — now imports the first and skips the second, naming the word in the skipped list. The Add form adds; the import does not.

Why they differ: the form adds because you are watching it and can see the running total. A file is not watched. Summing two rows would invent an amount nobody typed; overwriting is what used to happen when two rows shared a match, and it lost one of them in silence. Skipping puts the question back to the person who can answer it.
E2

The exported plan kept its bands and subtotals

You saw the file in C2. The approved mockup drew the "after" as a bare flat table — Category and Item as columns, no bands, no category rows, no subtotals. What shipped removes only the indented rows beneath a word and leaves everything else.

Why I deviated: the drawn file would have deleted the closing ladder §156 added on your own instruction — "with no grouping or subtotals it doesn't read like x + y = plan". The nesting was the part of that specimen that carried the argument; the column redesign was over-drawn. Flagged here rather than left for you to find.
E3

Two payments in one month, after a join

In B6 your $250.00 landed as its own May row beside the existing $900.00, rather than being folded into it. The months view adds them; the plan list shows them separately.

Built this way because a fold destroys words. Two chunks in one month can carry two different labels a coach typed — a deposit and a balance, say — and merging them has to throw one away. But a plan with several same-month rows reads busier than one that adds them up.
E4

The thing a coach can no longer do

A coach can no longer delete one piece of a word's money. Two tournaments under Entry Fees is one line of $2,750.00 with dates; dropping the March one means editing the total and the dates rather than deleting a row. Per-event words like "Regional qualifier" are a note now, not a row.

You accepted this at Q1 on paper. This step asks whether it still reads as an acceptable trade having used the screen — that is the only place the answer is worth anything. The escape hatch, if you want one, is the schedule: two entry fees at different times are one line with two dated payments, which is what the months view wanted anyway.
E5

⚠ Before this reaches production — read, then tick

Migration 286 must NOT be applied to production ahead of the code. Its first half — joining the duplicates — is harmless against what is deployed today. Its second half is the rule that stops duplicates, and the currently deployed form still tries to create them: applied early, a visitor adding a second line on the public coach demo would meet a raw database error. It rides the same promote as the code, in the file's own order.
Production holds one duplicate pair today — two lines on one word, on one of four plans, neither carrying a note — so the join rewrites one pair there. It is one-way: the pieces cannot be un-joined afterwards.
One build check reads red until then: the schema-parity gate sees the new rule on dev and not on production. It was deliberately not silenced — that ratchet reached zero last week and this is owed, not accepted.
