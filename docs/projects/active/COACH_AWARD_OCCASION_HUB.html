PM brief · drawn 2026-10-01 · ruled and built 2026-10-02

Which game an award is for

Started from a production report: Alex Tennant (Milton Bats U13 Purple) showed two MVPs for one game. The coach gave the award twice through two doors; the product kept both. Nothing has been changed on production.

The problem

  • From the Awards page, Give an award can’t say which game. A coach types the game’s name, and the award is tied to nothing and dated the day it was given.
  • A game without its score can’t carry an award, and nothing on the Awards page says so. The coach works around it with words.
  • Nothing notices a second award of the same kind to the same player a day apart, so a slip doubles every count: the leaderboard, the player’s profile, Season Wrapped.

The proposal

  • A “For” dropdown on the Awards page’s Give window: the season’s events that have happened, newest first, each with its day and a game’s result. It opens on the newest one. “Something else” keeps the free-text box and adds the date it happened; “The season” covers recognitions that aren’t about one event.
  • A game waiting for its score is listed, greyed, with “enter its score first”.
  • A quiet double-check in the one shared window, from either door: “Alex Tennant already has MVP for … , Oct 1.” It never blocks.

Who sees it

Coaches who can give awards (head coaches, and assistants given a record duty), on the Awards page and in a game’s window. Families and the club see only the results: awards tied to the right game, counted once.

Why it matters

Awards are what families screenshot and what Season Wrapped celebrates. A doubled MVP is the kind of error a parent notices first, and today the product leads coaches into it.

Success looks like

  • An award given from the Awards page for last night’s game is tied to that game and dated its day, in two taps.
  • A coach who gives the same award twice by mistake is told before saving the second.
  • No new duplicate pairs like Alex’s on production after release (the check on 1 October found this one).

Priority and size

Small: one window, no database change, no new screen. Suggested after the open walks; it touches nothing the club money work touches.

Which game an award is for — plan

Plan of record: all four questions ruled as recommended (2026-10-02). Built on dev the same day; /review and the commit are owed, then the walk (QA walk tab, §257).

As built — what differs from the drawing

  • The dropdown is the portal’s existing sub-lined dropdown (the one the money forms and the staff sheet use), not a new one. It floats over the window instead of opening inside it, because opening a field must not resize the window (an earlier walk rejected that on sight). It gained one ability every caller can use: a row listed but not choosable, saying why.
  • “Earlier in the season” is a band, not a ▾ row. The list shows the four newest events, then Other (Something else, The season), then Earlier in the season with the rest, all in one scrolling list. Four, because four events, the Other band and its two answers fill the list exactly: at six, Something else and The season opened below the fold.
  • The closed field shows an event’s day and result after a dot (“UAT probe practice · Thu Oct 1”); Something else and The season read alone.
  • The Awards page reads the schedule when Give first opens, only up to now (it had not loaded events before). If that read fails, the dropdown still offers Something else and The season, which is how an award was given before.
  • The server refuses a date in the future for a typed occasion (“An award’s date can’t be in the future.”). The window’s Date can’t pick one either.
  • The double-check sits between Award and Note, as drawn, from both doors. The game window passes its game’s own day.
  • The review (2026-10-02) changed four things before the commit: the list is read fresh every time the window opens (read once per visit, a game scored after the first open still said “enter its score first” until a reload); two events on one day list by start time, so a doubleheader opens on the later game; a Date box cleared mid-typing never raises the amber line; a greyed row shows keyboard focus as a quiet outline rather than the “choosable” fill. Left as is: a staff member whose Schedule access is off sees only Something else and The season, because the list is the schedule.
  • From the walk (W1 ✅ 8/8 · W2 ✅ 6/6, 2026-10-02) — two owner calls from W1, both fixed: the double-check’s second line said “close this and remove that one from the Awards page”, which is two actions and leaves the player with neither award; it now says one thing, “If it’s the same award, close this without saving.” And the line read louder than the fields around it at body size; it is now the portal’s note size, with a smaller mark.
  • Proven before the walk: unit tests for the list, its order and states, the second lines, the predicate (one, two and three days; another player; another award; the award being edited; the nearest and newest) and the sentence; a probe of the window at 1440 and 390, Warm and Dark (the list’s rows, which are visible without scrolling, the Something else date’s limit, and the amber line appearing at one day and going at three); the layout sweep of the Awards page in both themes, no new findings.

Findings

F01 — The Awards page can’t say which game

The shared Give window, opened from the Awards page, shows only a free-text “Tournament or occasion” box. The award is stored with that text and no game. The game window passes its game; the Awards page has nothing to pass.

F02 — A game without its score is invisible from the Awards page

A game can carry an award only once both scores are entered (the unlock rule shared with the server). From a game’s window the coach sees “Enter a final score to unlock awards for this game.”; from the Awards page there is no list of games at all, so nothing says it.

F03 — Nothing notices a near-duplicate

Once per occasion is enforced (two unique indexes plus the same check in the give and edit routes), but an occasion is a game, or words plus a date. The same award to the same player for a typed occasion and for the game it means are two occasions.

F04 — Typed occasions are always dated the day given

The window has no date; the route defaults it to today. An award for last night’s game, typed as words, reads tomorrow’s date.

Approach

  • One window, one more input. The Give window gains the team’s events and existing awards from its two callers (the Awards page and the game window both already load them). The “For” dropdown shows only for a new award without a game (the Awards page); the game window and edit mode are unchanged.
  • The list is one pure function beside the unlock rule: events that have happened, newest first, each with its unlock state and label (the existing occasion label: “vs Mississauga Majors”, a practice’s or tournament’s name). Unit-tested.
  • The double-check is one pure predicate beside the once-per-occasion one: same player, same award type, occasion days within two days, excluding the award being edited. It reads the awards the window already holds; no new request.
  • The server already accepts a game for an award and refuses an unscored one; a typed occasion’s date becomes the posted date instead of the default. No migration.
  • Words in the occasion module, one home: the dropdown’s two foot choices, “enter its score first”, the double-check sentence.

Checks

  • Unit: the list’s order and states; the near-duplicate predicate (one day apart, three days apart, the same award being edited, a different player, a different award).
  • Layout sweep on the Awards page and the game window, both themes; /review; help article on giving awards (/docs).
  • QA walk on this hub once built.

Not in this project

  • Removing Alex’s Oct 1 award: the coach does it with the bin icon; or, on your say-so, it is removed on production.
  • Changing which game an existing award is for (still remove and give again).

W1 · Say which game, from the Awards page

What this walk is for

Alex’s two MVPs started here: this window could only take typed words, so a game was given as words and dated the next day. Now it asks which event the award is for, and the answer ties the award to that event and its day.

It passes when

The list holds only what has happened, newest first, opening on the newest one that can carry an award; a game without its score is listed but can’t be chosen; an award given for an event lands on that event, dated by it.

Not in this walk

The double-check line (W2). Editing an award, which is unchanged: an award’s event still can’t be changed.

  1. Open Insights → Awards and tap Give an award.

    For sits at the top and already names an event: the newest one that has happened and can carry an award (on 2026-10-02, “UAT probe practice · Thu Oct 1”). Its day follows a dot, in grey.

  2. Open For.

    The list floats over the window (the window doesn’t grow). Newest first, each with its day; a scored game also shows its result (“W 8–2”, “L 1–2”, “T 3–3”). Four events, then an OTHER band with Something else and The season, all without scrolling. Scroll the list: EARLIER IN THE SEASON holds the rest.

  3. Find a game that has happened but has no score (on 2026-10-02, “vs Probe Rovers · Fri Oct 2”) and tap it.

    It is greyed and says “enter its score first”. Tapping it does nothing; the list stays open. (If the team has no such game that day, tick and note “none to test”.)

  4. Look for the team’s next game (the header’s Next) and any cancelled event.

    Neither is in the list. Only what has already started appears.

  5. Choose Something else. Open the Date picker.

    The field reads just “Something else”. Occasion and Date * appear beside each other (stacked on a phone). Date opens on today, and no day after today can be picked.

  6. Choose The season, then press Escape with the list open.

    Occasion and Date go away. Escape closes only the list, never the window.

  7. Give one for real: For = an older practice (e.g. “Team practice 5 · Fri May 29”), pick a player, choose an award (if the team has none, tap + New and add MVP), and Save.

    The window closes. In Full history the award is dated the practice’s day, not today, and says it was for that practice. Open that practice in Schedule: the award is under Awards given. (Saves one award; removed in W2 step 6.)

  8. Repeat steps 1–2 on a phone (or about 390 px wide), then once in Dark.

    The list fits the screen width and reads clearly; the greyed game is still visibly greyed in Dark.

W2 · Told before giving it twice

What this walk is for

The second MVP was given three minutes after the first, and nothing said so. Now, when the player already holds the same award for an occasion within two days either side, an amber line above Note names it. It never blocks: a tournament weekend can honestly hold two.

It passes when

The line appears for the same player and award within two days, from the Awards page and from the game, goes away at three days or for anyone else, and Save still works.

Not in this walk

The dropdown (W1). The exact same event is still refused at Save, as before (step 4 shows it once).

  1. On the Awards page, tap Give an award. For = Something else, Date = the day after W1 step 7’s practice. Pick the same player and the same award.

    An amber line above Note: “Player already has 🏆 MVP for that practice, its day.” and under it “If it’s the same award, close this without saving.” Save is still available.

  2. Move the Date to three days after the practice. Then back to one day, and choose a different player.

    At three days the line goes. Back at one day it returns; with a different player it goes. Close the window without saving.

  3. Tap Give an award again and leave For on the newest event. Pick the same player and award.

    No line, unless the newest event falls within two days of the practice. Close without saving.

  4. Open the W1 practice in Schedule, tap Give an award, pick the same player and award, and tap Save.

    The amber line shows here too, before Save. Save is refused with the sentence you had before (“… already has MVP for this practice.”). Close.

  5. On the Awards page, open the W1 award’s pencil (on a phone, tap its row, then the pencil).

    Editing it never warns about itself: no amber line.

  6. Clean up: remove the W1 award with its bin. If you added MVP in W1, open Manage awards… and delete it.

    The award is gone from Full history and from the practice. An award type nobody holds deletes outright.
