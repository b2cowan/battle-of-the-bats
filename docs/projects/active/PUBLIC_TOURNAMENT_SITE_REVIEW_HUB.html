What this round is

Codex reviewed the public tournament pages this morning. This is the second review: the same captures, checked against the code, with my own plan, end state and the first stage drawn. Everything is a proposal until you rule it on the Asks tab.

The recommendation in one line: a tournament page becomes the organizer's colour band over the club site's light ground, with the club's type, white tab row and footer, while the tournament keeps its own pages, tabs and game-day tools.

The venue feeling comes from the organizer's colour, name and logo across the top, and the band keeps all three. Today that is read in a dark theme with capital letters in a typewriter face, which is hard to read in daylight on a phone. Families mostly read these pages outdoors.

Fix first: a real defect

A schedule the organizer has not published still reaches families. The home lists its games while the Schedule tab says "coming soon" and each team page says it has no games. Codex read this as demo data. It is not: any club with an unpublished division gets it. It needs no drawing. See Stages and defects.

Two rulings would be reversed

Tournament pages "stay the venue" (Club Tier Stage 4, Ask 1) and "do not warm-theme tournament pages" (Nav Unification, 2026-07-20). Codex's light ground reverses both without naming them. I recommend the reversal too, on its merits, but as a decision you make knowingly (Ask 1).

Added: who owns the look

A club's tournaments are meant to wear the club's colours until they choose their own, but the first save on a tournament's Branding page silently drops them (D7). The club also has no say. The Who owns the look tab proposes a club rule, in the shape of the coach-names one.

Ask B, widened

A "Visit the club" button is a back link with a bigger face. What a club can honestly advertise to a visiting parent is what is open right now: a tryout, a house-league sign-up. The host card shows that list, which comes from the club home's own data.

New in round 2: every page

The Every page tab draws every public tournament page as a whole phone screen, today beside after, with one measured number each. All drawings are now the phone-browser view (no app bar, Sign in in the band); the end state adds one inside-the-app frame. Four new asks: 14 to 17.

How to read this

  • Every phone frame is 390px wide at true size; the window is 740px tall, about what a phone shows below the browser's bars. Frames scroll inside themselves. Open this page on your phone to judge sizes.
  • "Before" frames are the real captures from the demo. "After" frames are drawn with the club site's real colours, type and spacing, and the club colour roles worked out the way the product works them out.
  • Tags: New Restyled Unchanged. Orange flags open a note when you tap them.

3 · The schedule says "coming soon" while games are live

The home showed live and upcoming games a tap earlier. The Schedule tab says the schedule has not been published, and the team page says Riverdale Rapids are 0-0-0 with no games.

This is a real defect, not demo data. The organizer publishes a schedule division by division. The schedule page and the team page respect that; the home page and the live-scores feed do not, and the schedule feed sends every game to the browser and only hides the unpublished ones on screen. So a draft schedule can reach families from the home page. The demo hits it because its seed never publishes its divisions.

An organizer keeps a schedule unpublished while times are still moving. Showing it on the home page means families plan around times that may change, and the organizer has no way to know they saw them. Fix on the server, in every public read (home, schedule, the scores ticker, team page). Defect D1, Stage 0.

5 · From the club into the tournament

The club's site is light, warm-grey, with a white card per tournament. One tap later the parent is on a near-black page in different type. That is the jump the continuity ask is about.

Both tournaments carry a green "Live" chip, including the one three weeks away. That chip means "published", not "being played" (D3). This demo org has no club site, so it shows the plain fallback list; a club with a site gets the date-aware chips already.

Because the demo organization has no club site, the host card in the end state would be absent on the demo. If the owner wants the demo to show it, that is a /demos decision (giving the demo org a club site), not something to fake.

Direction you accepted on 10-27: in a phone browser a club or tournament page is a website, so there is no app bar and Sign in sits in the band, as on club pages (every other drawing on this page). The rule in the code is already "the club's look unless the tournament has its own". It is broken in two ways.

1. It locks itself off. The tournament's Theme card treats "nothing chosen" as the FieldLogicHQ default. 2. The club cannot see it or set it. Nothing on either screen tells the club its tournaments inherit its look, and the club has no way to require it. A volunteer tournament director can give the club's flagship event any colour.
