Product-manager brief

Admin Design Continuity

What it is

One design system and one theme choice across every working screen in FieldLogicHQ. The club admin, the tournament admin, house league, and the scorekeeper and gate screens adopt the coaches portal's look and patterns, and follow the same Warm / Dark setting the coaches portal already has.

What people see differently

  • Club owners, admins and treasurers: the admin looks and behaves like the coaches portal they already know: warm by default, dark if they choose it, with the same cards, lists, dialogs and phone bar.
  • Tournament organizers: first a restyle (same layout, new look, both themes), then a screen-by-screen redesign in its own project. Their organization's colours stay on their public tournament pages; the working screens stop taking the club colour.
  • Scorekeepers and gate volunteers: the same look, in warm (they arrive by link and have no account setting).
  • Everyone: the help guide follows the theme instead of staying dark.
  • Families and visitors: nothing changes. Public pages stay in each organization's own branding.

Why it matters

Today an owner who also coaches moves between two visual worlds: a warm, modern coaches portal and a dark, older admin. Redesigning club screens one at a time would have left warm pages beside dark pages in the same frame for months. Doing the shared frame and the theme first makes every screen consistent from day one, and makes each later redesign local.

Customer impact

Every tournament organizer who has never picked a theme will see their admin turn warm on the day the foundation ships. That is intended, and it gets a release note saying where the Dark choice lives. The organization colour leaving the admin chrome is also visible; the note says their colours stay on their public pages.

Priority and order

  1. Measure (now): size every admin area and put every admin screen under the automated screenshot check.
  2. Foundation: the frame and theme for all admin; every screen restyled to render correctly in both themes; the help guide follows the theme. Built out of sight behind a switch over several sessions, then released to everyone on one day outside a tournament weekend. Customers see nothing in between.
  3. Club Stage 1 in two halves: the behind-the-screens work (roles, members, invites, buying Club) runs after the baseline, beside the frame work; the club screens are redesigned behind the same switch and ship on the foundation's release day.
  4. Club screens: redesigned inside each club stage, drawn in both themes.
  5. Tournament screens: their own project, alongside; not holding up the Club release.
  6. House league screens: with the club project's house-league stage.

Success criteria

  • Every admin screen passes the automated check in warm and in dark.
  • No hard-coded colour can be added to admin code without the build refusing it.
  • Moving between the coaches portal and the admin feels like one product.
  • Public pages are unchanged.

Findings

T01 — The admin ignores the account theme

The theme setting exists and the coaches portal and consumer app honour it; the admin shell carries no marker, so every admin screen is dark. Phase 1.

T02 — The organization's colour tints the working screens

The organization's brand colours are set on every page under it, and the admin reads them in about 76 places (buttons, borders, glows). The coaches portal uses no team colours. Phase 1, ruling R1.

T03 — The restyle is large

86 admin screens (27 tournament), about 812 hard-coded colours in admin stylesheets and about 1,868 hand-set styles in admin components. The coaches portal's own warm rollout found its breakage in exactly these places. Phases 0 and 1.

T04 — The help guide is fixed dark

By an August ruling, whatever the account theme. Phase 1, ruling R4.

T05 — Scorekeeper and gate screens were left out

July's theming ruling excluded them because volunteers have no account. Excluded from the setting, not from the design system. Phases 1 and 3, ruling R3.

T06 — No admin screen is in the screenshot sweep

The layout sweep covered coach and marketing screens only. Closed by slice 0 (2026-09-25): every admin screen, the phone More menus and the volunteer screens are swept and pictured.

T07 — Two visual worlds

An owner who also coaches moves between a warm coaches portal and a dark admin, with different parts, type and navigation. The whole program.

T08 — The organization's card style reaches the admin

A club's public card style (glass, outlined) is stamped on every page under the organization, admin included. Phase 1, asked as F1.

T09 — Three gaps in the theme itself

33 of the 144 colour settings the admin reads have no warm value: some overlay shades, the standard card's shadow and highlight (227 cards), the organization's colours. Filled first in Phase 1.

T10 — The console type is everywhere

The monospace face is on titles, navigation, buttons and table headings: 561 uses in 47 files. The portal keeps it for eyebrows, chips and readouts. Asked as F2.

T11 — The scorekeeper runs its own colours

A private dark palette with nothing shared, so a new warm palette rather than a remap. Ruling R3; specimen 7.

T12 — The kit has never met a money table

The portal's table standard was drawn for record lists; the ledgers run six or seven columns with actions. Restyled in place and swept; asked as F4.

T13 — Page headers carry no subtitle line

The portal's header has no subtitle slot by an August 2026 ruling. The ratified Club Stage 1 drawings are corrected; asked as F3.
