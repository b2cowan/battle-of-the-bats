What’s included

Four jobs. One platform.

Club is the complete operating system for established clubs: every program, every role, one sign-in.

01
Tournaments

Run events year-round.

Registration, scheduling, brackets, and live scores — same tools your tournament organizers already use. Unlimited tournaments included.

02
House league

Full season management — included.

Player registration, draft, schedules, standings, and season-wide parent messaging. Club includes all of it, with no separate plan to buy.

03
Rep teams and Coaches Portal

Coaches run their team. You run the club.

Every coach gets a Premium Coaches Portal for roster, lineups, documents and team budget. You see each team’s roster, schedule and results, what it owes the club, and the cash it holds. Lineups and the team’s own books stay with the coach.

04
Accounting

Finances in one place — not three spreadsheets.

One ledger for the club’s money, a budget checked against what was actually spent, and club costs split across teams, with a reminder to each head coach and a tick when the payment arrives. Type in a bank statement’s balance and see whether your books agree; when they don’t, it helps you find the difference. The treasurer stops living in spreadsheets.

How it works

Registration to final standings — four steps.

From the moment registration opens to the final game night — everything runs in one place.

01
Registration

Players register online.

Your season page collects player info and contact details. You review applications, accept players, and manage the waitlist — no Google Form, no inbox.

02
Draft

Draft your teams before the first game.

Assign players to divisions and teams from your registered pool — by pick order or a random draw. The draft happens in the dashboard — no printed lists, no floor-level sorting.

03
Schedule and standings

Games generate. Standings update automatically.

Auto-generate a round-robin schedule, then adjust any game individually. Standings update the moment scores are entered — parents see results without asking.

04
Notifications

Tell every parent in one message.

Send a season-wide update — a schedule change, a postponement, a standings recap — to every registered family from one screen. No reply-all, no personal inbox, no copy-pasted address list.

The rule these follow

The pitch slides already set it (your ruling of 2026-08-21): a drawing may show anything the product does, as long as nobody can mistake it for a screen, and it never promises something the product lacks. The same six habits keep these eight honest.

  • The old way is on the left, faint and tilted. The answer is on the right, in ink.
  • Two words each side. Everything else is texture: bars stand in for names and amounts, so no invented person or figure appears.
  • One amber mark per picture, on the exact thing that hurts: the final nobody has heard, the address nobody is sure of, the cell that does not add up.
  • No window, no menu, no buttons, no column headings. The answer is sketched in the same hand as the mess.
  • Squares, circles and triangles stand for teams, so no picture belongs to one sport.

The same screens, drawn

Each one shows only what today’s screenshot of that screen shows, with bars in place of names and figures, so it promises nothing the screenshot does not. After the design check, none of them draws a window, a tab or a button shape: a decision is a pen stroke, and the email picture shows who gets the message and the message, not the window it is written in.

Your calls

Four, each with a recommendation. Pick, then copy your answers back into the chat.

D1Which pictures go on the cards

The plan records your picture call of 2026-11-20 as “A, the product’s own screens”, which is why screenshots shipped; either drawn option replaces that. Old way → answer says the most in each picture and matches the pitch deck, but For Clubs then says “before and after” twice, once in its old-way cards and once here. The same screens, drawn is calm and shows what you get, but once names and figures become bars, a registration list, a roster and a standings table look much alike; and with no faint old way beside it, an answer-only picture has less to mark it as a drawing, which is the one thing your 2026-08-21 ruling asks a drawing to make certain. Screenshots are the only ones that are proof, and the busiest at card size.

D2A drawn picture sits on a paper-coloured panel with no outline

Today’s screenshot has a hairline frame, which is what makes a picture read as a window. Either drawn option drops the frame and takes the page’s own paper colour.

D3The line under the cards

With drawings, “These are the product’s own screens…” is no longer true and comes off. Your ruling of 2026-08-21 on the pitch pages was to make no claim about the pictures and point at the demo instead, so the line is an invitation only. Its words go through the marketing and legal reads before it is built.

D4What happens to the screenshot upkeep

With no screenshots on these pages, the quarterly re-shoot has nothing to refresh. A drawing needs someone to notice when its screen changes; the old-way drawings are the least exposed to that, because they picture the job and not the layout.
