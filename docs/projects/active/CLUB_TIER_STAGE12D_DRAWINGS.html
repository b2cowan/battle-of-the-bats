Found first: a tournament team can pay twice, and nothing says so

I read the code that books a team's online payment. It records the amount the pay page was opened with, and never compares it with what the team still owes. House league is safe: its writer refuses a second payment and raises an item with a refund door. A team is not.

  1. The ordinary way it happens. The team's contact opens Pay now and leaves the pay page open (an open page lasts up to a day). Meanwhile the treasurer records the team's e-transfer. The desk only refuses a hand-recorded payment while a bank debit is clearing, not while a pay page is open. The contact then finishes the pay page. Stripe takes the money, our books record it, the receipt goes out, and the team has paid twice. No item, no flag.
  2. The same shape after a price change. A page opened before the entry fee was lowered, or before a part refund, can still be paid at the old amount.
  3. What does not overpay: two devices pressing Pay (they get the same single page), and a card payment while a bank debit clears (refused).

So frame 2 opens with a fix in two halves: a payment recorded by hand closes any open pay page for that team or child (ask 12), and a payment that lands on a team that owes less is booked and flagged, with a refund door (ask 10).

Where I push back, before the work

  1. A payouts list is worth building even though Stripe has one. Stripe's list cannot name the child or team, or our receipt number, and a treasurer matches a bank line to people, not to Stripe ids. Without it, a $1,284.50 deposit means a trip through Stripe and a spreadsheet. Settings is the owner's alone, so the list goes where the treasurer already works: a Payouts tab in Accounting (frame 3, ask 5). It also feeds the bank reconciliation project already on the list, which should read the same payouts rather than build a second copy.
  2. No dispute workflow we cannot finish. The club answers in Stripe with its evidence. We show the dispute, hold the book line, say when the answer is due, and link out. Nothing in the product submits evidence. Doing more means building Stripe's evidence form into our product: file uploads, per-reason fields, the due-date clock and Stripe's review states, for something a club meets a few times a year. The one cheap thing I would add is a Copy of the facts we already hold (sign-up, receipt, emails), ready to paste into Stripe (ask 13).
  3. The book line stays while a dispute is open. A lost dispute writes a returned line beside the receipt, exactly as 12c does for a reversed bank debit. A won one goes back to plain paid. Taking it off the book at once is drawn small in frame 1 and rejected: most disputes the club answers well come back, and taking money off and back on writes two lines for nothing (ask 3).
  4. A downgrade stops online payments; it does not disconnect. Pay now stops being offered; debits clearing, refunds and disputes still finish; the owner is told what is outstanding. Disconnecting is never a side effect of billing (ask 4).
  5. The person who can answer a dispute is one person. Only the person who set up payouts (here, the owner) can sign in to Stripe's dashboard. The registrar who sees "disputed" on Henry's row cannot act on it. So the item goes to the desk and the owner, and the desk's copy names who answers it. Amber goes only to that person, with an email on the day it opens and three days before it's due.
  6. Refusing foreign cards strands a US team. Bank debit needs a Canadian bank account, and e-transfer is Canadian too. Option c in D2 leaves a US team with a cheque. That is why I recommend absorbing the cost and counting it (D2, ask 9).
  7. Disconnecting leaves open pay pages payable. A card page opened before a disconnect can still be paid afterwards. The money reaches the club, but the club has just said it doesn't want online payments. I recommend disconnect closes open pages (ask 11).

D1 · GST/HST on the processing fee

The question, plainly

When a family pays the club online, FieldLogicHQ keeps a processing fee. If that fee is something FieldLogicHQ sells, sales tax may be owed on it. If so, either the family pays the tax on top, or the tax comes out of what FieldLogicHQ keeps. This page does not say which is true. That is the accountant's answer. It shows what each answer looks like on screen and in the margin, and ends with the questions to forward.

What FieldLogicHQ keeps today, before any tax

Our fee: lib/plan-config.ts rates, on the base, family pays (the default). Stripe's costs: card 2.9% + $0.30 and bank debit 1% + $0.40 capped at $5.00, on what the family is charged; the payout fee 0.25% of what reaches the club (stripe.com/en-ca pricing, confirmed live 2026-07-28 in the scoping plan). FieldLogicHQ online payments: GST/HST questions

Context. FieldLogicHQ is software for community sport clubs. Clubs can take registration fees online through Stripe. The money goes straight to the club's own Stripe account; FieldLogicHQ never holds it. On each payment FieldLogicHQ takes a processing fee (a percentage plus a flat amount), deducted automatically by Stripe as an "application fee". By default the family pays this fee on top of the club's fee; a club can choose to absorb it. FieldLogicHQ pays Stripe's own processing costs out of that fee. The receipt is issued in the club's name.

1. Is FieldLogicHQ registered for GST/HST today, or under the small-supplier threshold? (We can check our registration; nothing in our records says whether we charge tax on our own subscriptions.)
2. Is the processing fee a taxable supply made by FieldLogicHQ, or an exempt financial service? And who is the customer for it: the club (the merchant) or the family who pays it?
3. If it is taxable: do we charge tax on top of the fee, or treat the fee as tax-inclusive and remit a share? Which province's rate applies when a family in one province pays a club in another? How does Quebec's QST apply?
4. What must the receipt show? The receipt is in the club's name, but the fee is ours. Who issues the tax line, and does our registration number go on it?
5. When the club absorbs the fee (it comes out of each payout), and when we bill a club a returned bank debit fee (CA$5.00 on its next FieldLogicHQ invoice): is each a taxable supply to the club, and does the tax go on that invoice?
6. Stripe says its payment processing fees to us are tax exempt, and its software products are taxed. Does that change our net, and can we claim input tax credits on anything Stripe bills us?

D3 · Negative balances and what disputes cost

What the code does

  • Stripe covers a club's losses. If a club's Stripe balance goes below zero and Stripe can't recover it from the club's bank, the loss is Stripe's, not FieldLogicHQ's. Stripe's risk team manages that risk.
  • FieldLogicHQ pays Stripe's fees, including every fee a dispute brings. The disputed money itself comes out of the club's balance.
  • So the money question is not "who covers a bad club" but "who pays the dispute fees", and today that's FieldLogicHQ, every time.

A bad month, computed

Where it can't be passed on

  • A club that disconnects or cancels with disputes open. The disputes still run, and Stripe still bills us. A disconnected club still has an invoice to bill; a cancelled club has none, so those fees are ours.
  • A free or comped club (Founding Season 2027): ruled 10-26, invoiced too, so a comped club has no reason to run up fees we can't recover. It has no subscription invoice until its comp ends, so it gets its own invoice.
  • A won dispute: Stripe returns only the answering fee. The first $15.00 is gone whatever happens.

What I need from you
