# Public page layout standard — PM brief

**Status:** drawn 2026-11-04; every ask ruled 2026-11-04 (A1–A14, as recommended); build Part 1 (one page column on every page, two columns only from a 1,200 px window, the written standard) built 2026-11-05; Parts 2a–5 next. Plan: `PUBLIC_PAGE_LAYOUT_STANDARD_PLAN.md`.

## What changes for a visitor

On a computer, every public tournament page now lays out the same way. The content starts in the same spot below the
band, stops at the same right edge, and keeps the same spacing between sections. A game looks identical on the
Overview, the Schedule, Playoffs and a team's page: the time or "Live now" on the left, both teams stacked with their
scores in one column, and the field on the right or underneath. "Live" always looks the same, green. Lists of the same
thing (results, teams, rules) are one tidy list, not a scatter of separate tiles. The club link on the Overview is
still there, just no longer the loudest thing on the page.

## Why it matters

Tournament day is when strangers meet the club. When a parent clicks from Schedule to Standings to Teams and the page
shifts, re-learns its layout or draws the same game a new way, the site feels unfinished. A steady layout reads as
trustworthy and makes it faster to find a game. The written standard also stops the next page from drifting.

## Who is affected

Everyone who opens a public tournament page on a desktop or laptop: families, coaches, fans. Organizers see no
change in their admin screens. Phone pages keep their layout; only the shared parts (the game row, the live look,
the lists) change there too.

## Priority

After the public tournament site's Stage 8 switch-on. It reuses every part that work built. The club's public pages
get the same pass next, so moving between a club and its tournament feels like one site.

## Success looks like

- Clicking through the six main tournament pages, the content's left edge, right edge and top never move.
- A game, a live game, a door and a list each look the same on every page.
- The written standard exists and the club round is checked against it.
- The owner walks the pages and calls the flow smooth.
