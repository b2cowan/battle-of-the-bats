Product-manager brief

Manage staff, delegated — a head coach can hand the Staff page to a manager

Directed by the owner 2026-09-13 · Manager preset ON ruled the same day · Priority: medium — no data exposure today; a real friction for the Team manager role, and a standalone team's only alternative is a second head coach.

What a head coach sees and does differently

  • One more switch on a person's access sheet — “Manage staff”. It sits in the Sensitive group with money and family contacts, so turning it on asks you to confirm first. It is off for everyone by default.
  • A new Team manager starts with it on. When you invite someone as a Team manager, the sheet shows Manage staff already on before you send — switch it off there like any other starting grant. Managers you invited before this shipped are not changed; flip it for them if you want it.
  • With it on, that person can open your Staff page, invite people, change what others can open, resend or cancel invites, and remove someone — with three walls:
    1. They can never touch a head coach. Not your access, not your role, not remove you. Your row opens for them read-only.
    2. They can only hand out what they hold. Everyday access (schedule, attendance, lineups, development, chat, scouting book, documents) they may give freely. Sensitive access — money, contacts, internal notes, emailing families, tryouts — only up to their own level.
    3. They can't pass the switch on. Only a head coach decides who manages staff, so you always know exactly who holds it.
  • “Make head coach” stays yours. Roles are only ever changed by a head coach.

What a Team manager (or anyone granted) sees differently

  • Staff appears in their Team menu — desktop, phone bar, and the phone's More sheet — with Invite someone in the header. Today the page says “Only the head coach manages staff”.
  • Opening someone's access, switches they can't hand out are shown but locked, with the reason under each: “You don't hold this, so you can't hand it out” — or, on Manage staff, “Only a head coach changes this.”
  • Picking a role preset for someone applies it within their own level and says so on any switch it had to cap.
  • Their own row opens read-only (“Ask a head coach to change it”). A head coach's row opens read-only (“Only another head coach changes a head coach”).
  • The footer has Remove from team but no Make head coach.

What does not change

Treasurers, helpers and plain assistants: no Staff door, exactly as today. Two head coaches, hand-over, the last-head-coach rule, the club admin's oversight page and the invite emails are unchanged. Nothing in the database changes shape — no migration, nothing to apply to prod separately. The coach demo sandbox has no staff rows and no narration about the Staff page, so nothing there goes stale.

Why it matters

The Team manager role exists to run the team off the field. Inviting Saturday's parent helper and chasing a lapsed invite is that job, and today only a head coach can do it. The only way to hand it over is Make head coach, which also hands over every family's contact details, tryouts, internal notes and the power to remove the head coach. A switch bounded by “only what you hold” gives the manager the duty without the keys.

Trade-offs made

  • Default off everywhere except the Manager preset. Existing managers keep their current access — a preset is where access starts, and the master key is the one switch that should never widen by default.
  • The ceiling is about sensitive access, not everything. A manager without Attendance can still give an assistant Attendance — a coaching duty, not a data exposure. Bounding everyday grants would make the switch useless to the person it's for.
  • Removal is allowed for anyone who isn't a head coach — disruptive but reversible. One line to flip if you'd rather keep removal head-only.

One thing worth deciding separately (not built here)

There is no record of who changed someone's access, today or after this. With one head coach it didn't matter; with a manager also editing access, “who turned on Money for Marcus?” has no answer. Proposed follow-up: a “changed by …, date” line on each person's sheet. Logged on the Decisions tab as Proposed.

Success criteria

  • A head coach grants Manage staff to a manager; the manager invites a helper end to end without the head coach — and cannot widen anyone's money, contacts, notes, email or tryouts past their own, cannot touch a head coach, cannot pass the switch on (QA §179, parts A–E).
  • Treasurers and helpers still see no Staff door.
  • No migration; no demo drift; the help article answers “can someone else manage my staff?” in one Q.

Findings — what the code does today

F01 — Delegating staff means making a second head coach

Staff management is head-coach-only at every layer: the nav door (case 'Staff': return caps.isHeadCoach), the page gate, and every route (list, invite, member edit/remove, pending-invite verbs all call requireHeadCoachMembership). No grant exists for it. The only hand-off is Make head coach, which also grants money write, every family's contacts, tryouts, internal notes and the power to remove the head coach. Standalone Premium teams have no club admin to fall back on. Fix: a manageStaff grant; the four routes and the two doors gate on canManageStaff = isHeadCoach || manageStaff.

F02 — Staff management is a master key, and “protect the head coach” alone doesn't close it

Whoever can set other people's grants can reach anything one step removed — grant a friend money access, or invite a second account of their own with everything on. Protecting head-coach rows doesn't close that. Fix: the ceiling (D3): a delegate may widen a Sensitive grant only up to what they hold; they cannot edit their own row (D5); a delegate's bundle is clamped by the sheet (D8) and checked by the server.

F03 — Roles and head coaches

The owner's wall: a delegate can never do anything to a head coach's permissions. Fix: head rows are read-only for a delegate and refused on every verb; the coachRole branch checks isHeadCoach explicitly (D4); the Manage staff switch itself is never grantable by a delegate (D6).

F04 — The not-granted copy would lie

“Only the head coach manages staff” stops being true once a manager holds the page. Fix: “Managing staff isn't turned on for you · Ask your head coach if you need it.”

The one rule the server runs: for each Sensitive key, if the change widens it, the new level must be ≤ the actor's own level; manageStaff must be false in anything a non-head actor writes. Head-coach actors skip the check. Rank: off 0 · view/read 1 · manage/write 2; booleans 0/1 — moved from the sheet into the pure module so both sides read one definition.

Model

  • manageStaff joins the grant bundle and the resolved capabilities; assistant default false, head coach true; presets: manager true, the other three false.
  • canManageStaff(c) — the one predicate nav, page, header button and route gate read.
  • The manager's one-line description gains “the staff list” (read by dropdown, sheet hint and invite email).
  • The sheet's grantsFrom() returns a Required<> bundle, so adding the key is a compile error until the sheet handles it.
  • No migration. Grants are jsonb; an absent key resolves to the default. Nothing to backfill, nothing for a schema gate to miss.

The pure rule — new lib/coach-staff-delegation.ts

Framework-free, table-tested, imported by the server and the sheet: delegationViolation(actor, current, next) (null = allowed; names the key and whether it was above the ceiling or the master key), clampForDelegate(actor, current, next) (the sheet's D8, returns the clamped keys), delegateMayEditRow(actor, target) (D4 + D5). Head-coach actors: no violation, nothing clamped.
