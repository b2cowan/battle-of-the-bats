06The last row’s name

Your proposal — a paired header naming both directions, Shortfall (Surplus) — is the right shape and I’d take it over what round 2 drew. It is the standard accounting line label (Net income (loss)), it teaches the notation in the header instead of in a footnote, and it is honest about a row whose sign genuinely flips column to column.

One of the two words has a problem, and it is not a style preference.

“Surplus” is already taken, by real moneyOn the Player Dues tab, Surplus to share is the season-end settlement: cash actually left over, divided among families, with hold-backs and payouts. A coach can pay it out.

A bracketed figure on this row is nothing of the kind — it is a timing artifact of a plan, money that arrives in Q3 for bills that fall in Q4. Nobody can share it. Using one word for both means the same coach reads “surplus” on two screens meaning two different things, one of which they can spend.

“Shortfall” is milder but not free: the two states already have names on the List — Short of covering the plan and Planned buffer — and a help article draws the contrast in those exact words, saying collecting above plan reads as a buffer rather than a negative shortfall. Three candidates, drawn together in one grid so you can see what each does to the width of the pinned first column.

Buffer is what the List calls it, what the tile calls it, and what the help article calls it. The cost is that “Shortfall” is a slightly different word from the List’s “Short of covering the plan” — a shortened noun for the same state, which I think a header can carry.

Whichever you pick, the paired header earns its keep twice: it says what a bracket means on the row where the sign flips most, and the Costs less funding row above keeps its own bracket note, because that row’s brackets mean something different — funding ahead of bills, not dues ahead of the plan.
