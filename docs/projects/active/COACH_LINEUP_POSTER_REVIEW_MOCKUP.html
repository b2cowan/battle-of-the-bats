The dugout poster, reviewed — and a portrait sheet for the clipboard

This is the sheet a coach pins to the dugout wall or clips to a board: read at arm's length, in sun, written on in pen. Every note below improves how it reads or how it is used; none adds decoration. The four rulings already made on this document are kept as they are (listed near the foot).

Specimens are drawn from the poster's own geometry — letter paper, 13 mm margins, the same twelve players and seven innings as the screenshot — with type sizes stated in points. Recommendations

Numbered to match the red-pen marks. Each names what prints today, what would print instead, and why — argued from the sheet, not from taste.

The jersey number gets its own column

Restyled
Today
"#2 Blake Test" sits in a column whose heading is also "#" — two different numbers share one symbol on every row, and a coach cannot run a finger down the jersey numbers.
Proposed
Order · No. · Player. The number becomes a column the eye can scan; the name loses its prefix, so a long name shrinks less often (row 12 fits at full size). Costs about 5 mm of inning width in landscape.

The team name prints once

Restyled
Today
The quiet identity line reads the team name, and the headline 8 mm below opens with the same words. This is every team's poster, not just this one — on team paper the identity line is always the team name.
Proposed
The crest sits beside the headline; the duplicate line goes. Nothing is lost — the club's own name is not printed on team paper today either. The grid gains the room.

"vs" steps back; Home or Away is named

RestyledNew
Today
"vs" is set in the same 20-point bold as the two team names, padded out with spaces — it reads as a third name. Away games already print "@".
Proposed
"vs" / "@" at 12 pt in the team's accent colour. A small HOME / AWAY mark on the date line — the bench wants to know who bats first; a neutral site prints nothing. The never-truncate headline rule is untouched.

Inning numerals at cell size

Restyled
Today
The header numerals are 9.5 pt while the positions beneath them are 12–14 pt — the row a coach scans across to find "inning 4" is the smallest type on the grid.
Proposed
Header band 10 mm, numerals 12 pt bold, at least as large as what they head.

Row striping that survives a printer

Restyled
Today
The alternate-row tint is about 3% grey. On screen you can just see it; most office lasers print it as white, so the striping a wide grid relies on disappears on paper.
Proposed
About 6% neutral grey — visible on paper, toner-light, and no colour under a pen mark (a tinted cell makes a pencilled "SS" look like a correction).

A rule between the order and the subs

New9-player mode only
Today
Substitutes print as ordinary rows with "(sub)" after the name, striped like the rest — the order and the bench run together.
Proposed
A heavy rule after the last batter and "Subs" in the order column — the same separation the batting order card already makes at its foot. Subs keep their inning cells; they come in mid-game.

A portrait sheet for the clipboard

New
Today
Landscape only. Clipboards are portrait; a landscape sheet clips sideways or hangs off the edge.
Proposed
The same document, turned — chosen in the Print menu, remembered on that device. For twelve players the rows go from 12 mm to 17 mm: half again the room for a pen. Innings measure 17 mm at seven, 13 mm at nine, 10 mm at twelve; past nine innings landscape is the roomier pen sheet and stays one tap away.

Words that match the paper

Fix
Today
The Print menu and the "Print with open roles?" check both still promise "blank boxes". The drawn box was removed on 08-26 and the poster's own legend was corrected then; these two sentences were not.
Proposed
"Blank cells" in both places.

The paper speaks the sport's words

RestyledCross-sport
Today
The poster heading reads "Batter" and the card is titled "BATTING ORDER" for every sport — while the screen already says "Playing order" and "Quarter" for a basketball team.
Proposed
"Player" over the names; the card takes the sport's own order label. Free while the header is being re-laid.

Left alone, on purpose

Rulings already made on this document, checked against every note above and not reopened.

  • Full club identity on the poster. Crest and team name stay; only the duplicated text line goes.
  • Rows fill the sheet, no cap. Kept — portrait makes them taller still.
  • No inner pen-box in a blank cell. The ruled cell is the box. Nothing here draws one.
  • The headline shrinks, then wraps, never truncates. The lighter "vs" rides inside that same rule.
  • The batting order card stays one full page. Not reopened; it only picks up the sport's order label (note 9).

Decisions

Four calls that change what gets built. The recommended path is first in each.

D1How the orientation is offered

  1. One Dugout poster row with a Landscape · wall / Portrait · clipboard switch, remembered per device.Recommended One document with an option — not two documents that differ only by a turn.
  2. Two separate rows, "Dugout poster" and "Clipboard sheet".

D2First-time default

  1. Portrait.Recommended Your own premise — most coaches carry a clipboard — and the taller rows are the better pen sheet at seven or nine innings. Either way the coach's last choice sticks.
  2. Landscape — today's behaviour, nothing moves for anyone who has already printed one.

D3Column headings

  1. Order · No. · Player.Recommended Unambiguous, and sport-neutral without inventing a word.
  2. Keep "#" and "Batter"; add "No." only.

D4Scope of the pass

  1. Notes 1–9 together with the portrait sheet, as one pass.Recommended They share one header re-lay and one rendered gallery to approve; splitting them means laying the header out twice.
  2. Portrait alone first; the formatting notes as a second pass.

What a coach sees and does differently

  • Opening Print on a lineup, the Dugout poster row now carries a switch: Landscape · wall or Portrait · clipboard. Their pick is remembered on that device. The batting order card and the notes checkbox are unchanged.
  • The poster itself reads cleaner: team name once, the crest beside it, "vs" quieter, HOME or AWAY on the date line, jersey numbers in their own column, bigger inning numerals, striping that actually prints, and — in 9-player mode — a clear line between the order and the subs.
  • Nothing about building the lineup changes; the "print with open roles?" check still runs, and still prints exactly what is in the grid.
  • Head coaches, assistants and anyone who can print today can print both orientations — no new permission.
  • Basketball and hockey teams' paper stops saying "Batter".
