Product-manager brief

Run tournaments, delegated

Owner direction 2026-09-13 — two rulings: delegate with one switch; centralize hosting on the existing Tournaments page · ships after “Manage staff, delegated” (same staff sheet)

What changes for the coach

Today. Every Premium Coaches Portal can run one tournament — a round robin, an exhibition weekend. Only the head coach can actually do it. Every staff member sees the same “Your team can run local tournaments here too — Open setup” invitation on the team Overview, follows it into a tournament page that looks fully theirs, fills in the form, and is refused with a bare “Forbidden” at the last step. And the head coach has no way, anywhere in the portal, to let a manager run the weekend for them.

After.

  1. One Tournaments page, both halves. The page that already lists this season’s entries gains a second section underneath, Tournaments you run. With nothing yet it offers Set up a tournament; once one exists it shows the tournament’s name, dates, status and teams registered, with a Manage door. The section appears only for people who hold the right — everyone else sees the entries and nothing more. The “run local tournaments here too” banner on the Overview goes away, and the “Admin” link at the foot of the menu is untouched. Nobody is walked to a refusal, and there is one place to look.
  2. The staff sheet gains one switch: Run tournaments. It sits in the Sensitive group with Team money and Contacts, because a tournament’s registrations hold other teams’ coaches’ details and payments. Turning it on asks once and says what it hands over; turning it off is instant. Whoever holds it can do everything on the tournament page. There are no finer-grained tournament permissions in the coach portal, on purpose.
  3. Defaults follow the role. A new Team manager starts with it on (their invite email says so); an assistant coach, treasurer or helper starts with it off. Managers already on a team are not changed — it is one tap on their sheet.
  4. Running it happens where it always has. Manage opens the tournament’s own Dashboard — Teams, Schedule, Results, Check-in and the rest, in the admin look, the same screen a standalone tournament account works in. Set up opens the create wizard directly and finishes on that Dashboard. Neither path stops at a list page. A delegate lands on the tournament and nothing else: no Members, no settings, no billing.
  5. Premium workspaces only. In a club, the tournaments belong to the club; club coaches don’t get the switch, and the section shows only for club admins who also coach.

Why it matters

The head coach who bought Premium is a volunteer with a job. “Run the exhibition weekend” is the most delegable thing on the team, and today it is the one thing the portal will not let them hand off — while advertising it to the very people who can’t do it. Fixing both halves turns a dead end into the reason a manager logs in.

Customer impact

  • Head coach: one switch, one confirm, done. Nothing else on their screen moves.
  • Team manager: invited from today, they can run the weekend the day they accept.
  • Assistant / helper / treasurer: a misleading banner disappears; nothing they had is taken.
  • Club coaches / club admins: no change.
  • Do nothing: every assistant keeps hitting “Forbidden”, and the head coach keeps running the tournament themselves or handing over their login.

Trade-offs made

  • All-or-nothing on purpose. No per-area tournament permissions in the coach portal (the owner’s call). A head coach who needs finer control than “run it” is running a tournament business, and that product exists.
  • The switch is per person, not per team, because the tournament belongs to the workspace. Someone on two of your teams gets one answer.
  • Existing managers are not switched on retroactively — widening access silently on ship is the one thing this portal’s access model has never done.

Success criteria

  • Nobody reaches a “Forbidden” from the coach portal; a person without the right sees no hosting section at all.
  • A head coach can grant Run tournaments to a manager from the staff sheet and that manager creates and runs the workspace’s tournament without any admin-side help.
  • A newly invited Team manager has the section without a second step; a newly invited assistant does not.
  • Taking the switch back, or removing the person from their last team, removes the section and the right together.

Run tournaments, delegated — implementation plan

Written 2026-09-13 from the code at dev 87b94167. The source of record is docs/projects/active/COACH_TOURNAMENT_DELEGATION_PLAN.md; this tab is the same plan, readable.

What the code does today

F01 — The “run tournaments” banner is a dead end for everyone but the workspace owner

The banner on the team Overview shows whenever the workspace has the tournaments module and no tournament yet. It never asks who is looking. An assistant coach holds a workspace role that deliberately carries zero rights (a past audit found coach-role members reading org-wide finance through admin routes, so the role was emptied). On the click: the admin shell admits any member except a scorekeeper; the tournament page renders with its list and its create button (neither is gated on the right); the create request is refused with a bare “Forbidden”. That is the standing rule — if a surface cannot honestly serve someone, hide its entry point — broken.

F02 — A head coach has no way to hand tournament-running to their staff

The head coach is the workspace’s owner and can run its one tournament. Every staff member they invite is a rights-less coach. The staff sheet offers thirteen grants; none touches tournaments. The right already exists one floor down: the workspace keeps a per-member override map that every tournament page and route already honours, and the admin Members page can set it — but that page speaks the vocabulary of a tournament company (“override individual capabilities above or below role defaults”) and no head coach will ever find it.

F03 — The coach portal has no home for “the tournament we run”

The banner hides itself once a tournament exists (by design). After that the only door is the “Admin” link at the foot of the coach menu, owner/admin only, into the whole admin area. Meanwhile the portal already has a Tournaments page — this season’s entries, with an empty state that teaches what lands there — and hosting lives nowhere on it.

Design — one grant, Run tournaments

Where it livesOn the person’s workspace membership, not on a team-staff row. The tournament belongs to the workspace (one slot) and a workspace may hold several teams; a person on two teams must get one answer.
What “on” doesWrites the full tournament bundle as that person’s overrides — every tournament action plus the tournament module and its look-and-feel (D4). Never members, settings, billing, or any other module.
What “off” doesRemoves exactly those keys; leaves anything else an admin set there.
How the sheet reads itON when the one key every write route checks is on. A partial hand-set map reads as OFF; switching on writes the whole bundle. The sheet is the front door; the Members page stays the advanced view of the same stored truth.
Group on the sheetSensitive — asks before granting. Registrations carry visiting coaches’ contact details and payment records. Revoking is instant and never asks.
PresetsTeam manager ON · assistant, treasurer, helper OFF. The manager’s invite email gains “the team’s tournaments” (the email must promise what the preset grants).
Existing staffNot changed on ship (D3). A preset is where access starts.
Head coachAlways on; not switchable for themselves.
Where offeredStandalone Premium workspaces only (D2). In a club-attached team the row is not rendered.
Who may flip itThe head coach. When “Manage staff, delegated” ships, its “never more than they hold” wall covers this switch with no extra rule.
LeavingRemoving a person’s last staff seat in the workspace already deletes their workspace membership — the right goes with it. Pin it with a test. Someone still on another team keeps it: the person-level rule holding.

The door — one section on the existing Tournaments page (owner ruling 2)

  • “Tournaments you run”, a second block under this season’s entries, rendered only for a viewer holding the right. Nobody else sees a heading, a row or an empty card.
  • No hosted tournament yet: one quiet card — “Run your own tournament. A quick round robin or exhibition weekend, set up from here.” → Set up a tournament (the acquisition event the banner used to fire moves here).
  • Hosted tournament(s) exist: one row each — name · dates · status word · teams registered · Manage → straight into that tournament’s Dashboard. Normally one row in a Premium workspace (one slot).
  • Below the entries, never above (D8) — entries are the page’s primary content.
  • The Overview banner is retired (D7); the foot-of-menu “Admin” link is untouched — no nav change on any device.
  • The page’s own gate stays: the Tournaments page opens with the Schedule grant (every preset gives it). A holder of the right with Schedule hidden would not reach the section; the switch’s sentence says “on the Tournaments page”, which is the honest answer.
  • In a club: the section renders for a club admin who also coaches; ordinary club coaches see nothing.

What the delegate lands on

Manage opens that tournament’s own Dashboard — the working screen with its rail (Dashboard, Teams, Schedule, Results, Check-in, Staff Kit, Communications, Chat, Setup, Admin), the same screen a standalone tournament account lands on. Set up a tournament opens the create wizard directly and finishes on the new tournament’s Dashboard. The workspace-level “All tournaments” list is never a stop on either path (it remains reachable by the owner through the Admin link, as today). The rail already hides Members and Subscription from anyone without those rights — the bundle does not include them. Same one-slot limit. No admin-side page changes.

Build

Sequencing. Lands after (or with) Manage staff, delegated — both add a Sensitive switch to the same staff sheet and both edit the same presets; that sheet carries another session’s uncommitted work today. Do not build against a stale copy.

Chunk A — the section replaces the banner (independent; ship first)

  1. A small coach-portal call answers “can this person run tournaments here, and which exist?” — empty for anyone who cannot.
  2. The Tournaments page renders the “Tournaments you run” block only when the answer is yes; both existing empty states keep their copy.
  3. The Overview banner and its call are removed; the acquisition event moves to the section’s button.
  4. Tests: no section for a rights-less coach; rows for the owner. This alone closes F01 everywhere and F03 for the head coach.

Chunk B — the grant

  1. Add tournaments to the grant vocabulary and the four presets; update the manager’s invite promise. Document that this key is the one exception — resolved from the workspace membership, not the staff row.
  2. A small pure module owns the bundle: the key list, read, apply. Unit-tested; the single home.
  3. The staff-access save route: when the body carries the switch, the org is a Premium workspace and the caller is the head coach — write the bundle onto the target’s coach membership, re-asserting “still a coach row” in the write (never widen an admin’s map from here). Refuse honestly in a club.
  4. The staff list reads the same truth so the row chip and the sheet agree.
  5. One Sensitive switch on the sheet, rendered only in a Premium workspace; asks-first copy as drawn.
  6. Invite acceptance: when the accepted invite carries the switch (manager preset), write the bundle in the same step that creates the membership.
  7. No nav change (owner ruling 2). The delegate’s door is Chunk A’s section, which already reads the right the switch writes.
  8. Tests: bundle round-trip; refuse in a club; never touch a non-coach row; last-seat removal clears the right; the section appears for a granted manager.

Chunk C — words

  • Help: the staff-access article (grant list + manager sentence) and the coach “run a tournament” mention.
  • Demo: the sandbox’s head coach now finds hosting on the Tournaments page instead of an Overview banner. Re-read any dock/tour line mentioning the banner or “Open setup” at build (grep finds none today), and consider pointing the Tournaments tour step at the section.
  • Spelling gate: no variant words introduced.

Not built here

  • Per-tournament / per-area permissions inside the coach portal (owner: not needed).
  • Backfilling existing managers (D3).
  • Any club-side change.
  • A tournament-slot change: still one.
