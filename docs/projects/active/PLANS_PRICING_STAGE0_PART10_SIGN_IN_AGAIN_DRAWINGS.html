Sign in again before a change that reaches many customers

Two screens that exist today get one new small window: Bulk changes and switching a Production price in the Approval queue. This page draws that window, where it opens on each screen, the email to you afterwards, and the phone. Frames are true size (1440 at a desk, 390 on a phone); open it on a desktop at full width. Every name, count and price is an example. Click any blue number to see why it is there. Your rulings are on tab 8.

One small window, on two doors. Everything else stays as it is.

When a staff member presses the last button of a bulk change, or approves a Production price switch, and their password was last typed more than 10 minutes ago, a small window asks for it. Once it is accepted, the change goes ahead without another press, and an email lands in the owner’s inbox saying what happened. If you signed in a few minutes ago, nothing asks. One-customer work (refunds, plan changes, free time) never asks.

Where I disagree with the spec, or go wider than it

ABulk changes asks because of reach, not money

Today it never touches Stripe. It still changes up to 100 accounts in one press, and that is the reason Ask 17 gave (“an open session should not be enough”). So the rule becomes: many customers at once, or what every new customer pays. Later doors join by the same rule. Ask 1.

BThe password goes on Approve, not on Submit

The live switch happens in the Approval queue at “Approve & apply”. Plans & pricing only files a request. Asking at Submit would protect nothing. Ask 1.

CIt stops a stolen session. It does not stop someone at your unlocked computer.

If your browser saves the password, it fills this window in. That is still worth having: a copied session cookie cannot get past it. Against someone at your desk, the email is the alarm. A second sign-in factor would be its own project. Ask 12.

DThe email names the person, not “You”

It goes to the owner’s inbox, and Billing and Product staff can run bulk changes too. “you@… changed 37 accounts” is true whoever pressed the button. Ask 8.

ENever type your password to be refused

Bulk changes refuses more than 100 accounts, but only after the button is pressed. The server checks everything else first and asks for the password last, and the screen shows the limit. Ask 13.

FOne wider door: Campaigns “Send now”

It emails a whole audience, and that cannot be taken back. Not money, but it fits the rule better than anything else in the console. I recommend it as the next door, drawn on its own. Not in this build. Ask 11.

What it does, step by step

OpensOnly when the server answers “sign in again” to the press. If the password was typed in the last 10 minutes, the press just runs and nothing opens. The cursor starts in the field. Enter presses Sign in and run.
AcceptedThe window closes and the same request goes again once. The screen shows its usual busy state and finish notice. The person stays on the page; nothing reloads.
RefusedStays open, nothing runs. A wrong password is written to the audit log (Ask 7).
Cancel / Esc / tap outsideCloses. Nothing runs. The screen is exactly as it was, still showing “Confirm bulk operation” or “Approve & apply”.
BrowsersThe field is marked as the current password, so password managers offer to fill it (tab 7, the honest limit).

Bulk changes: one quiet sentence by the button, then the window

The screen keeps its own two presses (“Review bulk operation”, then “Confirm bulk operation”). Under the button, one new sentence says a password may be asked and an email follows. It is always there, so the screen never counts down. Step 3 also says the 100-account limit, so nobody types a password only to be refused.

While we are here · the Plan change note says something the server stopped doing (Ask 13)

Before · today

This changes access only — not billing. A plan change here updates each org’s plan and resets its tournament limit to the new plan’s default. It does not touch Stripe — no charge, refund, proration, or cancellation. Any paid org keeps billing at its old price and goes out of sync. Re-price each org from its own Billing & Access screen (downgrades, prorated) or the Stripe Dashboard (upgrades).

After

This changes access only, not billing. Each account’s plan and tournament limit change. A club that Stripe still bills is skipped and keeps its plan: its owner moves plan on their Plan & billing page.

The Approval queue’s detail window is today’s own, not the shared window, and it does not yet fill the phone. The small window opens over it the same way as on Bulk changes. Moving that detail window onto the shared window is Stage 2 work, not this build.

One-customer work stays as it is. I agree with the spec.

A password prompt on everyday support work teaches the hand to type the password without reading. Then it protects nothing on the two doors where it matters. Each of these touches one customer, needs a reason, and is written to the log before it happens.

One customerWhy it can stay without it
Refund a chargeMoney only goes back to that customer’s own card, never anywhere else. It is capped at that charge, and Stage 0 already emails you every refund on a subscription charge.
End now / cancel at renewalOne customer, the red button, a reason. Cancel at renewal has “Keep” to undo it.
Plan change, free time, sectionsAccess only. A club Stripe bills is refused. “Put it back” undoes each one.

Many customers, but not in this build

DoorRecommendation
Campaigns · Send now (one email to a whole audience)The next door (Ask 11). It cannot be taken back and it reaches every customer in the audience. It gets its own short drawing, because its screen is not drawn here.
Plan availability (Live / Early access) and plan trial days or limitsStay out (Ask 1). They change what new customers can buy, but nobody is charged, and the queue can switch them straight back.
Email templates, Retention “Process expiry”Stay out. A template edit can be reset; retention runs on its own schedule anyway.
Stage 3–8 doors: schedule the price moves, Stop a changeAlready ruled (Ask 17, Oct 31). They use this same window when they are built.

The honest limit

Staff sign in with a password alone; there is no second factor anywhere in the console. This window proves the password again. It defeats a copied session, for example a stolen cookie. It does not defeat someone sitting at an unlocked computer whose browser fills the password in. For that case the email is the alarm. A code from an authenticator app at sign-in would close the gap, and it would be its own project for all staff (Ask 12). Until then: let the password manager ask before it fills, and lock the screen.

Thirteen asks, each with my recommendation ticked

Untick what you disagree with, tick an alternative, add a note, then copy the summary at the bottom and paste it back. A blank ask counts as undecided, never as a yes.

Ask 1 · tabs 1, 3, 4
Which actions ask: “many customers at once, or what every new customer pays”?
Recommend yes: all three Bulk changes actions, and Approve & apply / Apply price on a Production Stripe price request. Not Submit, not Test requests, not plan availability or trial days. Later doors (schedule moves, Stop) join by the same rule.
Ask 2 · tab 3
Bulk changes asks on every run, whatever the count?
Recommend yes. A threshold (“only above 10”) is one more rule to remember, and a one-account change belongs on that account’s record anyway.
Ask 3 · tab 2
How long does a typed password count: 10 minutes from the last time it was typed?
Recommend 10 minutes, counted from the last password entry (a sign-in or this window), not from the last click. These actions come in runs: monthly and annual prices together, or a bulk change and then a fix to it. Asking twice in two minutes trains reflex typing. “Every time” is the stricter choice.
Ask 4 · tab 2
After the password is accepted, the change goes ahead by itself?
Recommend yes. You already confirmed it on the screen. The window names the change, and its button is the action (“Sign in and run”, “Sign in and switch”). Pressing the screen’s button a second time adds nothing.
Ask 5 · tabs 2, 6
It is the shipped small question window: 416 wide, no ×, a centred card on a phone that lifts above the keyboard?
Recommend yes. One field and a yes/no is a question, not a form. A typed confirmation already uses this window. A full-screen phone form would hide the screen you were confirming.
Ask 6 · tabs 3, 4
Each screen says beforehand, in one fixed sentence, that a password may be asked and an email follows?
Recommend yes, always shown: under Bulk changes’ button, and above the buttons of a Production price request. No timer, no “in 4 minutes”.
Ask 7 · tabs 2, 5
A wrong password: red line, window stays open, nothing runs, each try written to the audit log, no email?
Recommend yes. The sign-in service sets the “too many tries” wait. An email per typo is noise. A run of wrong tries in the log is what you would look for later.
Ask 8 · tab 5
The email: names the person by email, says the result, the reason, and when the password was entered, links to the audit log, on the warm envelope?
Recommend yes. One email per run, sent after it finishes (a partial result too). Not sent when nothing happened. Owner’s inbox only. Clock “9:14 a.m. Eastern”.
Ask 9 · tabs 3, 4, 5
When the email cannot be sent: the change stands, the audit log gets “Owner email: not sent”, and the finish notice says so?
Recommend yes (the spec’s choice). The log is the record, the email is the alarm. No retry later: a late alarm is worse than an honest “not sent” on the screen of the person who did it.
Ask 10 · tab 7
Single-customer refunds, cancels, plan changes and free time stay without it?
Recommend yes (the spec’s choice). One customer each, a reason, logged first. A refund can only go back to that customer’s card, and refunds already email you.
Ask 11 · tab 7 · wider than asked
Campaigns “Send now” becomes the next door that asks, drawn on its own later?
Recommend yes, later. It emails a whole audience and cannot be taken back. Not built in Part 10: its screen gets its own drawing first.
Ask 12 · tab 7
Accept the honest limit now (a saved password fills the window in), with a second sign-in factor as its own later project?
Recommend yes. This window still defeats a copied session. A second factor changes sign-in for every staff member, so it needs its own plan.
Ask 13 · tabs 3, 4 · small fixes the window relies on
Do these ride along in the Part 10 build?
Recommend all four. (a) and (b) make the window and the email true; (c) avoids typing a password only to be refused; (d) is a note that tells staff something the server no longer does.
