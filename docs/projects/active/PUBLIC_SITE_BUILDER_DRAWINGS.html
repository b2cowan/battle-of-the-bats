Round 1 · drawn 2026-10-21 · RULED 2026-10-21, A1–A9 (A9 in the first build) · Option A: admins change the look; the owner keeps the on/off switch and the web address

Make the site with the site beside you

Public site becomes the one place a club makes its website. The site sits beside the controls and changes as you work. The controls fold into five rows. On a club's first visit, those rows read as a short checklist that ends in Go live. Settings keeps only the club's name, its web address and deletion.

You see what families see

The home page sits to the right of the controls, on a computer or a phone. Pick navy and the bar turns navy. Turn off Tournaments and the section disappears. Nobody has to imagine the result.

Five rows, not nine cards

Look · Home page · About the club · Contacts and documents · Visibility. Each row opens where it sits and shows a one-line summary while closed. The whole editor fits on one screen.

Everything families see, in one place

Logo, colours, type, banner photo and the on/off switch move in from Settings. The read-only Look card and its "Change in Settings" link go away.

Why the long page has to go

  1. It breaks a standing layout rule. In July you ruled that a page holding several different jobs is a grid or tabs, and that you never scroll past one job to reach the next. Today's editor stacks nine cards, so reaching the Look means scrolling past the notice, the sections, About, three lists and Social.
  2. The site is set up in two places, and they save differently. The words autosave in the editor. The look waits for a Save button in Settings, which an admin can't open.
  3. Nothing shows the site. The Look card is a picture of a setting kept somewhere else, and Open takes you to another tab. "Wow, it looks great" needs the site in view while you make it.
  4. Setup and the weekly notice compete. The club notice is the one thing used week to week, and it sits in the middle of things set once a year.

Three things found while drawing

  • Card style doesn't change anything on the club's own site. The club's pages are built without the cards it styles. Only the tournament pages use it. Moving it into a club site editor would add a control that does nothing visible there (Ask 5).
  • Two of the four fonts look the same. "System" and "Barlow" both give the club Barlow titles over Inter text, so there are three real looks (Ask 4).
  • Settings' colour preview still draws the old dark site. It shows a dark card on navy-black, a site the club no longer has. Moving the colours next to a real preview fixes this without a separate repair.

The end state

Public site (owner and admins)

  • Header: Online / Offline · the address · Copy link · Open
  • Club notice: one strip at the top, shown only while the site is online
  • Look: logo · colours (from your logo, a preset, or your own) · type · banner photo
  • Home page: sections, their order, show and hide
  • About the club: tagline · about · questions email · social links
  • Contacts and documents: who to contact · documents and policies · questions families ask
  • Visibility: site on/off and directory listing (owner); the web address reads as text
  • Preview: the home page, on a computer or a phone

Settings (owner only)

  • Organization details: name · web address
  • A door: "Your public site's look and its on/off switch are in Public site."
  • Delete organization

The Visibility row, owner and admin

Owner. Two switch rows as in Settings today, and the same "Take it offline?" window before it goes off. The web address reads as text, with a door to Settings, where changing it gets its own warning.

Admin. The values read as text with one sentence saying who changes them. There are no greyed-out switches (absent beats disabled).

Phone, 390 wide

Open this page on your phone to judge it. The frames are drawn at real size. On a phone the rows open where they sit, as settings cards do everywhere (October 12). There's no room for the preview beside them, so it comes two ways: a strip of the club's bar at the top of the Look row, and the whole site in a window.

① The editor, online. Copy link and Open are their symbols alone (October 20). The five rows and the notice fit on one screen.

② Look, open. The club's bar, drawn live at the top of the row, changes as you pick. The type tiles stack so each sample keeps its width. Clickable.

③ Preview, while offline. With the site offline, Open becomes Preview (an eye) and opens the whole site in a full-screen window with a back arrow. Once online, Open shows the real site, which is always current because everything autosaves.

Settings, before and after

Settings stays the owner's page. It keeps the two things that change the club everywhere, not just on its site: the name and the web address. Changing the address breaks every link families have saved, which is why it stays behind the owner's door.
