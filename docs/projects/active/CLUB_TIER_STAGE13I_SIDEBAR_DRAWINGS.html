The owner's sidebar on five screens today, each at true size. The red dashed line is the bottom of a 900 px laptop screen. The sidebar's length changes with where you are: 10, 21, 18, 15 and 10 rows. The cause is that a program's pages are handled three different ways, so a row doesn't tell you what a click on it will do.

            Changing the screens without changing the nav standard would leave it describing a shape that no longer exists, so each rule this changes is redrawn here, old beside new. Wording is as it will be written into the standard, as ruled on 2026-11-06 (Asks 1–3 and 5–9 A, Ask 4 B).

            This reverses half of a ruling, and says so. On 2026-09-30 (Club Stage 3a Ask 2) the owner ruled: "a program whose pages are views of one body of records is one page with tabs; a program whose pages are separate jobs lists them in the rail… Rep teams stays in the rail." 13i keeps the first half and drops the second. That ruling's own reason is why: on a phone, the sidebar shape reached a sibling page only through the program's first page (three taps), and a tab row reaches every one in one. That holds for Rep teams’ separate jobs as much as for Accounting’s views, and the split it created is the "three ways" the owner now finds clunky. Its other clause stays: a record’s own page one level down (an allocation, a team’s account) has a back arrow and no tab row.

            It also re-opens three rulings on purpose. N3, the arrow rule (Nav Consistency Review Ask A2, 2026-10-17). The collapsed sidebar's program menus (Ask 3 of the same review): with no pages under a row there is nothing for a menu to hold, so every icon in the collapsed sidebar becomes a straight link. N7's "the club sheet's Organization group opens with the Organization page itself" (Ask B3).

            The Stage 13 plan contradicts itself, and the later entry wins. Its ladder note says "Not on it on purpose: regrouping the sidebar's headings"; the owner's 13i entry of the same day brings the PROGRAMS regroup in. The ladder line is superseded; the plan's status log will say so, so a builder isn't misled.
