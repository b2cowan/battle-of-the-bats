Product-manager brief

Groups at every level of a practice

What changes for the coach

A coach can split players into pairs or groups anywhere players are listed in a practice plan, not only inside a rotating circuit. On any block, “Split into groups…” sits next to “Choose players…”. On a station that runs on its own, the same door appears. The practice keeps one list of tonight’s groups at the top of the plan, and a block can reuse a set that already exists (“same pairs as the warm-up”) instead of drawing again. A set of groups can also be brought forward from an earlier practice this season, which is how pitcher-and-catcher pairs stop being re-made every week.

Wherever the plan is read, the pairs are there too: the run screen lists them large, the coach running a station sees that station’s pairs, and the printed sheet and the staff email name them.

Why it matters

Partner work is most of a youth warm-up, and battery work is a standing part of every baseball and softball practice. Today the product can only group players inside a circuit with two or more rotating stations, so a coach who wants “warm-up in pairs” either fakes stations or writes the pairs in a note. And every circuit draws its own groups, so a late arrival has to be placed twice.

Customer impact

  • Head coaches and assistants who write plans (Coach Premium): fewer taps per practice, and pairs that carry across blocks and across weeks.
  • Staff and helpers on the field: they see who is paired with whom on their phone and on paper, before and during practice.
  • No change to who can see what. Groups show player names wherever the plan already shows them, under the same permissions.

Priority

Medium-high. It is the next gap coaches hit once they use the practice builder for real, and most of the parts exist: the Groups room, the draw and the drag were built and walked in September. The work is mostly letting the room open from more places and giving groups a home on the practice.

Success criteria

  • A coach can put a no-station block in pairs in three taps or fewer from the Players line.
  • A second block reuses tonight’s pairs in two taps, and moving one player updates both blocks.
  • Pitcher-and-catcher pairs are brought forward from last week without re-making them.
  • No coach is ever unsure which groups a block uses: every block that uses a set names it, and a shared set says where else it is used.

Not in this project

  • Automatic pitcher-to-catcher pairing (sport-specific, and the draw never sorts by ability or position).
  • A team-level library of saved groups (build only if copying from a past practice proves not to be enough).
  • Named pairs inside each group at each station of a rotating circuit.
Full plan · design stage

Groups at every level of a practice: implementation plan

Status: drawn and ruled 28 Sep 2026 (G1–G8 as drawn), built on dev the same day — see “Build record” below. The owner walk is §247, on the QA Walk tab.

Problem and context

Owner ask (28 Sep 2026): “we need a way to split the team into groups outside of the stations… players paired up during warm ups or for pitcher/catcher practice… options of applying groups at the stations level, block level, or practice level, and make it easy for the coach to understand.”

What the code does today, read from the model and the editor, not from the plans:

F01 — Groups live only inside a rotating block

A set of groups is stored on a block’s rotation, which exists only while the block has two or more stations and “Groups rotate” is pressed. The Groups room opens only from that board. A block with no stations has a flat player list: Whole team, or chosen players.

F02 — Every rotating block draws its own groups

Two circuits in one practice hold two independent sets. There is no way to say “the same groups as before”, so a late arrival must be placed in each.

F03 — “Same groups as last practice” was ruled and never built

D21 (owner-accepted 31 Jul 2026) listed three ways to make groups: pick them myself, draw at random, and same groups as last practice. The model still carries a “previous” source value that nothing sets. The only reuse path today is copying a whole earlier plan.

F04 — A separate station cannot be split

A station in a block that does not rotate holds its own player list, with no grouping. The bullpen case (three batteries while the rest play long toss) has nowhere to go.

Scope

  • A practice-level list of sets of groups (“Groups” fold on the plan page), each with a name and a list of groups.
  • “Split into groups…” on a block’s Players line and on a separate station’s Players line, reusing tonight’s sets, making a new one, or copying one from another practice.
  • A rotating block’s groups become a set on the same list (G3).
  • The Groups room gains a name box, a “used in” foot and “Make a separate copy”.
  • Pairs read as pairs (G5).
  • “From another practice…” (G6).
  • The run screen, “My station”, the helper home, the printed sheet and the staff email name the pairs (G8).
Out of scope

Position-aware pairing; a team-level saved-groups library; pairs inside each group per round at a rotating station; any change to the random draw’s rules (still a shuffle and a deal).

Approach

The one idea

Groups belong to the practice; a block or a station points at one set. The existing rule that people live in exactly one place (1 Aug 2026) is extended, not broken: with no stations, the block’s people are its player list or its set; with separate stations, each station’s people are its list or its set; in a circuit, the circuit’s set. Whichever does not apply is settled away, and the settle step moves people rather than dropping them (the D8 rule from 15 Sep).

The model (technical)

  • The plan gains a list of named sets: an id, a name, its groups (the existing group shape), and how they were made.
  • A block with no stations, a separate station, and a rotation each gain a pointer to one set, in place of (never beside) a player list. A rotation keeps its clock and its hand arrangement; its groups come from the set.
  • Plans saved before this change: the sanitiser lifts each rotation’s groups onto the plan list on read, with a set id derived from the block id so the step is deterministic and idempotent (it runs on every read and write and must hand back the same plan twice). The lifted set is named after its block (“Skills circuit groups”). Plan version bumps.
  • No database migration expected. A plan is stored as a document on the practice, so this is a change to its shape, not a new column. To be confirmed at build time against the live schema snapshots.
  • A set used by nothing is kept (it may have been made up front). Deleting a set that blocks use asks first, names those blocks, and hands each block its players back as a plain list. People move; they never vanish.
  • The hand-arranged rotation’s fit check already resets when its groups change. A change to a shared set therefore resets a hand arrangement on the circuit that uses it, and the editor says so (the D14 rule).

The alternative this beat

Each block keeps its own groups, and “same as Warm-up” copies them. Cheaper (no plan-level list, no lift), but two blocks can then quietly disagree about who is paired with whom, which is the confusion the owner asked to avoid. Rejected unless G1 rules otherwise.

Stages

  1. Groups on the practice. The model, the lift, the fold, the block door and menu, the room’s name and foot, pairs wording, and the circuit using a set. The field screen and the sheet read sets in the same stage, so nothing is written that cannot be read.
  2. Stations and other practices. The separate station’s door and column line; “From another practice…”.

If the owner prefers one pass, both fit in one build; the split exists only so the first walk is shorter.

Build record (28 Sep 2026)

Ruled the same day on the drawings (“looks good, go ahead” — G1–G8 as recommended) and built on dev in one pass, both stages. No database migration: a practice plan is a document on the practice, and its shape gained the practice's list of sets. Plans saved before today read forward — a circuit's groups become one of the practice's sets, named after the circuit.

Where the build differs from the drawings, and why

  • The run screen has no countdown. Screen 7 drew one by mistake; the field screen has had no clock since your ruling of 17 Sep (P10). The build keeps today's run screen and adds the pairs under the block's words.
  • The staff email names no children. Screen 7 said the email carries the pairs. The email was ruled an outline that names no child (it can be forwarded, and it reaches people whatever their settings), so it says “In pairs · Throwing partners” and the button leads to the plan and the printed sheet, which carry the names.
  • The set's name leads the room's body, rather than sitting in the head row as drawn — the shape every sheet on the page shares (the block sheet and the station form both lead with the name).
  • The run screen's list keeps who RUNS a block first, as it always has; the set appears on the block's own screen.
  • Names inside a drawn pair stand in roster order — the shuffle decides who is together; every list in the practice is in roster order.
  • Groups the plan dealt for a circuit dissolve when the circuit goes. Today, pressing “+ Stations” and abandoning it puts every player back on the block. To keep that, a circuit's automatic first deal (and a circuit's groups carried over from before) turn back into the block's players when the circuit stops being one — unless something else uses them. Groups you made yourself stay on the list.
  • A template shows its sets read-only. A template keeps each set's name and “in pairs”, with nobody in it; the practice draws them. Sets are made on a practice, not in the template room.
  • The printed sheet names a set's players once, under the first block that uses it; a later block says “In pairs, Throwing partners”.

Revised the same day, on trying it (G9 · G10)

  • G9 · A set lasts as long as something uses it. You asked why a set nothing links to stays on the plan. It no longer does: when its last block, station or circuit leaves it — back to the whole team, another set, a copy made for the one place that used it — it leaves the Groups list. Its players stay wherever the leaving put them. The one exception is a set made from the Groups list itself (“+ Groups for tonight”, “+ Make groups”, or brought in from another practice there): it stands unused until a block picks it, and from then on it is ordinary. Before a coach leaves a set's last use, the menu says so at its top: “‘Pairs’ is only used here — choosing anything below removes it from tonight's groups.” A shared set warns nothing, because it stays for the other block.
  • G10 · A set made as pairs keeps saying pairs. Hand moves had left “Throwing partners” uneven, and it switched to “3 groups” while its names still said Pair 1–3. Now the set's shape — pairs, or so many groups — is saved the moment it is chosen in the room, and an uneven set of pairs says what is uneven: “3 pairs · one of 3, one of 1”, the way an uneven draw already does.
