# PM Brief — Are the money screens telling coaches the truth?

**One line:** every money form promises the coach what will happen when they save; nobody has ever
checked those promises, and three of them were wrong.

---

## What this is about

When a coach records money, the form tells them what it will do — *"$200 comes back in, Umpires drops
by $200, nobody is owed anything"*, or *"nothing moves yet — this waits as an unpaid cost"*. These
sentences exist because the consequence is **invisible**: the coach cannot otherwise tell whether
cash moved, which report line changed, or whether a family is now owed money.

That makes them the most trusted words on the screen. It also makes a wrong one worse than no
sentence at all — it is confidently misleading at the exact moment money moves.

## Why now

Three have been found wrong, and none was found by a check — all three by someone tracing behaviour
by hand:

- A club bill said it **"doesn't appear on Budget vs. Actual."** It always appeared, under
  *Not itemized*. The product taught the opposite of its own design. *(Fixed.)*
- Recording a **refund** tells the coach the budget line drops and nobody is owed — but never says
  cash on hand went up, while the income branch of the same form says exactly that. *(Live today.)*
- A Payables total read **"still owing"** over a figure that was not what was still owing. *(Fixed.)*

**All three read perfectly.** Several of these lines had already been through review. That is the
argument for a deliberate pass rather than more reading.

## Why it is its own project

Owner ruling, 2026-09-02: it would be the first thing dropped inside the larger forms-review walk,
and it is a **different kind of work** — verifying claims against behaviour, not reviewing form
design. Small, isolated, quick.

## What a coach gets out of it

Nothing new on screen if every sentence turns out true — which is the good outcome. Where one is
wrong, they stop being misinformed about their own money. Where one is silent (the refund case), they
learn what actually happened to their cash.

The second half is the durable part: **a way of writing these lines so the next one cannot go wrong**,
rather than trusting the next author to be careful.

## Size and shape

About 25 sentences across the record-money form, player dues, sponsors, the club fold and the
unplanned-item warnings. Roughly half a session to verify, plus whatever the owner rules on the
mechanism.

**Mockup first.** Four options get drawn and scored before anything is built, from "just fix them" to
"the sentence reports figures it gets from the same code that does the saving, so it cannot
disagree." The middle options are the interesting ones; the strongest is deliberately not the
recommendation, because these sentences earn their keep by explaining in a coach's language and a
table of chips would lose that.

## Priority

**Medium-high, small.** No customer is on the platform yet, so nothing is currently being misled in
production — but this is cheap now and gets more expensive with every sentence added. It should land
before the first live club.

## Success

Every sentence marked verified, corrected, or flagged as untestable with today's fixture; the refund
inconsistency settled; an owner ruling on the mechanism, built; and a written convention so the next
consequence line is checkable by construction.

## Open question for the owner

The in-app help articles restate many of the same claims in their own words. Sweeping them too
roughly doubles the job. Recommendation is **screens first** — the screen is where the coach acts,
and the help pass already has its own sweep.
