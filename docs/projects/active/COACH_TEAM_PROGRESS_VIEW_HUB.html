Why the team figure is a count, not an average

Stage 0 sketched the team trend as “best-of average 8.41 → 8.30 since May” — a figure naming no child, so the refusal to rank seemed to hold. Read against the fixture’s real records it fails a different rule first: the denominator.

The 60-yd sprint’s “team average” per session, and who was actually in it

Date“Team average”Who it is the average ofWhat moved it
6 May8.62Devon alone (a bench-side reading)A “team” figure that is one child’s number. Anyone who knows only Devon was timed in May can read it as Devon.
20 May8.41Devon aloneDevon’s second reading — still one child.
10 Jun8.50Avery 8.70 · Devon 8.24 · Emerson 8.55Two new players enter the sample. The figure rises 0.09 while Devon’s own time fell 0.17.
15 Sept8.42Avery 8.75 · Blake 8.33 · Devon 8.28 · Emerson 8.30Blake’s first-ever result (8.33) lowers the figure on its own; without Blake it reads 8.44. Avery went up 0.05. “8.62 → 8.42 since May” reads as the team getting faster, and nothing on the line says the sample changed.

The same four players, paired — each against their own first result: Devon 8.62 → 8.28 0.34 s lower · Emerson 8.55 → 8.30 0.25 s lower · Avery 8.70 → 8.75 0.05 s higher · Blake one result. The honest sentence is “3 of 12 have two or more results · 2 lower · 1 higher · lower is the aim” — which is what the proposed row says. A mean of those changes (−0.18 s) would hide that one of three went the other way; a chart would draw a line through populations that are not the same population. This page is the reason T3 recommends counts.

This is the plan’s §9 rule applied to one number — “each report needs an explicit denominator; do not compare 4 of 6 from a session against the current roster” — and it fails before the ranking question is reached. The names appear on this page only because it is the owner’s working paper; the product screen names no one.

Design intent: the refusal of a team average is not only the brand’s posture (G7) — on a roster this size it is an arithmetic honesty problem the product would be manufacturing. The count survives because it carries its denominator in the sentence.

Decisions — Team progress

Ruled 2026-09-16 — all eight Build as drawn ("I agree with your mockups", in chat) and built the same day; the controls stay so a later change of mind has somewhere to land. Each says which of the two it is — what goes in, or formatting — what rides on it, and where I’d push if you disagree. Two things the build did differently from the frame, both recorded in the plan’s “Built as” table: the row’s door is the standard’s bare chevron (a row with one door does not name it — register K-19) rather than the words “Coverage →”, and the retired rows sit under the Metrics tab’s own “Retired (N)” fold beneath the table rather than a fold-row inside it. The owner’s one change on the built screen (A, 2026-09-17): the “Two or more” column is gone and the change cell carries its own denominator — “3 compared · 2 lower · 1 higher” — under “Since their first result”. One wording change from the review: the count line reads “…have at least one result or observation this season” so it counts exactly what the Overview’s “Players measured” counts — a player whose only record is an observation would otherwise have been counted on one screen and not the other.

T 1 · what goes in · the question D8 parked

Team progress is a fourth report in Insights → Development — and the Overview does not change

The team-level read belongs where the other team-level reads already live: Results reads the record, Attendance a team percentage, Playing Time the bench balance — Development is the one Insights tab that reads nothing about the roster as a whole. Insights is where a coach already goes to have the season read back; the Overview stays state (D7), and its rail row (“Reports in Insights · 4”) is the door the owner pictured on that screen.

What rides on it: every other decision. The honest alternative: nothing — D8 stays parked. On a young season the report reads thin (on the fixture, four of five active metrics say “none has two results yet”), and that is true rather than useful. Where I’d push: the gap is a screen the other tabs already have, the counts cost no new read, and “none has two results yet” is itself the answer a coach acts on (run a session). “Not now” is a legitimate ruling and the plan records it as one.
T 2 · formatting · the shape and the name

Rows are metrics, never players: Metric · Players with a result · Since their first result · Last recorded — the row is a door to Coverage on that metric

A table whose rows are tests has no child to sort — that is the structural guarantee, ahead of any wording rule. Active metrics in library order, retired ones under a collapsed “Show retired (N)” fold in the quiet ink. The metric name carries the unit and the aim as its sub-line (“seconds · lower is the aim”, “mph · aim: 62–68 mph”, “km/h · record only”, “skill”). The name is called Team progress, pairing with Player progress; the Report field reads Coverage · Team progress · Player progress · Practice review (whole → one).

What rides on it: the phone line (one line per metric, name left, counts and date right, chevron to Coverage — the 390 idiom Coverage already uses). Alternatives: “Season so far” or “By metric” as the name; a fourth Show choice inside Coverage instead of a report (rejected — Coverage’s rows are players, and an all-metrics view there is the P2 matrix with a figure per child per column). Where I’d push: keep the columns to these four; a fifth is where a per-child figure would creep back in. Ruled A on the built screen (2026-09-17): the “Two or more” column the frame drew read as a bare number — it is gone, and the change cell carries its own denominator: “3 compared · 2 lower · 1 higher”.
T 3 · what goes in · the figure

Counts of direction in the aim’s own words — “2 lower · 1 higher”, “1 moved into the range” — never a team average, a mean change, a percentage or a chart

For every player with two or more results on the metric, the same question Player progress answers on “This season”: is the latest lower or higher than the first, in the unit? Counted, not named. A range test counts “moved into the range” / “moved out of the range” / “in range on both”. A record-only test counts higher and lower with no aim stated. Screen 5 shows why an average fails on the fixture’s own records before the ranking question is reached: the sample changes between sessions and the figure moves with it.

What rides on it: one reader behind every number — the count is the number of players whose Player-progress answer line says “lower”, computed from the same series module, so the two screens cannot disagree. Where I’d push: if a mean is ever wanted, the honest one is paired (the same players on both sides) and it still hides the split; counts show it.
T 4 · what goes in · the floor

No minimum — the denominator says how thin it is (“1 of 12 · 1 higher”)

The findings strip on the Insights Dashboard is silent below three players with a result because it is a nudge, and a nudge on thin data erodes trust. A report is a different instrument: it states its denominator in the sentence, so “1 of 12 have a result · 1 higher” is true and reads as thin. On a roster this size, a floor of three would blank four of five rows for most of a season.

Alternative: hide the Since-their-first cell under three players with two results, showing “—” and the legend’s “too few to count”. Where I’d push: a coach who can already see on Coverage that only Devon has a throw-speed result learns nothing new from “1 higher”; the count leaks nothing the roster table does not already hold, and the family never sees this page (T8).
T 5 · formatting · the window

Since each player’s first result this season — the “This season” reading — with no Compare control in this version

Player progress offers “This season” and “Last two records”. The team row reads the season: first to latest per player, results grouped per session exactly as the chart’s points are. A “last two” team count is drawable later on the same address convention if the season reading proves too coarse; it is not drawn now because a second window doubles the sentence on every row.

What rides on it: the legend’s one sentence (“latest lower or higher than their first this season”). Where I’d push: none — this is the plain reading.
T 6 · what goes in · skills

An observed skill’s row is coverage only: “3 of 12 observed · 15 Sept” — no count per descriptor

A skill has no unit and no direction; its descriptors are the coach’s own judgment words (“Independently”, “With a reminder”, “With support”). A count of children under each word is a rating distribution without names, and the names are one click away on Coverage — the plan’s chart rules refuse a line through ordinal labels for the same reason. So the row says who has been observed and when, and stops.

Alternative: “latest descriptor: 1 Independently · 1 With a reminder · 1 With support”. Where I’d push: hold the line here; if a skill’s descriptors are ever ordered by the coach (a later definition question), this row can read the order — not before.
T 7 · what goes in · the Overview

The Overview is unchanged; its rail’s report count reads 4 by computation

Screen 6. No tile, no line, no chart. The one thing that could honestly be argued for — a Needs-attention line per metric, “8 players have one result or none on the 60-yd sprint” — is recommended against: it is one line per metric, and that list is for things a coach acts on today, which “8 have no result this season” already covers with a door.

What rides on it: D7 as ruled. Where I’d push: if the owner wants a door with the report’s name on it rather than “4 reports”, the rail row could read “Reports in Insights · Coverage · Team progress · …” — a formatting call, and it makes the row longer than its siblings.
T 8 · what goes in · who reads it, and where it never goes

Every coach with record access reads it (Insights’ own gate, as Coverage); it never reaches the handout, the family recap, or the findings strip in this version

Team progress is read with the same gate as Coverage — record access, not the Development grant — because a read must not sit behind a write grant (G1’s rule). Skills ride the notes gate as they do on Coverage: a coach without Internal notes sees no skill row. The report never feeds a family surface: the handout reads one player’s records and prints nothing about the team; the recap likewise. The Insights Dashboard’s “What stands out” strip gains no rule from it now — a team sentence there needs a threshold and a tone, and that is a findings-engine decision for after the report has earned it.

What rides on it: the boundary the PM brief promises first — nothing here ranks or compares children, and no parent can reach it. Where I’d push: none.

Product-manager brief · 2026-09-16 · proposal, nothing built

Team progress — a team-level read for Skills & Goals

The one thing to check first: nothing in this proposal ranks or compares individual players. The new report’s rows are metrics, not players; every figure is a count of how many players, never which; and no family surface — the handout, the season recap — reads it. A parent cannot reach it and a coach cannot sort it.

What changes for the coach

Insights → Development gains a fourth report beside Coverage, Player progress and Practice review: Team progress. One row per metric the team measures. Each row says how many players have a result this season, how many have two or more, and — of those — how many sit lower or higher than their first result, in the test’s own unit with the aim stated (“2 lower · 1 higher · lower is the aim”). A last column says when a result on that metric was last recorded. A metric’s row opens Coverage on that metric, where the names are, in roster order. On a phone it is one line per metric.

Skills & Goals’ Overview does not change. Its “Reports in Insights” row already counts the reports a coach can open, so it reads 4.

Why it matters

Today a coach can see who has a result (Coverage) and how one player has changed (Player progress), but not whether the thing they are coaching is moving across the group. Answering “is the sprint work landing?” means opening Player progress once per player. Development is the one Insights tab with no team-level read — Results, Attendance and Playing Time all have one. And “when did we last measure throwing?” has no answer short of reading a column per metric.

What was deliberately not proposed

  • A team average or a trend line. On the fixture’s real records the 60-yd sprint’s session average reads 8.62 → 8.42 “since May” — but May is one child and September is four different children, and one newcomer’s first result moves the figure by itself. A team average on a roster this size is a child’s number wearing a team label. Counts carry their denominator in the sentence; an average hides it.
  • Anything on the Overview. It was ruled state, not analysis, and that ruling holds on its merits: the Overview counts what a coach acts on today.
  • A per-player change column on Coverage. A column of changes beside names is a ranking by eye.
  • A count per skill descriptor. A distribution of children across a coach’s judgment words is a rating table without names.

Customer impact

Every coach with record access on a Premium Coaches Portal team — head coach and assistants alike — the same audience as Coverage. Read at a few natural moments a season: mid-season, before planning a session, at the season’s close. If we do nothing, the product stays complete and the coach keeps touring Player progress; the cost is time and a question left unanswered, not a broken flow.

Priority

Low-cost, self-contained: no migration, no new data read (the counts come from a walk the product already makes), one table on the existing list recipe, no change to any screen a family sees. Fits between larger work; it does not block anything and nothing blocks it. “Not now” is a legitimate ruling.

Success criteria

  • A coach can answer “how many of my players have moved on X, and which way” from one screen, without opening a player.
  • A coach can answer “when did we last record X” from the same screen.
  • No development surface — this one included — carries a figure beside one child that another child’s figure can be read against; the vocabulary guard stays green.
  • Team progress, Coverage, Player progress and the Overview never disagree on a count, because they read one denominator and one series.
