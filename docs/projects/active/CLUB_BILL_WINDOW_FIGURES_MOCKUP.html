A team’s bill: can the figures fit on one row?

You asked whether the bill window could be wide enough to hold all its figures on one row, and if not, whether there is a more efficient way to show them. The short answer is no: five figures don’t fit in this window at the portal’s figure size. Four do, and one of the five repeats the payment list right under it.

Ruled 2026-10-16: option A, with its words, as recommended (“I agree with your recommendations, go ahead”). Built and committed the same day (6952c544, after /review). One wording settled at the build: a payment the team sent and the club hasn’t confirmed outranks the next payment’s date. Sent and late are the two reasons a team is under “needs you”, so the line names whichever applies, late first. Version 1 of this sheet said the sent line showed only when nothing was coming due. Walk §316 passed 2026-10-16 (your word). The checklist stays below for the record.

Why the fifth figure drops to a row of its own

The money band holds at most four figures across. That is its standing recipe, three or four per band. Stage 11d added Credited as a fifth figure on a bill that has a credit. The design pass chose to keep the four built figures and add it beside them, and the band put it on a second row with the band’s own grey filling the three empty cells. That grey area is what you circled. The window is not too narrow by accident. Its 800px width is the house width for a form with a list in it.

Ask 1 · What does a team’s bill lead with?

  1. A Four money figures that add up, with the next payment’s date as a line under Outstanding.Recommended Billed, less Credited, less Collected, is Outstanding. The band becomes a sum the treasurer can check by eye: 2,700 − 432 − 0 = 2,268. The date was the only figure that wasn’t money. It also repeats the first payment row a few lines below (“Due Oct 1 · 15 days late”). Under Outstanding it keeps the bill’s verdict where the eye lands first, in red when a payment is late, the way the coach’s Money › Club band already says “⚠ 2 overdue” under its figure. A bill with no credit reads three figures (Billed · Collected · Outstanding) with the same line. The shared band doesn’t change.
  2. B Five across, in a window 96px wider (896px) for every team’s bill. This is what you asked for literally. It fits every realistic bill, but the money band would need a five-across form it has never had. That form needs its own rule for the widths between a tablet and a desk, where five don’t fit and the fifth would orphan again. The window would also grow for a list of three short rows, and the fifth figure would still be a date the list already shows.
  3. C As A, with no date line at all. This is the quietest option, and the payment list says everything. But the bill’s verdict (late or not) moves out of the band, which is the first thing read when you step through “4 of 7 needs you”.

The date line’s words under A (a /marketing words pass at the build):

  • one payment late: “Oct 1 · 15 days late”, in red with the warning mark
  • two or more late: “2 late · since Oct 1”, in red
  • nothing late, a payment the team sent and the club hasn’t confirmed: “Sent · confirm it” (two: “2 sent · confirm them”), quiet. Settled at the build: this outranks the next date, since sent is one of the two reasons a team needs you.
  • nothing late or sent, something still owed: “Next due Dec 1”, quiet
  • nothing owed: no line (Outstanding $0.00 says it)

What else this touches

  • Only the club’s bill window changes under A or C: a team’s bill opened from an allocation or from “needs you”. The shared money band keeps its recipe of three or four figures, so no other screen moves.
  • A team’s account page is unchanged. It leads with Outstanding · Next due · Paid to the team · Cash on hand, where Next due is an amount with its date, already four across.
  • The coach’s own bill room is unchanged. It reads Billed · Credited · Paid · Left · Next due, and its room is wide enough for five on one row at a computer. Its Next due is the amount the coach pays next, a figure of its own. Found on the way, not part of this ask: on a tablet (about 768 wide) that room fits four and the fifth falls to a row of its own, the same thing you circled. It is a separate fix if you want it.
  • Under B every team’s bill window grows to 896px, and the money band gains a five-across form with a rule for the widths where five don’t fit. That is a change to a shared component that twelve money summaries draw through.
  • The help article for a team’s bill gets one line about the date under Outstanding, at the build.

Design check

The /design pass ran on these drawings before they reached you. It found nothing against A. It changed six things in the drawing:

  • A state the words list missed. A bill whose remaining payments are all marked sent has money outstanding but nothing coming due. It now has its line, “Sent · confirm it”. Late beats sent, and sent beats next due (the order the build settled; see the ruling at the top).
  • The phone’s window keeps its 20px sides (it was drawn at 14px, which made each figure 11px wider than the real screen).
  • On a phone the date line may wrap. “Sep 30 · 120 days late” is 140px, and a two-across figure on a 361-wide phone has 134px. At a computer it stays on one line.
  • The computer window’s body starts 0.9rem under its head, as the built window does (it was drawn at 1.1rem). The 53px and 44px figures were measured after this.
  • Specimen 3’s state names are drawn as the sheet’s labels, so they don’t read as part of the screen.
  • Specimen 1 says that its dashed outline is yours, not the product’s.

Why A passes the house rules: colour sits on the verdict (the date line), never on the total (Outstanding stays in plain ink). The four figures are the product’s own definition, Outstanding = Billed − Credited − Collected. Each line is under six words. The band keeps its recipe of three or four, and on a phone a bill with no credit gives Outstanding and its line the full width.
