Game-Day Board Flags

Every warning on a board row — the pitching count, the sitting streak, Call-up, Out — sits on the same line as the name, so on a phone the name gets whatever room is left. Proposed: on a phone, the name keeps its own line and the flags sit on a line under it. Round 2: the word “Call-up” comes off every row; a call-up is shown by the colour of their name. Open this on your phone; the frames are drawn at true size.

What I found

It is every flag, not only the pitching one

The bench’s “2nd straight inning sitting” is wider than the pitching count, so a benched player is squeezed harder than your pitcher was. Call-up and Out add to it.

The name is being cut mid-word

“Dev / on / Test” is Devon Test broken inside the word. With about 50px left, the name wraps wherever it can, not at the space.

The same screen already solved this once

The Who’s here list on this screen gives the name its own line on a phone and puts the controls below it. The board rows never got the same rule.

  • Where the word stays: the Call-ups group in the builder, where you add a borrowed player to a game. It names the group once and its pills are already amber.
  • The printed batting-order card keeps “(Call-up)” for now, because it goes to the umpire and the other coach, who don’t know who you borrowed. The dugout sheet already prints the bare name. See question 4.
  • A screen reader still says “Logan Test, call-up”, so the cue isn’t lost for a coach who can’t see the colour.

What changes

  • On a phone, a flagged player’s name stays on one line and the flags sit under it, inside the same tap area. The row stays the same height as an unflagged row. A player with two flags at once, which is rare, takes one extra line.
  • The words do not change. “2 of 2 innings pitched” still turns red at the cap, and the screen reader still hears the flags as part of the row.
  • On a tablet or computer nothing moves. There is room for the name and the flags side by side, and the switch happens at the same width the Who’s here list already uses.
  • A call-up’s name is amber, with no word beside it, on the game-day board and both lineup builder views. Amber is the colour the builder already uses for a call-up, and it is also visibly lighter than the other names, so it still reads for a coach who doesn’t see colour well.
  • Not changing: the position button, the swap flow, bench order, or what each flag means.

Your call

1 · Flags under the name on a phone

Applies to all four flags on both the field and the bench, so the next long flag cannot cause this again.

RECOMMENDED
2 · Keep the words as they are

Shortening to “2 of 2 pitched” (as the Console re-draw drew it) only buys room for a short name, and a long name still breaks. Once the flag has its own line, the full words cost nothing. Say so if you would rather have the shorter words anyway; they combine fine with option 1.

RECOMMENDED: KEEP
3 · A call-up is an amber name, with no word, on every on-screen row

The game-day board, the per-inning list on a phone and the batting order on a computer, so a borrowed player looks the same while you build the lineup and during the game. One thing to weigh: an amber name can sit beside an amber pitching flag on the same row. The flag is in a box and the name isn’t, so they still read as two different things.

RECOMMENDED
4 · The printed batting-order card keeps “(Call-up)”

That card is read by people who don’t know your team. Your reason, that the coach already knows, doesn’t apply to them. Say so if you want it gone from the card too.

RECOMMENDED: KEEP
