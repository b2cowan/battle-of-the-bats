How a treasurer reaches a team today

There is one way in. Open the Overview, scroll past three sections to “The teams”, pick a row, and you land on the team’s account with a back arrow to the Overview. To look at the next team you go back and scroll again, ten round trips for ten teams.

The page you land on leaves out what a treasurer opens it for. 11U AA has $2,358.00 late and its page never says so. A request waiting on the treasurer is counted but can’t be opened. The head coach, the person to call, isn’t named, and a treasurer can’t open Rep Teams to find out.

Ruled Oct 16, 2026: all eight asks as recommended. Writing the plan found two corrections to these drawings, drawn in this version: the Overview card counts every year as its band does (8 teams late, not 7; screen 4), and the earlier year’s line is red when that money is late (screen 2).

Where I land: the Teams tab is right, and the dropdown belongs in it, the way the Ledger’s Book pill switches books inside the Ledger tab. The bigger fix is the team’s page itself: it should say what’s late and what’s waiting, and let you act on each one. Screens 2 and 3 draw both.

The Teams tab

Teams becomes the second tab, so every team is one click from anywhere in Accounting. The Overview’s teams table moves here whole: the same columns, the same Year pill, the same group bands and closing row. A row opens that team’s account inside this tab (screen 3).

Ask 1 Ruled as recommended

A Teams tab, second in Accounting’s row, for a club that runs rep teams (the same rule as Allocations and Payment requests). The teams table moves here from the Overview unchanged, gaining a quiet Group filter and its own Export (the table, as the board report already writes it).

Why second: it is the tab a treasurer opens most after the Overview, and the order reads from the club (Overview) to its teams to its books.

Ask 8 Ruled as recommended

Explain the jump between two Outstandings. A team still owing on a closed year gets one line under its Outstanding: + $600.00 overdue from 2025–26, red while any of it is late, quiet otherwise (correction C2). The row keeps the year’s figure, so Allocated − Collected = Outstanding still holds, and the line says why its account reads $8,280.00.

Alternative, not recommended here: one Outstanding everywhere (this list reads every year). It changes a Stage 3b figure and its board report, so it needs its own session.

Screen 5 of 6 · your question about Rep Teams · Ask 6

How Rep Teams and Accounting meet

Two pages about one team, on purpose. Rep Teams’ team page is where the club runs the team: its coaches, seasons, roster and details. Accounting’s team account is the club’s money with that team. The split exists because a treasurer can open Accounting and not Rep Teams (Stage 1), and because Accounting holds the club’s money as one set of records, which is what earns it tabs.

What’s missing is the way back. Today Rep Teams links to the money and nothing links home. The proposal links both ways for the people who can open both, and gives the treasurer the one Rep Teams fact they need: who coaches the team.

Not proposed: a Money tab on the Rep Teams team page. A treasurer can’t open Rep Teams, so it would split the club’s money across two programs to serve the person who needs it least.

Product-manager brief

Club Accounting · Teams

What changes for the treasurer

  • A Teams tab, second in Accounting’s row. Every team and its money for the year, one click from anywhere in Accounting, instead of a scroll to the Overview’s fourth section.
  • Moving between teams without going back. Opening a team keeps you in the tab; a Team dropdown at the top switches to any other team, and “All teams” returns to the list.
  • A team’s page says what to do. What’s late is in red under what the team owes. A “Needs you” list shows each late payment, each payment the team says it sent, and each request waiting, and opens it in one click to record, confirm, remind or approve.
  • Who to call. The team’s season and head coach are named at the top (or “No head coach”).
  • The Overview gets shorter. The full teams table moves to the tab; the Overview keeps a short card (how many teams are late, how many have a request waiting) with a door.
  • A team still owing on a closed year says so on its row (“+ $600.00 overdue from 2025–26”), so the list and the team’s page never seem to disagree.

For owners and admins

Everything above, plus a door from a team’s money to its Rep Teams page, the mirror of the “With the club” door that already goes the other way. A treasurer doesn’t see that door, because a treasurer can’t open Rep Teams.

Why it matters

A treasurer’s most frequent club-money job is checking a team and chasing a team. Today that costs a scroll and a round trip per team, and the team’s page hides the two facts they came for: what’s late, and who to talk to. On the dev club, 11U AA has $2,358.00 late and its own page never says so.

Customer impact

Every club that runs rep teams, weekly through the season and daily near a due date. Nothing changes for a club without rep teams (no Teams tab, as with Allocations and Payment requests). No figure changes meaning; one line explains a gap that already exists between a team’s figure for the year and its figure for every year.

Priority

Inside the Club release, which holds everything in the Club Tier plan. Small: no new money rules and no data model change; one new read (a team’s open items).

Success criteria

  • Any team’s account in two clicks from any Accounting tab.
  • Moving from one team to the next never passes through a list.
  • A team’s late money and waiting requests are on its page, each one click from being acted on.
  • The head coach is named on the team’s page for everyone who can open Accounting.

What the build changes

  1. The tab. Teams joins Accounting’s row, second, for a club that runs rep teams, and stays lit on a team’s account.
  2. Accounting › Teams. The Overview’s teams table, moved whole, read from the Overview’s own read so the two screens can never disagree; a Group filter; one Export; the earlier year’s line under Outstanding (red while late).
  3. A team’s account, inside the tab. Its own header and back arrow go. A lead Team pill (the Book pill’s control, which learns to group its choices) switches team; “All teams” returns. Then the season line, the band with its line under Outstanding, the lock line, Needs you, the statement in its own card, and “In Rep Teams” for owners and admins.
  4. Requests open over the account. Payment requests’ request window and its Approve, Decline and Reverse questions become one shared flow both pages use.
  5. The Overview. A teams card with two counts, read from the same figures as its band, and a door to the tab. The board report is unchanged.
  6. The reads. The Overview’s read gains the two counts; the teams list gains each team’s group; a team’s account gains its season and head coaches and its Needs you rows.

What doesn’t change

How any figure is worked out, the statement, the bill window, the board report, Rep Teams’ screens, who can open Accounting, and group limits (a member limited to some groups sees only their teams, in the table, the pill and Needs you).
