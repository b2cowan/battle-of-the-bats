One Word, One Line

A team's budget is four levels deep today: a category holds a word, a word holds one or more lines, and a line holds its payment dates. This proposes cutting the third level out. One budget word carries exactly one line on a plan — pick a word that is already there and you add to it, rather than starting a twin nobody downstream can tell apart.

New Restyled Unchanged Goes away Every specimen below is tagged. Warm frames are the product; the cool sheet is this document.
The question

Which level is doing work, and which one is only doing words?

Here is one tournament entry fee as the plan holds it today. Read down the indents: the third level is the only one that never reaches a total, a variance, or a match against real spending.

CategoryTournamentsgroups the plan, the grid, the report
WordEntry Feenames the row; matches real spending to the plan
LineRegional qualifier · Provincialswords only — never totalled, never matched
WhenNov 15 · Mar 1drives the months view and cash flow

Money never moves at the third level. When a coach records what they actually paid, it is matched to the word — never to the line. So two lines on one word are, to every figure this product prints, one thing wearing two labels.

And it already fails quietly. On our own test team the word "Entry Fees" printed three times in one column for weeks and nobody reported it, because the rows still added up. A level of detail that can be visibly wrong that long is not being read.

The panel appears the moment the word is picked, not at save. A surprise on the way out is the thing worth designing away.

"Open that line instead" is the escape. A coach who meant to correct the $600.00, not add to it, has one click out — which is also the undo story: today a mistaken twin is one delete, and after this it must not become manual arithmetic.

No sentence explains the pre-filled note. Under the standing rule against over-explaining, a coach who can see the note in the field does not need a caption telling them it is the word's one note.

The two schedules combine. November keeps its $600.00 and March gets its $900.00 — the months view is where this change actually pays off, so the added money must never land in "No date yet" by accident. See Q4.

Specimen 7 · the choice that sizes the prize

What happens to the plans that already have twins

Closing the door on new twins does not remove the ones already written. Three doors create budget lines — the form, "bring last season's plan", and the spreadsheet import — and every plan on both databases may already hold a few. This is the decision that determines whether anything actually gets simpler.

Option A — leave them readablesmaller change

Old plans keep their sub-lines; new saves cannot make more. Every screen, the file and the importer must still know how to nest.

Option B — one tidy, oncerecommended
▾Entry FeeNov · Mar$1,500.00✎
Nov 15$600.00
Mar 1$900.00

Twins are joined into one line: amounts summed, dates kept, notes joined — two notes "Regional qualifier; Provincials". Nesting comes out of every screen, the file and the importer.

Option A changes the form and nothing else. The nesting stays in the product forever, because some plan somewhere still needs it — so the coach learns one less concept, but no screen, file or importer gets simpler.

Option B is the version that was worth asking about, and there will never be a cheaper moment to take it: no live customers are keeping a real budget on production. It is a one-way tidy — a coach cannot un-join the pieces afterwards.

Before either is built, we count what is actually out there on both databases: how many plans hold twins, and how many of those twins carry words worth keeping. If the answer is "a handful, mostly on our own test and demo teams", Option B is close to free.

Decision sheet

Five rulings this needs before any code

Q1 · the change itself

One word, one line on a plan?

A budget word can be used once per season plan. Picking it again adds to what is there. Categories and payment dates are untouched; the plan goes from four levels to three.

Recommend Yes. Nothing below the word is ever totalled or matched to real money, and the level has been visibly broken on our own fixture without anyone noticing.
Q2 · the form

"Add to it", or "go open the line"?

When the coach picks a word already on the plan, the form can add the new amount to the existing line (Specimen 1), or refuse and send them to that line to edit it themselves.

Recommend Add to it, with "Open that line instead" as the door out. Adding is what the coach came to do; being told to go somewhere else to do it is a worse form.
Q3 · the existing plans

Leave the twins, or join them once?

Option A keeps old sub-lines readable and leaves the nesting in the product permanently. Option B joins them in a single pass — summing amounts, keeping every date, joining the notes — and lets the nesting come out of the screens, the file and the importer.

Recommend Option B, and now. It is one-way, and the window where no customer has a real budget on production is the cheapest it will ever be. First we count what is out there, and report the number before touching anything.
Q4 · schedules that do not line up

May one line's money be partly undated?

Add $900.00 for March to a $600.00 line that never got a date, and the line is now $1,500.00 with only $900.00 scheduled. Today the form insists a split adds up to the total.

Recommend Allow it — the months view already has a "No date yet" column and shows the gap honestly. The alternative is forcing a coach to invent a date for money they deliberately left undated.
Q5 · the report's panel

Does the Budget figure still open a panel?

With one line per word, the panel behind the planned figure has one record to show. It could become a straight link to the plan line instead.

Recommend Keep the panel, showing the schedule (Specimen 4). It is the only door from the report back to the plan, and "what is this figure made of" is still a real question — the answer is just the dates now.
