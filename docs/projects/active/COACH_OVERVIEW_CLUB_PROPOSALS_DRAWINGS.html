A lighter Overview on game day

You asked whether three short notices should fill the whole Overview, and then whether the game card itself needs to be so big. Neither should dominate. The club’s proposals become one row each in a small list, and the game card loses a row without losing any information. Every tag and numbered pin opens an explanation when you click it.

What changed since round 1

  • Round 3: the game card is smaller in layouts A and B (tab 2). It goes from about 185px to about 135px tall on a desktop, from about 195px to about 150px on a tablet, and drops one 44px row on a phone. After the design review, the lineup chip drops only when no lineup has been started. Today’s layout still shows the card as it ships. I also corrected the phone frames: they now show the phone’s tappable readiness rows, which round 2 left out.
  • The recommendation moved. Round 1 put the proposals in a box between the game card and the team board (now option B). The design review showed that breaks two July 30 Overview rules: the full width belongs only to the day’s card and the team board, and everything that isn’t today’s work goes in one quiet list at the bottom. A reply that can wait a day is exactly that, so A puts the proposals in that bottom list.
  • Each proposal is now two short lines (name, then what it holds), drawn from the portal’s shipped row list rather than a new one.
  • A late reply uses the ⚠ amber warning the game card already uses, not a new dot.
  • All text is on the portal’s type sizes. States that were missing are now drawn: “You asked for a change” and “all answered”.

The same facts in three rows instead of four

Games are often, so this card sits on the Overview a lot. It should stay the first thing on the page and the one lime button, because a game morning is when coaches open the portal most. But it doesn’t need four rows to say who, when, where and what’s left to do. Two changes:

  • The readiness chips move up onto the time-and-place line. On a desktop that line has room to spare, and the separate row and its divider go. On the August 12 pass that last shrank this card, the other cards already put their quick answers on this line; game day kept a fourth row only because its chips came later.
  • “Lineup not started” drops, in that one state only. When no lineup exists, the chip and the Build lineup button say the same thing and open the same page. The button stays Build lineup until a coach marks the lineup ready, so “Lineup still a draft” and “Lineup needs review” keep their chips: they say something the button doesn’t. “Lineup ready” keeps its chip too.

The cost of the second change: on the morning the lineup is the problem, the amber warning goes and only the lime button says it. The chips also stop listing every prep item. And when a draft is saved, its chip appears in the lineup’s usual place in the row, so the call-time and uniform chips shift one place to the right. That is ask A8.

What stays: the kicker, the headline size, the lime button and the green edge. The score row after the game and the arm-care warnings still appear when they have something to say. They are what a coach opens the portal for on a game morning (the August 12 ruling), so they still earn their height.

Nine calls for you

Answer them by number in the chat, for example “A1 a, A2–A9 rec”. Nothing gets built until you say go. A7 to A9 are about the game card (tab 2).

A1 · Where do the club’s proposals sit?

  1. Recommended: in the bottom list (layout A). This follows the July 30 rules as written: the day’s card and the team board stay together at the top, and a reply that can wait a day goes to the list for everything that isn’t today’s work. The cost: on a phone the proposals are one scroll down, so the bell and the email do the reminding, which they already do.
  2. Under the game card (layout B, round 1’s recommendation). Always visible, but it changes the July 30 rule that only the day’s card and the board get the full width, and it pushes the board down. Once one kind of notice holds that spot, the next one will claim it too.
  3. Let a proposal take the day’s card. The July 30 rules do have a “question” form for a decision only this coach can make, which a proposal is. But game day never gives up the card, and the existing order puts the one question card it has today below the next event. In season, a proposal would almost never get the card, so this adds a priority rule for little gain. Not drawn.

A2 · A button on each row, or the whole row?

  1. Recommended: the whole row opens the window, with a chevron at the end (pin 4). This is the portal’s list rule, and it leaves Build lineup as the only lime button on the screen.
  2. Keep a “Review it” button on each row, white rather than lime. It makes the action more obvious, but puts a button inside a list row, which the list rule rules out.

A3 · How does a late reply look?

Two written rules point different ways. One says red in this portal means something has gone wrong: money owed, overdue, a save that was refused. Another says amber means needs attention or late. A late reply is past its date, but it never blocks the club.

  1. Recommended: amber “⚠ Your reply was due Wed, Nov 4”, with late rows first (pin 3). It is the same warning the game card uses, and it keeps red for money.
  2. Red, the way Money shows something overdue.
  3. No change: keep saying “Reply by Wed, Nov 4”.

A4 · The club’s welcome card

The one-time card the club shows when it starts a new season or welcomes a coach the club invited still sits above the game card in every layout here. It has the same problem: something that isn’t today’s work sitting above the day’s card.

  1. Recommended: fix it in the same change. It moves under the same rule as the proposals (the bottom list in A, under the game card in B), as one row that goes away once read. Otherwise the headline claim, that the game card is back on top, is only true for teams without one.
  2. Leave it for later and note it in the plan.

A5 · What happens to an answered proposal?

  1. Recommended: it stays as a quiet row (“You said it looks good” or “You asked for a change”) until the club finalizes, sorted last (rows up close, 2 and 3). This keeps today’s rule that the coach can change the answer until then.
  2. It leaves the Overview once answered, and the coach changes the answer from the bell or the email. Cleaner, but it removes the only way back to the answer from the Overview.

A6 · Only if you pick A1(a): the bottom list’s look

The bottom list today is a single ruled line on a desktop (a tournament’s row with no frame). The portal’s list rule says a list keeps a white frame with hairlines between rows, and you chose the frame every time the two were compared.

  1. Recommended: the proposals sit in a framed list under their label, as drawn, and the tournament row stays as it is. They read as two separate things, which they are.
  2. One frame for the whole bottom list: the tournament row and the proposals together, with a label for each group.

A7 · Game card: chips on the time-and-place line?

  1. Recommended: yes, on a desktop and tablet (tab 2, pin 1). The card loses a row and a divider, and nothing is hidden. The phone keeps its tappable rows.
  2. Keep the chips on their own row.

A8 · Game card: drop “Lineup not started” when no lineup exists?

What it gives up: the amber warning on the morning the lineup is the problem (the lime Build lineup button says it instead), a chip row that lists every prep item, and a fixed chip order (a draft’s chip appears once one is saved).

  1. Recommended: yes, in that one state only (pin 2). On a phone it removes a whole 44px row on the morning a coach is most likely to open the portal. Draft, needs review and ready keep their chips.
  2. Keep it. Choose this if you want the amber warning beside the action; A7 alone still saves the desktop row.

A9 · Game card: keep the headline and button their size?

The other way to make the card dominate less is to shrink the type: a 16px headline like every other card, and a smaller button.

  1. Recommended: keep them. With A7 and A8 the card is about 30% shorter but still reads as the page’s one headline. Shrunk type would make game day look like any other notice, and a game morning is when coaches open the portal most.
  2. Shrink the headline to 16px as well. This saves about 6px more and makes the card noticeably quieter.

What doesn’t change

  • The review window itself: Your time, Your bill, Ask for a change, Looks good.
  • Only the team’s head coach sees the proposals, as with today’s cards.
  • The bell and the email still bring the coach to this Overview.
  • A proposal goes away once the club finalizes it.
  • The game card’s information: every chip, the score row and the arm-care warnings. Only its layout changes (A7–A9).
  • The season setup chip and the team board.

For the build

  • Rows are the shipped CoachRowList / CoachRow, with each row as a button that opens a dialog: title = schedule name, caption = slots and bill, trail = reply-by or the answer, chevron door. Write no new row class.
  • The list is labelled by its eyebrow. Late text uses the warning token with the ⚠ glyph; the caption and the trail are at the support size.
  • Wording comes from the existing coach-side schedule words file. It never says “From the club”.
  • Game card (A7): the chips move into the One Thing card’s meta-row answers slot, which is empty on every game branch, as their own group (chip gap, wraps to a new line left-aligned). Above 640 only; the phone keeps its door rows. The chips now come before the score row, and the first-screen guard test that checks the chip row is hidden on a phone needs updating with the move.
  • Game card (A8): key the drop on the lineup badge being not started, not on the primary being Build lineup, because the primary is Build lineup for every not-ready state.
