Your four questions

The drawings are on Drawn proposal. The evidence is on Walk.

1 · Are the portals following the same rules?

On type and size, yes. On how the menus are put together, no.

The three sidebars in your screenshots use exactly the same type: 14px rows, 10px capital headings, 14px icons, and one olive highlight on the page you're on. The admin sidebar was built as a copy of the coach sidebar's settings. I sampled the text in your three captures, and it's the same colour in all of them. On a phone, all four bottom bars share one stylesheet.

What differs is how each menu is put together:

  1. Headings. The club's are fixed labels; the tournament's and the coach's fold. That's fine on its own (next card), but the two kinds are spaced differently in the same sidebar.
  2. Chevrons. On the club sidebar a › means "this program has pages". Public site carries one for a single page, and that page is the row itself.
  3. The foot. The admin sidebar ends with five links, Sign out in red; the coach's ends with two. Send feedback and Sign out are also in the admin's account menu. The coach sidebar dropped its copies on 09-01; the admin never did. The club sidebar also shows "Public site" twice, with two icons and two destinations.
  4. Icons. The trophy means Tournaments, Results and Season's End. The clipboard means Schedules and Tryouts. Public site has three different icons.
  5. More sheets. A club admin on a phone has no Help. A paid coach on a phone has no Account, Home or Send feedback. The tournament's rows have no icons. "Back to club" breaks the 07-31 rule that navigation is named by where it goes.
  6. The free coach sidebar's rows have a look of their own: 12px bold monospace, bigger icons, rounded pills, a bar on the page you're on. The 10-16 decision says the tiers differ in season tools, "and now not in the chrome either". On 10-15 you ruled to leave this menu's width and team header, so I only ask about the rows.
2 · Are there good reasons for the differences?

For six of them, yes. The rest is drift.

KEEP These are deliberate and right. They go into the standard as written exceptions.

  • The club sidebar groups programs; the coach sidebar groups pages. A club runs six programs and a coach runs one team. A program opens because you're inside it. Folding "Programs" would add a click for nothing.
  • Tournaments replaces the sidebar instead of opening under its row. An event has about 17 pages in three groups; nested under one row, they'd run off any laptop screen.
  • The tournament phone bar changes with the phase (ruled 10-16).
  • Phone rows are bigger than sidebar rows (16px against 14px). They're for thumbs.
  • The coach's More sheet never folds. You open it to find something.
  • Platform admin keeps its own look (a ruled exception).

DRIFT Everything listed under question 1. I found no record that any of it was chosen. Each screen was built carefully on its own, and the differences only show when you put them side by side, or when one person wears two hats.

3 · Should the coach's tiles come to the other More sheets?

Yes for the club and tournament admin, in one form everywhere.

  • Tournament admin, on game day. On 10-16 you moved Check-in into More on game day, and Teams left the bar for Results. Tiles for Check-in, Staff kit and Teams put them back one tap from anywhere. Before game day I wouldn't show tiles: the pages the bar has no room for are set-up pages you visit once, mostly from a computer, and a tile is for a page you reach for again and again.
  • The club admin already does it, in another shape. Its sheet opens with two big rows, Calendar and Schedules, above Notifications. Turn them into tiles (plus Families) and every sheet starts the same way: Notifications, then the tiles.
  • The free portal stays as it is. Its tiles mean "your team's tools", every tool the coach has turned on. That's a different job.

One thing worth knowing: the tiles are the biggest targets on the sheet, but their words are the smallest (12px, against 16px for the rows). That size was measured so "Practice plans" fits on one line at 360px, and it limits what can be a tile. "Communications" is one word too long for a tile at 360px, which is why it isn't one.

4 · What else would I improve?

Write it down as one nav standard, and fill four gaps.

  • A written standard. Nine rules (The nav standard), so the next screen can't drift. The Club Tier plan already holds a place for this: its Stage 8 asks to "recommend one model, the portal's as the benchmark". This is that recommendation.
  • Doors missing on a phone. Help for admins. Account, Home and Send feedback for paid coaches. The Organization page. Tournament Accounting.
  • Name the admin portal in its top strip ("Admin Area", its name in the Workspaces list), the way the coach strip says "Coaches Portal".
  • Widths, which I'd leave. Admin 240px, coach 220px, free 248px. One width sounds tidy, but the coaches portal's layouts are tuned to its 220px sidebar, you ruled the free width on 10-15, and 20px buys almost nothing. It goes in the standard as a written exception.
  • Small fixes found on the way, no decision needed: tournament groups show closed for a moment on first load, two tournament rows never show as the page you're on, and the paid coach's phone tabs don't tell a screen reader which page is current.

The problem

Our menus were built one portal at a time. The type already matches everywhere, but how each menu is put together doesn't. Someone who runs a club and also coaches a team flips between the admin and the coaches portal and finds:

  • Sign out in a different place.
  • Help missing from the admin's phone menu.
  • No way to their account from the coach's phone menu.
  • One icon meaning three different things.
  • The phone menu in a different order in every portal.

None of it is broken on its own. Together it makes the product feel like several products.

What changes for people

  • Every sidebar ends the same way: Help, the other portal, Collapse sidebar. Signing out and sending feedback happen from the account menu at the top right, in every portal.
  • Every phone More menu has the same order: notifications first, then shortcuts, then the same groups as the sidebar, then your account, then Help, feedback, the app download and Sign out. Admins get Help on a phone. Coaches get their account, Home and feedback on a phone. The club's Organization page and a tournament's Accounting get phone doors.
  • One club phone bar. Overview, Calendar, Rep teams, Accounting and More, on every club.
  • Shortcut tiles come to the admin. A club admin's menu opens with Schedules, House league and Families. On game day, a tournament director's menu opens with Check-in, Staff kit and Teams.
  • One icon per idea. The trophy means Tournaments, and only Tournaments. Results gets a medal and the club's scheduler a timeline; twelve icons change in all.
  • Small words: "Rep teams" in sentence case like every other menu label, "Club overview" instead of "Back to club", and "Admin Area" in the admin's top bar, the way the coach's says "Coaches Portal".
  • The free coach's sidebar rows look like the paid ones (its width and team header stay as you ruled on 10-15).

Nothing is removed; every page stays where it is in the sidebar. No data changes.

Who sees what

Club owners and admins, tournament directors, Premium coaches and free coaches all see the changes in their own portal. Volunteers' game-day screens, the public site and platform admin are unchanged.

Why it matters

The Club release sells one platform to clubs whose people wear several hats. A consistent menu is the cheapest proof that it is one product, and a written standard stops the next screen from drifting.

How it's built

Four small stages, each its own commit and walk: A, the same words, icons and headings; B, one sidebar foot and one More order; C, the admin tiles; D, the free coach's rows.

Success looks like

  • In any portal, on any device, Help, your account and Sign out are where you expect them.
  • No icon means two things in any menu.
  • A new screen's menu entry is checked against the written standard.
  • The QA walks pass.
