9c drawn: teams, ratings, coaches, and telling families

The third drawing round. It turns Teams & draft into the place where a division's children become teams: rate them, run a balanced draft, adjust it, then publish, which tells every family and gives each coach their team. It also draws the coach's own page and the family status page with the team on it. Four push-backs come first, because two of them change what you are being asked.

Four push-backs, before the drawings

1 · A "light" coach isn't light if it's the Coaches Portal. Give them a team page of their own.

  • What a house-league coach would land in today. Nothing links a house-league team to a person: the coach is a typed name . If a club added that volunteer as a "coach" member, their sign-in card opens the Coaches Portal, which says "No team assignments yet" and nothing else, forever. At a club without an active subscription, there is no card at all.
  • The portal is the heavy product. A head coach there has about 16 menu entries: schedule, practice plans, lineups, tournaments, skills, insights, money, chat, email, roster, tryouts, staff, documents, settings and Season's End. It also has rules a house-league team doesn't fit, like the one-door closed-season gate.
  • A · A team page of its own (recommended).
    • One screen, built for a phone: the roster with each family's contacts, then the schedule (9d), then scores (9e).
    • The coach opens it from the invite email, or from their sign-in card. The card opens the page directly, the way a scorekeeper's card opens straight into the scorekeeper today.
    • It uses the same account as the rest of FieldLogicHQ. A parent who also coaches a rep team sees two cards.
    • What it costs. The coach has nothing to learn. The convenor types an email address on the team. We build one new screen and one column of the gate, and the portal is untouched.
  • B · The portal, with a house-league workspace. The coach gets the portal's frame with most of it hidden. Every portal screen and rule would have to learn "this is a house-league team" and hide itself: the season gate, tryouts, money, the closed-season page and the billing card. Each one missed is a door showing a write button the server refuses, which the portal's own one-door rule warns against.
    • It does make later features cheap: practice plans, and chat when it returns.
    • If house-league coaches ever need those, that's a decision to make then. Q7's list doesn't include them.

Ask 1. Drawn as A on the "Coaches & who sees what" tab.

2 · A rating is data about a child. "Convenor-only" is ruled; three things around it are not.

  • Does the registrar see ratings? Q17 says the registrar "reads the rest", and Q8 says ratings are convenor-only. They collide here, and the narrower ruling should win: a registrar sees the teams but never a rating. Ratings are absent from every screen they open, including the export. Ask 2a.
  • How long is a rating kept? A rating has done its job once the teams are published.
    • I recommend it belongs to this season's registration and is erased when the season finishes.
    • Carrying it into next year's draft is a returning-player question, and that belongs to Stage 14 (one player across seasons), not here.
    • Keeping ratings forever "in case" is the option that ages worst. Ask 2b.
  • Can a guardian ask to see it? Canadian privacy law generally lets a parent ask a club what it holds about their child. So a rating should be a number from 1 to 5 with no comment box: nothing written about a child that the club couldn't show the parent.
    • No family screen shows it.
    • If a parent asks, the club reads it from the child's record and answers them. Ask 2c.

3 · The balanced draft is honest about balance. It ignores the first thing every convenor fixes.

  • What it does well. It deals children strongest first and reverses the order each round (1-2-3-4, then 4-3-2-1), so no team gets all the top players. Each team's average rating is shown, so you can see the balance rather than trust it. Today's draft does neither: it repeats the same order every round and reads no skill at all .
  • What it doesn't know. In a house league, the coach is usually a parent who volunteered because their child plays. Siblings, and "put her with her friend", come next.
    • None of these is ruled, and none is drawn.
    • Without any of them, the convenor's first move after every draft is to put each coach's child on that coach's team by hand. Each such move undoes some of the balance, and "Draft again" would throw it away.
  • The smallest fix, as an ask: let the convenor lock a child to a team before the draft runs, and have the draft deal everyone else around them. That covers the coach's own child. Siblings and friends are a bigger feature, because both children float; they're noted, not asked. Ask 3.

4 · Placement notices don't need to wait for 9d. The batched sender already exists.

  • What the code has today. The club's batched sender, built for Families › Email families in Stage 7c, already:
    • sends one email per household;
    • works through a large list in batches of 100;
    • adds the family unsubscribe footer and honours the club's suppression list.
    9d's job is to move the season's typed email onto it, which is a different thing.
  • So in 9c, Publish teams sends through it.
    • A family with three children in the division gets one email naming all three teams.
    • A season of 400 families is 4 batches of 100, sent within a minute or so.
    • Q12c (ruled) says an automatic notice never counts toward a cap. A placement notice is written by the product, not typed, so Publish teams never uses up the season's 3 sends a day.
  • What not to copy. Today's other automatic house-league emails (approval, waitlist, decline) go one at a time through a separate path, with no batching . Publish teams shouldn't use that path.
  • One thing to check at build. A family that unsubscribed from club news must still be told their child's team, because it's a service message. The batched sender's suppression list must not drop them. If it would, the build splits "unsubscribed" from "bounced".

Ask 4. The alternatives (wait for 9d, or publish with no email) are on the asks tab.

(d) The manual path

Keep dragging; retire today's "Draft mode" overlay

  • Today there are three ways to fill teams: drag, Randomize, and a full-screen Draft mode. In Draft mode, someone clicks "Pick" for each team in turn, the same order every round, and can't finish until every child is picked .
  • After: the balanced draft replaces both Randomize and Draft mode. Dragging stays: it's how the convenor adjusts a draft, and how a convenor who wants no draft at all builds teams by hand. The board works without the draft ever running, and Publish works the same either way.
  • What retiring Draft mode loses: a "draft night" where coaches sit in a room and pick in turn. Some house leagues do run one. Q8 ruled a balanced draft from ratings, which is a different thing. If a club wants a draft night, it's a request to weigh then, not a mode to keep alive now. Ask 5.

An odd number

5 children in 2 teams make 3 and 2. The extra child goes where the order lands; the balance line shows sizes and averages, so the convenor can see it and move a child if they want. No rule tries to "fix" an odd number.

An unpaid accepted child

Drafted like everyone else (Q3). The convenor sees "owes $165" in small type on the board. The coach's page and the family's team row never mention money. Publish isn't blocked by anything owed.

A family asks to withdraw (9b-2)

Asking changes nothing until the club ends the registration, so the child stays in the draft. The board shows a quiet "Asked to withdraw" under their name, so the convenor isn't surprised. Once ended, see below.

A withdrawn child

Before publishing: they leave the board and their rating goes with the registration.
After publishing: they leave their team with one quiet line on the team card; the coach's page drops them; no team email goes (their withdrawal email covers it). Today an ended child keeps their team .

Two divisions for one age group

U10 Division A and U10 Division B are two tabs and two separate drafts. Children aren't balanced across them: the family chose the division by its note (9b·13, for example "Tuesday nights"), so moving them would undo their choice. A child moves between divisions on Registrations, not here.

  • Q7 light coach accounts: invited by email, their own team only, roster contacts, schedule and scores. Drawn as the coach's team page. Scores (9e) and the schedule (9d) are dashed slots that say plainly they aren't there yet.
  • Q8 a 1–5 rating feeding a balanced snake draft, convenor-only: the rating window and the draft.
  • Q9 volunteers are their own project: not drawn.
  • Q17 one gate, a coach as its column: the table on the Coaches tab.
  • Q19 age groups as divisions: a draft per division, one tab each.
