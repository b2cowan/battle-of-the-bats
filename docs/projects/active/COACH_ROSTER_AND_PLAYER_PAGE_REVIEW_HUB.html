Product-manager brief

Roster + player page review

The August rework got the shape right. This pass closes the seams it left — no new data, no migration, two screens.

What changes for the coach

On the roster. The list keeps its five columns and its toolbar. A player's name is underlined quietly so it reads as the door it already is. Jersey numbers take the same data face the rest of the portal uses for numbers. A pitcher's row says Ace or P2 in the Positions column instead of "Add a position". The two "Add a…" prompts lose their dashed underline, so a brand-new roster reads as a list of names with quiet gaps rather than a grid of dashes — and tapping one lands on the right tab of the player's page, scrolled to the right field. On a phone the whole card opens the player, and a card whose family has a phone number on file carries a thumb-sized Call button in its corner.

On a player's page. The header gains previous / next arrows, in the coach's own roster order, that keep whatever tab is open — so logging a note for twelve players is twelve taps of the arrow, never a trip back to the roster. The four glance tiles become doors: Attendance jumps to attendance, Dues to dues, Awards to awards, Family to the guardian contact. The red "Medical notes" chip jumps to Safety. Five tabs replace three (round 2, owner): Details lands first, then This season (the record: attendance, playing time, awards, dues), then Skills & Goals (the judgement, named for the sidebar item it belongs to, with the room its growing content needs), then Notes, then Family & paperwork. Everything inside every tab is flat — the drawers are gone, each section is a card with a heading, and the tab a coach opened shows what they opened it for. The family season recap moves from a tinted box in the middle of the season to one quiet line at the foot of the record tab. A family that has over-paid reads "credit" in both places the balance appears, instead of "Paid" above and "$-945.15" below. An assistant coach who cannot edit sees a clean read-only record instead of editable boxes that refuse to save.

Notes. A coach can already write about a player in four places — a line from the bench on game day, a skill observation, a goal review, and an undated private box on Details — and nowhere to read them together. The Notes tab is that place: one timeline, newest first, grouped by month, every entry marked with where it came from (Game · vs Milton, Skill · Sets feet before throwing, Goal · First-step quickness, Note) and who wrote it, linking back to its source. Nothing is written twice. Two things move to make room: the private box becomes the pinned "About" note at the top of Notes, and "Moments you logged" stops being its own section. One general "Add a note" door covers what fits nothing else, gated exactly like goals; families never see the tab and the season recap never reads from it.

Why it matters

The roster is the page everything else reads from, and the player page is where a coach answers "how is this kid doing" and "who do I call" — often standing on a field with a phone. Today both pages hide their answers one step further away than they need to: a name that doesn't look like a door, a contact that isn't on the card, an attendance figure shown three times with no way to reach it, a form folded inside a tab that holds nothing else. None of it is broken; all of it costs a tap or a moment of doubt, dozens of times a week.

Customer impact

Every coach with portal access, on every team, every time they open the roster or a player. Assistants without contact access stop seeing blank editable boxes. Families are not affected — the recap they receive is unchanged; only the coach's preview of it moves.

What is deliberately not changing

  • No dues or attendance on the roster list. The reason from August still holds: a coach opens this list beside a parent. Both re-examined on merit, both left off — recorded as open questions rather than rulings.
  • No filters, counts or sort. Twelve rows on one screen.
  • The three tabs, the glance card, the named removal at the foot of Details, the depth chart, the off-roster shelf — untouched.
  • Reordering stays desktop-only.

Trade-offs

  • Flattening the tabs makes "This season" longer for a player with many measurables. The tiles are the index, and the whole tab fully open measures about two desktop screens today.
  • The player stepper is the portal's first record-to-record navigation. It is drawn so it can be judged before it becomes a pattern; if it earns its place here it should be offered to the tryout board and the staff sheet later, not copied silently.
  • Five tabs is more rail than three. It is within what the shared tab bar already handles (the Money hub has seven) and each tab now answers one question: who is this · what happened · how are they developing · what have we noticed · who do I call.
  • The Notes timeline shows observations and reviews that also appear under their goal on Skills & Goals. That is a read in two places, never a write in two places. The trims (Q7–Q10) exist to keep it that way.

Priority

After the depth-chart and development work in flight. It touches no data model, so it can slot in without a migration window. The two type-and-tab fixes (F02, F13) are the ones the layout gate and the recipe guard already want.

Success criteria

  • A coach can call a parent from the phone roster in one tap, and open the player in one tap.
  • A coach can log a measurable for every player without returning to the roster.
  • "Add a contact" on the roster lands on Guardian contact; reloading a player page keeps the tab.
  • No cell on the roster is off the type ladder; no accordion remains inside a player-page tab.
  • One balance, one reading: no page shows "Paid" and a negative dollar figure for the same player.
Full assessment

Roster + player page — evaluation and plan

How this was evaluated. Three read-only code maps (the roster page, the player page, and every list-to-detail table in the app) plus a browser pass against the running dev server at desktop and phone width, signed in as the UAT coach: full-page screenshots of the roster and every player-page tab, the measured type, ink and padding of every table cell, and the open/closed state of every fold. Findings cite what the code does, not what a plan says it does. The predecessor plan's reasons were re-tested on merit (§2); nothing is kept "because it was ruled".

1. The verdict

The August rework got the shape right — glance card, three tabs, removal named and moved, status column gone — and the instinct behind it ("less on this page") still holds. What it left behind is a set of seams. On the roster: two right-hand columns that are dashed prompts on every row of a new team; three cells that mint their own type size; a phone card that lost the contact line the plan promised; a pitcher who reads "Add a position" forever. On the player page: three tabs that are right, with everything inside them folded a second time; a Details tab that is one accordion with nothing to fold against; four glance tiles that name Attendance, Dues, Awards and Family and open nothing; a family recap filed among the records in a different visual language; tabs with no address, so the roster's prompts land on the wrong tab; and no way to step from one player to the next. None of this needs new data. Every fix reads fields the pages already fetch.

3. Roster list — findings

F01 — The name is the door, and nothing says so

Today: the name is bold body text with no underline and no colour shift; the row has no hover and an arrow cursor. The table standard is explicit — a row that navigates has the name as the link, never a row-level click — so the roster is compliant, and the row-tappable rows elsewhere in the portal (Player Dues, Payables, Fundraisers) are rows that expand or open a room in place, a different affordance by the 2026-08-15 table. But compliant is not legible: this is the one list where every row is a door.

Proposal: keep name-as-link and an inert row (the Family cell holds tap-to-email and tap-to-call links a row click would swallow). The name takes the portal's quiet-link treatment at rest — a 1px underline in the line-strong token, solid ink on hover. No chevron column. The standard's K-08 ("every list table's last column is one chevron") has no register row excusing the roster, and the register's preamble says a difference is registered or it is a bug — so this pass writes the register row: Roster — no action column; the name is the door; the trailing cell is Family, whose contact links a chevron would compete with. Same shape as club-admin Families. (Q1.)

F02 — Three cells mint their own type size and ink

Today: the #, Positions and Family cells carry an inline 13.6px size, and # an inline ink outside the three named tiers. The ladder has no 13.6. This is the defect the table standard retired in September, in an inline style the CSS guard cannot see.

Proposal: cells on the body step; the jersey number in the data face, tabular, secondary ink — the treatment the off-roster shelf and the depth chart already use. The three inline declarations go on the recipe guard's known-debt list until removed.

F03 — On a new team, half the table is dashed prompts

Today: Positions and Family both fall back to a dashed-underlined "Add a position" / "Add a contact"; on a fresh twelve-player roster that is twenty-four dashed underlines across two thirds of the table. The reason for in-row prompts holds; the dashed treatment is what turns them into texture.

Proposal: tertiary-ink text with a leading "+" and no underline at rest (underline on hover and focus, like every other quiet door). Same words, same place, same link.

F04 — A pitcher reads "Add a position" forever

Today: the Positions cell shows the top-two field Best positions. The mound is deliberately not in the position picker, so a player whose job is pitching has no field positions and the cell prompts "Add a position" — on the team's ace.

Proposal: the Positions cell leads with the pitching chip when the player pitches — "Ace" for rank 1, "P2"…"P5" otherwise, the editor's own wording — then the field positions; the prompt only when there is neither.

F05 — A bare "P", and a pitcher marker in the wrong column

Today: the name cell carries ★, the pitcher chip and ✚. On a pitcher whose rank is unset the chip renders a bare "P" and its accessible name reads "Pitcher, rank"; the glance card has the same hole ("Pitcher · rank "). The depth chart already defends the same field with a fallback; the roster and the card do not.

Proposal: a rank fallback everywhere the profile is read; the pitcher marker moves to the Positions cell (F04); ★ and ✚ stay beside the name because they are about the player, not a position. The card chip reads "Ace · no cap" or "P2 · 4 IP cap" — the whole stored profile, in the form's words.

F06 — The phone card has no way to reach a parent

Today: at phone width the card is one line — name, flags, number — and the Family cell is hidden outright. The August plan says "guardian keeps its own line"; the stylesheet hides it. A coach at the field who needs a parent's number opens the player, switches tabs, finds the Call link: three taps on the one device where a phone number is most useful. The register already names half of this: row F-23 says the card's 19–22px name link is below the tap floor and offers two exits — raise the link, or make the card the door.

Proposal: (a) the whole card is the link, the way practice-plan and scouting rows already are; (b) a second card line — positions chip on the left, and a corner-pinned 44px Call button on the right when a phone is on file (the standard's "icon-only action is corner-pinned" rule; gated exactly like the desktop cell). No email on the card. Rows with neither stay one line.

F07 — "Add a contact" and "Add a position" open the wrong tab

Today: both prompts link to the bare player URL, which always opens This season. The contact lives on Family & paperwork; positions on Details. Cause: the tabs have no address (F13). The help article's deep links and the Family tile's own prose ("Add one on Family & paperwork") have the same gap.

Proposal: once tabs are addressable, the prompts link to the tab and section they name.

F08 — Small type-and-touch debt

  • The drag grip is ~23px with no floor and still renders in the 641–768 touch band (register F-21). Give it the floor.
  • The heading rule is the plain line token; the standard says line-strong under a heading row.
  • The help article "How to build your roster" still says use the pencil to edit and the trash icon to remove — true of the free-tier editor, false here. A docs pass.
  • Three code comments assert "Add Player keeps its word on a phone"; the CSS renders a bare "+", which is what the house rule (words → symbols on a phone) wants. Fix the comments, not the button.

F21 — The view switch sits at the wrong end of its row

Today: List / Depth chart is right-aligned beside Export, with the left of the toolbar empty since the player count was removed in August. Every other view switch in the portal (Schedule, Money, Insights) sits at the left of its row, and the table standard puts the arrangement control left with only the export pinned right.

Proposal (owner, round 2): the toggle moves to the left end of the toolbar at every width. Export stays pinned right. On a phone the row already reads toggle-left, export-right.

4. Player page — findings

F09 — Folds inside tabs: the thing the tab was opened for is behind a second click

Today: every tab is a stack of drawers. This season: Development open, then the recap panel, then Attendance / Awards / Dues closed. Family & paperwork: Guardian contact open, Safety / Documents closed. Details: a single drawer, "Player", with nothing to fold against. A coach who taps Family & paperwork to check an allergy has picked the tab and must still open Safety. The measured page does not need this: the whole This season tab, every fold open, is about two desktop screens.

Proposal: no folds inside tabs. Each tab is a flat page of sections, each its own card with the heading the fold used, minus the chevron. Development's internal structure is unchanged. The glance card is the page's index (F10). The section deep-link's scroll-and-flash behaviour is kept — it was the only good thing the folds did.

F10 — The four glance tiles open nothing

Today: Dues, Attendance, Family and Awards are plain boxes. The Family tile's foot literally says "Add one on Family & paperwork". Attendance appears three times on This season (tile, Development's Context line, the Attendance drawer) and none is a door.

Proposal: every tile is a door — Dues, Attendance and Awards to their section; Family to the Family & paperwork tab at Guardian contact. Same mechanism the Overview's tiles use.

F11 — The family season recap is a preview of an output, filed among the records

Today: between "Moments you logged" and Attendance sits a tinted olive panel with a blue border and a small-caps title — a different visual language from every card around it — holding one sentence and a Preview button. It is the coach's preview of what the family will read at season's end: not a record, not something read weekly.

Proposal: one quiet row at the foot of This season — an eye glyph and "Preview what Maya's family will read at season's end" — opening the same preview in place. Same fetch-on-open, same component, same gate. The tinted box goes.

F12 — One figure, two readings: "Paid" above, "$-945.15" below

Today: the Dues tile reads "Paid" for any balance at or below zero; the drawer header prints the raw signed balance through a formatter with no negative branch, so an over-credited family reads "$-945.15", and inside the drawer the same figure is tinted good. "Manage dues →" also renders when no schedule exists.

Proposal: one treatment for the page — a negative balance is a credit: tile "Paid" with foot "$945.15 credit held"; section meta "$945.15 credit"; positive balances unchanged. The Manage dues door renders only with a schedule. The house currency helper gains the negative branch so no page writes this again.

F13 — Tabs have no address

Today: tab state is local. Reload returns to This season; Back does not undo a tab switch; nothing can link to a tab; a section deep-link to a non-default tab lands on the wrong tab silently because its target is not in the DOM. The Money hub, Insights and Skills & Goals all use the shared tab bar, whose tabs are links with real addresses, for exactly this reason.

Proposal: the player page uses the shared tab bar with ?tab=season|details|family (default season); ?section= keeps scrolling within the tab. One tab component in the portal; the underline turns olive like its siblings.

F14 — A coach opening a player one at a time has no next

Today: the header has the back arrow and nothing else; there is no record-to-record stepping anywhere in the coach portal. The workflow that wants it is weekly: log a measurable for every player after practice; check every family has a contact before the season email; read twelve development records before an evaluation.

Proposal (revised round 2, R2-5): a Switch player dropdown in the header's trailing slot, listing the roster in the coach's own order with the current player marked; picking one opens that player on the same tab (the tab rides in the URL). Active players only. The after-practice pass is open-then-pick per player. Phone: a 44px icon-only button opening the same list. It reads the roster the depth chart already fetches; no new endpoint. Off-roster players stay reachable from the shelf only. A combined arrows-plus-dropdown control was drawn first and withdrawn.

F15 — Safety is a fold, and the red chip that points at it is not a door

Today: the one warning chip on the glance card ("⚠ Medical notes") is inert, and the notes it warns about are behind the closed Safety drawer two clicks away.

Proposal: the chip opens Family & paperwork at Safety; Safety is flat.

F16 — The Details form is editable for coaches who cannot save

Today: every field on Details and Family & paperwork is a live input for every coach with record access, and only the head coach can save. An assistant types, gets a save bar, and the server refuses. Contact fields redacted for that assistant render as blank editable inputs, which reads as "nobody filled this in".

Proposal: a coach without roster-write sees read-only label / value rows, "—" for empty, and one line naming the fields kept to the head coach; no save bar. The head coach keeps inline editing exactly as today. (Q6.)

F17 — Awards can only count

Today: the page receives awards as a total and a per-type count, so the Awards section can say "Player of the game ×2" and nothing else, though every award has a date and a game.

Proposal (revised round 2, owner): flat, in the same shape as the other summaries — each award and its count, the data the page already receives — plus one door, "When each was given →", to the Insights awards log opened at this player. No dates carried here; no payload change.

  • "Moments you logged" shows the last few with no way to see all; add "All n moments →" when truncated.

4b. Round 2 — five tabs, and Notes (owner rulings 2026-09-13)

The owner ruled three things on reading round 1: Development becomes its own tab (it is where metrics and coach notes go and will be data-intensive); Details is the first tab; and a per-player Notes section exists, as its own tab, with a general "Add a note" allowed. The resulting rail is Details · This season · Skills & Goals · Notes · Family & paperwork. Each tab answers one question: who is this · what happened · how are they developing · what have we noticed · who do I call.

F19 — Development outgrows This season

Today: the Development section is 698px tall on a light fixture and sits as one of five things on the This season tab. Phase 2 of the development lifecycle (in build this week) adds attempts per test, observed skills, goal reviews and a four-way view switch inside that same section, planned as a control nested in a card on a summary tab.

Proposal: a tab of its own, named Skills & Goals because that is the sidebar item it belongs to. The view switch gets the tab's full width. The "Context" block leaves: playing time becomes its own section on This season (Q9), and attendance already has one. Everything else inside Development — continuity rows, the carry-forward offer, the tryout snapshot, focus areas, measurables, the previous-seasons scrapbook, print — moves with it unchanged. This season becomes the record tab: attendance · playing time · awards · dues, with the recap row at its foot.

F20 — Four places to write about a player, none to read them

Today: a coach writes about a player in four places on three tabs — a one-line game moment from the bench (read on This season), a skill observation and a goal review (inside Development, new this week), and an undated "Notes (private)" box on Details. Nothing shows them together, and the undated box is the one most likely to hold what belongs in the others.

Proposal — the Notes tab, and how the sources are organised:

  • One timeline, newest first, grouped by month. Every dated entry about this player, whatever wrote it. Each entry carries a source chip that names its origin and opens it — Game · vs Milton (the schedule), Skill · Sets feet before throwing (Skills & Goals), Goal · First-step quickness (the goal), Note — and the staff member who wrote it.
  • Written once, at its source. Observations and reviews are recorded on Skills & Goals; moments at the bench; the general note here. The timeline is a read. Nothing is duplicated in storage and nothing is written twice.
  • The pinned About note. The "Notes (private)" box moves from Details to the top of Notes, unchanged in field, limit and gate, as the one undated standing note per player (Q8).
  • One general door. "Add a note": a date (today), the words, an optional link to a goal or a game. Gated exactly like goals — writes need the Development grant plus notes access, reads ride notes access. Families never see the tab; the recap never reads from it. The tab says so in one line.

The trims, to lower the complexity (each an open question):

  • Q7 — no separate Observations view inside the player's Skills & Goals. Phase 2 plans Goals · Results · Observations · Previous seasons; with a Notes tab the Observations view is a third listing. Observations read under the goal or skill they belong to, and in the timeline. This needs the Phase 2 session's agreement and is drawn, not built.
  • Q8 — the Details box leaves Details. One fewer place to write.
  • Q9 — playing time is a season fact and moves to This season.
  • Q10 — no filter or search on Notes in the first build. A season is a few dozen entries; the source chip does the sorting a reader's eye needs.
  • "Moments you logged" stops being a section on This season; the moments themselves are captured and stored exactly as today.

Privacy posture, stated once. The product's standing position is that development content about a minor stays skill-and-goal oriented and never becomes behavioural profiling. A dated free-text log is the surface most likely to drift there. The mitigations are the ones goals already have (the notes grant, no family visibility, no recap read) plus a one-line statement on the tab and a help article that says what the log is for.
