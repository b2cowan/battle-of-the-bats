# Milton Bats demo world — PM brief

**What it is.** A pretend Milton Bats Softball Association on our staging system, so the club's president sees his own club, in its
purple, running in our product for the whole hour, with made-up families and teams.

**Why it matters.** The president decides whether Milton becomes our first club. Thirty-one minutes of the meeting are five live demos.
Before Phase B, no single club could carry them: the dome schedule lived in a test club, and the coach demos in the read-only sandbox.

**What he sees (Phase A, house league).** A public sign-up page in Milton purple. Five divisions, each at a different moment of the
season (opening, ready to draft, mid-season with scores to check, full with a waitlist, just published). Eight diamonds across three parks.

**What he sees (Phase B, the rep side and the five demos).**
1. **Winter dome.** Seventeen rep teams with coaches, a dome with a winter permit (cost and field prices). The first eight Sundays are
   already sent to coaches and billed. In the room, the owner builds the rest of the winter from the same permit, live.
2. **A coach's books.** Milton Bats U15 A: the dome time on its schedule and the club's dome bill in its Money, a budget, player dues at
   every stage, a bottle drive, a sponsor, bills, Budget vs. Actual, and a live balance check and payment.
3. **Practice plans.** The coach's drill library, two templates, past practices with plans, a station circuit, an assistant to send to,
   and the club's standard drill set.
4. **Skills and insights.** Four tests over two testing days (trends show), observed skills, goals and a handout; fall-ball games with
   lineups, so attendance and playing time read.
5. **House league, end to end** (replaced the public-page demo 2026-11-21). A parent signs up a U9 child, answers the club's own
   questions ("Can a parent help?" → Coach) and names a friend by code. On the draft board the admin filters to the parent-coaches'
   children, puts one on each team, adds the coach, runs the draft: every family and every friend pair lands together, the new child
   with her friend on her parent's team. The Battle of the Bats phone card is left behind instead of demoed.

**What you can do.** Rebuild the whole club with one command, any time; the morning of the meeting, rebuild it for that day. Logins and
branding survive. When the club sends its real permit and team list, they go into one settings file and a rebuild.

**Customer impact.** None outside this prospect; nothing touches production or a real family. The owner's real U13 team and
tournament are never used.

**Priority.** High — the next sales step, owner's go 2026-11-20.

**Success.** All five demos rehearsed against the run sheet with no seeding gap; a rebuild twice in a row gives the same club; a reset
after a rehearsal returns it exactly; no other club's rows change.

**Update 2026-11-23, the evening before the meeting (2026-11-24).** The club is rebuilt for the meeting day and dressed for the
questions a board of coaches asks off the script. The dressed team now has three upcoming games, its eight past lineups marked
ready, awards, an opponent book with notes, two emails already sent to its families, and an emergency contact on every player; the
club has one invitation waiting in Members. The deck and the run sheet say what is true after the November 22 and 23 releases
(group email, the club's own registration questions and schedules across several permits are live; the club plans are on sale).
The run sheet gains a list, "If he asks to see more", of what can be opened and what belongs in a second session. Still empty,
and not small: tryouts, a finished season, the club's budget and bank, sponsorship packages and deals, a tournament in progress,
player documents. One lesson for next time: build the club the evening before; a rebuild in the last minutes is not safe, because
a failed one leaves the club empty until it is run again.
