Build outline, for the later build prompt

Enforced on the server, not by hiding screens

  • The sign-in service marks every session as password-only or password-and-code. The check reads that mark.
  • It goes in the server’s shared sign-in helpers, which about 265 admin routes and 87 platform routes already pass through, plus the handful of coach, families and team guards that check sign-in on their own. Because the request layer skips most server routes, a check there alone would leave them open.
  • Pages (club admin, platform admin) send a password-only session in a required seat to the code step or the set-up step, never to an error.
  • A census of every place that checks sign-in on its own comes first; one missed is a hole.

What we build ourselves

  • The sign-in service has the authenticator, its set-up, its check and an admin reset. Its own backup codes are marked experimental, and it has no “remember this device”.
  • Backup codes: ten, stored only as one-way hashes, each used once. Using one lets you in for that sign-in and offers to set up a new phone. Whether to use the sign-in service’s experimental codes instead is a build-time call, decided by whether they are stable then.
  • Asking again: the session records when the code was last typed. Required seats are asked again after 30 days, and before a short list of actions (ask 4).
  • The 24-hour owner reset, its hold and its cancel.

Probably one migration

  • An organization’s two switches (“require for the board”, “also coaches”, both off by default, with when each was turned on), whether the owner has closed the one-time prompt, and its “test organization” exemption.
  • Backup codes (hashed) and each person’s grace-period start.
  • A waiting owner reset (who asked, when confirmed, when it runs, held or not).
  • The audit lines use the club’s and the platform’s existing audit logs, so no new log.
  • The data dictionary and snapshots, in the same unit of work.

Must verify before building

  • That refreshing a session keeps it password-and-code. The sign-in service’s docs don’t say; test it on dev.
  • The hosted limit on authenticators per person (the docs give 10 for self-hosting only), and cleaning up a half-finished set-up.
  • Whether a Coaches Portal coach who bought their own team holds the organization’s owner seat. If so, the “offered” row in tab 1 needs its own rule in code.
  • Production Auth settings are not changed in this session. The authenticator is already switched on there.

Asks

All ruled 2026-11-10. Ask 1 as the owner amended it; asks 2–8 and A–F as recommended (“I agree with the recommendations on this mockup”). Asks 1–8 are the decisions; A–F came out of the /design check.

1Who must, and who may · RULED 2026-11-10

Ruled: offered to everyone. A club may require it for its board seats (owner, admins, treasurer, registrars, league admins) and, separately, for its coaches; both switches start off and only the owner changes them. FieldLogicHQ staff must always use it.

Owner’s reasoning: required two-step would be a nuisance for the average volunteer, while a switch satisfies a board with security-minded members. Agreed in the same reply: turning a requirement off asks for the owner’s code and emails the other admins; the owner sees a one-time prompt on Overview; the 14-day grace period starts when a switch is turned on. My earlier recommendation (required for those seats) is replaced.

What this leaves open, so it isn’t a surprise later: the draft privacy policy’s note about medical notes stays open rather than closed, and the policy says “available; an organization can require it”, nothing stronger. In our terms with clubs, the choice is theirs.

2Method: an authenticator app only

Recommended: authenticator app only. No emailed codes, and no text messages.

Emailed codes give no protection here. “Forgot your password?” already sends a link to the same inbox, so a password plus an emailed code is only the inbox, twice. The person who has stolen a treasurer’s email still gets in. We would also spend the daily email quota on sign-ins.

A person may set up a second phone or tablet as a spare. That is the friendliest backup for a volunteer who forgets where they put their codes.

3Recovery without you as the bottleneck

  • Backup codes: ten at set-up, each works once, new ones on demand. Recommended: yes.
  • The club resets its own people: the owner and admins, for anyone but the owner and themselves. The person is emailed and signed out everywhere. Recommended: yes.
  • But not across clubs. The authenticator belongs to the person, not the club. If Dana is treasurer here and coaches in another club, a reset here takes it off there too. Recommended: a club resets only people whose account reaches no other organization; anyone else goes to support. The club is given no reason (that Dana is elsewhere is Dana’s business, per /legal); Dana and support are told why.
  • The owner: only support resets it. They email from the account’s address and confirm by a link sent to it. The club’s other admins and treasurer are emailed and can put it on hold, there is a 24-hour wait, and signing in with the real code cancels it. Recommended: yes. The inbox alone is not enough of an identity check, because it is the same inbox a password reset uses; the wait and the club’s admins are what make it safe. The lost-phone screen and the confirmation email both say, before the owner confirms, that the club’s admins will be told. Gap you should know about: an owner with no other admin or treasurer gets only the 24-hour wait, and the notice goes to the same inbox a thief may hold. Recommended: accept it as a known gap, and have support phone the owner on a number from the club’s own records before resetting a sole owner.
  • An audit line for every reset, in the club’s audit log (by a club) or the platform audit log (by support), with the support note. Recommended: yes.

4“Remember this device” and asking again

Recommended: no checkbox. A person stays signed in on their own device, as today, and is asked for a code when they sign in again. Required seats are asked again after 30 days. Five actions ask for a code if the last one was more than 15 minutes ago: changing payout or bank details, adding an owner or admin, resetting someone’s two-step, turning your own off, and getting new backup codes.

A “remember this device for 30 days” box would mean a session that has not passed the code is let in anyway, which is a second rule for the server to trust and get wrong. Staying signed in already does the remembering. Recording a payment or a dues entry does not ask again; that would be every treasurer’s whole evening.

5Signed-in devices: one button, not a list

Recommended: “Sign out everywhere else”, and no device list.

A list means recording and showing each device’s name and location, which is more personal information for the privacy policy to explain and one more screen to keep right. The button does the job a lost phone needs.

6What the club sees: Members only, never The board

Recommended: the Two-step column on Members (owner and admins), the Overview reminder, nothing on The board tab.

The brief asked for it on The board’s seats too. I’d leave it off. The board tab is read more widely, and a list of which board members have no second step is a list of whose password is worth stealing. Members already shows it to the people who can act on it.

7Grace period: 14 days, then set up at sign-in; and Change password

Recommended: 14 days from the day the club switches a requirement on (or from the day a new seat is accepted in a club that already requires it). A reminder screen at each sign-in, an Overview reminder for owners and admins, and two emails with no link. After that, signing in sets it up first, and only the club’s admin waits. Platform staff: no grace.

Related gap found on the way: a signed-in person cannot change their password anywhere today, only reset it by email. The new Sign-in & security page is its natural home. Recommended: add “Change password” to this build. It is small, and a security page without it looks unfinished to a board.

8Test and demo accounts

Recommended: a “test organization” exemption that only platform admin can set (on the demos, the Milton demo, and the UAT and fixture clubs), set-up refused for the shared demo accounts, and one UAT account with a known authenticator key so the code step is tested on every run.

A · Where a coach’s reset lives

Coaches aren’t managed in Members: the Coaching staff list there says “Managed on the team’s staff page” and has no Manage window. So “ask your club to reset it” needs a door somewhere else for a coach.

Recommended: the state line on the Coaching staff rows (drawn), and the reset door in the team staff page’s own person window, for the club’s owner and admins only. It is drawn at the build session, from that page.

The 2026-11-09 Terms ruling keeps a sign-in screen’s button off until its box is ticked. The backup-codes “Done” instead stays on and explains when pressed without the tick, like a sign-up card.

Recommended: explain on press. It’s an account step, not an agreement, and a faded button doesn’t say what’s missing.

The sign-in column never uses the monospace face. Ten backup codes and the set-up key are the case for it: 0 and O, 1 and I must not be confused when someone types them back in from paper.

Recommended: a named exception for recorded codes only (the backup-code list and the key). The code field the person types into stays in the body face.

Recommended: stays amber (“Not set up”, no count). Nobody did anything wrong yet; the person simply hasn’t signed in since. Red is kept for something that failed.
