Screen 3 · decisions 1 and 2

The frame: one sheet, two layers, three heads

Nothing new to draw: the two references below are today’s Tools menu and today’s Lineup setup, which already follow the rulings. The frame makes them one component and gives every other sheet their rules.

Shared by every sheet

Full width, 18px top corners, the grab line, the portal’s dim (warm and dark), the card surface, a height cap, the sheet scrolls without dragging the page, Escape and a tap on the dim close it, focus goes back to what opened it.

Menu layer · nothing to lose

Sits on top of the bar, which stays visible and tappable. Not modal. Tools, Filter, the switchers, Print, Copy from, the record sheets, Who’s here.

Form layer · work in hand

Covers the bar and takes it out of reach for a thumb, a keyboard and a screen reader. A × is required. The keyboard stays inside. Setup, Save as template, Call up, the game-day Note, the Award sheet while editing.

Three heads

A small-capitals label for a menu; a sentence title with × for a form (a tall menu may keep its ×); a record head (a name and a context line) when the sheet is about one player.

The game-day button row docks on the nav

Owner, during the walk: the row of Who’s here · Note · Scouting · End game stops short of the screen’s edges, and at the bottom of the page it lifts off the nav. Measured on the live game: on a 390px phone the row is 358px wide (16px short of each edge), and scrolled to the very end it sits 32px above the nav. The same 32px on a tablet. Not new: step 3 never touched the row; it has looked like this since it was raised to clear the nav on 22 September.

Why it does that

  • The row sticks to the page, but it is still part of the page’s column. It takes the column’s width, so it keeps the page’s 16px side margins. When the page runs out, it drops back to its place in the column, which ends 32px (the page’s bottom margin) above the nav.
  • A docked bar is the portal’s shape for a row of real actions. The 14 September ruling gives a full-width bar only to a screen with real content in it; this row holds four of the screen’s jobs. The lineup builder’s Undo · Redo · Print bar was docked on the nav the same way on phones, until its actions moved into the toolbar.

Decisions · round 1 · drawn 2026-10-03 · ruled 2026-10-05

Six decisions, all accepted as recommended

Owner, 2026-10-05: “I agree with all of your recommendations.” Each marker on the Mockup tab opens its decision here.

D1 · Two layers, sorted by one test: would a stray tap on the bar lose something?

Accepted 2026-10-05

Every sheet is one of two layers. Menu layer (nothing to lose): sits on top of the bar, the bar stays live, not modal. Form layer (work in hand): covers the bar, takes it out of reach of a thumb, a keyboard and a screen reader, a × is required, the keyboard stays inside. The test replaces “acts and closes” versus “you type and commit” with the 23 September ruling’s own reason.

Recommended. It keeps the ruling and sorts the sheets it did not foresee: the Filter sheet and Who’s here stay open but save every tap, so they are menus (as built); the game-day Note holds a half-typed note, so it is a form (not as built, and the one visible change it causes). It also settles the screen reader: only a form is modal, so the Filter sheet and position picker stop claiming to be.

Alternative: three named variants (menu, live panel, form). More names for two geometries; the live panel would sit exactly where a menu does.

D2 · Three heads: a label for menus, a title with × for forms, a record head for one player

Accepted 2026-10-05

A menu’s head is the small-capitals label Tools and Filter carry. A form’s head is the sentence title with a 44px × that Lineup setup carries; a tall menu may keep a ×. A sheet about one record (position, RSVP, the player row) keeps its name and context line. Seven title styles become these three.

Recommended. Each head matches a rule already made: the label because the dim covers what opened the sheet (E1), the × because covering the bar earns a way out (2026-09-23), the record head because a sheet about Avery should say Avery. Visible changes: the switchers’ mono label, Print’s missing title, Call up’s own heading.

Alternative: one head everywhere, the portal’s 16px sentence heading. One look, but it restyles every menu, including the two drawers just ruled.

D3 · What stays different on purpose, and what is drift

Accepted 2026-10-05

Kept on purpose: the two layers; the record heads; More’s tiles and colour; Copy from filling the screen; RSVP stacked over the event window; the Award sheet switching layer as it edits; the Ledger Filter sheet with no Done. Drift, to move: the game-day Note above a live bar; the game-day dim on warm, its 72% height, its scroll and its hand-copied bar height; the switchers’ title; Print’s missing title and role; Call up’s head; the modal claim on menu-layer sheets; forms that let the keyboard out; focus lost after Escape on Copy from; the Schedule day list covering the bar.

Recommended as listed. Every kept item has a ruling behind it; no drift item does. The one judgement call is Who’s here’s height: nothing records why it stops at 72%, but if you want the game visible above it, it keeps 72%.

Alternative: keep the game-day console as its own recipe and fix only Note’s layer and the dim. Smaller, but leaves a fourth recipe to drift again.

D4 · The club admin’s sheet keeps its own component

Accepted 2026-10-05

The club and tournament admin’s sheet does not join the frame. When its screens are next worked on, it takes the frame’s look (18px corners, the portal’s dim); whether a filter sheet has Done gets its own ruling.

Recommended. It opens above everything on purpose (the admin has no coach bar), and two public pages use it, so merging would make the admin and the public site depend on the coaches portal for a look that differs by two numbers. The real question is behaviour: the tournament Teams filter has Done, the Ledger filter was ruled not to, a week apart. That deserves a ruling, not a merge.

Alternative: one sheet everywhere, admin and public included. One component, but it crosses three shells and touches public pages for a small visual gain.

D5 · The Schedule’s view menu and Add event become drawers on a phone

Accepted 2026-10-05

On a phone both menus rise from the bottom as menu-layer sheets with a title and the dim, like every other menu.

Recommended. The 22 September drawer ruling called a popover under a 44px square the wrong surface on a phone; these two are the last ones. Both sit at the top of the screen, the hardest place to reach with a thumb.

Alternative: leave them as cards. They open next to the button that opened them, which is a fair reading too, and Add event is one of the screens a coach uses most.

D6 · Five small steps, the lineup builder last

Accepted 2026-10-05

The frame is promoted from the Tools drawer first (nothing visible), then the small menus, then game day, then the record sheets, then the lineup builder’s drawers. Each step is its own build and walk.

Recommended. Lineup and practice screens are being worked on in parallel, and the builder’s drawers already follow the rulings, so they gain least and collide most. Game day gets its own walk because it is used under the most pressure.

Alternative: one sweep. Faster to finish, one walk instead of five, but it would land on screens another session is changing.

D7 · On a phone and tablet, the game-day button row docks on the nav, edge to edge

Ruled as drawn 2026-10-06

Wherever the bottom bar shows (up to 900px wide), the row of Who’s here · Note · Scouting · End game is pinned on the nav at every scroll position, top, middle and end. Its surface and top line span the screen; its buttons stay in the page’s column (16px in from the edges on a phone, the 480px column on a tablet). The page leaves room for the row, so its last line scrolls clear and ends about 32px above it.

Recommended. The row holds four of the screen’s jobs, which is what earns a docked bar (14 September). A docked bar sits on what it docks to at every scroll position; today this one does only until the page runs out. Nothing moves for a thumb: the buttons keep their places and sizes. Mockup › 7.

Alternative: close the 32px gap only and keep the row 358px wide. Smaller, but the row still reads as a shelf floating on the nav rather than part of the foot.

D8 · On a computer, the row stays as it is

Ruled as drawn 2026-10-06

Above 900px there is no bottom bar. The row stays at the foot of the window in the console’s 480px column, as today, including the 32px it lifts at the very end of the page.

Recommended: leave it. There is no second bar for a gap to sit between; the lift lands on the page’s own ground. A band across the whole window holding a 480px column of buttons would read as empty furniture, and game day is run from a phone. Step 3 also promised no change on a computer.

Alternative: dock it at the window’s foot too, so it never lifts. Consistent with the phone, at the cost of a wide, mostly empty band on a computer.

Product-manager brief

Sheet Frame

What changes for a coach

Very little on screen, on purpose. A coach writing a game-day note sees the bottom bar go dim under it, so a stray tap cannot lose the note. The Schedule’s Add event and view menus rise from the bottom like every other menu. Print, the team switcher and Call up get the same kind of title as their neighbours. On the warm theme, the game-day sheets dim the screen the same way every other sheet does. Behind the scenes, every sheet behaves the same way for a keyboard and a screen reader.

Step 4 (built 2026-10-06, committed 2a304b52): the sheets about one player, one award or one notice — the position sheet, the Award sheet, a player’s RSVP, the notification reader (now on the club admin’s Notifications page too) and the player row menus — come from the shared frame, nothing inside them moved. With the position sheet or the Award sheet open, a tap on the bottom bar now closes the sheet first instead of opening More on top of it. In Dark they wear the card colour; the grab line is the frame’s lighter one. For a keyboard, a sheet on a live bar no longer holds you inside it. Owner QA §273 ✅ passed 19/19 2026-10-06.

Why it matters

A coach can open 16 sheets on a phone, built from four recipes, beside two popover menus and four full-screen windows. Two rulings about how sheets behave reached some of them and not others, so the same gesture does different things on different screens. One shared frame makes the rulings hold everywhere and stops new sheets drifting.

Customer impact

Every coach on a phone. The most visible gain is on game day, the screen used under the most pressure. The least visible, and the most important for accessibility, is that a screen reader is told the truth about what it can reach.

Priority

Medium. Step 1 is already due by the drawer’s own notes; game day is the step with real user value; the lineup builder can wait for the work in flight there.

Success criteria

  • Every sheet that holds unsaved work covers the bar; every other sheet leaves it live.
  • Three title styles, one dim per theme, one height rule, across every sheet.
  • Escape returns focus to what opened the sheet, everywhere.
  • A new sheet is built from the frame, with nothing to copy.
