The word names the row

A money-in budget row used to be named by a word you could not see, edit or delete. It is now named by the budget word it is filed against — and two lines filed against one word are a single row that opens. Eighteen checks in five parts. Two of them are questions rather than ticks; those are the ones I actually need your eye on.

Part E

Two things I want ruled, not ticked

Neither is a defect. Both are choices this change made on your behalf, and either could be reversed cheaply now and expensively later.

E1

Alphabetical order

ruling

Your money-in rows used to appear in the order you created them. They now sort alphabetically, because that is how the spending side has always worked and one rule beat a special case for half the table.

Is that an improvement, or just a change? A coach who built their plan in a deliberate order has lost it. Keeping creation order for money-in only is a one-line difference — but it puts a special case back into a table whose whole theme is one rule.
E2

Two lines, one schedule

ruling

If two lines share a word, share a month and neither has a note, they render as two rows with the same name — told apart only by their amounts.

Narrower than what it replaced, but not gone. The old echo fired for every pair; this fires only for same-schedule pairs. Options: leave it; make a note required when a second line lands on a word already in use; or number them. The middle one puts a blocking question in front of a coach mid-save, which I do not like.
