What Make the link only does

  • It makes the same deal Send makes: Offered, holding its place (ask 7), with its one signed link. The only difference is that no email goes out.
  • No reason is kept, because none is needed. CASL is about messages sent. A link the club hands over in person, or reads out on the phone, isn’t one. What is kept: who made the link and when, in the deal’s history.
  • The foot’s sentence stays “Emails frank@…” because it describes Send, the main act. It changes only when Send can’t go (tab 3).
  • The note is for the email. Make the link only doesn’t keep it. A club that typed one and then made the link sees it again if it emails the offer later from the deal’s page.
  • It doesn’t check the package is full any differently. A full package can’t be picked in either case; a taken place warns and never blocks (asks 6 and 7).

The deal’s page opens with the link ready to copy, and Deals says the link was not emailed

No new window: the press closes Offer a deal and opens the deal’s own page (round 1 tab 5), in Offered, with Copy the link as its lime. The Offered callout gets one more sentence for this case. The deal can still be emailed later, once the club has a reason, from the same page.

The rules this tab keeps

  • Absent, not disabled. Send isn’t there when it can’t go, and the foot’s left sentence says why (round 1’s rule for the mailing address, applied to all four cases).
  • Nobody at the club can clear an unsubscribe, a family opt-out or Do not offer again (ask 13). The menu says each one plainly and nothing in the window undoes it.
  • Do not offer again is the club’s own stop for a business that said so by reply or phone. Its line names the date it was set. It stops email from every seat, the same as an unsubscribe.
  • It becomes the lime. With Send absent it is the window’s one act, so it takes the one lime, as every screen’s main act does.
  • Make the link only stays in all four. It sends nothing, so none of the four rules is about it. For an unsubscribed business it is the in-person route /legal describes for a business that comes back to the club. /legal re-checks this at Part 2’s close.
  • Withdraw stays on any offer, however it was made.

The rules this tab keeps

  • The date is the reason’s, not the offer’s. “Kept with today’s date” still records the day the offer went; When? records the day the reason rests on. Both are kept, with who sent it.
  • Only the two that run out ask. Paid in two years and asked in six months have a clock. A given or published address doesn’t.
  • The line says the way forward. It never just refuses: the in-person link is the next step, and Make the link only is in the same foot.
  • No day after today. The calendar refuses it, the way DateInput refuses any day outside its range.
  • A later email checks again. Send the link again and Email the offer read the kept date against the day they are pressed, so an offer can’t keep emailing on a reason that has run out.
  • Offer again reads it too (Part 4): a business whose kept reason has run out is listed but can’t be ticked, as round 1 tab 9 drew for the lapsed one.
