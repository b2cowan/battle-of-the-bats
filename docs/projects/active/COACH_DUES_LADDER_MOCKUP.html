The overpayment row becomes a caption

Avery's ($550.00) Overpayment (dues changed) row has no date, no pencil, no bin and no link to anything — it is derived from the schedule and the payments, which is why it says "Follows the schedule" instead of a day. It is a caption wearing a row's costume. Under the ladder there is no tile for it, because that money is inside Paid — so giving it a section would break the one-section-per-tile rule on its first day.

So it moves into the Payments heading: Payments — $1,250.00 received · $550.00 more than billed. Same fact, in the section where the money actually is, and the CREDITS list stops containing the family's own money — which is the last unfinished corner of §148.

What we'd want to keep: hovering that row today explains "change the dues total to change it". The caption needs to carry that too, or a coach loses the only place the screen says how to move the figure.
An edge case this turned up, and it needs deciding. A coach can add a credit by hand and pick Overpayment as its kind. That row is not a payment record, so under a strict reading it would belong to no tile and no section — it would vanish from the screen while still moving the balance. Fix: Other credits holds everything that isn't fundraising and isn't the engine's own schedule credit, so a hand-typed overpayment lands there. Honest, too — it is a credit a coach asserted, not money the family sent.

Two things this lets us delete

The explanations stop earning their place

The row's sub-line goes

"sent $550.00 more than billed" was the right fix for a four-column table and it is redundant in a six-column one. On the new row, Paid $1,250.00 sits on the same line as Dues $700.00 and the Status column already reads Overpaid — the sentence was saying out loud what two figures now say by sitting next to each other.

There is a better reason than redundancy, though. The sentence and its figure disagree for a family who has been paid out. It says sent, but the number behind it is what the team is still holding. Casey sent $300 more than billed and got every dollar back, so the line correctly stays away — while saying "sent" about a figure that means "held". With the columns, Casey simply reads Paid $1,200.00 · Handed back $300.00 and no prose has to thread that needle.

Worth recording rather than just doing. That sub-line is one of §148's two visible deliverables and it shipped three days ago. This supersedes it rather than reverses it — the columns do the job the sentence was invented to do — but the ledger should say so, or it reads as drift later.

The tile captions go

"Bottle Drive rebate", "Contribution · socks", "1 payment", "Paid out in cash" — each is a lossy summary of a section sitting a few centimetres below it, which now carries the same total in its heading and every record in full. Avery's Fundraising has three sources; a caption could only ever say "3 sources" or truncate to one of them.

It also fixes something visible in the row itself: tiles with a caption are taller than tiles without, so Dues and Balance sat short while the middle four sat tall. One line each, all the same height.

One place I'd keep them, and you should overrule me if you disagree. The phone receipt. On a desktop the sections are a glance away; on a phone they are a full screen of scrolling below, and that receipt is the only place the whole picture fits at once. Different distance, different answer — not an inconsistency.
