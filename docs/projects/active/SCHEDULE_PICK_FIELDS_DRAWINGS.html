A schedule on some of a permit's fields

Round 1 drawings for review. Nothing is built. The window is drawn at its true width (1024 px), from the styles that ship today. The example is a winter dome: one permit for Fields 1–6, Sundays 9:00 a.m. – 1:30 p.m., Jan 3 – Mar 7, 2027, cut into 90-minute slots (6 fields × 3 slots × 10 Sundays = 180). The permit's name, its issuer and the dome's name are made up for the drawing. New schedule is a desk screen by an earlier ruling, so no phone is drawn.

Ticking a permit brings in every field it holds, and today you can't untick any. The row names its fields as plain words. This drawing turns those words into the list with ticks the permit's own form already uses, so a schedule on two of a dome's six fields is made on the form itself.

It works as the shipped list works: you tick the fields you want. With nothing ticked, the row uses every field, and the first row says so. Tick Field 1 and Field 2 and the row uses those two. Ticking the first row again clears the ticks, which means every field. So the box can never be empty, the field has no star, and there is no "pick at least one" message. On the permit's form the first row reads "Every facility". Here it reads "Every facility on this permit", because a permit can hold fewer fields than its venue has.

The box names the fields even when it is all of them. "Fields 1–6", as the row says today, not "Every facility on this permit". Otherwise the commonest case would say less than it does now. This is one small addition to the shared tick list (it can already write a list this way, but only when some are unticked), so it is flagged for the build.

What else the row used to say, and where it goes

  • A row with no permit lists every field at its venue, and its first row reads "Every facility". The small "No permit" under the place goes: its card is already titled "Typed here".
  • A row added by "Find the time" keeps its amber note ("4 Thursdays on Diamond B are held by the draft…"), as a line under the box.
  • A venue with no fields listed keeps the plain venue name, as today. There is nothing to tick.
  • The word is "Facilities", as on the rest of the form, so it reads right for rinks, courts and gyms.

After the schedule exists

  • A draft or one in review: Settings… shows the same rows, so fields can be changed and the drafts made again.
  • A sent schedule: a narrowed row follows the rule that already ships for any edited row. The old row ends yesterday, the new one starts today, and nothing before today moves. Practices on a field taken off come off as changes to send, credited the way removed time is today.

Not in this drawing

  • Rows with their own dates (Fields 1 and 2 in January, all six from February). See Ask 5.
  • A second schedule seeing this one. Nothing here tells the next schedule that Fields 1 and 2 are taken on Sunday mornings. That is the next stage.
  • Half a field. The next stage, drawn with the "teams at the same time" rule.
  • A schedule with no permit at all. Its Venue and Facilities chips stay as they are, and its "Add hours" keeps its name.

Ask 3 · The "not on this schedule" line

  • A · Show it (drawn): "Fields 3–6 are not on this schedule." Only when a field the permit holds is on none of its rows. It names whole fields, never hours. On Add time it reads "not on this added time" (tab 4).
  • B · No line. The ticks already say it.

Recommend A. It is the one place the form says what is left for another schedule, and it costs one line only when it applies.

Ask 4 · A second row on a permit's card

  • A · Each permit's card gets its own "Add hours" (drawn). The row it adds belongs to that permit and is priced by it. The door under the cards stays for time with no permit, and says so ("Add hours with no permit", only on a form that has a permit ticked). Each card grows by about 34 px.
  • B · Leave "Add hours" as it is. One row per block of the permit's time. Different fields at different hours needs the "Find the time" door.

Recommend A. It goes past the smallest fix, but picking fields per row is only half useful if a permit can have one row.

Ask 5 · Rows with their own dates

  • A · Not now. One From and To for the schedule. "Fields 1 and 2 in January, all six from February" is made as the schedule plus Add time once it is sent, or with the "Find the time" door.
  • B · Add "only between" dates to a row. One schedule covers both periods from the start. Each row grows two more fields, on a row that is already full.

Recommend A. Nobody has asked for it yet, and Add time already gives a later period its own dates and bill.

Ask 6 · To, typed past the permit's last day

  • A · A permit's time stops at the permit's dates, with the line under To saying so (tab 4). One permit then behaves as several already do.
  • B · Leave it. Slots are made past the permit's end when To is typed past it.
