GST/HST in the books: what you paid, what you collected, and the worksheet for your return

Stage 4 is two jobs. 4a records the tax on what you buy properly: tax by type, the supplier’s GST/HST number, which CRA registration the supplier holds, the kind of purchase, and in one sentence the credit you can claim. 4b adds a worksheet for any dates you choose, set out line by line as the return asks, each figure opening to the invoices or expenses behind it. 4a can be built now. 4b reads the per-invoice tax record GST/HST Stage 3 builds, so it waits for that. Every figure is a made-up example. This round has the /design and /ux findings applied. Everything is a proposal until you rule on the asks in the last tab.

Where I disagree with the brief

  • The worksheet should be a view in Reports, not its own tab. The ruled Reports redesign already has the income-tax statement (T2125) as a view. The GST/HST return is the same kind of thing: a filing aid for a period. A fifth tab for a job you do once a year makes the page busier every day. Tab 7 draws both.
  • US sales: use the Bank of Canada rate on the invoice date, not Stripe’s figure. The brief recommended Stripe’s CAD figure. /accounting found Stripe’s filing amounts can be blank on exactly the zero-rated rows, and couldn’t confirm that Stripe’s rate source counts with CRA. Stage 3’s record has no rate column yet, so this answer goes back into Stage 3’s first step.
  • The credit is not always tax times business use. Equipment you keep gets all of the tax back above 50% business use and none at or below 50%. Everyday costs get all of it at 90% or more and none at 10% or less. Meals get half. So the form needs one new question, “Kind of purchase”.
  • “Foreign supplier charged tax” is really about which CRA registration the supplier holds. Tax charged by a supplier on CRA’s simplified registration can never be claimed, and there is no rebate form for it. The only fix is to give that supplier your number and ask for a refund. A number printed on the invoice doesn’t tell you which registration it is.

Found while drawing, and not in the brief

  • Sales made between the Stage 2 release and Stage 3’s first day would appear on no worksheet. Stage 3’s first step currently says “no backfill”. Once Stage 2 is live, a club that pays creates a taxed invoice that nothing records.
  • Nothing records what you filed. The worksheet works its figures out live. If a supplier’s number is added after you file, line 106 of a filed period would quietly change. A filed period should freeze, and a late credit should move to your next return.
  • Today’s form has no quick “no tax” answer. Leaving More details closed saves “tax unknown”, so five quick bank charges become five items to clear before you file. Round 2 gives the tax question three answers (tab 2).

Corrections from the return-lines note, already in the drawing

  • Credit notes go on line 107 (adjustments that decrease net tax). They don’t reduce line 103, which stays gross on every invoice issued.
  • Bad debts written off go on line 107. A debt you later recover goes on line 104.
  • Tax is owed in the period the invoice is issued, paid or not. The worksheet reads the issue date.
  • Regular method only. The quick method would take away the credits on your expenses.
  • Services bought before your registration date aren’t claimable.
  • The short line captions are paraphrases of CRA’s instructions and haven’t been checked word for word against the live return. The 4b build checks them.

A rule this overturns, and what sits next door

The Bookkeeping plan says Overview and Reports never show profit, cash, a bank balance or recoverable tax. The form says “This form does not claim a tax credit.” A return worksheet has to show credits and net tax. Proposed: the credit shows on the worksheet and on each expense with its reason. Reports shows tax paid, which is a fact. The Overview gets nothing.

  • Not touched here: Stage 1’s owner walk (§431) has no result, Stage 2’s redraw is owed, and the repayment race must be fixed before the books go live.
  • The Bookkeeping ladder gets two rows (4a, and 4b with the worksheet). Its Stripe income stage narrows to fees, payouts and bank matching.

What the design and flow checks settled without needing you

  • Tax stays inside More details. The tax question has three answers, so “no tax” is one pick and never a to-do. Nothing in the section blocks saving.
  • Business use stays visible on every expense. Kind of purchase starts from the category, and you can change it.
  • The credit sentence sits right under the figures that change it, neutral unless something is waiting or not claimable.
  • PST and QST are recorded as tax lines but never claimed. They count as part of the cost.
  • A missing number is said once: a chip in the record, a reason in Needs attention, an item in Before you file. A row with that reason opens straight to the number field. “I can’t get the number” clears it.
  • Credit notes and write-offs go on line 107, and recovered debts on 104. Line 103 stays gross. A voided invoice stays in 103 and its reversal shows on 107; the 4b build has /accounting confirm how a bare void is papered.
  • “Not available” is never shown as $0. A period that starts before the sales record does is marked partial until you answer for those days.
  • The worksheet table is the statement, with no band repeating it. Lines with parts fold open, a figure opens its window, and calculated lines open nothing.
  • Nothing on the Overview, ever. No sales rows in the Bookkeeping store.

Formatting rules that disagree with each other (listed, not picked silently)

  • Chevron on every row (K-08) vs. a chevron only where something opens (Standard §3.6). The worksheet follows §3.6: calculated lines have no chevron.
  • Row chevron vs. the dotted-underline figure button (§3.6). The worksheet uses the figure button, because the row also folds. Tables of records (invoices, expenses) keep name-link plus chevron.
  • Group rows fold (§2) vs. a row that opens a window (§3.6). No rule covers a row that does both. Drawn as: ▸ folds, the figure opens.
  • A joined band for money figures vs. “a row that restates another is deleted” (§3.5). The worksheet drops its band. Reports keeps a band, because its figures don’t repeat its rows.
  • §3.2 still says admin figures use the data face (K-10), but K-10 was retired 2026-10-24 and the kit says body face. Drawn in the body face.
  • Negative money: the coach Budget uses red brackets; nothing covers platform admin. The worksheet writes “refund” or “you owe” in words.
