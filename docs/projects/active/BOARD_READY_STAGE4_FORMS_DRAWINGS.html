The club’s sign-up form, first used by house league

One form a club builds from seven kinds of question and any number of documents to read. House-league season sign-up uses it first, drawn in full. Sponsor links and camp sign-ups reuse it later and aren’t drawn here. Specimens are drawn at true size from the parts that ship today (the season’s Registration block, the tournament question editor, the family sign-up page, the Registrations list, the export menu and the draft board), and say so where something is new. Tab 1 is the season as it stands today, before sign-ups open; tabs 2 to 7 are drawn as of late February 2027, a few weeks into sign-ups. Click any number, tag or red flag to read what it refers to. The last tab holds your asks; the first answer on each is my recommendation and is already selected.

Why its own page, and not a bigger block or a new season tab

  • Not in the block: a form of five questions and two documents, each with its own settings, does not fit inside one card’s edit mode on a phone. A question is a record and opens in its own window, as the tournament questions already do.
  • Not a season tab: the season already has seven (Overview, Registrations, Teams & draft, Schedule, Standings, Money, Email families). The form is set up once a season and read rarely, so a door from the Registration block is enough.
  • The same page later: a camp’s setup (Stage 7) and a sponsorship package (Stage 6) open the same page from their own setup, with their own fixed fields in the first card (tab 9).

Building the form: questions in two groups, each opening its own window

The list and its windows are the tournament question editor’s, which ships: a framed row list with a grip, the question in bold and a caption, a chevron, and “+ Add a question” opening a window. Three things are new: the question types widen to seven, each question carries help text, and the list is in two groups, one asked for each child and one asked once per sign-up. Which group a question sits in is the club’s choice, question by question (ask 1).

The seven types, and what each one asks for

  • Short answer and Long answer: a line or a box. Today’s tournament “Short text” and “Long text”.
  • One choice: round buttons for up to four choices, a dropdown from five, at every width. Choices go in a box, one per line, as today’s “Dropdown”.
  • Many choices: tick boxes. Same choices box.
  • Yes or no: two round buttons. It replaces today’s tournament “Checkbox”, which could only say “I confirm”. A required yes/no needs an answer, not a yes.
  • Date: the shared date field.
  • File upload: one file per question, a PDF or a photo, up to 8 MB (ask 9). It can’t be required, so a failed upload never blocks a sign-up. The window adds “Don’t ask for a health card number or a photo of one.” under this type (/legal).

Rules the window keeps

  • New questions are optional, and a health question stays optional unless the club turns Required on (/legal: PIPEDA 4.3.3).
  • “Asked” is absent on a form with one kind of answerer (a sponsor’s link). On house league it defaults to “For each child”.
  • “Show on the draft board” is off by default and can’t be turned on for a health question (ask 12).
  • The foot says who misses out. A question added after sign-ups opened names how many sign-ups will read “Not asked”; those families can answer it on their Your sign-up page (ask 20).
  • No branching. There is no “only ask if…”. Ruled; unchanged.

A document: a PDF or a typed text, a tick sentence, and versions that keep their ticks

A document is today’s waiver, generalized, and a family must tick it to sign up. A typed text shows in place on the sign-up page, as the waiver does now, and can be ticked straight away. A PDF must be opened first: its tick stays faded until our server has handed the file over. Typed text has to stay, because every waiver consent already recorded points at a typed text. The tick’s sentence is chosen per document, so the waiver keeps “I have read and agree” (ask 2). Each tick is kept with the exact version the family saw.

What a tick keeps (from /legal)

  • The document and its version, and a fingerprint of the exact file or text the family saw.
  • The exact sentence ticked, as the Terms acceptance keeps its box text.
  • The registration and the child, and which sign-up it came from, so one tick by a parent of two children is two records that point at the same sign-up.
  • The guardian’s email (and the account, once families sign in), the time of the tick, and for a PDF the time our server handed the family the file.
  • No IP address, as the Terms acceptance and today’s waiver keep none (ask 18).

The words the product may and may not say

  • It says families are asked to open the document first. It never says “we confirm they read it”.
  • Ticks from before versions show as “recorded before documents had versions; text as the club held it on {date}”. They never show a fingerprint or an open time, because none was taken.

What the build must do that the pictures don’t show (from /ux and /legal)

  • Answers are saved in the same step that signs the child up. Today a child’s friends are saved afterwards and a failure is only logged; an allergy must never be dropped that way. If saving fails, the sign-up fails and says so.
  • Opening the PDF never costs the family their answers. It opens in a new tab. On a phone the browser can reload the form when the family comes back, so the form keeps what was typed for that browser tab until it is sent; whether health answers are kept that way is a /legal call for the build. If the PDF won’t open, a line offers “Download it instead” and the club’s contact, so a broken PDF never strands a family.
  • The faded tick is still reachable by keyboard and screen reader, and says why it can’t be ticked; when the family comes back from the PDF, “Opened. You can now tick.” is announced.
  • A file is uploaded on its own, one per request, PDF, JPEG, PNG or HEIC, checked from the file itself. A file never attached to a sign-up is deleted within a day; Remove deletes it at once.
  • A form that changed while the family was filling it in comes back with the new question and keeps everything typed: “The club added a question while you were filling this in.”
  • A child who fits no division gets no questions, and the confirmation names that child as not signed up (today it lists only the children who were, finding F8).
  • Phone tap targets: the tick box is drawn 18px; the whole tick line (box and words) is the 44px target.
