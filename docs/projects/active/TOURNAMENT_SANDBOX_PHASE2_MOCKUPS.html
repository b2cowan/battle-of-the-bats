~390px. The dock scrolls horizontally if it must, but these three labels fit. The banner drops the long promise to its second line (existing wrap behaviour); the pill yields to the dots on the narrowest rows (existing stepper wrap).

Geometry. The chrome already measures itself and publishes its height, and every top-pinned bar in both halves of the product consumes the shared token (build notes 14 & 38). The dock adds ~36px inside the same measured element, so nothing new needs teaching — but the rendered page must be measured at 1440 and 390 on both halves after build, per the standing rule. This is the sixth defect-prone band; we verify, not assume.
Considered and set aside — folding the dock into an existing row. Cramming three moment tabs into the banner or stepper keeps the chrome at two rows but costs the one thing Phase 2 exists to buy: legibility. The whole lesson of the tour rebuild is that a control a stranger can't parse reads as broken. A dedicated row that says "the year" and names three moments is the legible version. If the owner prefers the two-row variant, it is drawable — say so and this file gets a rev.

The transition · navigation + narration, no theatre

What pressing a moment does

No overlay, no animation, no loading theatre. A press navigates — fan side to that event's public home, operator side to that moment's most telling admin screen — and the narration strip (the tour's existing feedback voice) says what just happened and when "now" is. The strip appearing is itself the visible change, which is the rule the whole tour was rebuilt on.

Jump narration. One sentence per arrival, written for a stranger, always naming the time ("three weeks back", "the day after") and the two or three facts the screen proves. Arrivals via the dock and via tour steps 5–6 share these sentences — one voice, two doors.
Restyled
The countdown slot tells each moment's truth. Game day: Replays in 38:12 (unchanged). Registration week: First pitch in 21 days. Morning after: Wrapped up yesterday. A stranger standing at a finished tournament must not read "Replays in 38:12" — that countdown belongs to the Summer Classic's loop and would be a lie anywhere else.
Restyled
"← Back to game day." The narration strip's existing return control, re-aimed: its home is the Summer Classic fan page, and its label now names the moment rather than "the tournament".
Unchanged
Which side you're on survives the jump. A visitor on the fan side lands on the target event's public home; a visitor in the organizer's seat lands on that moment's flagship admin screen (Teams for registration week, Dashboard for game day, Post-Event Summary for the morning after). The ⇄ flip keeps working inside every moment.
Where operator jumps land. The admin half selects its "current tournament" through the product's own switcher — the same control every multi-event customer uses (it appears in the sidebar automatically once an org has more than one event). A dock press on the operator side sets that selection and lands on the moment's flagship screen; the sidebar switcher stays visible and honest, so a curious visitor who uses it instead of the dock gets the same product truth.

The decision underneath · priced honestly, as the handoff asked

One org, three tournaments — why shape B wins

The Phase 2 handoff named three shapes and asked for honest pricing. Measured against the product as it actually is, the answer moved: the product's own multi-event support does most of Phase 2's work for free.

ShapeWhat it isHonest priceVerdict
A · Three orgs Each moment its own demo org; the dock navigates between them. Three organizer accounts — so switching moments in the operator seat means a session swap through the door on every press. Three entries in an allow-list whose safety story is "short and boring". Three hygiene surfaces (search, metrics, discovery). The world fragments into three associations, or three confusingly identical ones. The chrome unmounts at every switch. Only simpler for one file. Heavier everywhere a visitor actually stands.
B · One org, three tournaments Recommended Three events of the same association, in three lifecycle states. The dock is navigation between them. Two new seeds, two new one-sentence "what should this look like today" rules (dates only — neither new moment ticks), the sweep extended to prove each moment presentable at every hour. That's the bill. The product's native model: the admin's event switcher appears by itself, fan pages are already per-event, the plan has unlimited slots. One session, one world, one hat. Every Phase 1 guarantee is org-level and carries over untouched — write block, outbound silence, curation, hygiene, the self-healing no-cursor loop.
C · Per-visitor overlay One event; the dock changes what each visitor sees without changing what is stored. Per-session state in a sandbox whose deepest property is having none. Rejected. The self-healing invariant is worth more than the dock — the handoff said so, and it's right.
Untouched
Game day's two-hour loop, its 89–92 HEALTHY baseline, the "break the schedule" beat, the door, the ungated ruling, the marketing doors, and both new moments' safety: same org, same write block, same silence.
New
Both new moments are still, by design. Registration week and the morning after don't tick — they re-anchor their dates nightly through the same stateless reconcile run, so the Invitational is always 21 days out and the Opener always ended yesterday. No cursor, no migration, no schema change.
