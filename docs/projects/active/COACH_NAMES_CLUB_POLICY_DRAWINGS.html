Club Tier · Public team page · Round 1 drawings

Who decides which coach names are public

Today every coach on a team can put their own name on the team's public page, and the club has no say. These drawings give the club one setting, with four choices, that decides it for every team at once. Every name, number and club in these frames is an example.

What these drawings decide

  • One club-wide setting, four choices: Coaches choose (today), Head coaches always, Head and assistant coaches always, No coach names.
  • It decides display, it never overwrites a coach. A coach's own switch is left exactly as they set it. If the club later goes back to "Coaches choose", everyone is where they were.
  • A coach is never left guessing. Where the club has decided, their switch is replaced by one plain sentence that says what the club does.
  • Six asks at the bottom. Where the setting lives, who may change it, the other staff, when it asks first, telling coaches, and what a coach reads when no names are listed.

2 · When a choice saves, and when it asks first

A pick on this row saves by itself and shows the “Saved” pill, like the other rows. The exception is a pick that publishes people’s names: that one holds and asks first, with a count, because listing a volunteer’s name is something other people see. “Coaches choose” and “No coach names” only reduce what is public, so they save at once (ask A4 lets you rule otherwise). The numbers in these windows are examples; the real ones are counted from the club’s live teams.

“Their choice” means today’s personal switch, which starts off. The choice applies to every team in the club at once; there is no per-team exception in this round. It decides what is shown and never changes a coach’s own switch.

Asks

Pick one per ask, or write your own. The box at the bottom collects everything to paste back.

A1Where does the setting live?

Drawn on Public site, because that is where the club already decides what the world sees, and Settings points there for it. That page has had five rows, so this is a sixth. Settings holds the birthday cut-off today, which is a rule about eligibility, not about what is public.

A2Who may change it?

The Public site’s online switch is already owner-only, and an admin reads it as plain text. This would work the same way (the admin view in section 1).

A3Under an "always" choice, what about everyone not covered?

Drawn as: the roles the club picked are listed; every other staff member keeps their own switch (section 5). The other way is stricter.

A4Ask before saving?

Section 2. A wrong tap on either “always” choice would publish names on every team page. Hiding names only takes things away, and the Visibility row asks only when going dark breaks something.

A5Are coaches told when the club changes it?

A volunteer's name becoming public with no warning is the sensitive part. The sentence in Team settings is always there, but a coach may not open it for weeks.

A6When the club chooses "No coach names", what does a coach's row become?

Drawn as a sentence plus “Not listed by your club” in the control slot (state E). Removing the row is quieter but leaves a coach wondering where their switch went.

What the review changed in these drawings (a first version used looks copied from older drawings instead of the product’s own):

  • The four choices are one framed list with the product’s round radio, not four separate cards.
  • The new row is drawn like the real rows: summary under the title, 60 px head, no rule inside.
  • The whole-screen frames now include the live preview, and say it does not change.
  • Added the admin’s read-only view of the row (plain text, no radio, not even a greyed one).
  • The coach’s green and grey tags are gone. The row keeps its old label and says “Listed by your club” or “Not listed by your club” as plain text, the way Schedule visibility already does.
  • The coach’s Sharing card now shows the door row above the name row, and type sizes follow the product’s 16 / 14 / 12.
  • The phone no longer shortens any sentence.
  • One word pair throughout: listed and not listed.
  • Ask A4 now follows the house rule that a change which tells other people asks first, and one that only removes things saves at once. A1 gained a third place for the row.
