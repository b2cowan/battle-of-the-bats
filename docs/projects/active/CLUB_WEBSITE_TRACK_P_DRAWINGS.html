What the code says today

Six questions, answered from the code. The file and line for each are in the plan's Progress block (2026-11-25). The finding that matters most is the first callout: nothing today marks an organization as "not a customer" in one place.

  1. 1 · Today's prospect seed is not a preview, and it should not become one. It makes the club an active Club subscriber with nothing in Stripe behind it, which every count reads as a paying Club. It makes made-up children with birthdates, guardians, emergency contacts and two medical notes. Eight of its sign-ins are real Gmail inboxes. Its logo is a stock mark, not the club's own. It refuses production by design, and it stays that way: Milton's world on staging is untouched by this track.
  2. 2 · Nothing can make a club with no owner, and staff cannot own one. The only door that makes a club needs a signed-in person who is not staff, has accepted the Terms, and belongs to no other real club. There is no "preview" state: the trial lives in Stripe. Free time is the wrong tool: it is a customer grant with an end date that shows on the Free time list and sends reminders, and a whole-plan grant refuses checkout for that plan, so the club could not buy. /billing's shape: a preview marker that carries only the site's parts the preview shows, on the free starting plan, and is cleared in the same step as the claim .
  3. 3 · Nothing marks "not a customer" in one place (the table below). The two shared demos are kept out by a list in the code, checked by hand surface by surface. About ten surfaces check nothing at all.
  4. 4 · The claim is a new door. The Terms are per person, ticked at sign-up and checked again at checkout. Checkout makes the Stripe customer, so an unclaimed preview has nothing in Stripe to undo. The only way to hand a club over today needs the person to be a member already, and a club invite cannot give the owner seat. Someone who already runs another club is refused.
  5. 5 · Nothing removes a club by itself. The owner's deletion request deletes nothing; platform admin's Delete removes the club, its files and optionally its Stripe customer. A deleted club's address shows the generic "We can't find that page". A removal that runs by itself is a new job on production, which needs your word in the session that builds it.
  6. 6 · "Live now" is the clock. A game is live from its start time for its length plus 30 minutes, unless it is final. Only tournament pages show a live card and refresh every 30 seconds; house league pages never refresh. On the club home today, "Live now" is a chip on the tournament's card, judged by its dates. So the live game in the room has to be a tournament game, and the score on the home that tab 12 drew arrives with Stage 1. A score needs someone signed in with a role in the club, and staff cannot hold the owner seat.
Why the demo list is the wrong tool. The demo list means two things at once. One is "not a customer, never emails, not in search", and a preview needs all of that. The other is "read-only, shared by everyone, with the sandbox banner, the tour and a no-login door", and a preview must have none of it: the club has to claim it, and you have to type a score into it. So the shape is a separate preview marker that every surface below reads, with a build check that fails when a new count, list or send forgets it (the same idea as the coaches portal's look-back list) .

Why it is shaped this way

  • The score is real, entered by you. The game starts at the meeting time, so "Live now" is true because it is on, not because times were shifted. That keeps the 2026-09-02 ruling's reason intact: no scoreboard is faked.
  • Staff write one thing in a preview: this score. Nobody can sign in to a preview (T3 A), so the window, not a club seat, enters it.
  • "Change" on the meeting moves the live game and the removal date together. A meeting that slips does not cost the preview.
  • Remove now is red and asks for the club's name typed back, as platform admin's Delete does today.
  • New code replaces a lost card's code; the old one stops working. Support reads a code to nobody: a lost card gets a new code, sent by you.
  • The code leaves out 0, O, 1 and I, so it reads aloud without mistakes. The real QR carries a wide quiet border and medium error correction; the one drawn here is a pattern.
  • Mark final ends the game when it ends, so the page shows a final rather than a game that drifts out of "Live now" on the clock.
