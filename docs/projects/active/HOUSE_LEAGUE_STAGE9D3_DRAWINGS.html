9d-3 drawn: telling families their practice times, and a calendar for house league

Today a house-league family is never told when their child’s team practises, unless a practice later changes. A house-league coach has no calendar at all, although a rep coach has had one since 6d. This round draws the first practice email and the coach’s Subscribe. A family calendar link was drawn too and is withdrawn (see the amendment below). Four push-backs come first, and then seven things the code showed that the prompt did not say.

Amended 2026-10-29: the end state is a signed-in app

  • The owner’s direction: a mobile app where parents, coaches, admins and treasurers all sign in, a parent is linked to their child, and no stop-gap links are built on the way. A no-login family link would be exactly that, so the family calendar link is withdrawn.
  • Withdrawn: asks 9d3·2 and 9d3·8 (kept as grey cards, no checkboxes, numbers unchanged), the A family’s calendar tab, the family half of the Subscribe and Reset windows, the calendar buttons in the first email, and the family-link parts of the gate, build notes, edges and words.
  • Kept as drawn: the first practice email and its edge states, the hand-out-again rule, the coach’s “told on…” line and copy email, who is told, the coach’s own Subscribe, the history row. One detail changed inside them: with no calendar to hold the rest, the email now lists every date.
  • Not decided: native app or installed web app, what a parent sees in it, and which plan it belongs to. Nothing here rules on those.

The answer, before the detail

  • Yes, send a first practice email, and the convenor sends it. It is the last step of the Practice time window: after the hand-out lands, one button, “Send to 99 families”, one email per household. Nothing goes until that press (ask 1). A second hand-out can never move a practice families already know about without the same step (ask 6).
  • The coach gets the same Subscribe a rep coach has, on the Schedule heading’s line of the team page. Games as families last heard them, and practices, from today on, for the live season only (ask 5).
  • The family calendar link is withdrawn. The end state is a signed-in app, so the family half is the app and not a link. What survives is the first email, which lists every date because nothing else holds them yet.
  • One build, the notice first (ask 3). The notice fixes the silent re-hand-out that exists today. The coach’s Subscribe is small and can slide behind it without loss.
  • Everyone with a usable email is told, including a family who unsubscribed from club news, and it never counts toward a cap (ask 4). The ruling that says so is 9c·4, not the ones the prompt names.

What the code showed that the prompt did not say (every flag opens the lines I read)

  • The opt-out rule is 9c·4. The prompt cites 9c·10 and 9c·12 for it. Those are “the coach’s invite goes at Publish” and “moving a child asks first, then emails”. The rule that a family who unsubscribed still gets a notice about their own child is 9c·4, and the sender’s own header says so .
  • Background since the end-state direction. The guardian switch connects a parent to one child and does not touch calendars , and a connected-family calendar for rep teams already exists . Both argued for a family link. With the family half withdrawn they no longer drive a frame.
  • A hand-out is silent, and a second one is destructive. The Practice time window says “Families aren’t emailed”, and running it again deletes the team’s upcoming practices and writes new ones. A family told “Wednesdays at 6:00” can have that moved with nobody told . This is the conflict I name against 9d2·2 (“a practice change emails the team’s families”).
  • An email cannot say “Wednesdays at 6:00”. Teams take turns at the early and late times week by week, so the first practice email lists every date. Until the app exists it is the only place a family reads them .
  • Background: “games only, via the public page” is not free. House league has no public team page, and the public season schedule has no Subscribe . It mattered while a family door was being chosen. It does not now.
  • Today a coach cannot tell whether families know. The team page has no calendar tool and no line saying whether the practices were sent .

Push-backs

What the end state changes

The owner’s direction, 2026-10-29: a mobile app where everyone signs in; public means tournaments and team public pages only; a parent is linked to their child; no stop-gap links; no rush.

What survives

  • The first notice. It stays the way a family hears a time, and may later carry the invitation into the app.
  • The rule that a hand-out never moves a told team silently.

3 · One build, or two?

One build, the notice first. With the family link gone there are two pieces of work and neither needs the other.

The first notice (M)

  • The last step of Practice time, the household list, “left out”, the send.
  • The family email, the coach’s copy, and the coach page’s “families told” line (it reads the record the send writes).
  • The Automatic history row and the “not told yet” count.
  • The re-hand-out rule: a second hand-out reuses the built change email.

The coach’s Subscribe (S)

  • A house-league team’s feed, with one mapper from games and practices into the shared feed words (F1–F10).
  • The coach’s personal link, its Subscribe window and its Reset.
  • One migration: the coach row’s link counter and last-read date.
  • Why the notice first. It fixes something wrong today: a second hand-out silently moves practices families already know (D04). The Subscribe adds a tool and moves nothing that exists, so it can slide behind without loss.
  • What a split costs. Nothing is built twice either way. The two share no code. A split costs a second walk and a second set of records; one build costs a larger walk. I recommend one build, in this order.
  • What changed from the earlier answer. It said “calendars first, so the email is written once”. That reason was the family link, and the link is gone.

4 · Who is told, and can they say no?

Everyone with a usable email, and no. The precedent is 9c·4 (“a family who unsubscribed from club news still gets it”), which was ruled for the placement notice and extended to the moved-team, practice-change and both schedule emails.

  • It is the same kind of message. It says where the family’s own child needs to be. That is the dues-reminder ground the sender’s header names: “news about the family’s own child, so an unsubscribe from club news does not mute it.” It is not marketing-adjacent: there is no offer, no ask, no link to buy.
  • What a family can control: nothing in the email, because it carries no unsubscribe link (same as the other service notices). The footer says why it arrived: “Sent by Dev League Org because your family is registered in Draft Test 2027.”
  • Never counts toward a cap (Q12c), and appears as an Automatic row in the season’s history (9d1·4).
  • Not decided here: whether a service notice to a registered family needs anything under CASL beyond what 9c·4 already relied on. That is counsel’s question, not mine, and it applies equally to the placement email already built.

Carries

  • Every practice from the team, cancelled ones kept and marked, the same as the coach page.

Edges

Each edge says what the person sees. Where a frame already draws it, the card points there.

A household with two children on two teams

  • Handed out the same day: one email with a part for each child, subject “Practices for Twins and Rays” (c4). Counted once in “Will be sent”.
  • Handed out on different days: two emails, each about one team. The second is not “a second first notice”; it is the first for the other team.
  • One team told, one not (after a re-hand-out): one email, a “changed” part for the told team and a “first” part for the other.
  • Two parents with different emails on separate registrations are two households and each gets an email. That is how the built sender already groups.

A practice that changed before the first notice

  • The email reads what is on the table when you press Send, so the family never sees the old time.
  • A practice cancelled before that is left out, never announced.
  • A practice that changes after the notice goes through the club’s Send the changes (built), or a re-hand-out (c2, ask 6).

A team with no practices handed out yet

  • “Tell the families” lists it as not handed out, with the reason, and skips it. The count says so.
  • The coach’s page: no “Practice” row, no told-line; Subscribe is there (b2).

A family with no email, or an unusable one

  • Counted in the window (“2 families have no usable email”) with See them. Not sent.
  • They are not told by this email, so the window lists them with See them, where the convenor can fix the address. Nothing else in this round reaches them. The coach’s copy (c5) counts families sent to, not families reached, so the coach cannot see who was missed. Recorded, Low.
  • If a send fails for an address, the result says “Reached 97 of 99 · 2 didn’t send”, as every other send does.

A family who unsubscribed from club news
