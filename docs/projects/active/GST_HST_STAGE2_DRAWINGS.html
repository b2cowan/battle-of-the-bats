Tax on every quote: what the buyer sees before paying

Turning tax on changes every price we quote. Where our own window charges a card (a plan move, a portal becoming a club), the window shows the amount, the tax by name and what is due today, using Stripe’s own figures for the buyer’s province. Where the buyer goes on to Stripe’s payment page, our screens say “plus applicable taxes” and Stripe’s page shows the tax before they pay. Every figure here is a made-up example worked out at Ontario’s 13% HST. Everything is a proposal until you rule on the asks in the last tab.

What every tax row follows

  • The figure is Stripe’s, never ours. Stripe works the tax out for the buyer’s billing address when the window opens; the card is charged exactly what was shown.
  • The row is named the way Stripe names the tax. “HST 13%” in Ontario, “HST 14%” in Nova Scotia, “HST 15%” in New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador and PEI, “GST 5%” everywhere else until we register for a province’s own tax. A club that typed its GST/HST number still pays it.
  • If Stripe can’t work the tax out, the window doesn’t show a figure without it. It says so in one sentence and offers Check again (tab 3, frame 5).
  • A US address pays no Canadian tax. While US sales are closed it can’t reach a paid button anyway (Stage 1).

Checked against the code before drawing

  • “Tax and Total rows” would give some windows two totals. “Start your club” already ends in Due today, which is the total. The drawing adds one tax row above it rather than a second total.
  • The monthly-or-yearly question was missing from the list. Stage 1 put the “Billed to … Change” line on it so that this stage could hang the tax there. Tab 5 draws it.
  • “Quote the typed club address” needs a second look. The figures load when the window opens, before the coach has touched the address, so a changed province has to load the charge again. And today, while the figures load, the club name and address disappear from the window. The drawing keeps them on screen.
  • Nothing checks the charge against the quote when the button is pressed. Tab 3 frame 4 adds that check: a charge that no longer matches is not made.
  • Two of Part 1’s refusals have no place on the screens yet (“we need to look at your account” and “we couldn’t send your club’s address”). Tab 3 frames 6 and 7 and tab 2 draw where they show.
  • The shipped “couldn’t work out the tax” sentence named the wrong place on two screens (“Check the address on Plan & billing”, where there is no such page yet, or the address is in the same window). Reworded to name no place.

How it behaves

  • Pressing Move works the charge out once more first; if it no longer matches what was shown, nothing is charged and the new figures show (tab 3, frame 4).
  • The tax row’s name and rate come from Stripe’s answer for the club’s billing address. Two taxes (a province we later register for) would be two rows.

  • The renewal row keeps its price and adds “plus applicable taxes” underneath. It doesn’t show a taxed renewal figure: that would be our arithmetic, and the rate can change if the address changes before the renewal.
  • A move down charges nothing today. Its sentence “From then it’s $219 CAD / month.” gains “plus applicable taxes” (tab 7).
