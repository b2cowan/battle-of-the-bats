Each cell is the width of one Sunday column on the Schedules grid, measured by laying the screen out at that width in this page with the product’s own fonts and spacing, and how many lines the longer time, “9:30–11:00 a.m.”, takes. One line is the goal: it makes every slot shorter, so more fields fit on the screen.

What the table says. At every width, moving Fair shares out of the grid’s row (option B) is the biggest single gain, and from 1,366 up it is the only single change that puts the times on one line. The sidebar strip on its own adds 9 to 26 per Sunday on laptops and nothing from about 1,500 up. On a bigger screen it pays only if grids may fill the screen.

    Your calls

    Ruled 2026-10-12: all seven as recommended (“I like fair shares B, other than that I agree with your recommendations”). The rows are on the Decisions tab.

    Ask 1 · Collapse to icons, not away

    RecommendA 64-wide icon strip, as drawn. Hiding the sidebar entirely (with a button or an edge to bring it back) gains 64 more but costs a click on every trip, and an edge that opens on hover fights this exact screen, whose Field column sits against the sidebar.

    Ask 2 · Where the switch sits

    RecommendThe last row of the sidebar, “Collapse sidebar”, as drawn: you set it once per device, and a rare door ends the body. The alternative is a small button at the top of the sidebar.

    Ask 3 · A program’s pages in the strip

    RecommendA program with two or more pages opens a small menu beside its icon on click; a program with one page goes straight there. Hover-only menus are out: they fail on a touch laptop or a tablet held sideways, which gets the desktop layout.

    Ask 4 · May a time grid fill the screen?

    RecommendYes, for screens whose columns are days or times only: this Schedules grid first, then the club Calendar week and the tournament Schedule once each is checked. Reading pages and record tables keep 960 and 1,200. Without this, the strip changes nothing on your screen or anything wider (screen 2).

    Ask 5 · Fair shares: A, B or C

    Recommend BFigures above, teams below. It is the largest gain at every width (+316), has nothing to open or close, keeps the figures in view while dragging, and is what narrow screens already get. A gains 166 and adds a switch; C hides the table and covers the grid to show it.

    Ask 6 · The coach strip’s groups

    RecommendEvery page as an icon with hairlines between groups, as drawn. Every page always sits in the same place, which is how the coach sidebar was built. The cost: the strip is 945 tall and scrolls on a laptop, as today’s sidebar does with every group open (885). The alternative folds a closed group to one small button.

    Ask 7 · Order of work

    RecommendFair shares first, on the Schedules screen, alongside the scheduler stage that is already in its walk. Then the sidebar strip in both sidebars as its own small project. Then the fill width, screen by screen, if you rule yes on Ask 4.

    Product-manager brief

    Sidebar Strip & Grid Width

    Status, 2026-10-16. Fair shares B is built (with the club scheduler’s Stage 11d part B). The sidebar strip is built in every portal: club and tournament, the Premium Coaches Portal and the free Coaches Portal (your call at the build start). On the free portal, while the sidebar is folded, the team’s header shows on team pages so its name stays on screen. Walks are QA §325 on the QA Walk tab. Next: grids that fill the screen, one screen at a time.

    What changes

    • Every portal’s sidebar can collapse to a strip of icons. Club, tournament and Coaches Portal sidebars get a “Collapse sidebar” row at the bottom. Collapsed, the sidebar is a 64-wide strip: the same doors in the same order, waiting counts and chat counts on the icons’ corners, each icon’s name on hover or keyboard focus. A program with several pages opens a small menu from its icon.
    • It is always the person’s choice, remembered on that device. Nothing collapses by itself. Phones and tablets held upright are unchanged; they already use the bottom bar.
    • The Schedules screen gives its grid the room. Fair shares moves out of the grid’s row (option to be ruled; B recommended: figures above the grid, team table below it).
    • If ruled: screens whose columns are days may fill the screen rather than stopping at 1,200.

    Why it matters

    Dense screens are squeezed. On your own screen the Schedules grid gets about 109 per Sunday, so every time wraps onto two lines and only three fields fit above the fold. Measured, the sidebar is only part of the cause: the page already stops at 1,200, and Fair shares takes 316 of it. The fix that helps every customer on every screen is the Fair shares placement; the sidebar strip helps laptop users and, with a fill width, everyone with a bigger screen.

    Customer impact

    Club schedulers and treasurers working on laptops; tournament directors on the schedule timeline; coaches on laptops. A collapsed sidebar affects only the person and device that chose it. Nobody who leaves it alone sees any change except the Fair shares move.

    Priority

    Fair shares rides with the club scheduler work already in its walk. The sidebar strip is a small cross-portal project after it. The fill width is decided screen by screen.

    Success criteria

    • On a 1,366 laptop the Schedules grid shows every time on one line.
    • A first-time user finds “Collapse sidebar” and back again without help.
    • No count, dot, badge or door is lost in the strip, in any portal or role.
    • Nobody who never collapses sees the sidebar move.
    Full assessment

    Sidebar Strip & Grid Width · plan

    The repo copy is docs/projects/active/SIDEBAR_STRIP_AND_GRID_WIDTH_PLAN.md. This tab carries the same plan.

    1. What the product does today (measured)

    • The club and tournament sidebar is one component, 240 wide, pinned under the top strip. The coach sidebar is 248, its width read from one setting that the page padding and the team header also read.
    • Every page centres at one of two widths: 960 for reading, 1,200 for tables and grids. The page area has 32 of padding on each side.
    • The Schedules grid is a 116 facility column plus seven day columns. Fair shares is a fixed 300 card with a 16 gap beside it, dropping below the grid when the window is 1,100 or narrower.
    • Your window measures about 1,530 × 680 (a 1,920 display at 125%).

    F01 — The page stops at 1,200, so a narrower sidebar cannot widen it on bigger screens

    A 1,200 page needs a window of about 1,504 beside the full sidebar. Your window is 1,530, so the page already sits at its limit with 13 to spare on each side. Collapsing the sidebar adds 176 of empty margin and nothing to the grid. The strip pays on its own only on windows up to about 1,440, or anywhere if grids may fill the screen (Ask 4).

    F02 — Fair shares takes 316 beside the grid at every width

    Inside the 1,200 page the grid keeps 884, about 109 per Sunday, and “8:00–9:30 a.m.” wraps in every slot, even on a 24-inch monitor. Option B gives all 316 back at every width.

    2. Scope

    Part 1 — Fair shares placement on the Schedules screen. Part 2 — the sidebar strip in both sidebars (club and tournament; Coaches Portal). Part 3, if ruled — a third page width, “fills the screen”, for time grids only.

    Out of scope. Phones and tablets held upright (bottom bar, unchanged); the public site’s side rail; the volunteer shells; the help guide’s own contents list; the tournament public preview.

    3. Part 2 — the strip, as drawn

    • 64 wide; 40-square targets; 18 icons. Section labels and group headings become hairlines. Every page is an icon, in today’s order.
    • Counts keep their own look: the amber waiting pill and the red chat count on the icon’s corner; the Help dot on the Help icon.
    • An icon’s name shows on hover and on keyboard focus; each icon keeps its name for screen readers.
    • A program with two or more pages opens a menu beside its icon on click (Enter or Space from the keyboard, Escape closes, focus returns to the icon). Inside a rep team or a house-league season, that block sits in the menu too. A one-page program goes straight there.
    • The tournament picker and the coach team picker become initials tiles that open their lists.
    • “Collapse sidebar” is the last row; in the strip it is the expand icon. One setting per device, shared by both portals, kept in the browser. Never automatic (ruled).
    • Layers: the program menu is a menu and sits on top of the sidebar; windows and forms cover the strip exactly as they cover the full sidebar today (the ruled form-covers-nav, menu-on-top order).

    Risks to check in the build

    • Anything pinned to the sidebar’s edge must follow its width. The admin has nothing tied to 240 beyond the sidebar itself (searched); the coach portal reads one setting in three places.
    • The layout sweep baselines need a strip-mode pass; a screen whose content assumed exactly 240 of sidebar shows up there.
    • Demo tours: they start on the default full sidebar, and the strip keeps every door, so tour anchors stay. No change to the demos is needed.
    • Help: the in-app guides say “sidebar” in 39 places. One short section, “Collapse the sidebar”, goes in through /docs.

    4. Part 1 — Fair shares (ruled 2026-10-12: B)

    B (recommended): the four tiles become one joined band, four across, above the toolbar (the money-tab order); a week’s clash or short sentence sits under the toolbar, above the grid; the team table moves to a card under the grid, with the bill door in its title row. A door at the band’s end, “Fair shares by team ↓”, scrolls to it. At 1,100 and narrower, the same. The phone’s By team view is unchanged. A: folded, a 150 column of the four figures ending in Show teams; unfolded, a fold button in the title row. C: the band above the toolbar; the table in the admin’s standard window, opened by an outlined button.

    5. Part 3 — grids that fill the screen (ruled 2026-10-12: yes, time grids only)

    A third page width for screens whose columns are days or times. Candidates, checked one by one before each moves: the club Schedules grid, the club Calendar’s week, the tournament Schedule. Recorded in the exception register as an exception to the two-width rule, with its reason. Record tables keep 1,200.

    6. Asks

    Seven, on the Mockup tab’s screen 10. All ruled 2026-10-12 as recommended; the rows are on the Decisions tab.

    7. Stages

    1. Fair shares on the Schedules screen (with the scheduler stage’s walk) — built 2026-10-16, walks QA §322.
    2. The strip: the club and tournament sidebar, then the coach sidebar, then the free coach sidebar — built 2026-10-16, walks QA §325.
    3. The fill width, one screen at a time (if ruled).

    Each stage: drawing ratified → build → /review → owner walk.

    8. Rollout

    No database change, no migration, no plan gating. The setting lives in the browser. The demo sandboxes open on the full sidebar.

    9. Stage 2 as built (2026-10-16)

    Your calls at the build start, all as recommended: the free Coaches Portal’s sidebar gets the same strip and the same setting; on the free portal, while the sidebar is folded, the team’s header shows on every team page (the sidebar was where a computer read the team’s name there); the build went ahead before “Needs attention clears itself” committed, since the two shared no code.

    How it works. One setting per browser, shared by every portal. It is applied before the page first appears, so a collapsed sidebar is collapsed on a reload with no flicker. The full sidebar is unchanged apart from its new last row. Phones and upright tablets are unchanged.

    Where the build departs from the drawings

    1. Organization’s menu starts with the Organization page itself. Its full-sidebar row opens that page, which is not one of its listed pages, so the drawn menu would have lost it.
    2. A tournament-only account’s Account rows show icons in the strip (the ones the phone’s More menu gives them). The full sidebar still lists them by name.
    3. House league inside a season opens a menu: Seasons, the season’s five pages, and its season switcher as “Other seasons” rows (only with two or more seasons).
    4. Page counts sit on the menu’s rows, as they do on the full sidebar’s page rows; an open program keeps its count on its icon.
    5. A thin line sits under the Premium coach’s team tile: the full sidebar’s divider, narrowed. The drawing goes straight to Overview.
    6. The free sidebar’s team block (name, status, next event) shows in the header band on team pages while folded. A one-team coach’s portal home page shows no team name while folded; widening it would make the header jump in after the page loads. Your call if wanted.
    7. The tile’s letters: an age-group first word stands (13U), otherwise the initials of the first two words (SC).
    8. The layout sweep accepts the corner counts’ overhang (the drawn 7 past the icon) once, with its reason, instead of once per screen.
