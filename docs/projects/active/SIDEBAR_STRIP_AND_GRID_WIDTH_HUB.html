4 of 15 ·what each change is worth

Every width, measured

Each cell is the width of one Sunday column on the Schedules grid, measured by laying the screen out at that width in this page with the product’s own fonts and spacing, and how many lines the longer time, “9:30–11:00 a.m.”, takes. One line is the goal: it makes every slot shorter, so more fields fit on the screen.

What the table says. At every width, moving Fair shares out of the grid’s row (option B) is the biggest single gain, and from 1,366 up it is the only single change that puts the times on one line. The sidebar strip on its own adds 9 to 26 per Sunday on laptops and nothing from about 1,500 up. On a bigger screen it pays only if grids may fill the screen.

    Your calls

    Ruled 2026-10-12: all seven as recommended (“I like fair shares B, other than that I agree with your recommendations”). The rows are on the Decisions tab.

    Ask 1 · Collapse to icons, not away

    RecommendA 64-wide icon strip, as drawn. Hiding the sidebar entirely (with a button or an edge to bring it back) gains 64 more but costs a click on every trip, and an edge that opens on hover fights this exact screen, whose Field column sits against the sidebar.

    Ask 2 · Where the switch sits

    RecommendThe last row of the sidebar, “Collapse sidebar”, as drawn: you set it once per device, and a rare door ends the body. The alternative is a small button at the top of the sidebar.

    Ask 3 · A program’s pages in the strip

    RecommendA program with two or more pages opens a small menu beside its icon on click; a program with one page goes straight there. Hover-only menus are out: they fail on a touch laptop or a tablet held sideways, which gets the desktop layout.

    Ask 4 · May a time grid fill the screen?

    RecommendYes, for screens whose columns are days or times only: this Schedules grid first, then the club Calendar week and the tournament Schedule once each is checked. Reading pages and record tables keep 960 and 1,200. Without this, the strip changes nothing on your screen or anything wider (screen 2).

    Ask 5 · Fair shares: A, B or C

    Recommend BFigures above, teams below. It is the largest gain at every width (+316), has nothing to open or close, keeps the figures in view while dragging, and is what narrow screens already get. A gains 166 and adds a switch; C hides the table and covers the grid to show it.

    Ask 6 · The coach strip’s groups

    RecommendEvery page as an icon with hairlines between groups, as drawn. Every page always sits in the same place, which is how the coach sidebar was built. The cost: the strip is 945 tall and scrolls on a laptop, as today’s sidebar does with every group open (885). The alternative folds a closed group to one small button.

    Ask 7 · Order of work
