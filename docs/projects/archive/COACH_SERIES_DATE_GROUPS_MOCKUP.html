A weekly series' dates, grouped by what they have to say

The same move as the permit's price rows: say a thing once, with everything it applies to beside it. Here the list is something the coach edits (any date can be removed), so each date stays one tap.

1What the code does today

One row per date, 56px eachEvery date is a row with a 44px remove button. Thirteen Wednesdays are about 800px of list; a fall-to-spring season of 30 is about 1,700px.
A date with nothing to say repeats the start time"6:00 p.m." on 9 of the 13 rows: the start time typed a few lines above, on every row.
A marked date says its note on its own row"Outside the club’s permit" four times in a row. The sentence under Venue already says "4 of 13 dates are outside…" with what the permit covers, but it is below the list, so the coach scrolls past every row to reach it.
The list sits above Venue, though its marks come from VenueIt sits in the When part of the form, under Arrival. Its marks only exist once a venue is picked, so they appear above the box the coach just used: on a phone, off the screen. On a game series it also sits above Opponent, the box that fills any date left blank.
Games are differentA game series' rows each hold an Opponent box. That list is a form (one answer per date), so it can't group. Its rows stay; only where the list sits changes (section 5).

6The asks

Group a practice series' dates by what they have to say; each date a chip you tap to remove.
What changes
  • One group per note (outside the permit, a date off, each booking that holds the place), then The other dates.
  • Each date is a chip with a plain ×, shaped like a selected tag: tap to remove it, tap again to put it back. 32px on a computer, 44px on a phone or tablet.
  • A date with nothing to say no longer repeats the start time.
  • With no notes at all, the list is the count and one set of chips, with no group heading.
Applies to
Add with Repeat weekly, for practices and every other type without an opponent. A game series keeps a row per date, since each row holds its own Opponent box. So does Import a schedule's preview: each of its rows carries its own time, place and opponent. Every date still shows its verdict (through its group), which is what the 2026-07-31 ruling asks of both previews.
Not this
A literal copy of the price rows, one row per run of weeks ("Oct 28 – Nov 18 · 4 practices"). It reads well, but removing one date means opening the run first, and the list stops showing the dates themselves. Showing the coach the actual dates before anything is written is why this list exists.
Groups with a note come first (by their first date); The other dates comes last.
Why
The marked dates are the ones the coach has to decide about. In your screenshot they happen to be first anyway; a clash in January would otherwise sit below nine plain dates.
The alternative
Every group by its first date, the rule the permit's price rows use. Predictable, but a price isn't something to act on and a clash is.
A marked group has one door: “Remove these 4” (“Put these 4 back” once they are all out).
Why
The four Wednesdays outside the permit are usually one decision, not four. The line under Venue already says "Remove them" for a clash; this puts that door next to the dates it means.
The alternative
No group door: four taps instead of one. Fewer controls, and still fine for a group of one or two.
A series edit's list (This & future, All) groups the same way, with each group's dates said as words.
Why
It is the same list, read-only. Grouping it the same way keeps one shape for a series; words instead of chips because nothing there can be tapped.
Words
"Tap a date to remove it", "The other dates", "Remove these 4" and "Put these 4 back" are new and go to /marketing before the build. Every verb is today's: remove, and put back.
The list moves below Venue: the last thing before More and Add. On a game series, below Opponent and Home / Away too.
Why
The list adds up everything above it: the dates come from the When boxes and each date's mark comes from Venue. Above Venue, the marks appear over the box the coach just picked from, and on a phone they are already scrolled out of view. Below it, the form reads in the order it is filled (when, where, who, then what that makes), and the review sits right above the button that commits it.
What it costs
Changing the First or Last date updates a list a few lines further down instead of right under the box. The Add button is pinned and names the new count as you type ("Add 13 practices"), so the instant answer stays on screen.
On a game series
The Opponent box is what any date left blank is saved with, yet today it comes after the dates. Moved, it comes first. Proposed with it, and separable: a blank date's box shows that opponent in grey as its hint, so leaving a date blank says what it does.
Also
A series edit's list (This & future, All) moves the same way. Unchanged: the line stays directly under Venue (Stage 6's one way to say where, on every form), and a single event has no list.
Not this
Moving Venue up above the dates instead. The list depends on both, so it would still belong after both.
The permit sentence under Venue never says when a part of the permit starts or ends.
What it does
It names the days and hours a permit covers, and its last day. When part of a permit has its own dates, a Wednesday at 6:00 p.m. can be marked outside next to "It covers Wed, 6:00–9:00 p.m.", and the coach can't tell why. Checked after drawing this: your screenshot was not this case. That permit covers Tue and Thu, so its four Wednesdays are simply outside.
If you want it
Add the part's dates to the sentence ("It covers Wed, 6:00–9:00 p.m., from Nov 25 until Mar 28, 2027"). A separate small change; not drawn here.
