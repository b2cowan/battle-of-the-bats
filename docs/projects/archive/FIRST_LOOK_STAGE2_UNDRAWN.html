The states Stage 2 never drew

Stage 2 (the wait and the way back) was drawn and ruled on 2026-10-19, but the drawings stopped short of eight states the build would need. Each is drawn below at true phone size (390 × 844, the dashed line is where the screen ends) beside what the product does today, with a recommendation you can accept or change. The screens already drawn and ruled (the verification email, the Continue page, the expired-link notice, plain sign-in, a waiting account) are in the first-look hub and are not redrawn.

A wrong password

Today the red box says one sentence and offers nothing beside it; the only way to a reset is the small line under the form. Red stays for this case (D-F9: nothing else is red).

Stage 2 · U2 · sign-in

An address that already has an account

When someone starts a sign-up with an address that already has an account, they are sent to sign-in with the email filled in. That hand-off keeps working; today it arrives with no word of why.

U2A word of why on the hand-off

The hand-off exists so nobody makes a second account; the notice says so in one line.

What rides on it: only the sign-in screen when it is reached from a sign-up
Stage 2 · U3 · the Continue page

A Continue link that does not work

Ask 8 ruled that a broken link goes to the sign-in notice. This carries that to the Continue page: no screen of its own, and no “Create account” button that opens the organization door whatever door the person came from.

U3The Continue page’s broken link

Fewer screens, and the one sentence reads the same wherever a link fails.

What rides on it: whether a person can tap Create account from a dead link; they cannot after this
Stage 2 · U4 · forgot and reset password

Forgot password and the two reset screens

Six screens, all in the start column and type. An expired or used reset link opens the forgot screen with the amber notice, so it needs no screen of its own. The tap page is the Continue page again: it exists so a mail scanner cannot spend the one-time link, and it stays.

U4The forgot and reset screens

Six screens instead of seven states: an expired link borrows the forgot screen.

What rides on it: the words on all six screens; sending the email and the destination after saving do not change
Stage 2 · U5 · the password-reset email

The password-reset email

Named in Ask 1 and never drawn. It wears the same warm envelope as the verification email (drawn in the hub at 600px). It has no first name: the platform sends it to an address, not a person.

U5The reset email’s words

The button matches the tap page (“Reset my password”), as the verification email’s matches the Continue page.

What rides on it: the email text only; the template stays customisable with its single variable
Stage 2 · U6 · the verification email

What the verification email says comes next, for every door

The hub drew one sentence, for Start a team. The email is sent from six doors. The old email named whatever team or organization had been typed; this one names the next step instead, so the typed name drops out.

U6The next-step sentence at each door

The person has just left a screen that promised something; the email should keep the promise.

What rides on it: whether the email can show a name; the second option needs the build to carry the typed name through
Stage 2 · U7 · sign-in

What “Email me a new link” says

The button is drawn; what it says afterwards is not. It sends a fresh link only when the address is still waiting to be confirmed. For a link that was already used, the account is confirmed and nothing is sent, and the system answers the same either way on purpose so nobody can test which addresses have accounts. So the words after it cannot promise a link.

U7The words after “Email me a new link”

A plain “Sent” would be untrue for a used link: the person would wait for an email that never comes.

What rides on it: whether a person is told something false; the notice above keeps “Sign in” first either way
Stage 2 · U8 · the emails

On a phone, and in a dark mail app

The hub drew the email at 600px only. Most people open it on a phone, and some mail apps recolour a light email in dark mode. The second picture is a simulation of one such recolouring, not a screenshot: the build checks the real apps (Gmail, Apple Mail, Outlook) with the real email.

U8Dark mail apps

Apps that honour a light-only request keep the card light; others recolour it. Either way it must stay readable.

What rides on it: one layout or two; the first keeps the single warm envelope The public face will migrate onto
