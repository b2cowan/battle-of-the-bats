# Session prompt — PDF Export Quality, Phase 2: POSTERS, CARDS & BRACKETS

**A BUILD session. Open in a fresh chat. Written 2026-08-25, after the Schedules pass shipped
(commit `bbed53c6`, Owner QA §102).**

This is **pass 6 of 6 — the last group.** Phase 2 works the six document groups one pass per
session; when this one has owner sign-off, only **Phase 3** (the rendered check wired into the
verify pipeline) remains and the programme closes.

**⚠ This group's definition of good is *"big type, high contrast, one job"*.** These four documents
are not read — they are **glanced at, in the open air, while something is happening**: a poster
taped inside a dugout, a card handed to an umpire, a bracket pinned to a fence that a parent
photographs. Nobody sits down with any of them. If a fact cannot be read from two metres away or
found in one second, it has failed, however complete it is.

**The four documents:** the lineup poster, the batting-order card, the playoff bracket, and the
blank bracket (print-and-fill).

## ⚠⚠ THE HEADLINE IS STRUCTURAL, AND I CONFIRMED IT IN THE CODE BEFORE WRITING THIS

**These are the only documents in the product that never received Phase 1's identity pipeline, and
two of them are literally incapable of carrying it today.**

Phase 1's whole promise was that every PDF knows whose paper it is. Five passes have made that true
for every table-engine document and for the drawn family statement, run sheet and board summary.
But:

- **The lineup poster and the batting-order card have NO CREST AND NO CLUB NAME AT ALL** — not
  mis-drawn, *absent*. Their options contract takes only `accentColor` and `showBranding`. It has no
  `logoDataUrl`, no header lines, no footer text, no date stamp. They cannot be branded without a
  contract change, which is why five passes went past them.
- **The bracket draws its crest into a hard-coded `20 × 10` mm rectangle** — a fixed 2∶1 box. That
  single line is the whole "squashed crest" defect: any square club logo is stretched to double
  width. It never calls `drawLogoSlot`, which the engine rules name as **the one aspect-fit crest
  helper**, and it hand-rolls its own header band instead of `drawIdentityBand`.

So the first real question of this pass is not cosmetic. It is: **do the poster and card join the
identity pipeline (taking resolved settings like every other document), or is a dugout poster
deliberately unbranded because the team already knows who it is?** There is a real argument either
way and it is the owner's to make — but it must be made deliberately, not inherited from an options
interface nobody revisited.

## What rendering them already turned up — your checkpoint-1 starting material

These are findings, not decisions. Bring each to the owner with a recommendation and the honest case
against it. **All four were rendered through the real exporters on 2026-08-25 and looked at.**

### The bracket (and the blank bracket)

1. ⚠ **THE BRACKET DOES NOT DRAW ITS FIRST CONNECTOR.** QF3+QF4 join to SF2, and SF1+SF2 join to
   FIN — but **QF1 and QF2 have no line joining them to SF1 at all.** On a document whose entire job
   is showing who plays whom next, one quarter of the structure is missing. It is unmistakable on
   the blank bracket, where there is nothing else to look at. **This is the biggest defect in the
   group and nobody has ever recorded it.**
2. **Roughly half the page is empty.** The bracket floats in the upper-middle with a deep band of
   dead space above the first match box and below the last. On a document meant to be read from a
   fence, everything could be substantially bigger for free. (This is the "vertical centring" item
   the plan parked — it is real, and it is more about *scale* than about centring.)
3. **The write-on lines on the blank bracket are barely visible** — hairline grey, at the far right
   of each row. The Working-sheets pass already settled the principle for exactly this case: a
   column somebody fills in by hand gets **a real drawn box** to aim a pen at, because a cell with
   no edges gives a volunteer nothing to target. The blank bracket is the purest fill-in-by-hand
   document in the product and it never got that treatment.
4. **It has no title block.** Every other document prints a bold report title with the subtitle
   under it; the bracket folds "U13 — Playoff Bracket · Riverdale Summer Classic 2026" into the
   identity band's second line, so the division and event read as quiet metadata rather than as the
   thing the page is about.
5. **The blank bracket never says it is blank**, and offers nowhere to hand-write the division, the
   date, or which bracket it is — which is the entire point of printing an empty one.

### The lineup poster

6. **No crest, no club name, no date stamp** (see the headline above). Its footer says only
   "Generated by FieldLogicHQ" — the one document where the branding line is the *only* thing in the
   footer.
7. **The position legend shreds mid-phrase.** It wraps to a second line and breaks
   "Blank box = fill in / at the field" across the wrap, so the second line reads as an orphaned
   fragment at the left margin.
8. **A blank cell and a bench cell are the same silence.** "BN" prints in light grey and an
   unassigned inning prints as nothing; the legend explains the difference in small type at the
   foot, but at two metres, in a dugout, both read as "empty". ⚠ Consider whether the *blank* — the
   cell a coach fills in at the field — should get the same drawn box the check-in sheet got.

### The batting-order card

9. ⚠ **IT IS NOT A CARD.** It is a full portrait letter page carrying twelve names and roughly a
   third of a page of white space beneath them. A batting-order card is a thing you hand to an
   umpire and an opposing coach — usually more than one copy. **Ask what this document actually is**
   before styling it: half-page, three-up on a sheet, or a genuine card. This is the group's second
   real design question and the plan does not raise it.
10. **It carries no positions at all** — batting order and jersey number only. Whether an umpire's
    copy needs starting positions is a domain question for the owner, not a styling one.
11. **The bench/substitutes are missing from it** even though the data carries `isSub`, and there is
    a third of a page of room where they would naturally go.

## Engine rules you now inherit (§82 + §84 + §86 + §91 + §102)

- **`drawLogoSlot` is the ONE aspect-fit crest helper**, and **`drawIdentityBand` is the ONE header
  band**. ⚠ **The bracket currently reinvents both — that is finding #1's root cause and this pass
  is where it gets fixed.** A drawn document must not hand-roll a third copy.
- **A column heading is measured in the BOLD face it prints in, and is never capped.** A heading
  broken mid-word is a bug. **Cells may wrap at a space; headings may not shred.** Do not "fix" a
  wrapping cell by widening the floor rule.
- **A fixed-column report must fit by construction** — the "didn't fit this page" line is reserved
  for customer-shaped tables. Seeing it on a fixed-column report is a bug.
- **Continuation pages carry the identity band**, universally, via a per-page hook plus a reserved
  top margin. A DRAWN document does its own.
- **A grouped document's SECTION carries its name onto any page it spills onto**, marked
  "(continued)", and a section heading cannot strand itself at the foot of a page (§102). The
  heading's height is derived from the offsets it draws with — do not reintroduce a magic number.
- **All shared-header sections of one grouped document share ONE pinned column grid** (§102) —
  pinned only when every column can have its full width; an over-subscribed table keeps the existing
  squeeze, because pinning it to the no-shred floor shredded team names and grew a page.
- **A report may declare `penColumns`** — columns the reader fills in by hand print an empty box
  (§91). Opt-in. ⚠ **The blank bracket is the obvious next customer for that idea** (finding #3),
  though it is drawn rather than table-engine, so it needs the same *principle*, not the same code.
- **One measurement pass**: floors and grid demands are measured together (§102). If you need a new
  per-column measurement, extend that pass rather than adding a second walk.
- **The scanner flags the family-statements batch file `FOOTER✗` BY DESIGN** — page numbers restart
  per family. It is the one expected red line in a full-corpus scan; do not "fix" it, and do not let
  it mask a real footer failure on YOUR documents.

## Lessons from §102 and its /review that will save you a defect

- ⚠⚠ **AN EXHIBIT BUILT FROM A FIXTURE YOU WROTE IS EVIDENCE ABOUT YOUR FIXTURE.** The Schedules
  prompt claimed the date column repeated "Fri, Jul 10"; production actually printed the **stored**
  `2026-07-31`, because the previous session's exhibit was hand-written and never called the code
  the screen calls. Two of that pass's five defects were invisible until the fixture was rebuilt
  from the page's own row-builder. **Feed these renderers the shape the real screen hands them.**
- ⚠⚠ **A TEST THAT CANNOT FAIL IS NOT COVERAGE.** Verify every new test by breaking production and
  watching it go red — every time. §102 ran fourteen such mutations; two of them exposed tests that
  would otherwise have proved nothing.
- ⚠ **The empty/absent case is where the defects live.** §102's `/review` found a day heading that
  printed as bare separator dots because a playoff game had no date yet — found by **two independent
  lenses**, missed by every human read. **These four documents are full of that shape**: a bracket
  before any game is played, a lineup with nine players instead of twelve, a card with no opponent
  set, a poster with no notes. Probe them.
- ⚠ **Measure and draw must share one source.** Two copies of the same arithmetic disagreeing is
  this programme's most repeated bug (the run sheet's overflow, the group heading's height).
- ⚠ **Prove any shared-engine change is free by diffing full-corpus scans** — render the whole
  corpus with the pre-change renderer and with yours and diff the scanner output. §86, §91 and §102
  all did this; §102's cleanup was proved **byte-identical** across 78 documents, which is the only
  reason a tidy-up touching the shared engine was allowed to land. ⚠ **When a diff DOES appear,
  attribute it before defending it** — §102's corpus showed two changed rows and only one was that
  pass's (the other was uncommitted run-sheet work from a concurrent session).
