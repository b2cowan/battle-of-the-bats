Coach portal · Money forms review · walk two · 2026-08-30

The dues forms, read from the code

The sponsorship cluster was walked and rebuilt (§121). This is the same first pass over the Player Dues cluster: the bulk generator, the single-player schedule editor, Add a credit, the payment correction and the season-settlement refund sheet.

Eleven carried findings were re-checked against today's tree — three are gone or downgraded, and eight new ones surfaced, two of them on the write path. Where a number appears it was measured on the live dev server at the width it claims, or produced by executing the product's own arithmetic. Where the first pass overstated something, this page says so.

00Before you walk — what changed under the carried list

01The floor under the schedule editors

Two write-path defects, both on the doors that change what a family owes — the single-player Edit schedule form and the bulk generator. Neither is visible on screen; both were produced by running the product's own arithmetic.

Why this section is first The review's sharpest finding so far was SP-1 — a sponsor edit shrinking a credit that had already been handed back in cash. That hole was closed on the fundraising side five days ago, and a shared guard now exists for exactly this question. Neither dues-schedule door asks it.

D-12 · A dues change credits the family, and the next dues change credits them again

When a coach lowers a player's total below what the family has already sent, the difference is saved as an overpayment credit — correct, and stated out loud. But those credits are written standalone, and every later reconcile only looks at credits attached to a payment. So the credit it created a moment ago is invisible to itself.

Run the product's own reconcile three times over one family who paid $1,200 in full: 

A · the coach lowers the total twice
   lower to $800   →  credits: [$400]                      total credit  $400
   lower to $600   →  credits: [$400, $600]                total credit $1,000
   true excess at a $600 total                                            $600
                                                     over-credited by $400

B · the coach puts the total back
   lower to $800   →  credits: [$400]                      total credit  $400
   restore $1,200  →  credits: [$400]                      total credit   $400
   true excess at a $1,200 total                                            $0
                              a family keeps $400 off a bill they owe in full

C · control — the same reconcile on a RECORD-TIME overpayment
   family overpays →  credits: [$400 · linked to the receipt]
   restore $1,200  →  credits: [ ]                                      correct

C is the proof this is a seam and not a design: the identical arithmetic is right when the credit carries a receipt and wrong when it does not. The unit test that covers this exercises the pure function in isolation — where the stale credit is passed in by hand — so it passes while the query that feeds it returns nothing.

Both doors are affected, and the bulk one reports the wrong figure to the coach as well: re-run the generator at a lower total twice and its success state announces the full excess as "saved as overpayment credits" the second time too.

D-13 · Raising a total can delete the credit that a cash payout was standing on

A family overpays by $200; the coach hands the $200 back in cash. Correcting the schedule total upward afterwards makes that credit stale, and the reconcile deletes it — leaving the books saying the family was owed nothing and the family holding two hundred dollars.

This is the same hole as SP-1, on a door nobody looked at. The refusal already exists, in a shared module, with its sentence written: "$200.00 has already been paid out to this family — … would leave the books owing them less than they have received. Remove the payout first." Three doors speak it today. These two do not call it, and neither route reads the payout table at all.

04Edit schedule — one player

The single-player twin of the generator, reached from Edit schedule in a player's drawer (or Set dues schedule when there is none). It replaces the drawer contents in place. Every habit its bulk sibling has, this one lacks — and it is the form a coach reaches for when a family needs something different from everyone else, which is exactly when care matters most.

D-1Stands
Both of its doors are shown to a read-only money assistant, and only the server stops them. Precisely two controls, both inside the drawer: Edit schedule in the actions row and Set dues schedule in the no-schedule empty state. The tab's own toolbar and its empty state gate correctly, and so does every payment and payout control beside them — which is what makes these two read as an oversight rather than a policy.
D-2Stands · measured
No heading, and the sum rule arrives as a scolding. Opened at 360px the form reads, in order: TOTAL AMOUNT * · NOTES · INSTALLMENTS · #1 · #2 · #3 — nothing says what this form is or whose schedule it is beyond the drawer's own title bar.

The installments-must-equal-total rule speaks only after Save, as "Installments sum ($800.00) must equal total ($1,200.00)". Its bulk sibling runs the same comparison live, in plain words, before the coach has committed to anything.

D-3Re-framed · measured
The phone is fine; the desktop is the problem. The first pass said the rows "crush at phone width". They do not — at 360px the amount box measures 112px and the date 157px, tight but working.

On a 1280px desktop the same row renders an amount box of 112px beside a date input of 809px measured — the date field is seven times the width of the money field, because one is pinned and the other is told to fill. That is the composition defect, and it is on the wide screen, not the small one.

D-15New · Med
One stray click outside the drawer throws the whole schedule away, with no warning. Typed a five-installment hardship plan, clicked the backdrop: measured the drawer closed, no confirmation appeared, and reopening showed the old figures.

The bulk generator — the identical act for the whole roster — refuses to close on "You haven't saved what you entered on this form. Leave without saving it?" and guards against navigating away as well.

D-16New · Med-Low · measured
The remove-installment control is a 21×21px "×" with no accessible name, and + Add installment measures 119×25px — both well under the 44px tap floor that holds to 768px across this portal. They survive because the layout gate cannot open a drawer, so nothing has ever measured them.

07Set refund — the settlement's per-family sheet

Season settlement → a family's row. Three choices for what this family takes out of the surplus, plus forgiving a balance, plus undoing that. Correctly gated, correctly scoped, and the forgiveness arithmetic is derived rather than typed. Its three findings are all about what it says.

D-9Stands · High
A failed save shows nothing at all. The error is written into the settlement window's own error slot, which is behind this sheet. On screen the Save button simply stops spinning and the sheet stays open — the coach's only signal is that nothing happened.

This is the one form in the product where a refusal is invisible, and it sits on the screen that hands families money.

D-10Stands · Med-High
A blank or garbled "set amount" saves as $0.00, no warning, no refusal — the field is read as a number, and anything that isn't one becomes zero. On this sheet zero means a family takes nothing home.
D-11Stands · worse than written
Only the option already saved states its dollars — and it stops the moment you touch another one. Pick "no share" on a family currently taking an even share and the even card's "$210 — $150 owed back plus one of 4 shares" is replaced by "Puts Riley back in the split with everyone else". The one figure the sheet exists to compare vanishes exactly when the coach starts comparing.
D-19New · Low
The three options are plain buttons with no selected state exposed. Sighted coaches see the tint; a screen reader hears three buttons and no answer to "which one is chosen".

  4. D · The words commit (per Q20). One name for the bulk act everywhere; the capitalization sweep (D-17); the correction's heading (D-8b); the credit form's default date (D-18).
