00

The summary block — the ladder

Once an estimate and expected funding both exist, the top of this page has six numbers to carry: line items, estimated total, difference, expected funding, funded by players, per player. A row of equal tiles was the wrong instrument — it presents six unrelated statistics and then needs a caption under each one explaining how it relates to its neighbour. They aren't unrelated. They're one sum, worked downward:

Line items + the part of the estimate you haven't itemized = total planned budget − expected funding = what players fund ÷ the roster = per player.

Both frames below show the same team in the fullest state — a $10,000 estimate, $8,000 itemized, $4,000 of expected funding, 10 players. Teams with no estimate and no funding lose those rows entirely and are left with three.

The ladder Chosen 2026-08-12

The plan Line items 6 lines $8,000.00 Not itemized yet — from your $10,000.00 estimate Edit $2,000.00 Total planned budget $10,000.00 Expected funding 2 lines −$4,000.00
$600.00 per player $6,000.00 funded by players ÷ 10 on the roster

Reads top to bottom as the arithmetic it is, so nothing needs a caption explaining it — the position of a number is its explanation. The one number a coach acts on, per player, is the biggest and sits at the bottom where a total belongs. Collapses gracefully: no estimate drops row two, no funding drops row four, and a plain team sees three lines. Identical on a phone — it's already a single column, where tiles wrap into that ragged shape you spotted.

This block is stated once. It replaces the tile bar everywhere below; the plan's foot keeps a plain grand total and nothing else.

Budget, estimate, difference — stated, not inferred

Today the screen shows Total Planned Budget and Season Total side by side, and the top number is quietly "whichever is bigger". Nothing says what the second one is for. After this change the top of the page states all three facts plainly and in order: Budget — the sum of the lines below — then an optional Estimated total, then the Difference between them. No estimate, no difference tile.

02

Expected funding — budget the money that isn't dues

A budget line can now be a cost or expected funding: money the team plans to bring in from fundraising, a sponsor, or a club grant. Costs less expected funding is funded by players, and that — not the gross cost — is what drives per player and the installment generator. Budget $4,000 of fundraising against an $8,000 season and every player's dues drop from $800 to $400.

03

The plan, in month or quarter columns

Month columns exist today, but only on the Budget vs. Actual tab, mixed in with actuals — which is why the plan itself feels like it hasn't got them. This puts a List / By period toggle on the Budget Plan tab and adds quarters, reading straight from the period splits coaches already enter on each line.

Settled, and what's left open

Settled 2026-08-12: the ladder is the summary block; the estimate is optional and named Estimated total; the un-itemized difference is calculated, never typed; per player and dues follow the estimate whenever one is set, including when the lines run over it; expected funding is a plain total that doesn't link to individual campaigns, and its actual is the team's share.

  1. Where funding sits in the season's cash flow. Expected funding reduces total dues, but the money often arrives mid-season while costs land early. The installment generator will keep proposing even installments unless you want it to shape them around when funding is expected.
  2. Existing teams. A stored Season Total becomes that team's Estimated total, untouched — the difference simply starts being shown. Teams currently sitting below their own stored total see a red difference for the first time, rather than having it overridden.
