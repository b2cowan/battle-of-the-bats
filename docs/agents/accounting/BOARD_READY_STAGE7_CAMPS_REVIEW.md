**Last verified:** 2026-11-24 (our side from existing files; the club-side words from CRA pages read today)

## Question(s) this answers

## 1. Our books: nothing new (confirms the Stage 6 file, `BOARD_READY_STAGE6_SPONSOR_PAYMENT_FEE_REVIEW.md`)
- Same fee, same method (R1): base is our application fee only, tax inside it, place of supply is the CLUB's billing-address province (we supply the club). The camp's own tax, the family's province and the camp's location do not enter our base.
- Camp payments must write the same per-fee record as a family payment and a sponsor payment (source kind `camp`, who paid the fee, paid_at, livemode, province, rate, tax in cents). Stage 5's record is still not built; a camp table that skips it drops fee revenue and tax from lines 101/103.
- Stripe: direct charge, `application_fee_amount`, NO `automatic_tax` or tax rates on a camp session. The club's tax (if Taxable) is inside the amount the product computes.
- Never print our name or GST/HST number as supplier on a camp page, receipt or invoice. A footer credit is fine (Part 3a ruling).
- A club's refund on withdrawal does not refund our fee (only a returned bank debit does), so no credit note from us. If the owner ever rules a withdrawing family gets its card fee back, that is a product ruling for `/strategy`, and a refunded fee's tax reverses only with a credit record.
- Margin note (not tax): the fixed $1.50 falls on every charge. One payment per sign-up, not one per session, keeps the club's cost and our per-fee records fewer. Early-bird changes only the amount owed; the fee is worked out on the amount owed.

## 2. The per-camp Exempt / Taxable words (club's tax; product research)
**What the rules are.**
- Exemption: Excise Tax Act Sch. V **Part VI s.12** (supplies by a public sector body, which includes a non-profit organization or charity). Text as quoted in a CRA ruling (taxinterpretations.com node 606102; Justice Laws would not load): a membership in, or services as part of, "a program established and operated by the body that consists of a series of supervised instructional classes or activities involving athletics, outdoor recreation, music, dance, arts, crafts or other hobbies" where (a) it may reasonably be expected the program will be provided **primarily to children 14 years of age or under, except where the program involves overnight supervision throughout a substantial portion of the program**; or (b) it is provided primarily for underprivileged individuals or individuals with a disability.
- GI-037 (Dec 2014; page dated 2021-12-13): "primarily" generally means more than 50%. Day camp mainly 14 and under: exempt. Mainly over 14, day or overnight: taxable. Overnight supervision: generally taxable regardless of age (volunteer supervision counts). Underprivileged or disability: exempt even overnight, any age. GI-037 cites no Schedule V section.
- ⚠ The brief's "Part VI = the child-care exemption" mixes two things. **Child care is Part IV s.1** (Memorandum 21-1, Dec 2019): primary purpose is care and supervision of children 14 and under for periods normally under 24 hours; any supplier; instruction in an athletic or recreational activity is NOT child care. A camp usually relies on Part VI s.12 (the club is a public sector body), not Part IV.
- Two edges the plan's "overnight = taxable" shorthand loses: the statute says overnight supervision "throughout a substantial portion" (a CRA ruling kept a program with some overnight off-site camps exempt); and s.12 needs a "series", so a one-session clinic is an accountant question.
- Supplier must be a public sector body. A for-profit operator is outside s.12 (a full-day non-overnight camp may still be child care under Part IV s.1).

**Words (static, plain brand voice; brand wording left to `/marketing`).**
- Under the switch (default Exempt): "Day camps and clinics for children 14 and under are usually not charged tax. Choose Taxable only if your accountant says this one is."
- Ages include 15 or older (computed from birth year at the camp's start; wording says "most", because it is a 50% test on children, not on birth years): "Some of these birth years are 15 or older. A camp is tax-free only when most of its children are 14 or under. If most will be 15 and older, choose Taxable."
- Overnight = yes: "Camps with sleepovers are usually taxable, at any age. If the children sleep over for much of the camp, choose Taxable. One overnight trip inside a day camp may not be; ask your accountant." Warn, never block; do not flip the default (the ruled default is Exempt).
- Optional clause: "Camps made for children with a disability, or for families who need help, are tax-free at any age."
- Safe: "usually", "choose Taxable only if your accountant says". Not safe: "you must / need not charge", "child care" as a label (it implies Part IV and the parent's claim), "tax receipt", "donation".
- Shown only to a registered club (a small supplier neither charges nor registers).

**Rate and place of supply (differs from our fee).** A camp is a service rendered to the child in the child's presence, so Memorandum 3-3-6-1 (April 2026) applies: personal-service place-of-supply rules (New Harmonized Value-added Tax System Regulations ss.16 to 18). The camp is in one place, so the rate is **the province where the camp is held**, never the family's address. I read the multi-province rules (primarily performed in a participating province, then highest rate, then supplier's closest address); the single-province rule I did not see in the text read, but it is the starting rule. A camp that moves province or has a big out-of-province leg: accountant.

## 3. The family receipt (line 21400)
- CRA says the individual or organization that provided the care "must give you a receipt indicating the services provided"; if an individual provided it, the receipt shows that individual's SIN; the receipt can be in the claimant's or the spouse's name; the folio (S1-F3-C1 para 1.47) says made out to the individual who paid. Nothing is sent with the return; keep it.
- CRA's list of what a daycare receipt must include (its page is written for home daycares, so by analogy for an organization): payer's name, the child's name, the amount received, the period (from and to dates), the provider's name and address, the provider's SIN (individual only), signature, date signed. One receipt per child.
- **Minimum fields:** paid-by name; child's name; camp name; first and last session dates; amount received FOR THE CAMP (net of refunds; the card-fee line shown apart and excluded); whether overnight (the parent applies the weekly cap on lodging, which is $200, $125 or $275 a week by child, my arithmetic from the folio); the club's name and address; issue date; a named issuer. For a taxable camp also the club's GST/HST number and tax amount (Stage 6 Invoice details). Never a SIN; never our name or number.
- ⚠ **Do not call it a "child care receipt" or promise a claim.** CRA counts a day camp when "the primary goal of the camp is to care for children" (an all-day camp); where instruction and training dominate (a skills clinic), it is not child care (folio paras 1.17, 1.18). So: a plain "Receipt for camp fees" carrying the CRA fields, plus one quiet line: "Whether this counts toward a child care claim is up to you and CRA. They look at whether the camp mainly looks after children rather than teaches a skill." The plan's "use it for a child care claim" overpromises for a clinic.
- Services must fall in the claim year; do not issue before the sessions have run, or show the dates so a December payment for July is plain. Reissue (never edit) after a refund.
- **Québec RL-24 correction:** the RL-24 is issued by the PROVIDER (to the person who paid), and Revenu Québec says camps and day camps that qualify must issue it (search summary; the Revenu Québec page returned 403, so re-read before Québec opens). The product must never imply it issues or replaces an RL-24 and should not say "T778-ready". Québec stays closed; if a Québec club ever opens, this is a separate item.
- Signature: whether a typed issuer line and PDF suffice as "signature" for CRA is a CRA or accountant question; the page is for home daycares.

## 4. The club's books
- Heading **"Camps and clinics"** (money in), item **"Camp and clinic fees"**: fine; matches the Sponsorship pattern. Card fees to Payment processing; a passed-on family fee as in Stage 6.
- Exempt camp: income only, no tax lines. Taxable: income plus a "GST/HST collected" line (cash basis, as sponsorship; tax point is really earlier of invoice or payment, so for a prepaid camp it is the payment).
- **Early-bird: nothing special.** The early-bird price is the price; record what was received, no discount line. Only a reduction after payment is a refund.
- **Withdrawal refund:** Exempt: reduce income, no credit note needed (keep the note for the family anyway). Taxable: a credit note as in Stage 6 (ETA s.232; SOR/91-44 s.3), dated the refund day, tax proportional to the part refunded. Either way the family receipt is reissued net.
