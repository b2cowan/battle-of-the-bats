## 1. The offer email (tab 3, ask 13, F9)
- **It is a CEM, to a business too.** s.1(2): an offer to sell a service or to advertise or promote. A non-profit's sale is a "commercial activity" (no profit test). CASL has no business-to-business carve-out. Not relied on: SOR/2013-221 s.3(g) (registered charity, primary purpose raising funds): most minor-ball clubs are not registered charities, and selling advertising is a sale, not a request for a gift. s.3(a)(ii) (organization to organization where "the organizations have a relationship") needs a relationship the product cannot see; fallback only.
- **Sender.** s.6(1) bars sending *or causing or permitting to be sent*. Both the club and FieldLogicHQ are in reach (as `CASL_COACH_GROUP_EMAIL.md` finding 3). The identification rules need the sender and, if different, the person on whose behalf it is sent, a mailing address and one of phone/email/web. Position: the club is the sender for all purposes; FieldLogicHQ is the software and says so in an unlinked foot line. **Gap:** FieldLogicHQ has no mailing address on its own mail today (blank constant). Whether that matters when the club's address is present is a lawyer's-eye point; the controls below are the due-diligence answer.
- **Consent bases the club can honestly claim** (s.10(9)–(10)), the only ones the window should offer:
  1. **Existing business relationship:** the business bought from or paid the club (a past sponsorship) within the last **2 years**. (An *offer* that was never accepted is not a purchase.)
  2. **Inquiry:** the business asked about sponsoring within the last **6 months**.
  3. **Address given to the club** by the person (a business card) without saying they do not want such mail, and the offer is relevant to their business role.
  4. **Address conspicuously published** (their website, a directory) with no "no unsolicited messages" statement, and the offer is relevant to their business role.
  Not offered in version 1: **referral** (s.4 of the regulation needs the referrer's full name in the message and a "sent because of a referral" statement, and the referrer must have a relationship with both); a club with a referral hands over the link in person (Copy the link). A cold list has no basis: the window must refuse it.
- **Burden of proof is on the sender.** The product must record, per offer, which basis was ticked and the date (or the last paid date). Without that record the club has nothing to show and neither does FieldLogicHQ. The 2-year clock must be computed, not trusted: an old sponsor past 2 years with no other basis cannot be ticked in Offer again.
- **The six sponsors the club typed last year** (F5) carry **no basis**; migrating them must not mark them "relationship".
- **Content (s.6(2), SOR/2012-36):** club name, mailing address, a working unsubscribe, contact by email. The drawing has all four. The unsubscribe must be valid 60 days after sending (s.11(2)) and acted on within 10 business days (s.11(3)); the shipped tokens never expire and the confirm page writes at once (`CASL_UNSUBSCRIBE_CONFIRM_STEP.md`), so both are met. **The send must be blocked until the club's mailing address is set** (F2: the product has none today).
- **Scope of "an unsubscribed address can't be offered again": right, with these edges.** Per **club**, per **address**, **sponsorship offers only**: a new token family `club + address + ":unsubscribe-sponsor"` (the fifth, like `:stop-second-parent` in `CASL_SECOND_PARENT_EMAIL.md`), so a sponsor who is also a parent does not lose family email, and an invoice or receipt for a live deal still goes (not a CEM). The check runs the other way too: an address on the club's **family** opt-out list is not offered either (they refused that club's non-essential mail). The record must outlive the deal, the sponsor record and a deleted offer. A reply or call saying "stop" needs a club-side "Do not offer again" button on the sponsor, honoured at once (10-business-day limit applies to replies too). The club cannot clear a suppression; only the business can (a new inquiry from them is solicited, regulation s.3(b); the club hands the link over in person).
- **Penalty exposure:** up to $10M per violation for an organization (s.20(4), from memory); FieldLogicHQ as a sole proprietor is personally liable (`LEGAL_FACTS.md`).

## 2. The sponsor link page (tab 4)
- **What it collects** is business contact information: name, work email and phone, invoice address, website, a logo, the club's questions. PIPEDA s.4.01 takes business contact information out of Part 1 when it is used only to communicate in relation to the person's business. That is the **club's** exemption to rely on, not ours; we are its service provider. It fails for an individual sponsor ("The Ferreira family" is one in the drawing's own list) or a home address used for non-business purposes. So **no fixed privacy notice is needed**, consistent with `CLUB_FORM_NOTICE_REMOVAL.md` (the notice is the club's job; keep the club's link at the foot). The page already says why it wants each detail ("Your invoice and receipt go here"). One cheap change below.
- **A logo is someone else's property and the club will publish it.** The page needs a permission sentence, and Terms §5 ("do not upload anything you do not have the right to share") covers the upload only as the club's act. Add the sentence under the logo.
- **Public no-account page:** the link is a bearer link; it shows the business's contact details and receipt. Fine for the team pay link's class; "Send the link again" should replace the old link, and a withdrawn offer's link must show no business details.
- **Card wording** "Riverdale Minor Ball receives your payment. Your card is handled by Stripe." is true for a direct charge and matches `PAYMENTS_TERMS.md` §3 (the club is the seller; receipts and statements show its name). Two checks, not word changes: the statement descriptor must show the club's name before we say anything about the statement; and the **"Refund policy" link beside the pay button** (Payments Terms §6, "can read") is missing from the drawing's card version.
- **After pages:** fine. "Paid, thank you" and the receipt line carry no tax claim beyond the invoice.

## 3. The club's invoice (tab 6)
- "Invoice", "Thank you for sponsoring", the deliverables listed, and no "donation", "gift" or "tax receipt": **right.** A package with deliverables is a purchase; "gift" or "tax receipt" would also be wrong for a registered charity under the income-tax receipting rules. The paid invoice as the receipt: fine; do not add a Stripe receipt as a second document without saying so.
- **A registrant's (supplier's) invoice for a business buyer** claiming input tax credits generally needs the supplier's name and registration number, date, total and tax, the buyer's name and the payment terms; the drawing shows all. The product design risks: registration number captured with no format check; the rate chosen by the buyer's province (F3, the club's exposure if wrong; the drawing already says a professional confirms). Club tax is product work, not `/accounting`.
- **One sentence in Invoice details is a tax statement by us:** "Recognition only (a logo, a banner) usually isn't taxable, and a club under $50,000 a year doesn't have to register." The CRA treats advertising given in return for a sponsorship payment as a supply in many cases, and the registration threshold differs by kind of organization. Drop both claims (below). Route the correct sentence to `/accounting`.

## 4. F10 — the card fee passed to a business
`CARD_SURCHARGE_PAYER_FEE.md` supports the **recommendation**, not the reasoning, as drawn.
- Supports: a passed-on fee on a card is a surcharge-or-service-fee question the networks set a 2.4% ceiling on (and no more than the cost of acceptance), notice to the network, and a Québec ban; our card fee is 3.63% on the drawing's $1,130.00 example. Having the **club pay** on sponsorships keeps that whole question out of this stage, and is consistent with the owner's ruling (the passed-on fee is the club's own charge; the default for families unchanged): it does not reopen it.
- Does not support: "a business payer is where a surcharge is most visible". Nothing in the FCAC or network text found distinguishes a business cardholder. And "our card **rate** is above the 2.4% the networks allow" is loose: the cap is on a **surcharge** amount, which could be up to 2.4% if disclosed and not above cost. Fix the sentence in the drawing's tab 10 and F10 (internal, not customer-facing).
- **A consequence the drawing must carry:** ask 3 makes sponsorships always the "your organization pays it" mode, regardless of the club's Payouts choice. Payments Terms §4 describes the choice as the club's; it needs one sentence (below).
- Our application fee on a sponsor payment goes to `/accounting` as the drawing already says (F11).

## Part 4a Offer again — rulings 8A and 12A, read before any code (2026-11-25)
CASL s.10(10)–(14) re-read on laws-lois.justice.gc.ca today (current to 2026-10-29; the fetch capped quotes, so paraphrased). Code read: `reasonFloor`/`reasonHolds` (`lib/club-sponsorship-deals.ts` ~40–56), the payment route's date check (`deals/[dealId]/payments/route.ts` ~43: a day after today is refused), the dictionary's `club_sponsor_deal_payments` (`voided_at`, `returned_at`, `dispute_lost_at`), `club_sponsor_deal_refunds.ends_deal` and `club_sponsor_deals.paid_on`. Rulings: plan §5 Stage 6 "Part 4a".

**The basis is sound.** s.10(10)(a): a purchase "within the two-year period immediately before" the day the message is sent, by the recipient, from the sender. A sponsorship is a purchase of a service (advertising) from the club. Para (d) (a written contract) covers only matters *not* in (a) to (c), so a sponsorship rests on (a), not on the deal's paperwork. s.10(14)(a): for a purchase under "a subscription, account, loan or similar relationship" the period begins when that relationship ends; a season-long sponsorship *might* be read that way (the clock would start at the deal's last day, later than its payment). Not relied on. **Dating by the latest payment that still stands is the conservative reading**: a payment is the clearest evidence of a completed purchase. The one place it could run ahead of the purchase is a payment made long after the deal ended (a late cheque): a lawyer's-eye point, not a build change.

**What counts as a payment that stands** (the four edge cases asked):
- **Voided:** never counts (`voided_at` set).
- **Refunded in full / a dispute lost:** that payment never counts (`returned_at` set; the dispute loss sets it too). **A refund that ended the deal:** the deal is `withdrawn`, and none of its payments count. Since mig 530 a withdrawn deal can still *hold* live payment rows, so the filter must be on the deal's state as well as the payment's.
- **Partly refunded, or a part payment on an invoiced deal:** counts. The business did buy; a partial refund doesn't undo the purchase.
- **Merged businesses:** the product has no sponsor merge (checked). If one is built, payments from two records may be added together only when they are truly the same business (s.10(12) is about the *sender's* business being sold, not the recipient's records). A flag for that design.

### Must-fix build rules
1. **The server works out the date, at the moment of sending, for each row,** from this club's payments that stand (above), across all the business's deals at this club, received on or before the club's today. It never uses a date the browser posts; the window's "Paid us on Mar 3, 2027" only displays it. The window's Ready group and the send use the **same** function, and the send refuses any row that function says can't go, whatever the browser sent.
2. **Every check is read again at the press, for each row,** not when the window opened: the sponsorship stop, Do not offer again, the family opt-out, the two-year date, the mailing address and the contact. A business that unsubscribes between the window opening and Send is not emailed. The shared per-offer function in the build prompt gives this; the batch must not keep the bars from the window's open.
3. **One message per business address,** with its own To, its own unsubscribe link and the same foot as a single offer (club name, mailing address, contact, "You get this because you sponsored or paid {club} in the last two years.", the unlinked "Sent through FieldLogicHQ…" line). No CC, no BCC, no one email to many.
4. **Each sent email writes its own offer record** (`paid_recently`, the computed date, the sender's name as it was, the address, the club), after the email goes, as Part 2 does. A row whose email failed writes nothing and its deal is undone (ruling 10A).
5. **12A never pre-fills a reason that doesn't hold.** If the business's latest payment that stands is more than two years old, the button is not shown (ruling 14's "can't be ticked", read into 12A; the batch's Can't be emailed). When shown, the server works the date out again at Send, and a "paid" date the club types that is later than the latest payment on record is refused ("The last payment on record is Mar 3, 2027."). An earlier date is accepted (it only shortens the clock).

### Should-fix
- **A refund still pending that would end the deal:** its payments don't stand until the refund fails. Low exposure; a courtesy as much as a legal point.
- **A card payment with an open dispute:** doesn't count while the dispute is open (the business is contesting the purchase); counts again if the club wins. Owner call; recommend not counting.
- **Keep which payment the date rests on** with the offer record (its id beside the date). Date + business already lead back to it (payments are voided, never deleted); the id makes the proof one step. Optional.
- **Two of a business's deals ticked = two emails to one address in one press** (ruling 2A's two rows). Lawful, each with its own link; the window could say so. `/ux` and `/marketing`, not a legal requirement.
- **Owner call (the single Offer window):** apply rule 5's check (a typed "paid" date can't be later than the last payment on record) to every single offer of a business with payments in the product, not only the one opened from 12A. A business that paid before the club used the product has no record, so its typed date works as today. Recommend yes: it closes the gap between an unverified typed date and a computed one.

### What the batch does NOT need
No new foot, sender line or unsubscribe beyond the single offer's. CASL sets no sending rate (the throttle and the 100-business cap are for deliverability). No note, amount field or Make the link only in the batch (ruling 9A), so no free-text commercial content. No new personal information is collected: the decision uses payment records the club already keeps. The policy sentences owed (Privacy §5, §9, §12) are on Part 4b's close-out list; Offer again adds one clause to §5's draft: "and, for an offer sent to a past sponsor, the date of the payment it rests on."

### For a lawyer's eye (no gate, owner's choice)
(a) Whether the s.10(10)(a) "purchase" is dated by the agreement or the payment: matters only for a payment made long after the deal ended. (b) Whether a season-long sponsorship is a "similar relationship" under s.10(14)(a), which would start the clock at its last day (not relied on). (c) The edge at exactly two years: `reasonFloor` counts a payment made exactly two years before the sending day; "immediately before the day" arguably includes it.
