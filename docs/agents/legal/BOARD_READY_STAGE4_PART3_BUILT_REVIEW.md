## Question(s) this answers
Does what Part 3 built keep every rule the earlier reviews and the build prompt handed it? What must be true before Parts 1–3 reach production, and what may the policy and Data Terms say now?

## Verdict
**No MUST-FIX that stops the dev walk. Two MEDIUM findings to settle before the retention sentence is published (F1, F2), and two release conditions (mig 516; Parts 1–3 together).** Every ruled rule is kept in code; the weak points are two promises that are true by design but not on every failure.

## Findings
1. **F1 · MEDIUM — a removed file keeps its name in the database.** `club_form_files.file_name` is `NOT NULL` (mig 513:164). The clock sets `status='removed'` and empties the answer's values (`mig 518:191-203`) but never touches the name; a replaced or deleted-question file keeps it too. The staff screens are honest (they read a file only while `attached`, `lib/club-form-staff.ts:202`, and show "File removed {date}"), but family-chosen names carry health words ("…allergy…pdf"), so "removed" in a policy sentence would leave health words in a row. **Fix (to `/plan`, small):** overwrite `file_name` with a fixed word in the same UPDATE and in the removal trigger's `removed` branch (`mig 518:136-154`) and in the replace path. Settle before the §9 retention sentence publishes.
2. **F2 · MEDIUM — "each opening is recorded" fails open.** `openFamilyFile` makes the one-hour link, writes the audit line, and returns the link (`lib/club-form-staff.ts:308-312`); `writeOrgAudit` swallows a failed write and opens an issue for us (`lib/org-audit.ts:20-29`). The health export reads the answers and then writes its line (`registrations/answers/route.ts:23-24`) with the same swallow. So the screen note, the record's file note ("The link lasts an hour. Each opening is recorded in the audit log.", `staff-words.ts:111`) and the new policy/Data Terms sentences are true by design and by the log's normal behaviour, not on a failed write. Same class as the pay-link caveat (leftover 9b). **Owner call:** (a) fail closed for these two actions (refuse the link or the export if the line did not write: needs a variant of `writeOrgAudit` that reports failure), recommended because the line is the only accountability for health data leaving the system; or (b) keep as built and let the sentence say "is recorded" (what it says now).
3. **F3 · LOW — the 90-day sentences vs the 120-day case and a reopen.** (i) The finish confirm's "Family files and health answers are removed 90 days after the season is finished." (`lib/house-league-status-words.ts:192`) is honest for the finish path (the confirm is the finish) **only when mig 516 is running**; today nothing runs it (see release condition 2). It need not name the 120-day case. Optional addition: "Reopening it later won't bring them back." (ii) The two removed notes ("Health answers are removed 90 days after the season finishes." / "Family files are removed 90 days after the season finishes.", `staff-words.ts:117-118`) show only after a removal has happened, so the unscheduled job cannot make them false, but they are wrong for a season the backstop removed (never marked finished: 120 days after its last day). Suggested: "Health answers are removed once a season is over." / "Family files are removed once a season is over." or the long form "…90 days after a season is finished, or 120 days after its last day if it is never finished." (iii) **Reopen surprise:** reopening clears `finished_at` (mig 377:80); a season whose last day passed more than 120 days ago then falls to the backstop (`mig 518:185-187`) and loses its files and health answers at the next sweep. Consider a reopen line or skipping seasons reopened within the last 90 days.
4. **F4 · LOW — "Agreed to documents on {date}" says more than it knows for a form with several documents.** It is true when any tick reads "I have read and agree" (`lib/club-form-tick-summary.ts:35-45`); a family who only "read" the waiver and "agreed" to a code of conduct reads as having agreed to documents. Part 1's finding 1 ("Waiver accepted" for any document) is fixed in the three staff readers (`lib/child-page.ts:192-196`, `lib/children-duplicates-read.ts:126`, `lib/families-export.ts:185-186`); the residue is this aggregate. Acceptable; "Agreed to a document" is more exact.
5. **F5 · INFO — the plain exports are not logged, and the scope note had gone stale.** Confirmed: only the opt-in health export and a file opening write a line (`lib/member-audit.ts` `FORM_AUDIT_ACTIONS`); the build prompt's drawing line "each export is already recorded" was false before this Part (open since `AUDIT_LOG_SCOPE_WORDING.md`). **Applied 2026-11-20:** the Audit log screen's scope note now lists the two new actions and says "other exports" and "changes to family records" are not listed (words only, `components/admin/kit/club/AuditLogKit.tsx:145-151`); the file note and policy sentences never say every export is recorded.
6. **F6 · INFO — the audit log is owner-only, the people who open files are registrars.** The note "recorded in the audit log" is true (owner reads it; admins and registrars cannot, the owner's need-to-know ruling 2026-11-19). A registrar is told a record exists that they cannot open. Not a defect; the same pattern as the pay-link lines.
7. **F7 · INFO — IP addresses: the published policy over-described.** "A record of the consents given, including when and the internet (IP) address" (published `privacy/2026-11-17.md` §5, ~L82). Checked: the house-league waiver and email-consent ledger rows hold no IP (`lib/org-people.ts:45-108`); the new document ticks hold none (mig 513, no column); a tryout registration does (`consent_ip`, `app/api/rep-teams/.../tryouts/[yearId]/register/route.ts:126`); a guardian account's agreement on accepting an invitation does too (`lib/family-invitation.ts:179,205`, `app/api/family/claim/[token]/route.ts:45`). The draft is corrected (below). **Open for when the guardian tier is switched on:** the guardian wording and the policy need to say the IP is kept there (`GUARDIAN_CONSENT_STAGE4.md`).

## For a lawyer's eye (flag only; no gate)
The six from the build prompt: a club-written health question beside our fixed notice; Ontario health card numbers (the builder warns, nothing blocks); Québec Law 25 for a Québec family answering health questions; the tick plus the server hand-over as evidence; our liability if a health answer or file leaked; a public no-login endpoint that takes files. **New from Part 3:**
- **The opt-in health export is a file leaving our control.** Once downloaded, the Excel file sits on the registrar's computer outside every retention rule we can promise; the hint ("Keep it private.") and the log line are the only measures. Whether the Data Terms should place a duty on the club for downloaded copies.
- **The audit log names a child.** The line stores ids only, but the owner's screen and its Excel/CSV export print "{child name}, {season}" read at that moment (`audit/route.ts:201`). A downloaded log is a second copy of a child's name.
- **A removed file's name persists (F1)** and a cleared health answer leaves a "changed on {date}" mark by design.
- **What the clock leaves.** The 90/120-day clock removes health answers and files; every other answer, the ticks, the sign-up record and the guardian email stay until the registration is deleted (L9, retention of player information, remains open). A lawyer may ask whether "limited retention" (PIPEDA Principle 4.5) is met for the rest.
- **Access by our own staff.** The files sit in our private bucket under the service role; our staff could open them. The policy's staff-access paragraph and the new sentences ("the club's staff who can open its registrations can read the answers") should not be read as "only".
- **Tick plus "link opened" as evidence of agreement** in a dispute: the tick is the family's own statement; the link opening proves our server served the file, not that a person read it.
- **IP on a guardian account's agreement** (F7) when the guardian tier is switched on.
