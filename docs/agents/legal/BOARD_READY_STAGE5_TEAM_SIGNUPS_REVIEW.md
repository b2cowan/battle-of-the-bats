## Finding 1 — a coach entering other people's information, and food needs

**Part A (own teams): the roster names are the club's own information.** The club already holds the players (and any medical notes, emergency contacts, dates of birth on them). Ticking players from the roster for a club event is the club using its own rep team's list for the club's own purpose, with the same people who already run the team. No new notice is owed for that. What is NOT fine is the event list quietly reaching back into the roster for more than a name.
- **Rule A1: the event list takes the player's name from the roster and nothing else.** No date of birth, no medical note, no emergency contact, no allergy field copied across. A roster medical note is given for the team's safety; an event is a different purpose (clause 4.5, limiting use; the same reasoning as `CLUB_WEBSITE_E9_PHOTO_QUESTION_REVIEW.md`).
- **Rule A2: ticking a child is the family's yes, collected by the team, not by the product.** The coach says who is coming after the families told the team. The page says so (words below), so the coach does not read a tick as the product asking the parents.
- **Rule A3: adults typed by the coach are names only, adult or child, no email** (as ruled in round 1 for sign-ups).

**Allergies: do not ask them on a team sign-up, at all, in v1.** Reasons, in order of weight:
1. Health information is sensitive (PIPEDA clause 4.3.4; the OPC treats it as needing express consent: `MINORS_IMAGES_AND_CLUB_FEED.md`, OPC #2025-005). The person typing is a coach or manager, not the person, and for a child not the guardian. The coach cannot give that consent, and the product cannot show the subject the purpose.
2. A coach typing an adult's allergy is a coach holding a health record about a colleague or a parent on a phone, with no notice, retention or access path for that person.
3. It breaks the structure round 2 built. Round 2's allergy question sits on a sign-up where the payer is told what it is for and the club reads it only through the logged health-answers export. A team list has no single person to tell.
4. It is a safety question as much as a legal one: leaving it out means the club will NOT know someone's food needs unless the person tells it. The honest answer is to say that plainly, not to hide it.

**The lightest honest way (recommended):** the team list carries names, adult or child, and the club's ordinary Meal choice (a dish the caterer offers; set the dish names yourself, not diets, because "vegetarian", "halal", "kosher" can point to belief: round 2 finding 5). One fixed note, shown to the coach on the list and repeated in the confirmation, tells everyone how to tell the club:
- On the list: "**We don't ask food allergies on a team list.** Each person coming, or a parent for a child, tells {Club} their own food needs by {date}: {club contact}. {Club} will not know unless they do."
- Never promise "the caterer will accommodate", and never tell the coach "we'll ask everyone for you".

**Optional heavier path (ask for it only if the owner wants it, default OFF):** a team-level "Food needs" link the coach drops in the team's chat; each person opens it, types their own name and food needs on a health-marked form that shows the club's notice, and reads nothing back. It is the only shape where the person speaks for themselves, but it is a new public submit-only surface (spam, a bearer link in a chat) and needs the health-answers export, the retention clock and a "who can read" line. Not for v1. The coach-types-a-yes/no-flag shortcut ("has allergies") is NOT acceptable: it is still health information about someone else, typed by someone else.

**Children (the "name of a child" question).** A name plus "child" plus a team and an event is information about an identifiable minor but carries no health or contact data; the roster path (A) has it already. For an OUTSIDE team (B) the club has no relationship with the child or the family: **take first name and last initial for a child**, full names for adults (a name tag or head count needs no more; round 2's camp reason for a full child name was a child-care claim, which a social event does not make). Owner call: full names are acceptable if the club wants them; the minimum is the recommendation. Québec: a child under 14 needs a parent's consent (Law 25, text not opened); no Québec team is excluded from a free invitation, but no paid offer is made to Québec (payouts refused), so a free-only Québec team is the only live case. Lawyer's eye if one appears.

## Finding 2 — the outside buyer listing people the club has no relationship with (B)

- **Who the club is to them:** the organization collecting the names (it decides the purpose); FieldLogicHQ hosts the page as the club's service provider (Data Terms). The buyer is the club's contact for the team, not the club's customer for the whole team.
- **Collection through another person (indirect) is workable when the lister vouches for it.** PIPEDA needs the person's knowledge and consent for a collection, and consent can be reached through someone authorized to speak for them; here it is only a head count the people already agreed to join. So the page does what the second-parent flow does: **a required tick, recorded as a yes with the date, no IP**, and a notice above the list.
- **Notice above the list (exact words, braces are fills):** "**{Club} is collecting these names to plan {Event} and to contact your team about it.** Only list people who know they are on this list. {Club} deletes the names {60} days after the event." Tick: "**The people on this list know I am giving their names to {Club} for this event.**" Do not add a second paragraph of privacy text; the club's own privacy link stays at the foot as Terms §4 promises.
- **Retention (the clock must take team lists):** names and Meal choices of people the club has no other relationship with are deleted **60 days after the event** (the house-league friend-request clock is "finished or 60 days after its last day"; same idea, `FRIEND_REQUESTS_PRIVACY.md`). Counts, totals, the payment, and the buyer's name and email stay with the payment record (tax and refund record; `/accounting` for how long). The invited-team contact the club typed (an address of a stranger) is deleted with the event, except the suppression entry (address only) that must outlive it (finding 4). Round 2 says names and meals stay "while the organization exists"; that was right for a family's own sign-up and is the wrong default for strangers. Round 1's clock (mig 516) must take event forms for this to be true; **no retention sentence in the policy before the sweep runs** (the same rule as every earlier release condition).
- **Access and removal:** a listed person who asks the club to remove their name must be removable by the club in the event window ("Remove from the list"), and the buyer can remove a name from their own link until the cut-off. The count and any amount change by the club's own refund rule.
- **The buyer's own information** (name, email, the team's name): billing contact, the same as any family sign-up.
- **Policy text owed (next versions, nothing before the feature ships):** Privacy §5 gets one sentence: "If a coach or manager signs a team up for a club's event, they give us the names of the people on the team. We keep those names, with the team and the event, for 60 days after it, for the club. We ask the person who lists them to make sure they know." §9 the retention sentence (after mig 516). Data Terms §12 the same. Terms: nothing.

## Finding 3 — the per-team link and "Only people with the link"

**Honest only as "not listed", never as "private".** The sponsor link is the model (`BOARD_READY_STAGE6_SPONSORSHIPS_DRAWING_REVIEW.md` §2: a bearer link; "Send the link again" replaces the old one; a withdrawn link shows no details) and the Track P review has the traps (a pasted link shows its page title and share card; the code on a card is a bearer secret).
- **Words for the choice and for the link, verbatim:**
  - Choice: "**Only people with the link.** Not listed on your website, or in search. Anyone who has the link can open it."
  - Under the Copy-the-link button: "**Send this only to people you want at the event.** Anyone who is sent it can pass it on."
  - On the per-team page header: "This invitation is for {Team}." (name the team, not a person).
  - Never use: "private", "secure", "invite only", "only {Team} can open this".
- **Build rules (must hold or the words are false):** `noindex` and `X-Robots-Tag`; **no robots.txt line and no sitemap entry** (a robots line would publish the event; Track P finding); excluded from the club website's News and Open now rows, the calendar feed, the share image, the club home and any "Also on these schedules" list (E11/E12); **the invited team's name is NOT in the page title or share card** (a link pasted in a group chat shows the preview).
- **What the per-team page shows a holder:** the event details and the team's own list as first names (adults) and first name plus last initial (children), counts, the total. **Never any other team's name or list.** A holder can change the list until the cut-off; the page after the cut-off or a stop shows "This invitation has ended." and nothing else.
- **Stop and Send again:** Send again replaces the old link (the old one stops at once); a stopped link shows no event details and no names. Link life: until 30 days after the event, as ruled 18A. The link carries no name and no email in the URL.
- **Forwarding is the buyer's act, not ours** (same rule as Track P): no forward button, no "send to a colleague" box, no collection of further addresses.
- **Rate limit** the link as the sponsor and Track P codes do (per link and per visitor), because the team list is a write.

## Finding 4 — emails

**An invitation to an outside team's contact.**
- **When it is a CEM.** CASL s.1(2): a message that encourages participation in a commercial activity. A paid event (a ticket price, or a bill to the team) is a commercial activity; a non-profit's sale counts (no profit test) and there is no business-to-business carve-out (`BOARD_READY_STAGE6_SPONSORSHIPS_DRAWING_REVIEW.md` §1). **Treat the invitation as a CEM whenever the event has a price or the team will be charged.** A purely free social invitation is most likely not a CEM on its face, but the free/paid line is the club's to set at the press, so build the one path for both: sender line, club's mailing address, a stop, a record.
- **The club is the sender; FieldLogicHQ is the software** (same position as the sponsor offer and group email: unlinked foot "Sent through FieldLogicHQ, the software {Club} uses."). **Send is blocked until the club has set its mailing address** (the same rule as the sponsor offer, F2).
- **Consent bases the club can honestly tick** (s.10(9)–(10), read today): (1) an existing business relationship, which includes a **purchase within the last two years** (s.10(10)(a)) — the outside team's coach who paid the club's tournament entry fee fits; **an inquiry within six months** (s.10(10)(e)); (2) the address was **given to the club** by the person (s.10(9)(c)) or **conspicuously published** with no "no unsolicited messages" statement (s.10(9)(b)), and **the message is relevant to their role** (a team manager invited to a team event: yes; a parent: no). **Not available:** an "existing non-business relationship" (s.10(13)): it needs a donation to a registered charity, volunteer work or a meeting organized by the sender, or **membership in a club**; an opposing team's coach is none of these, and a game is not a meeting the club organized. **No referral basis in v1.** A team the club has never dealt with gets the **Copy the link** route, and the club hands it over in person, by its own message or by phone.
- **Reuse the sponsor machinery, do not build a second:** the basis ticked and dated per invitation (the burden of proof is on the sender, s.13); the two-year clock computed on the server, not trusted; one message per address; "Do not invite again" honoured at once; a stop link per club and address that outlives the event; the family opt-out is respected (an address that refused that club's non-essential mail is not invited). Owner call: one shared "events and offers" suppression for sponsors and teams, or separate (recommended: **one list per club and address for "offers and invitations"**, so a contact who stopped one stops both; a family's stop is separate).
- **Content of the email:** the club's name and mailing address, the event, the team's link, a working stop line (valid 60 days, acted on within 10 business days: s.11), a contact by email. **No other event, no "bring a friend", no logo-heavy marketing.** Names of people never appear in an invitation (there are none yet).
- **Tournament invitation precedent: none found.** No tournament-side invitation email is in `LEGAL_INDEX.md`, and none was checked in the code. Part C's invitation is new ground and takes the same path.

**The email to the buyer after the list is saved.** Transactional (the round 2 class: confirms what was bought; most likely not a CEM while it adds no promotion; the club's name, Reply-To to the club). **It carries the counts and prices, not the people's names**, because an email is forwarded and sits in an inbox; the list lives on the team's link. For a paid sign-up it is the buyer's copy of the agreement (round 2 MUST CHANGE 1) and stands alone.

**The club's notice to its own coaches about an offered event (A).** Most likely not a CEM: the same class as the late-bill email and the group email to coaches (`CASL_LATE_BILL_EMAIL.md`, `CASL_COACH_GROUP_EMAIL.md`: it concerns the organization's own activity, sent inside it; SOR/2013-221 s.3(a)(i) is the fallback exit, and "representative" for volunteers is the open point already flagged there). Send it as a bell and the usual notice through the existing path, to the head coach and the staff the seat reaches; no price pitch, no "sell more tickets" line, no link to anything but the portal. Do not mark it "transactional" as the reason: s.6(6) lifts consent, not the stop line.

## Finding 5 — consumer protection and "one buyer, many attendees"

- **A, the club billing its own team: not a consumer sale.** The team is the club's own rep team; the bill moves between the club's and the team's books on the existing path (`We've sent it`, the club confirms). The head coach acts for the team, not for personal purposes, so the Consumer Protection Act, 2002 (an individual acting for personal, family or household purposes) does not reach it, and nothing in round 2's list applies to this bill. The late-bill words stay. **Product rules:**
  - **Only the head coach (the seat that can sign the bill) commits the team**; other staff may tick names and add adults but the page says "{Head coach} sends the bill." A bill is a financial commitment on the team's books.
  - **The bill states what it is for**: "{Event}: {n} adults × $X, {n} children × $Y = $Z." and the date until which the list can change. After that date the club decides; the bill is not "paid in full by the team" in a way the club can't adjust.
  - **No payment or refund words aimed at families.** The team collects from its families itself, outside the product, and the product must not become the seller to those families. No "Pay here" for a parent; no family-facing receipt; no seller line, because nothing is being sold to a consumer through FieldLogicHQ. If the club or the team wants families to pay online, that is round 2's own sign-up, a different path. Tax on the bill → `/accounting`.
- **B, an outside team that pays: round 2's rules apply in full**, because the person paying is an individual who may be paying with their own card and recovering it from parents (can't be told apart). The seller line, the all-in total before the processing fee, the club's refund policy open on the page, the review step, the confirmation email that is the copy of the agreement, the cancel path: all unchanged, with these changes for one-buyer-many-people:
  - Seller line, verbatim, unchanged: "**Sold by {Club}. FieldLogicHQ provides this page and is not part of the sale.**"
  - Lines count people, not names: "{n} adults × $40.00 · {n} children × $15.00". **No name, meal or allergy ever goes to Stripe** (round 2 rule 5).
  - **Refunds go to the card that paid, to the buyer, as one amount.** Say so: "**Any refund goes back to the card that paid. {Club} does not refund individual people.**" Whether a person who drops out gets their share back is between the buyer and the people they collected from.
  - **Change until the cut-off:** "You can change this list until {date}. After that, ask {Club}." The club decides after.
  - **Cancellation by the club:** unchanged from round 2 (the club presses the notice; "It will refund what you paid, back the way you paid").
  - Whether the Ontario internet-agreement rules apply to a volunteer club selling a team ticket to a coach is **not settled** (round 2 flag); build for all.
- **C:** the existing tournament registration already carries whatever terms it has; this review does not re-read it.

## Finding 6 — what the drawing must NOT do

1. **No allergy, medical, date of birth or emergency field anywhere on a team list**, and nothing copied from the roster beyond the name (A1).
2. **No photo question on a team list.** A coach cannot answer for a guardian; a person on a team list is **"no photo"** by default (round 1/E9: blank = no). A club that wants photos at the event asks its own way.
3. **No automatic matching across lists.** A family that signed up and a team list with the same name must not be silently merged or de-duplicated by name (the Stage 14 rule: a person joins only by a fact or their own yes). Show the club a count warning "{n} names appear on both a team list and a family sign-up (matched by name only, not merged)", and let the club decide. The same applies to counts in the "{n} spots left" figure: count each list's people and say so.
4. **No other team's name or list on any team link; no total of "teams signed up" on a public page.** The club sees the invited / signed up / not answered board; a team never does.
5. **No marketing in an invitation or confirmation**; no calendar link, no event-page link to other events.
6. **No sharing or pre-filling a team contact from another event, tournament or sponsor record without a basis**: a contact the club typed for a sponsor is not an invitation basis for a team event.
7. **No coach-facing wording that treats a child's tick as the parent's consent** ("the parent has agreed").
8. **No name in a URL, a page title, a share card, a push line or a bell line** for an outside person; first name and last initial at most inside the page behind the link.
9. **No "invite only" or "private" claim for a link anyone can open** (finding 3). Part C: a tournament with **one shared link** says "Not listed"; only a tournament with **one link per team that works for that team's slot** may say "By invitation". Never both on one setting.
10. **Do not let the team's coach add the people's emails or phone numbers** (round 1 asked names only); a team-level contact for the buyer is all.
11. **Do not copy the outside list's names into any of the club's other records** (families book, Find a child, house-league registrations). It is the event's, and goes with the event's clock.
