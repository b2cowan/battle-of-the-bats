## Question(s) this answers
Commit 64bb5544d: a club admin with Accounting bills a finalized schedule's teams later; each team's coaches get one email and one in-app notice. (1) CASL status and whether the envelope identifies enough. (2) Is the wording safe, and does it bind a coach personally. (3) Privacy beyond the Finalize bill email. (4) A note for the club in the Send window.

## Finding
1. **CASL: most likely not a commercial electronic message at all, and say that, not "transactional so no unsubscribe".** s.1(2) asks whether, reading the content, links and contact details, one purpose is to encourage participation in a commercial activity. A statement of what a team now owes for time it already got, with no offer, advert or promotion and links only to the team's own Money tab, does not. If it is not a CEM, s.6 is not engaged: no consent, no identification block, no unsubscribe. ⚠ **Correction to how this is usually put:** s.6(6) ("confirms a transaction previously agreed", "factual information about an ongoing … account … or similar relationship") lifts **only** the consent requirement, paragraph 6(1)(a). Paragraph 6(1)(b) stays, so a CEM that fits s.6(6) **still needs** the s.6(2) identification, contact information and unsubscribe mechanism. So the safe position rests on "not a CEM", and it holds only while the email stays free of any promotional content. "Existing business relationship" (s.10, implied consent) is not needed on this reading.
   - **If a regulator read it as a CEM anyway:** the two exits are the organization-to-organization exclusion (Electronic Commerce Protection Regulations SOR/2013-221 s.3(a)(ii): recipient is a representative of another organization, the organizations have a relationship, and the message concerns the recipient organization's activities) and the legal-obligation exclusion (s.3(c): "to provide notice of an existing or pending right, legal … obligation"). Neither is clean: a club-owned team is arguably not "another organization", and a team's bill to its club is a debt in a loose sense. Do not rest on them.
2. **Identification.** If CASL applied, the form regulation (SOR/2012-36 s.2(1)) needs the sender's name, who it is sent on behalf of, a mailing address and a phone, email or web address. The email names the club in its first line, carries the FieldLogicHQ wordmark, and replies reach the admin who pressed Send; it carries no mailing address. On the "not a CEM" reading none of that is required. **No change recommended**, and nothing should be added that would make the email look like marketing.
3. **Wording: "billed … for the time you got".** The first line bills the **team** ("{Club} has billed {team}"), which is right: the debtor in the product is the team's book, and the club is named as the creditor in the same line. The list title said "for the time **you** got", under a greeting to one person; read alone it sounds personal. **Fixed today** to "the time your team got" (the same line is in the Finalize and review emails; wording only). The email creates no contract and no new right: the amount and dates are the club's own terms; whether the team owes them is between the club and the team (the club's own arrangement, outside FieldLogicHQ). Say who it is owed to: already done by "{Club} has billed". **How to dispute: the email gives nothing,** and the late bill skips the review step the Finalize bill had (no "Ask for a change" before it is made). No law requires a dispute line here (a volunteer coach acting for a team is not a consumer buying from the club). It is a fairness and support point: owner question 2.
4. **Privacy: no new data kind, one real defect.** Personal information in the email is the recipient's first name, the team's name and the club's charges: the same as the Finalize bill email. The club decides to send and supplies the people; FieldLogicHQ sends on the club's instructions (service-provider role); Resend is already listed (policy §6). Law 25 is not engaged until a Québec club exists. **The defect:** the audience is "head coach and staff with schedule access" (`schedulePeople`, `lib/club-schedule-send.ts:174-190`, filter `canViewSchedule`), but money terms belong to people who hold **Money** access (`canViewMoney`, `lib/coach-capabilities.ts:551`). The **Assistant** and **Helper** presets hold schedule with money `off` (`coach-capabilities.ts:268-271, 317-319`). So a game-day helper whose head coach deliberately withheld Money gets the amounts and dates by email, with a sentence ("The bill is on **your** Money › Club tab") that is false for them and a link that goes nowhere. That is a need-to-know failure (PIPEDA principle 4.7, safeguards by sensitivity and access; and the policy's own "people see what their role opens" promise) and a broken product promise. The same is true of the bell (`scheduleUserIds`, `:193`) and, since before this commit, of the Finalize bill email. Not changed (send logic); build item below. **BUILT 2026-11-13** — committed `507f4505b` (the send engine) and `39eb59955` (the Send window); the owner's walk is owed (OWNER_QA_LEDGER §527).
5. **The Send window.** `lateBillStatement` said where each share lands and that the schedule stays final. It did not say coaches are emailed, or that replies reach the admin. **Added today:** "…and its coaches are emailed the bill; replies come to you." Both halves are true as built (recipient list, `replyTo` the sender).
